# Rhythm

The quietest tell and the hardest to fake. Machine prose is metrically flat: when every sentence weighs the same, nothing is emphasized, and a reader loses the thread without being able to say why.

## Detect

### Flat cadence

- Sentence lengths clustered in one band, nearly everything between fifteen and twenty-five words.
- Paragraphs of uniform size, three or four sentences each, all the way down.
- Every sentence subject-verb-object. No subordination, no inversion, no question.
- Nothing under eight words anywhere. No fragment on the page.
- Sentences opening with the same connective: `Additionally`, `Moreover`, `Furthermore`.
- Triads used as cadence rather than because there are three of something.

### Fixed footing

- One tense held across material that changed in time.
- One person held with unnatural consistency, never shifting for an aside.
- Uniform distance from the subject — no moment closer, none further back.

### Overload

- A sentence carrying more clauses than it earns, patched with commas, dashes, and semicolons.
- Parentheses holding material the sentence could not fit.
- Three or more stacked modifiers before a noun.
- A dangling modifier — the opening phrase attached to nothing that follows.
- Two independent clauses joined by a semicolon where two sentences were the honest form.

## Write

Vary length on purpose. If every sentence in a paragraph lands in the same fifteen-to-twenty-five-word band, the cadence has gone flat — break it with something short or something long. Treat this as a diagnostic, not a quota. Do not mint a four-word sentence to hit a count. `restraint` governs.

A short sentence after a long one carries weight. So does a short sentence that opens, as a thesis. Put it where the emphasis should fall.

Fragments are permitted. So is a sentence that runs long and picks up clauses as it goes, provided a reader can still follow the thread to the end of it.

One idea per sentence is the default. Every clause spends the reader's attention, and once a sentence has spent more than it earns, no punctuation rescues it. Split it in two — or three.

Treat punctuation density as a symptom. When a sentence needs a comma, a dash, and a parenthesis to stand up, the construction went wrong earlier than where you are patching it. Start it over.

Let paragraph length vary too. A one-line paragraph is emphasis, not an error.

Move. Shift tense when the footing changes, drop into first person for an aside, pull closer for a detail and back for a judgment. Holding one position throughout is what flat prose does.

Read it aloud. Where you run out of breath, cut.

## Examples

**Uniform cadence**

> The company was founded in 2011 and initially focused on enterprise software. It expanded into consumer products in 2015 after raising a Series B round. The consumer division grew quickly and now accounts for most of the company's revenue. Analysts expect this trend to continue over the next several years.

Four sentences, 18-22 words each, all subject-verb-object. Nothing is wrong. Nothing is alive.

> The company started in 2011 selling enterprise software. Then, in 2015, flush with Series B money and impatient with a market that took eighteen months to close a single deal, it went after consumers instead. That side of the business now brings in most of the revenue. Analysts expect more of the same.

**Sentence carrying more than it earns**

> The proposal, which was submitted in March after several delays caused by staffing shortages, would, if approved by the board at its June meeting, raise fees by 4%.

> The proposal would raise fees by 4%. It went in during March, delayed by staffing shortages, and the board votes in June.

**Punctuation as patch**

> The grant — awarded in 2021 (the program's second year) — funded three positions; two were later cut.

> The grant came in 2021, the program's second year, and funded three positions. Two were later cut.

**Stacked modifiers**

> A community-led early-intervention family-support initiative.

> A support program for families, run locally, that steps in early.

**Dangling modifier**

> Having reviewed the figures, the discrepancy was obvious.

> Having reviewed the figures, we could not miss the discrepancy.
