# Domain: Non-Fiction

Long-form true prose. The essay, memoir, biography and history, criticism, literary journalism. Chicago mechanics, and `domains/citation/chicago18.md` wherever the piece carries notes.

Non-fiction and fiction share nearly every technique in the craft — scene, summary, psychic distance, withheld information, the ordering of what a reader learns and when. One thing separates them. Here the writer is answerable for what the sentence asserts, and there the writer is not. A reader who opens a memoir has agreed to believe you, and that agreement is the only thing the genre has.

So the temptation is exact and technical. The sentence would be better if the detail were slightly different — if the rain had come on the Tuesday, if the aunt had said the harder version of what she said, if the crowd had been four thousand rather than eleven hundred. Every one of those improvements is a lie. That is the whole ethical content of this domain, and it does not become smaller when the improvement is small.

A source-by-source inventory can fail a task that asks for analysis, even if its citations are real. A requested survey or annotated bibliography is still legitimate. The source dossiers discuss limited observations of this problem, not a universal mechanism of machine writing.

A comparison should illuminate the subject and suit the voice. It can draw on a shared reference, an explained analogy or an author's genuine experience; it need not pretend that the author personally lived it. Do not invent memories or researched detail to make figurative prose sound authentic.

## On the shared toolkit

Everything fiction can do, this genre can do, under one constraint: the reader must be able to tell what kind of sentence they are reading.

Use scene where it serves the piece. Reconstruct only as far as the grounds and publication policy permit, explaining the method and any material local uncertainty. A general memory disclaimer does not authorize fabricated dialogue or imply a verbatim record.

Compression can summarize several visits without turning them into one factual afternoon. Preserve the actual chronology or explicitly identify a permitted reconstruction; disclosure does not override a venue's prohibition. A composite character must not be presented as a real person. Use an honest summary when the record cannot support a scene.

Interiority is where the line runs cleanest. Your own head is open to you. Another person's is open only on evidence — a diary, an interview, a letter they wrote at the time. A thought attributed to a subject with no source behind it is fiction with a footnote attached, and the footnote makes it worse.

An argumentative essay should make its supported claim clear. A memoir, descriptive account or lyrical ending need not state a moral or force a verdict. Preserve the task's deliberate narrative distance and openness.

## Detect

### Argument

- Research delivered without analysis when the task asks for an argument, rather than a legitimate requested survey.
- The manufactured closer: a concession built on praise, then a reassurance or a forecast. *Despite its considerable achievements, the movement faced several challenges.* Real difficulties are worth writing about; this is the shape reached for when there is nothing to say.
- Both-sides balance from a writer who has a view and is declining to spend it.
- A thesis announced in the opening and never put at risk.
- Chronology standing in for causation. This happened, then this happened, and the reader is left to supply the *because*.

### Sourcing

- A quotation with no locatable origin.
- Attribution folded into the passive so nobody is answerable: *the decision was reached*, *the letter was understood to mean*.
- Consensus asserted rather than shown: *scholars agree*, *it is widely believed*, *most historians now hold*.
- A rounded figure presented as exact, or a precision choice that obscures the comparison. An honestly labeled approximation can be useful.
- A note that supports the sentence beside the one it is attached to.
- An archive cited as a collection when the claim needs a box and a folder.

### Biography and history

- Notability argued by cataloging coverage — *profiled in*, *independent coverage*, *national outlets*, *maintains an active social media presence*. The subject disappears behind the evidence that the subject matters.
- The label written about instead of the thing: *X refers to a period during which*, *is a curated compilation of*.
- Motive imported backward from a later century.
- Every gap in the record filled and none of them marked.

### Memoir and criticism

- Figures that are plausible rather than specific or shared.
- Retrospective omniscience: the child at seven understanding precisely what the writer at fifty understands.
- The redemptive arc bolted on, so that everything before the last page turns out to have been for something.
- Other people rendered as functions of the narrator, with no life outside the scenes they appear in.
- Evaluation smuggled in as description — *ambitious*, *uneven*, *self-indulgent* — with nothing in the piece that earns it.

## Write

For an argument, make the claim accessible where the structure supports it. Do not require every nonfiction form to announce a thesis or forbid one in the opening.

Verify wording and support in the source, using an available locator. A familiar quotation is not self-verifying. If the original cannot be checked, disclose the limitation rather than claim retrieval or invent a page.

Name who says. *A colleague described him as difficult* is worth almost nothing; *his deputy at the Bureau, in a 1974 oral history, called him difficult to work under* is worth the sentence.

Where the record is thin, identify the actual limit and what evidence would help. *The supplied collection lacks those two years of letters* does not establish that nobody has found them anywhere.

Use figures that clarify the subject, with enough explanation for the reader to follow. Preserve deliberate voice without inventing personal experience.

End where the piece has done its work: on a supported claim, an image, a consequence or an open question appropriate to the form. Do not add a universal significance statement just to sound finished.

## Mechanics

Chicago's prose conventions, unless the field the piece is written for sets another. The citation forms live in `domains/citation/chicago18.md`.

- Notes-bibliography for narrative work, author-date for argument that cites on nearly every line. Pick one and hold it for the whole piece.
- Use the applicable Chicago/venue guidance for block quotations, with indentation and quotation marks handled accordingly. No universal word/line threshold has been verified for this module.
- Three spaced periods for an omission inside a quotation; brackets for anything you supply; `[sic]` only where a reader would otherwise think the error is yours.
- Never alter a quotation silently to fit the syntax of your sentence. Recast your sentence.
- Serial comma. Title case for titles of works in running text, italicized if the work stands alone and in quotation marks if it is a part.
- Spell out whole numbers through one hundred and their round multiples, and use numerals above that — looser than AP, and the reason a piece cannot switch manuals halfway.
- Attribute an epigraph accurately and follow the venue's reference and permissions requirements; do not invent missing source fields.
- Quoting a living writer at length raises a permissions question that a correct citation does not answer. `legal` governs that question. Raise it early, because it can change a chapter.

## Formats

### Essay

Follow a question or idea with enough development to make the piece worthwhile. A turn can be useful, but an essay need not pretend the author changed their mind. Digressions should contribute rather than merely fill a length.

### Memoir

The narrator is a character and is not the writer. That gap is the form's whole instrument: what the person on the page believed at the time, set against what the person writing knows now.

Memory has limits and can coexist with documents and other witnesses. Explain the evidence being used and distinguish recollection from a transcript. Do not present reconstructed dialogue as verbatim or treat a general disclaimer as permission for invention.

### Biography and history

Choose a structure that makes the subject intelligible. Chronology is a legitimate organizing method, not automatically a failure to interpret. Support any larger reading of the life against the surviving record.

Show the gaps. An archive is a survival of accidents, and a history that reads as complete is either about a very small thing or is hiding something.

Attribute inference as inference. *He must have known* and *he knew* are different claims, and a reader can tell them apart if you let them.

### Criticism

Describe accurately, then judge, and let the description carry enough that the judgment is half made before it arrives. The order matters: judging first turns description into a search for supporting evidence.

Engage the work directly through relevant quotations or precise descriptions. Visual, musical and other nonverbal work does not need an invented quotation. Distinguish description from a supported interpretation or judgment.

### Literary journalism

Reporting written at length with the techniques of narrative. Everything `press` says about attribution applies.

Reported scene is the form's engine and its liability. You were there, or someone was and told you, or a document records it. A fourth option does not exist.

## Evidence

The legacy `evidence_floor: 4` emphasizes examining relevant sources and their limits. State what was actually consulted; do not pretend an inaccessible source was read or infer an author's motive without support.

Deadlines and genre do not lower the truth standard. Historical, remembered and quantitative claims need different evidence handling. Explain uncertainty in the form the record supports, without requiring a numerical interval for every narrative claim.

Memoir does not get a lower rung. It gets a different instrument, which is disclosed method: tell the reader once and plainly what kind of evidence they are holding, then hold to it.

An invented citation is worse here than an unsourced sentence, because it borrows credibility it has not earned and costs a reader real time to catch. Responsibility for every reference under your name is yours regardless of how the draft was produced.

An optional education preset supports the actual academic task or reader. It does not override selected publication mechanics, create research obligations from credentials or weaken fidelity to the record.

## Boundaries

Against `fiction`. Select the genre by the work's declared contract with its reader. A novel may invent within its brief; a memoir remains answerable for its factual assertions. Neither genre has one mandatory ending, and fiction's freedoms do not excuse misleading claims about real people or sources.

Against `press`. Both are reported and answerable, and they differ on time and on apparatus. `press` files under a deadline, carries its sourcing inside the sentence because there is no room for notes, and follows AP. Non-fiction has time, carries notes, and follows Chicago. A feature running at length with a bibliography lands here even when a newspaper prints it, and a story filed to a wire is `press` even when it is beautiful.

Against `general`. Choose by purpose and reading experience, not a word-count boundary. Functional updates and brief explanations often fit `general`; a developed narrative or critical essay may fit this module at different lengths.

Against `technical`. Product instructions, specifications and architectural explanations usually belong to `technical`. Documentation can legitimately explain a decision or compare alternatives; an argument does not automatically move it into literary nonfiction.

Against the regulated modules. A history of a drug trial, a biography of a judge, a founder's account of the company she built — the prose is this module's, and any live claim about treatment, law or a product belongs to `medical`, `legal` or `marketing`, whose floors are higher and are not negotiable by a writer at book length. Narrative license stops where a reader might act on the sentence.

With `domains/education-level/`. Select a preset for an actual classroom or research-writing task, not merely the reader's age or degree. Describe subject familiarity and support needs explicitly.

## Examples

The archives, sources and people below are invented, and stand in for records you would actually have to hold.

**Research with no argument in it**

**Supplied facts:** In these fictional records, Perrin (1984) interprets the 1911 strike economically and Okonkwo (2003) emphasizes social organization; both rely on union records. Vance (2019) examines mill agents' letters describing September negotiations over a night-shift demand absent from those union records. The letters do not establish what everyone knew or the strike's sole cause.

> Several scholars have addressed the 1911 strike. Perrin (1984) situates it in the regional textile economy, while Okonkwo (2003) emphasizes the role of the fraternal societies. More recently, Vance (2019) has drawn attention to the correspondence of the mill agents. Each of these approaches offers valuable insight into a complex event.

> Perrin and Okonkwo offered different readings of the same union records. Vance's account of the mill agents' letters adds a night-shift demand negotiated in September but absent from that record. The letters make working hours part of the explanation without establishing that they were the strike's only cause.

**Attribution laundered into the passive**

**Supplied facts:** Two fictional agents understood an order to permit removals. One recorded that interpretation in a letter; the other sent notices to tenants in November.

> The order was understood at the time to permit removals, and considerable pressure was subsequently brought to bear on the tenants.

> Two of the agents read the order as permitting removals, and one wrote to say so; the tenants got notice in November from the agent who had not written.

**A figure that is merely plausible**

**Supplied facts:** The brief requests a plain account of an archive-indexing problem. The visitor spent two days understanding its box-numbering system. The responsible clerk's identity and dates are unknown.

> The archive was a labyrinth, each folder a door onto another room, the past unfolding like a map that redrew itself as she walked.

> She spent the first two days working out how the boxes were numbered.

**Notability argued by coverage**

**Supplied facts:** In this fictional profile record, Delacroix-Owens patented a shackle in 1968 and spent the next nine years suing three companies that had begun manufacturing it. No verdict or outcome is supplied.

> Delacroix-Owens has received independent coverage in national and regional media outlets, has been profiled in several trade publications, and maintains an active social media presence.

> Delacroix-Owens patented the shackle in 1968 and spent the next nine years suing the three companies that had already begun making it.

**Retrospective omniscience**

**Supplied facts:** The fictional memoirist recalls thinking at seven that the father's silence meant anger. Near age 40, the memoirist was told that the father's brother had died that spring. No childhood understanding of the father's grief is established.

> At seven I understood that my father's silence was not indifference but a kind of grief he had no language for, and that our family would never speak of it directly.

> At seven I thought he was angry with me. I was nearly forty before anyone told me his brother had died that spring.

**Significance manufactured at the close**

**Supplied facts:** The fictional mill closed in 1979. A 1986 state grant was awarded for demolition, but the town spent the money on its water system. The supplied present-day account says the building still stands. No population-wide claim about former workers is supported.

> The mill closed in 1979. In the end, the story of Harmon Falls is the story of America itself — of resilience in the face of change, and of the enduring human capacity to begin again.

> The mill closed in 1979. A state grant awarded for demolition in 1986 went to the water system instead. The building still stands.

**Several visits are not one afternoon**

**Supplied facts:** The memoirist met Ada on four visits in May to discuss the mill's closure. The notes do not place the whole discussion on one afternoon.

> In one afternoon, Ada and I went through the story of the mill's closure.

> Across four visits in May, Ada and I discussed the mill's closure.
