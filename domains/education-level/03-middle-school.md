# Education: Middle School

An optional classroom preset for grades 6 through 8. Useful practice includes tracing sources, connecting evidence to claims and responding to genuine competing views. Do not manufacture a counterclaim to a settled fact or assume schooling establishes subject expertise.

## Detect

### Under-reach

- One-step cause where the situation has several.
- No source or evidence language anywhere: no according to, no said, no named origin for a fact the writer could not have observed.
- Every term over-explained, as though the reader shares none of the writer's schooling.
- Facts listed with no grouping or contrast.
- A counterclaim that exists only to lose — built weak so it can be knocked down.
- Narrative names events but not viewpoint, tension, motive, or stakes.

### Over-reach

- Stacked nominalizations: `the utilization of renewable energy sources demonstrates significant potential for the mitigation of environmental degradation`.
- Clause nesting that makes the relationships hard to follow.
- Discipline terms used with no cue that the writer knows they are technical.
- A hedge wall standing in for a position: `it could be argued that this may potentially suggest`.
- Citation apparatus imported for its own sake — a works-cited page attached to a piece that names no sources in the prose.
- Vocabulary that would not survive the question "what does that mean?"

### Either way

- Evidence quoted and then abandoned, never connected to the point it was meant to prove.
- Sources treated as interchangeable, with no named origin distinguishing one from another.
- Unintended comma splices that obscure sentence boundaries.

## Write

- Assume the reader can track theme, systems, data, and competing views when the text signposts them.
- Define new academic terms in context, then reuse them without redefining.
- Keep sentences direct. Add a dependent clause only where it buys precision.
- Make relationships explicit: because, however, as a result, in contrast.
- For inform and explain, group facts by class, by part and whole, by sequence, or by cause.
- For argument, state the claim and address relevant competing explanations or objections fairly. A summary or an established fact does not need an invented opposing side.
- Identify a source with the details that matter here. Explain relevant credibility questions accessibly; evaluating a source is not reserved for an older reader.
- Tell a primary source from a secondary one and say which you are using.
- For narrative, orient the reader, control pace, and close the arc.
- Own the sentence. One clear qualifier beats three stacked ones.
- Reaching above grade level is fine when you control it. If you cannot define the term, or say why the sentence needs that clause, cut it.

## Mechanics

Useful teaching checks when the task calls for them. Publication-specific punctuation, dates, numbers and citation conventions remain with the selected genre and manual:

- Repairing comma splices and run-ons — join with a conjunction and comma, a semicolon, or a full stop.
- Commas separating items in a simple series, in whichever form the domain in force takes.
- Colons and other punctuation that make the relationship between clauses or a quotation and its introduction clear.
- Hyphenating a compound modifier before a noun when leaving it open would mislead.
- Quotation boundaries and attribution that preserve the speaker's words and follow the selected manual.
- `said` as the default attribution verb, carrying no verdict on truth.
- Units, ages, money and dates stated unambiguously, with formatting supplied by the selected style.
- Expanding unfamiliar abbreviations and retaining familiar technical terms.
- Commonly confused forms such as `its` and `it's`, without banning legitimate alternate senses of a word.
- Active or passive voice chosen according to whether the actor or the result matters.

## Evidence

The legacy `evidence_rung: 2` marks practice identifying where claims come from and distinguishing observation, report and interpretation. Use neutral attribution unless the evidence warrants a stronger verb. Discuss funding, sampling or other credibility limits when relevant and understandable.

The task determines how much source apparatus is useful. The preset neither lowers domain safeguards nor requires an unnecessary research protocol.

## Boundaries

These are classroom practice emphases, not developmental gates. Younger readers may understand named sources and warrants; older readers may need introductory support in a new subject. Match the actual task and preserve the author's supported position.

## Examples

Each example uses an independent supplied record. Fictional policy and reporting records are illustrative, not external research.

**Two supported effects of a river**

**Supplied facts:** In the fictional town history, river boats brought goods to market; later floods damaged streets used in that trade.

Before: The river changed the town and it was important.

After: The river changed the town in two ways. First, boats brought goods to the market. Later, floods damaged the same streets that helped trade grow.

**Clarifying the author's actual phone policy**

**Supplied facts:** The author supports bringing phones to school for family contact afterward, with phones stored in lockers during class. The supplied classroom observations report distraction from phones at desks. The edit must not reverse that position.

Before: Phones should be allowed because students like them.

After: Students should be allowed to bring phones, but keep them in lockers during class. Phones at desks can distract the class; the locker rule still lets students reach family after school.

**A mechanism without an invented national statistic**

**Supplied facts:** The supplied explanation says wind and solar generate electricity without burning fuel during operation. It provides no national energy-share figures, emissions series or estimate of the pollution reduction.

Before: The utilization of renewable energy sources demonstrates significant potential for the mitigation of environmental degradation.

After: Wind and solar can generate electricity without burning fuel during operation.

**A grammar edit without compulsory controversy**

**Supplied facts:** The task is to fix the comma splice while retaining the supplied historical claim. No additional historiography or counterargument is requested.

Before: The Civil War was caused by slavery, it was a very important conflict in American history.

After: The Civil War was caused by slavery. It was a very important conflict in American history.

**Naming a supplied source**

**Supplied facts:** In this fictional district brief, the student health committee recommends starting middle and high school at 8:30 a.m. or later. Its report explains that adolescent sleep cycles shift later during puberty. This is a supplied local recommendation, not a newly verified medical authority.

Before: Studies show that sleep is important for teenagers.

After: The district's student health committee recommends starting middle and high school at 8:30 a.m. or later. Its report explains that adolescent sleep cycles shift later during puberty.
