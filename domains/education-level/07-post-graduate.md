# Education: Post-Graduate

An optional preset for an explicitly requested dissertation, scholarly article or comparable research-writing task. Do not select it merely because the reader has a doctorate. A doctoral reader asking for ordinary instructions needs those instructions, and a scholar new to a subject may need introductory explanation. The genre, venue and citation adapter retain control of publication mechanics.

For work claiming a scholarly contribution, explain what the evidence changes, confirms, questions or makes possible. A requested survey, replication, research proposal or null result is legitimate; do not invent a finding or dispute to make it look more original.

Fluent source summaries can still fail an analytical task if they never compare evidence or explain the author's conclusion. That is an observable writing problem, not proof of authorship or a single theory of how models fail. The research dossier's lexical findings do not establish the effectiveness of this preset.

Make the warrant visible when the inference needs it: the reason these grounds support this claim. Do not infer a writer's mental state from an absent sentence. The research-writing checks below are house guidance, not a validated account of every discipline.

## Detect

### Under-reach

- A thesis that announces instead of claims: *This paper will explore the various factors...* If nobody could disagree with it, it is a topic.
- Nothing in the piece says what changes if the argument is right, and the conclusion restates the introduction rather than saying what follows from it.
- Sources queued rather than synthesized — *Smith argues, then Jones argues* — with no argument running between them.
- Evidence and claim set side by side with no warrant. The quotation is there, the assertion is there, and the sentence explaining why the first licenses the second is missing.
- Counterargument absent, or built weak enough to knock over.
- Method, archive path or coding decisions described too thinly for another person to retrace them.
- A limitation a reviewer would raise on first reading, left for the reviewer to find.
- Contestable assertions left uncited, or a quotation dropped in unframed and expected to argue on its own.

### Over-reach

- A contribution claimed larger than the work supports: novelty the literature does not actually leave open, or a finding generalized past the case that produced it.
- Signposting inflation: *In this section I will now turn to a discussion of...*
- Boosters compensating for thin evidence: *clearly, obviously, it is undeniable that.*
- Settled points cited to demonstrate reading rather than to support anything.
- Terms of art the writer could not define if stopped and asked.
- Nominalization chains and abstraction standing where the field has a concrete noun, reached for to sound rigorous rather than to name something.
- First person avoided by reflex, leaving agentless passives that hide who chose the method.

### Either way

- Hedging uniform rather than calibrated, so a reader cannot tell which claims the writer would defend, and no hedge names what would settle it.
- Sources characterized in ways their authors would not recognize.
- A source cited through another source's account of it, unmarked.
- Volume of citation standing in for engagement, in either direction: too little to locate the claim in a conversation, or too much to see the argument through.

## Write

- Put the research question, contribution or supported finding where the genre expects it. Do not manufacture an arguable verdict when the work has not produced one.
- Answer the so-what in the introduction rather than saving it for a conclusion nobody reaches. Say what is different if you are right.
- State the warrant. When you move from evidence to claim, name the principle that licenses the move — the disciplinary assumption, the methodological commitment, the definition you are relying on. This is the highest-value sentence in most academic paragraphs and the one most often absent.
- Put sources in conversation with each other. Organize by question rather than by author, and say where they part and why. Two scholars who disagree because they measure different things is a paragraph. Two scholars summarized in sequence is a list.
- Frame every quotation and then interpret it. A quotation is evidence for your sentence, never a substitute for it. If the interpretation only restates the quotation in other words, cut one of them. Quote for the wording and paraphrase for the content.
- Write the method, the archive path or the coding decisions so another person could retrace the route and see how you arrived. Past tense, with the choices attributed to whoever made them.
- Report what you found before interpreting it, and keep the two separable, so a reader can accept your evidence and reject your reading of it.
- Mark genuine uncertainty and, where known, explain what evidence could reduce it. Some uncertainty cannot be resolved from accessible records; do not invent an archive, experiment or resolution condition.
- Name the limitation that would most worry a hostile reviewer, in the terms that reviewer would use, and state it before the reviewer gets there.
- Address genuine objections in their strongest relevant form. A study, description or settled fact does not require a fabricated opponent.
- Attribute choices to the actual author or team and follow the venue's person/voice conventions. Use *I coded the transcripts* only when the supplied author actually did that work. Keep a useful passive; never invent personal research to sound accountable.
- Read the draft once as the reviewer who wants it to be wrong, and fix what that reading turns up before anybody else sees it.
- Give sections clear jobs and an order that serves the argument. Independent analyses can remain independent; do not force transitions or one universal dissertation structure.
- A term the field genuinely needs is not over-reach, however forbidding it looks on the page. The over-reach at this tier is a claim of novelty or reach the work does not support.

## Mechanics

Do not infer mastery from credentials. The apparatus and its governing manual belong to the genre, venue and citation adapter rather than this preset:

- One citation system throughout a piece, never two, per the `domains/citation/` module loaded beside this one.
- Terms defined at first use and then held. One concept, one name, for the length of the work.
- Quotation meaning and source identity preserved. Follow the selected manual's rules for permitted capitalization changes, brackets, omissions and editorial notation; do not generalize AP's handling to scholarly styles.
- Block quotations set off at the threshold the manual sets, without quotation marks, with the citation placed where that manual puts it. A block quotation that carries the point the prose should have made is a defect no formatting fixes.
- Data, code, instruments and archival finding aids cited so a reader can reach the same material, not merely described.
- Epigraphs attributed accurately; acknowledgments, funding and conflicts reported under the venue's requirements without inventing contributors or support.
- Generative-AI use disclosed under the venue's current policy. Do not credit a tool as a human researcher. Identify material uses as required and retain human accountability for every submitted claim and citation; choosing a style manual alone does not establish an AI-use policy.

## Evidence

The legacy `evidence_rung: 6` emphasizes critical review and traceability for substantial scholarly work. These duties also apply to relevant work at other educational stages; they do not begin at the doctorate.

Adversarial review means the argument must hold under a reader who wants it to fail and has the standing to say so. So you put the limitation in before the reviewer finds it, give the counterargument its strongest form, and claim no more than the work supports.

Traceability means reporting enough about evidence and method to evaluate the route to the claim. Supply reproducible analysis and source locations where appropriate, and state legitimate privacy, ethics, copyright or access limits. Do not promise that every study can be rerun from prose alone or publish restricted data to satisfy this preset.

Report uncertainty in a form the underlying work supports. An invented interval, robustness check, archive visit or replication is worse than a disclosed limitation. Numerical uncertainty is not mandatory for every qualitative claim. Whatever produced the text, the submitting author remains answerable for its claims and citations.

The task and domain determine applicable safeguards. This preset does not lower accuracy or create evidence that the work never produced.

## Boundaries

Use the substantial-research checks only when the assignment or submission calls for them. A master's project can already require critical review and reproducibility; a doctoral writer can produce a plain explanation without a literature gap or contribution claim.

Postdoctoral and faculty status do not automatically select this preset. Load no education preset when none fits; the genre, actual reader needs and task contract still govern.

## Examples

These independent synthetic records specify what is known. Missing findings remain missing, and first-person actions belong to the supplied author, not the assistant.

**A topic is not yet a finding**

**Supplied facts:** The supplied proposal concerns the decline of guilds in early modern Europe. No findings about regional chronology, credit or rural competition have been supplied.

Before: This paper will explore the various factors that contributed to the decline of the guild system in early modern Europe.

After: This paper examines the decline of guilds in early modern Europe.

*Review note: a sharper finding requires evidence. Do not invent one to replace a legitimate proposal topic.*

**Reconciling different measures**

**Supplied facts:** In the fictional source records, Smith (2019) reports stagnant hourly wages. Jones (2021) reports rising total compensation, including employer-paid insurance. Patel (2022) attributes the apparent conflict to those different measures.

Before: Smith (2019) argues that wages stagnated in this period. Jones (2021) argues that wages rose. Patel (2022) argues that measurement is difficult.

After: Smith tracks hourly pay; Jones measures total compensation, including employer-paid insurance. As Patel explains, stagnant wages and rising compensation need not conflict. The comparison must distinguish the two measures before judging whether the findings disagree.

**A missing baseline stays missing**

**Supplied facts:** The parish record states that day laborers headed 40% of households by 1780. No earlier percentage, land-tenure record or enclosure chronology is supplied.

Before: Forty percent of the parish's households were headed by day laborers by 1780. The enclosure acts therefore remade rural labor.

After: Day laborers headed 40% of the parish's households by 1780. That figure alone does not establish whether enclosure changed rural labor.

**Comparison without claiming causal identification**

**Supplied facts:** The fictional source Ruiz writes, "the enclosure acts remade rural labor." Over the same 30 years, supplied records show day-laborer households rising from 12% to 40% in enclosed parishes and from 12% to 13% in unenclosed parishes in that county. The comparison does not control for other differences between the groups.

Before: Ruiz notes that "the enclosure acts remade rural labor." This shows the importance of the enclosure acts.

After: Ruiz's account is consistent with the parish records: day-laborer households rose from 12% to 40% in enclosed parishes, compared with 12% to 13% in unenclosed parishes over the same 30 years. The contrast supports examining enclosure, but does not isolate it from other differences between the groups.

**Uncertainty without an invented archive**

**Supplied facts:** Enrollment rose after a policy change in the only two districts with usable records. Both also advertised the change. No additional archive holdings or comparison data have been checked.

Before: It could perhaps be suggested that the policy may have had some effect on enrollment, although further research would be needed.

After: Enrollment rose in the two districts with usable records, but both also advertised the policy change. These records cannot separate policy from publicity. Comparable data with different publicity exposure might help, if available.

**Accountability for supplied research actions**

**Supplied facts:** In this fictional methods record, the author recruited 22 participants through two community clinics. Eligibility required already having sought care. The author built the codebook from six initial interviews and revised it twice; the codebook and both revision logs are present in Appendix B.

Before: Participants were recruited and the interviews were coded thematically, revealing several key themes.

After: I recruited 22 participants through two community clinics and coded the transcripts against a codebook built from the first six interviews, revising it twice. The codebook and both revision logs are in Appendix B. Recruiting through clinics means everyone in the sample had already sought care, which is the limit on what these accounts can say about people who did not.

**A research question without a predetermined conclusion**

**Supplied facts:** The author compares accreditation reports and trustee reports from three fictional colleges in the same year. Accreditation reports seek approval. Findings about how that purpose changes the reports have not yet been supplied.

Before: This analysis interrogates the multivalent discursive formations through which institutional legitimacy is constituted and contested.

After: I compare what three colleges told their accreditor with what they told their trustees in the same year, asking how the need for approval shapes each account.
