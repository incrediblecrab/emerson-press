Let me get a quick sanity check before finalizing.**Bus timetable sets the clock at Rillstone Switchgear, and a shuttle idea hasn't cleared it yet**

Assembler Omi Doren steps off the 6:05 a.m. bus and walks seven minutes to Rillstone Switchgear, arriving with three minutes to spare before her 6:15 a.m. shift starts, according to a field note taken Aug. 6.

The margin narrows at the other end of the day. Doren's shift ends at 2:15 p.m., and the return bus leaves the same stop at 2:25 p.m. Miss it, and the next one under the current weekday timetable is usually an hour away.

That hour shapes decisions on the factory floor. Doren, who does not own a car, said she sometimes turns down optional extra work at the end of a shift because of the connection.

"Ten more minutes at the bench can become an hour at the bus stop," Doren said.

The factory, which makes electrical cabinets on an industrial estate developed before the area's current housing expansion, has 85 employees. Forty answered a voluntary commuting survey; 14 of those respondents said they regularly depend on the bus. The survey does not establish how the other employees, who did not respond, get to work.

Rillstone's production line includes a shared morning briefing, which makes staggering start times difficult for some jobs, according to the factory. Production manager Vann has requested a quote for a shuttle running between the central bus interchange and the factory, separate from the public bus route.

A local operator quoted $140 per working day for a limited morning-and-afternoon pilot, or $2,800 for 20 working days, Vann said. The employer has not approved a budget for the pilot or set a start date.

The public bus operator said its vehicles also serve school journeys and cannot simply shift this route's departures without affecting other runs. Driver Quill, who works the route, said the published timetable functions as a chain of trips rather than a vehicle held in reserve for a single factory's shift changes.

"Moving one departure means deciding which other passengers will wait," Quill said.

The proposed shuttle would serve Rillstone employees only; it would not replace the town's public bus route, and no cut to the existing route has been adopted.

The factory currently lists six advertised vacancies. Its recruitment records do not isolate transportation as a cause of those openings, and there is no confirmed link between the shuttle proposal and hiring.

For Doren, the calculation is more personal than institutional. She said a reliable connection home would matter more to her than a workplace parking benefit — a perk the factory has floated in past discussions with staff, though none is currently on offer tied to the shuttle plan.

No attendance penalty against Doren is on record, and no shuttle start date has been set. Vann said the quote remains under review.

Doren said she plans her shift around the 2:25 p.m. bus the way she has for months: watching the clock at the bench, then walking back to the same stop where the morning started.
