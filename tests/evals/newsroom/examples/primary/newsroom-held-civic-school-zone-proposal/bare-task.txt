Merrowfield weighs new school boundary map, but key questions remain unsettled

Merrowfield's school board has released a proposed attendance-zone map that would reshape which future pupils are assigned to North Meadow and River Step schools starting with the 2027-28 school year, but district officials stress that the document is a draft for public comment, not a final assignment notice.

The proposal comes as North Meadow, which has a planning capacity of 360 pupils, currently enrolls 398 — well over that figure. River Step, by contrast, has room to grow, with 332 pupils against a planning capacity of 400. The draft map would shift several blocks of future enrollment toward River Step in an effort to ease that imbalance.

District officials caution that the whole-school numbers don't tell the full story. The figures don't show whether particular year groups have open seats or whether specific classrooms are suited to the students who would fill them, the district said. Planning capacities reflect current assumptions about how rooms are used, not a guarantee that every empty seat works for every age group.

Geography complicates the picture further. Because the two schools sit near a river with limited crossing points, a home that appears close to River Step on the map could require a much longer walk if the family must first reach the nearest bridge. Planning officer Dena Orr said the district is trying to account for that reality. "We are drawing a route to a school, not just a line around a number of houses," Orr said.

Some older printed maps still show a former footbridge that is no longer part of the designated school walking route, a discrepancy the district is asking residents to flag during the comment period, along with other route barriers, anticipated housing changes, and family circumstances not reflected in the draft data.

Importantly, the proposal would not force every family in a redrawn block to switch schools. Currently enrolled pupils could remain at their present schools under the draft. However, how younger siblings of those pupils would be handled remains unresolved and is listed as an open question for the consultation process. Lora Senn, a parent group representative, said that issue needs to be settled before families are asked to treat the map as final. "Please answer the sibling question before asking families to treat the map as a settled plan," Senn said.

The district has scheduled public sessions at both North Meadow and River Step, and will accept written comments from residents who cannot attend in person. Officials said comments will be used to inform a revised proposal to the school board, rather than serve as a household-by-household vote on individual boundary lines.

The draft also does not establish final transportation arrangements for any specific address; any changes to busing or transport entitlements will be published alongside the final adopted enrollment policy, not inferred from the current map.

Enrollment projections used in the draft include one housing development that has planning approval but no confirmed occupancy schedule. The district said it would revise pupil-total projections if that development's timetable changes before the plan is adopted.

Officials emphasized that neither school is being considered for closure or sale, and that the enrollment figures reflect only this planning exercise, not a broader measure of school quality. The board has not taken a final vote on the proposal, and no implementation instructions have been issued for current pupils.
