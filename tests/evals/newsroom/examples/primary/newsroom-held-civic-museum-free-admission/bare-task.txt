# Dellmere Museum to offer free general admission for one year

Dellmere Museum will make general admission free for one year starting Jan. 1, replacing its $8 ticket for visitors ages 16 and older with a trial financed by a civic grant and reserve funds.

Trustees approved the change Sept. 10. It will cover the museum's permanent galleries, but workshops and visiting exhibitions that have their own tickets will remain separately priced. Children younger than 16 already enter without charge.

The museum has budgeted for $68,000 in forgone admission income during 2027. That figure comes from its recent accounts, not a forecast of attendance under the new policy. A $45,000 civic grant and $23,000 from an unrestricted reserve will cover the planned gap.

Funding beyond the first year has not been approved, and trustees have not committed to making free admission permanent. They considered a reduced-price ticket before selecting the one-year trial.

Museum director Lora Tenn said the change is intended to make shorter visits easier.

"We want someone to be able to come back for one room, not feel they must see everything to justify a ticket," Tenn said.

The permanent collection focuses on local manufacturing, river transport and domestic life. Its three galleries are arranged around a former engine hall.

The museum will retain a staffed welcome desk to count entries and explain which activities require separate booking. Those counts will include repeat visits, so they will not, by themselves, show how many different people the museum reaches.

Trustees will review actual entries, voluntary donations and operating costs after six months. The museum has not predicted that additional donations or shop purchases will replace the lost ticket income. It has not yet measured any change in audience composition or repeat visits resulting from the policy, which has not taken effect.

The grant agreement requires a report on the trial's costs and access findings, but it does not set an attendance target the museum must claim to have achieved.

A neighborhood advisory group supported removing the admission charge while identifying bus costs and limited Sunday hours as barriers that will remain.

"The price at the door is one part of the journey, not the whole journey," said advisory group member Ilan Moss.

The approved budget does not extend opening hours, subsidize transportation or add staff positions.

Some visits and activities will continue under separate arrangements. The existing school-visit program will remain separately scheduled rather than being replaced by ordinary walk-in admission. A visiting photography exhibition planned for spring 2027 will retain its separate ticket because its loan and staffing costs fall outside general admission.

Members will keep advance booking for selected talks and the existing members' newsletter, rather than receiving an admission discount that will no longer have value during the trial. The museum will contact current members before renewal to explain the changed benefits.
