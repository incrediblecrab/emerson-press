# Dellmere Museum to make general admission free for one year

Dellmere Museum will make general admission free for one year beginning Jan. 1, 2027, under a decision its trustees approved Sept. 10.

The change will cover the permanent galleries, replacing the current $8 general-admission ticket for visitors ages 16 and older. Visitors younger than 16 already enter without charge. Separately priced workshops and visiting exhibitions with their own tickets will remain outside the free-admission policy.

The museum’s budget anticipates $68,000 in forgone admission income, covered by a $45,000 civic grant and $23,000 from an unrestricted reserve. Funding beyond the first year has not been approved, and trustees have not committed to making free admission permanent.

Museum director Lora Tenn said the change is intended to make shorter, return visits more inviting.

“We want someone to be able to come back for one room, not feel they must see everything to justify a ticket,” Tenn said.

The permanent collection focuses on local manufacturing, river transport and domestic life, with three galleries arranged around a former engine hall. Trustees considered a reduced-price ticket before selecting the one-year free-admission trial, according to the recorded decision.

A neighborhood advisory group supported the price change but identified bus costs and limited Sunday hours as remaining barriers to access. The approved budget does not extend opening hours, subsidize transport or add staff positions.

“The price at the door is one part of the journey, not the whole journey,” advisory group member Ilan Moss said.

The museum will retain a staffed welcome desk to count entries and explain which activities still require separate booking. Those counts will include repeat visits, so they will not, by themselves, show how many different people the museum reaches.

Trustees will review actual entries, voluntary donations and operating costs after six months. The grant agreement requires the museum to report the trial’s costs and access findings but sets no attendance target it must claim to have achieved. The museum has not yet measured any change in audience composition or repeat visits resulting from the policy, which has not taken effect.

The $68,000 estimate of forgone income is based on the museum’s recent accounts rather than a forecast of future attendance. The museum has not predicted that additional donations or shop purchases will replace the admission revenue.

For members, benefits will include advance booking for selected talks and the existing members’ newsletter, rather than an admission discount that would no longer have value during the trial. The museum will contact current members before renewal to explain the changed benefit structure.

The existing school-visit program will remain separately scheduled and will not be replaced by ordinary walk-in admission. A planned visiting photography exhibition in spring 2027 will also retain its separate ticket because its loan and staffing costs fall outside general admission.
