# Fenharbor approves creekside lot purchase for future floodwater storage

The Fenharbor Council voted 6-1 Sept. 8 to buy a creekside parking lot for future floodwater storage, but demolition and construction funding remain unconfirmed.

The $460,000 purchase of the 2.4-acre Mill Bend lot will use $240,000 from a city reserve and a $220,000 regional land-acquisition grant. The seller has accepted the city's offer, with closing scheduled for November. Ownership has not yet transferred.

The city intends to remove pavement beside Narrow Creek and create land that can hold water when creek levels rise. The vote authorizes the land purchase, not a final engineering plan. Design work and an environmental assessment must precede construction.

"Buying room for water is the first step, not a promise that every nearby property will stay dry," city engineer Hollis Vare said.

City records show the parking lot flooded during storms in 2019 and 2024, but those records do not measure how much the proposed project would reduce flooding. The draft concept calls for a planted basin rather than a permanent pond. Plantings and public access arrangements remain undecided.

The regional grant covers land acquisition, not demolition or construction, and neither of those expenses has a confirmed funding allocation. The grant also requires the property to remain available for floodwater storage and bars later commercial development.

The privately owned lot has 87 marked parking spaces a short walk from the town's shopping streets. A merchants group says those spaces are important to workers whose shifts begin before nearby short-stay parking becomes available.

"We support fixing the flooding, but the replacement for a workday parking space cannot be a two-hour space," merchants group chair Rima Doss said.

The dissenting council member wanted parking alternatives identified before the city committed the reserve money. The council has not announced when parking will end after closing, leaving the timing of that change unresolved for workers and other users.

Under terms described in the council report, the current parking operator will continue running the lot until the sale closes. The city will also examine converting six curb spaces to loading bays, but that is a separate proposal and has not been approved.

The environmental assessment will include soil sampling because the parcel previously served as a vehicle storage yard. The reporting packet contains no finding that the soil is contaminated and no estimate of cleanup costs.

An existing public path runs outside the purchase boundary and will remain under the parks department's management. Its status is separate from decisions about public access within the proposed project, where arrangements have yet to be settled. The council has not announced when any public recreation area might open.

A public design meeting will be scheduled after the assessment contract is arranged. No meeting date had been published as of Sept. 9.
