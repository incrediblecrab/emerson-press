Headline: Behind the Familiar Sign: What "Locally Owned" Really Means at Cormere's New Ilex Pantry

CORMERE — When Ilex Pantry opened its doors here in July, shoppers saw the same green-and-white signage, the same loyalty cards and the same shelf layout found in Ilex stores across the region. What they may not have realized is that the store itself is owned not by the national chain, but by two Cormere residents, Omi and Rella Doss.

The distinction matters, and it illustrates a model common in American retail but often misunderstood by customers: the franchise.

Under their agreement with Ilex Pantry, the Dosses own the operating company and lease the building — a former carpet dealership that still has its wide loading door in back. The national brand does not own the Cormere store. Instead, the Dosses pay for the right to use the Ilex name, its purchasing system and its loyalty program.

That right comes at a price spelled out in the franchise contract: a 4% royalty and a 1% advertising contribution, both calculated on gross sales rather than profit. For a month with $100,000 in sales, that adds up to $5,000 in fees — before the Dosses pay rent, wages, stock and other operating costs. Separately, the couple's opening budget included a one-time $28,000 initial franchise fee.

Beyond the fees, the agreement sets requirements. The Dosses must stock a core list of 600 products but can add local items through the approved purchasing process. They must follow the brand's food-handling and shop-presentation standards, though day-to-day staff scheduling is theirs to set. They hire and pay all 16 employees at the store. They can price most items as they choose, but must honor advertised national promotions during specified periods; the company reimburses part of the cost of some of those promotions, though the reporting record does not specify the formulas involved.

Omi Doss said the arrangement's biggest practical benefit is buying power. "The name gets us into a buying system," he said. "It does not pay the electricity bill for us." He said the purchasing network allows the store to secure deliveries that would be difficult for a single, independent shop to arrange on its own.

Leda Vint, who owns a nearby independent grocery, said the branding can blur an important distinction for shoppers. "Two matching signs do not mean the same person is making the wage payment," Vint said. She added that shared advertising helps customers recognize a store but can make separately owned businesses look more alike than they are.

Ilex Pantry's regional network includes both company-owned and franchised locations, and the ownership structure at any single store cannot be assumed from the signage alone.

The Cormere store's franchise agreement runs for a stated 10-year term; renewal depends on conditions in the contract rather than any automatic, permanent right to keep operating under the Ilex name.

The Dosses have not released net profit figures, and nothing in the available record offers a projection of guaranteed returns. Among their early plans is a display area for local producers, though it is too new to show sales results.

For now, the Cormere store offers a familiar face with a locally signed lease — a distinction that, as Vint put it, isn't always visible from the sidewalk.
