Federal Engineers Flag Aging Harlan Dam for Accelerated Repairs, Monitoring

CASCADE VALLEY — Federal dam safety engineers issued a Level 2 structural concern designation Monday for the Harlan Reservoir Dam, requiring its operator to accelerate monitoring and submit a remediation plan within 18 months.

The designation, announced Aug. 5, followed a structural assessment that found the dam's concrete is deteriorating at 2.4 millimeters a year, more than double the 1.1 millimeters a year documented in a 2014 study. At that pace, engineers estimate the dam would reach a threshold requiring emergency intervention within 12 to 18 years without repairs.

The dam, built in 1958 and owned by the Cascade Valley Water Authority, is classified as a high-hazard-potential structure because its failure would likely cause loss of life. Its downstream inundation zone covers 140,000 people across three counties.

Level 2 is the second of three concern levels under the federal dam safety framework. Level 3 requires immediate emergency action. Under the new designation, the authority must submit progress reports to federal engineers every 90 days.

Cascade Valley Water Authority director Keith Obi said the authority accepts the designation and has commissioned a full remediation design study. He said a Level 2 designation does not indicate imminent failure risk.

Dr. Pamela Johansson, the structural engineer who conducted the assessment, said the dam is not in danger of collapsing in the near term.

"The dam is not in danger of failing today or next year," Johansson said. "But the trajectory of deterioration is clear, and addressing it will cost far less now than after further degradation."

Engineers attribute the deterioration primarily to alkali-silica reaction, a chemical process in which silica in the concrete's aggregate reacts with the cement's alkaline compounds over time.

Preliminary estimates put the cost of full remediation — including concrete resurfacing and drainage system upgrades — at $620 million to $740 million. The authority has $80 million in reserve funds available for immediate monitoring upgrades, but officials said the full project would require bonding or federal assistance.

The reservoir supplies about 28% of drinking water for a metropolitan area of 800,000 residents and irrigates 180,000 acres of agricultural land in Harlan County.

The dam passed its most recent comprehensive federal safety inspection in 2021 with a satisfactory rating. Engineers said that rating reflected conditions at the time and predates the updated deterioration data.

Federal emergency action plans for the dam call for evacuating portions of the downstream zone within six hours of any determination that failure is imminent. Officials in two downstream counties said they would review and update their evacuation plans in light of the new designation.

The authority said it plans to hold public meetings in affected counties within 60 days to explain the designation and the remediation timeline.

Nationally, about 1,200 high-hazard-potential dams are more than 60 years old, according to the National Dam Safety Program's most recent data.
