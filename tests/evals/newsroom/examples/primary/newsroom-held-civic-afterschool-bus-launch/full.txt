**Headline:** Dunmere schools launch late-bus service for after-school activities

MEDFORD — Dunmere School District will begin a late-bus service at four secondary schools on Oct. 27, giving students a ride home after clubs, study sessions and sports practices that run past the regular bell schedule.

The buses will run Tuesdays and Thursdays, following district-supervised activities such as gardening club, drama, debate and homework sessions, not only competitive sports teams, according to the district.

The service is funded through a one-year, $96,000 education-access grant that covers a leased bus, driver hours and fuel under the district's approved plan. The grant does not guarantee funding for the service in future school years, and the district has not published a single districtwide departure time or a guaranteed length for the ride.

There is no fare, but students must register through their school before they can use a route. The bus will stop at designated neighborhood locations rather than at every student's home, and school staff will confirm the passenger list before departure and tell families which stop has been assigned.

Transport director Orla Venn said the change addresses a gap that fare waivers alone do not fix.

"An activity can be free to join and still be out of reach if the ride home is missing," Venn said.

The initial route plan groups the four schools into two collection routes, with departure times set separately to match each school's activity schedule. Existing morning and afternoon bus routes are not affected, and the added driver hours are separately compensated rather than an extension of current drivers' shifts without pay.

Schools will continue their existing supervision arrangements for registered students who are waiting for the late bus, the district said. Students leaving a private activity that is not supervised by the school cannot expect a seat on the bus without going through the registration process.

A families group has asked the district to add a third service day, particularly to cover activities that meet on Wednesdays. Organizer Teren Moss said two days are a start but do not match how clubs actually schedule their time.

"The two days help, but a club does not move its whole calendar just because a bus is available," Moss said.

The district said it will track student registrations, actual boardings and requests it was unable to fill during the first term, and will use that data to decide whether to seek funding for additional service days or another bus. No expansion beyond the four participating schools has been approved.

The district has not presented evidence that the new service has increased club participation or improved academic outcomes; officials said data collection begins with this term's rollout.

Each participating school will send registration information to families through both printed materials and electronic notices in the coming weeks, the district said.
