# Alder Reach to test earlier restaurant waste pickups after noise complaints

ALDER REACH — Commercial waste pickups will move from about 2 a.m. to an 8-10 p.m. window for 26 businesses in Alder Reach’s restaurant district during a six-week trial aimed at addressing residents’ complaints about overnight collection noise.

The sanitation department’s trial will run Oct. 12 through Nov. 22 across the 20-block district, with collections remaining on the same two nights each week. Residential waste schedules will not change.

The council authorized $24,000 for additional route staffing and temporary collection equipment. Twenty-six of the district’s 41 eligible businesses enrolled; the remaining businesses will keep their existing collection arrangements.

Residents living above restaurants sought the earlier schedule after repeatedly reporting noise from bottles being emptied overnight. The complaint log records contacts rather than individual complainants, so it does not establish how many separate residents objected.

“The truck arrives after the music has stopped, just when we think the street is finally settling down,” said Tavi Lorn, a residents committee member.

Restaurant operators have raised a competing concern: Moving collection into evening service hours could put waste carts in the way of customers and outdoor dining.

“Eight o’clock is not the end of our working day; it is often the middle of it,” restaurant operator Mera Voss said.

The trial plan calls for participating businesses to use designated collection points in side lanes instead of placing carts beside outdoor tables. Businesses must remove carts from those points after collection rather than leave them there throughout the night.

The district’s narrow service lanes were laid out before its current concentration of restaurants and outdoor dining areas. Drivers will have a route marshal on the first two collection nights to identify problems at the shared pickup points. Before the trial begins, the department will distribute a printed map of those points to residents and businesses.

The department will track missed collections, reports of blocked access and the time spent on each route. Those records will document how the earlier service operates, but the department has not set a numerical noise-reduction target or supplied sound measurements from the current service.

As of Sept. 18, no trial outcomes are available. The department has not claimed that shifting collection earlier will resolve every source of nighttime noise.

The earlier route will not collect cooking oil, which will remain covered by separate private arrangements. A business may leave the trial after notifying the department, but it must arrange for its waste to be collected rather than assume it will receive an automatic extra pickup.

A review meeting with residents and enrolled businesses is planned after the third week. Any permanent schedule change will require a new council decision after the final report; completing the six-week trial will not, by itself, make the earlier collection hours permanent.
