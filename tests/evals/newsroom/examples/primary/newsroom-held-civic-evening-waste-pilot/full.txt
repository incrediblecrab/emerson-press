# Alder Reach to test earlier restaurant waste pickups after noise complaints

Alder Reach will test evening commercial waste collection in its restaurant district beginning Oct. 12, moving pickups for participating businesses from around 2 a.m. to between 8 and 10 p.m.

The six-week sanitation department trial will cover a 20-block district and run through Nov. 22. It follows requests from residents living above restaurants who repeatedly reported overnight bottle-emptying noise. Restaurant operators have raised a competing concern: Waste carts could obstruct customers and outdoor service during the earlier hours.

Twenty-six of the district's 41 eligible businesses have enrolled. Collections will take place on the same two nights each week, while businesses outside the trial will keep their existing collection arrangements. Residential waste schedules will not change.

The council authorized a $24,000 budget for additional route staffing and temporary collection equipment. Any permanent schedule change will require a new council decision after the final report; completing the trial will not make the earlier hours permanent.

Residents committee member Tavi Lorn described the timing of the current pickups as a disruption after other activity has quieted.

"The truck arrives after the music has stopped, just when we think the street is finally settling down," Lorn said.

The complaint log records contacts rather than unique complainants, so it cannot establish how many separate residents objected to the overnight service.

For restaurants, the proposed collection window overlaps with business hours. Restaurant operator Mera Voss said the earlier schedule could put waste collection in the middle of service.

"Eight o'clock is not the end of our working day; it is often the middle of it," Voss said.

Under the trial plan, participating businesses will take carts to designated collection points in side lanes rather than place them beside outdoor tables. They must remove the carts from those points after collection instead of storing them there throughout the night.

The district's narrow service lanes were laid out before its current concentration of restaurants and outdoor dining areas. Drivers will have a route marshal on the first two collection nights to identify problems at the shared pickup points.

The sanitation department will record missed collections, blocked access reports and time spent on each route. It has not set a numerical noise-reduction target or supplied sound measurements from the current service. No trial outcomes are available, and the department has not claimed that earlier pickups will resolve every source of nighttime noise.

A review meeting with residents and enrolled businesses is planned after the third week. Businesses may leave the trial after notifying the department, but they must arrange for their waste collection rather than assume they will automatically receive an extra pickup.

Cooking oil will remain covered by separate private arrangements and will not be collected on the earlier route. Before the trial begins, the department will distribute a printed map of the collection points to residents and businesses.
