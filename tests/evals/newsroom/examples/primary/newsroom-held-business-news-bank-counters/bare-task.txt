**Alder Span Bank to Close Two Branches, Add Mobile Counter Service**

FAIR ALDER — Alder Span Bank announced Tuesday that it will close its Elwick and North Nettle branches Nov. 6, consolidating counter service into its larger Fair Alder branch while rolling out a mobile counter to serve customers who still need in-person banking.

The closures will leave Elwick customers an 11-mile drive to the nearest counter and North Nettle customers a 17-mile drive, unless they use the mobile unit, which is scheduled to visit each town two mornings a week starting Nov. 9.

Bank officials said cash-dispensing ATMs will remain at both closing branches, but neither machine will accept deposits, meaning customers who need to deposit cash or checks will have to wait for the mobile counter or travel to Fair Alder.

The mobile counter will allow customers to deposit cash, obtain cashier's checks and meet with an employee about account problems, the bank said. But its daily cutoff for business deposits will be noon, four hours earlier than the 4 p.m. cutoff at the existing branches — a change that could affect merchants who count and deposit cash later in the day.

Benno Keel, who owns a hardware store in Elwick, said he typically deposits his cash after closing his shop at 1 p.m. on Saturdays, a routine the new schedule would disrupt.

"The money may move on a screen, but somebody still has to take my till to the bank," Keel said.

The Elwick senior center has requested an appointment system for the mobile counter to help older residents plan visits, but the bank has not announced one.

Chief Operating Officer Mira Doss said the bank remains committed to in-person service despite the consolidation.

"We are reducing buildings, not eliminating the need to speak to a person," Doss said.

The two branches employ 14 people. The bank has offered 10 of them jobs at the Fair Alder branch and four positions on the mobile service. Alder Span said it would cover employees' additional commuting costs for six months but declined to say how many workers have accepted transfers.

Bank records show counter transactions at the two branches fell from 42,000 in 2023 to 29,400 in 2025, a 30% decline. The bank did not specify how many distinct customers or visits those figures represent, nor did it identify how many affected customers already use online banking.

Doss said the bank expects to save $460,000 annually after the first year of consolidation, a figure that includes the cost of operating the mobile counter. The estimate does not include any proceeds from a possible sale of the Elwick building, which the bank owns and which opened as a bank in 1968; no sale price has been disclosed. The North Nettle location is rented.

The bank plans to hold customer information sessions in both towns during September to explain the changes. Officials said account numbers will not change, and the announcement does not affect existing loan interest rates.

Alder Span gave no timetable for closing any additional branches.

The mobile counter's twice-weekly morning schedule, along with the earlier deposit cutoff, is expected to be a central topic at the September sessions, as customers like Keel weigh whether to adjust their routines or make the longer trip to Fair Alder.
