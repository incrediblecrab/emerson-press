# Hollowmere Market to launch $5 voucher offer for qualifying purchases

Hollowmere Market shoppers who spend at least $10 in a qualifying visit will be able to receive a $5 voucher for a later eligible purchase under a grant-funded program launching Oct. 10.

The Hollowmere Market Association's offer is limited to one voucher per shopper per market day, regardless of how much a shopper spends above $10. The first round covers produce, bread and pantry ingredients listed in the program rules, rather than every item sold at the market.

A $20,000 community-commerce grant will pay for the voucher value, with the association covering administration separately. The allocation can fund 4,000 vouchers at $5 each if it is fully issued. That figure is the program's capacity, not a count of shoppers who have participated.

Vouchers issued in the first round must be used by Dec. 19, the final scheduled market day of the round. The association has not secured funding for a second round after December.

Market coordinator Nella Orr said the offer is intended to give shoppers a reason to make another visit.

"The extra five dollars is a reason to return, not a discount we can claim has already helped every household," Orr said.

The program has no income test and is not part of a government food-benefit system. Shoppers can continue making regular cash and card purchases whether or not they use the offer.

To receive a voucher, shoppers will have their receipts from the same visit checked by the market office. The process includes a staffed alternative for traders who use handwritten receipts.

Participating traders will display the association's sign. The voucher is not cash, provides no cash change and can be used only with participating traders for eligible purchases. Traders are not required to join, and shoppers cannot insist that an unregistered stall accept a voucher.

For participating stalls, reimbursement timing is a concern. A traders committee asked the association to make payments promptly enough that small stalls would not have to finance the grant between market days.

"A token in the till still has to become money we can use to buy flour," bread seller Paven Rill said.

The association plans to reimburse traders for eligible vouchers they submit after each market week, rather than requiring them to wait until the program ends.

The association will track vouchers issued and vouchers redeemed separately. Receiving a voucher does not establish that a shopper later used it, so the number handed out will not by itself show how much voucher value went toward purchases.

There is no estimate of additional spending, dietary change or household savings the program will produce.

Each voucher will carry a serial number and an expiration date, but it will not display the shopper's name. The market office will also display a running notice when the grant allocation is close to being fully issued.
