Rillgate Council Approves $74,000 Contract to Reconcile Cemetery Maps, Burial Records

RILLGATE — The Rillgate Council has approved a $74,000 contract to compare cemetery ground plans, burial registers and existing plot markers, aiming to resolve years of conflicting records at the town's old municipal cemetery and its later eastern extension.

The project covers both sections of the grounds, which use different row-numbering systems, a discrepancy staff say has contributed to confusion in inherited records. Cemetery staff have identified conflicting references in several documents but have not supplied a verified total of burials recorded incorrectly.

The contract does not authorize excavation or the movement of remains, and no such work is included in the current plan.

The contractor will produce a digital working map for cemetery staff, with a public-facing location map planned as a separate, later step. No launch date or final search features have been approved for that public version.

Cemetery Superintendent Neris Pell said gaps in the older plans do not necessarily mean a plot is empty.

"A blank space on a plan is not evidence that nothing is there," Pell said.

The older plans include annotations made over several decades, without a consistent record of who added each note. Rather than resolve every ambiguity outright, the contractor will flag unresolved discrepancies for staff review instead of filling gaps with an assumed name or location.

Physical markers will be photographed from existing paths where possible, and arrangements will be made for work near scheduled services. The old and eastern sections will remain open during most of the mapping work, with short local restrictions announced when needed.

The contractor estimates an eight-month schedule beginning in November, though unresolved records may require additional work afterward. Funding comes from the cemetery's accumulated maintenance reserve rather than a new charge on families using the grounds.

A cemetery friends group has asked that the staff map preserve old spellings and explain corrections rather than replacing earlier entries without a trace. Group secretary Oren Cade said small differences between documents can carry real weight for families.

"Families sometimes arrive with a name written differently on three pieces of paper, and those differences can matter," Cade said.

The working database will retain links to scanned source pages so staff can see the basis for each reconciled entry. Those scans will not all be published online automatically; staff must first decide which details belong in the proposed public map. Questions about a family's existing burial documents will continue to be handled through the cemetery office rather than resolved by a map screen alone.

Volunteers will be invited to supply copies of relevant records, though submitted material will still require comparison with existing sources.

The contractor is not being asked to judge the historical importance of particular graves or to select monuments for removal. The grounds include a row of chestnut trees planted along the former boundary between the two cemetery sections.

The council will receive progress reports on completed comparisons and unresolved discrepancies, rather than a single percentage figure described as certainty, as the project moves forward.
