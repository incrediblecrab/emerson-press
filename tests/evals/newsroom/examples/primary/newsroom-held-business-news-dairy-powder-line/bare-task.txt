# Merefold Dairy to pause powder production; eight workers face job losses

Merefold Dairy Cooperative said Wednesday it will suspend milk-powder production for nine months beginning Oct. 1, with eight workers set to receive redundancy notices while the cooperative maintains contracted milk collections from its 87 member farms.

The shutdown will affect one part of the plant, not its entire operation. Fresh-milk packaging and cultured-products lines will continue running. The cooperative plans to offer transfers or maintenance assignments to 34 of the powder line's 42 employees, though the number accepting transfers is not yet known.

Chief executive Olin Wray said powder orders have fallen while storage and financing costs have increased. The cooperative's monthly stock report puts powder inventory at 3,600 tons, up from 1,900 tons a year earlier — an increase of about 89%.

The cooperative has not published customer-level order figures or attributed the decline to a single buyer or trade measure. The figures show a buildup in powder stocks, but the stock report supplies no evidence that milk is being discarded or that consumers face a shortage of fresh dairy products.

“We are pausing one way of processing milk, not telling our farms to stop producing it,” Wray said.

For employees, the announcement sets out different paths depending on their assignments. Of the 42 powder-line workers, 22 will receive transfer offers to fresh products, 12 will receive maintenance assignments and eight will receive redundancy notices. The wider plant employs 110 people, including those on the powder line.

The transfer offers preserve base pay but require different shift times. With acceptance totals unavailable, it remains unclear how many workers will take those offers.

Employee representative Tavren Cole has asked the cooperative to provide retraining for the eight workers receiving redundancy notices. No retraining agreement or severance total has been announced, leaving those details unresolved ahead of the October suspension.

For member farms, the cooperative's commitment means all contracted milk-collection volumes will be retained through the current contract year. Some of that milk will be processed under a tolling agreement at Larkspur Dairies, 76 miles away, rather than at Merefold.

That arrangement provides a processing route for some collected milk, but its financial implications remain unclear. The cooperative has not disclosed the additional transport and tolling costs, and next year's farm payment formula remains under review. The collection commitment therefore does not settle what members will receive under the next payment schedule.

Member farmer Ina Selk said she needs that schedule before renewing a feed contract.

“Collecting the milk answers one question. What comes back on the statement answers another,” Selk said.

The duration of the production pause also remains subject to review. Merefold will reassess powder stocks and orders in March 2027 before committing to a restart date. According to the board's notice, the nine-month planning period is not a guarantee that production will resume at its end.

The cooperative was formed by 19 farms in 1978 and added powder production in 2006. Its latest announcement keeps fresh-product operations and contracted farm collections in place while leaving the powder line's restart, worker retraining and next year's farm payments unresolved.
