# Merefold Dairy to pause powder production; eight workers face job cuts

Merefold Dairy Cooperative said Wednesday it will suspend milk-powder production for nine months beginning Oct. 1, with eight workers facing job cuts while milk collections from member farms continue.

The cooperative's fresh-milk packaging and cultured-products lines will remain open. Of the powder line's 42 employees, 22 will receive transfer offers to fresh products, 12 will receive maintenance assignments and eight will receive redundancy notices.

Merefold will retain all contracted milk-collection volumes from its 87 member farms through the current contract year. But additional processing costs remain undisclosed, and next year's farm payment formula is still under review.

The announcement sets out a nine-month planning period, not a guaranteed production restart, according to the board's notice. The cooperative will reassess powder stocks and orders in March 2027 before committing to a restart date.

Chief executive Olin Wray said powder orders have fallen while storage and financing costs have increased. Powder inventory stands at 3,600 tons, up from 1,900 tons a year earlier, according to the cooperative's monthly stock report.

That leaves the cooperative holding 1,700 tons more powder than it did a year ago. Merefold has not published customer-level order figures or attributed the change to a single buyer or trade measure.

The powder line's 42 employees are part of a wider plant workforce of 110. Transfer offers will preserve base pay but require different shift times. Acceptance totals were not available as of Wednesday, leaving the number of employees who will take those offers unresolved.

Employee representative Tavren Cole has asked Merefold to offer retraining to the eight workers receiving redundancy notices. No retraining agreement or severance total has been announced.

For member farms, the commitment to maintain contracted collections provides an answer about where their milk will go during the current contract year. It does not settle what they will be paid under next year's formula.

Some of the collected milk will be processed under a tolling agreement at Larkspur Dairies, 76 miles away. The additional transport and tolling costs have not been disclosed.

"We are pausing one way of processing milk, not telling our farms to stop producing it," Wray said.

Member farmer Ina Selk said she needs the next payment schedule before renewing a feed contract.

"Collecting the milk answers one question. What comes back on the statement answers another," Selk said.

The pending payment formula leaves farms awaiting financial details even as the cooperative promises to keep collecting their contracted volumes. For employees offered transfers, the immediate decision involves new shift times with base pay preserved.

Merefold was formed by 19 farms in 1978 and added powder production in 2006. The shutdown applies to that processing line rather than the whole plant. The stock report supplies no evidence of milk being discarded or of a consumer shortage of fresh dairy products.
