# Public access planned for Coppermead ballfield in April after club lease ends

COPPERMEAD — The city plans to open Mill Acre ballfield for general recreation in April 2027, following access and drainage work after the Coppermead Athletics Club’s exclusive lease expires Sept. 30.

The end of the 30-year lease will not immediately open the field to the public. The fence will remain closed during the work, and the parks department will publish a construction timetable after procurement.

The city has owned the 11-acre property throughout the lease. The transition does not involve buying the land from the club, whose lease will end without renewal.

The council has approved a $275,000 allowance for the work, though final contractor prices have not yet been obtained. Initial improvements include a step-free entrance, repairs to the perimeter path and drainage improvements in the wet corner nearest the pavilion.

“Opening a gate is simple; making room for different uses is the work that comes after it,” parks director Soren Vale said.

Under a new booking agreement, the club will retain 12 scheduled training hours each week but will no longer have exclusive control of the property. Other organized groups will use the city’s booking system, with casual recreation possible outside reserved sessions.

“We lose the certainty of having the whole place, but we keep a place to train,” club secretary Mara Keel said.

Nearby residents have asked for seating and informal play space rather than having every available hour devoted to organized bookings. The parks department will hold a management-plan meeting before the public opening.

Proposed public opening hours are 6 a.m. to 8 p.m., but the council still must confirm them in the final management plan. The current plan neither adds floodlights nor authorizes late-night events at the field.

Whether dogs will be allowed also remains unsettled. A dog-access policy will be considered alongside maintenance needs and sports-use requirements.

The city will assume responsibility for routine grounds maintenance, now handled by the club under its lease. The operating budget for that work will be considered separately from the $275,000 capital works allowance.

The pavilion toilets will remain closed until a condition assessment and the scope of repairs are complete. Their status is separate from the planned access and drainage improvements that are to precede general recreation at the ground.

As part of the transition, the club has agreed to transfer ownership of its small equipment shed to the city. It will remove its privately owned training equipment.

The club’s noticeboard holds handwritten match results dating to its first season at Mill Acre. The local history society has offered to photograph the board before the pavilion is altered.

The approved transition plan includes no attendance forecast or estimate of additional spending at local businesses. Contractor pricing, the maintenance budget and final management rules remain outstanding as the city prepares to move the field from exclusive club use to shared public access.
