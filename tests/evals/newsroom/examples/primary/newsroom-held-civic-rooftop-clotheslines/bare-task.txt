Bell Orchard's Rooftop Line: Sheets, Sunlight and a Sign-Up Sheet

By staff report

Bell Orchard housing cooperative has reopened its rooftop drying area after replacing the access door and repairing the shared lines, giving residents a common outdoor space to hang laundry for the first time since the arrangement was closed for review.

The space existed in the building's original plans but had been shut while the cooperative sorted out how residents would reach it and share it. That question is now settled: residents take the building's lift and a short, level corridor to the roof, rather than carrying wet washing up a final flight of stairs.

On a recent morning, residents could be seen carrying folded sheets up to the roof, checking the wind, and pulling pegs from a communal basket before clipping laundry to the lines. The basket holds donated spares, though residents also bring their own pegs and always supply their own washing.

Booking the space does not require an app. Resident coordinator Mara Dene keeps a paper sign-up sheet by the door so households can claim a period for a designated section of line — not the whole roof, and not exclusive use of the space itself.

"The small negotiation is usually whose sheets will be dry before the next person arrives," Dene said.

The booking rules also build in time for residents to collect their own dry items, rather than treating the end of a slot as an invitation for the next household to handle someone else's clothes.

For resident Elra Fenn, the appeal is space, not scenery. Drying bulky bedding indoors, she said, eats up much of the usable room in her small apartment.

"Up here the bed linen is somebody's washing," Fenn said. "Downstairs it can become the whole room."

The rooftop itself offers a modest view — mostly the backs of nearby shop signs, rather than the landscaped frontage visible from the main street. The cooperative has been clear the project is not meant to turn the roof into a public terrace or a venue for gatherings.

The area has posted opening hours and closes under conditions spelled out in the cooperative's building-use plan, though officials did not detail those triggers beyond noting they exist. Residents are not permitted to move drying racks or lines into other parts of the roof outside the designated section.

A separate group of residents asked the cooperative to preserve quiet hours around the drying area, noting that apartments directly below the roof pick up the sound of footsteps and baskets being moved overhead. The cooperative's caretaker continues to check the shared equipment and field any fault reports through its usual maintenance process.

The project cost the cooperative $14,800 for the door, lines and related repairs. Officials said they had not calculated a corresponding energy-savings figure for households that might dry laundry outdoors instead of using machines.

The arrangement does not require every resident to use the rooftop space, nor does it replace anyone's private laundry equipment. The cooperative said it plans to revisit the length of booking slots once residents have used the area through a full season, adjusting the system based on that experience.

Officials were careful to note the arrangement is specific to Bell Orchard and does not establish any right to use rooftop space at other buildings.
