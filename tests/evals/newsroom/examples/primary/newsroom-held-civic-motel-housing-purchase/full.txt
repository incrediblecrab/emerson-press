Headline: Nonprofit Buys Former Brook Lantern Motel to Create 18 Apartments

The Alder Key Housing Trust has completed its purchase of the closed Brook Lantern Motel, with plans to convert the vacant property into 18 self-contained apartments for people moving out of temporary housing, the nonprofit said.

The trust closed on the $1.8 million purchase Sept. 16, financed with a $1.2 million housing grant and $600,000 from the trust's own acquisition fund, according to the organization.

The motel has stood empty since its operator closed it in 2024, and the purchase does not displace any current guests, the trust said. The building has 24 former guest rooms; under the trust's current concept, some would be combined to create 18 apartments, though no units are ready for occupancy and the purchase itself does not approve a final conversion design.

"A purchased building is an opportunity for homes, not eighteen keys we can hand out tomorrow," Trust Director Dera Quill said.

The apartments are intended for households in the trust's locally funded allocation program who are currently in temporary accommodation. Final eligibility rules, rents and application dates have not been published, the trust said.

Under the trust's design brief, the conversion must add kitchens to units, improve insulation and revise fire-separation arrangements throughout the building. The trust has commissioned an assessment of that work but has not set a renovation budget or chosen a contractor. Officials said the acquisition grant does not automatically cover every renovation expense.

A group representing neighboring residents said it supports reusing the empty building but wants details on how the trust will handle parking and support services at the site.

"We want to understand the daily arrangements, not argue about an empty building in the abstract," said Olin Sare, the group's secretary.

The trust said it plans to staff an on-site management office during business hours but has not proposed round-the-clock staffing. The motel's existing parking court will remain part of the property, though its final layout will depend on the conversion design, the trust said.

The project will use the motel's existing footprint in its first phase rather than add a separate apartment building, according to the trust. The former breakfast room is being considered for a shared laundry and meeting space rather than a restaurant, and the roadside lantern sign remains on the property, though the trust has not decided whether to keep it.

The trust said it will hold a public design meeting once the building assessment is finished. Under the completed purchase agreement, the previous owner has no continuing role in selecting future residents, the trust said.

Officials said further information on the apartments, including eligibility and applications, will be released through local advice organizations as well as the trust's own communications once available.

The trust did not provide a completion date for the conversion or an estimate of the project's effect on the city's overall housing waiting list.
