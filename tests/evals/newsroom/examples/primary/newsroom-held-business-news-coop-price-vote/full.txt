**Larch Hollow Food Cooperative Members Vote to Trade Rebate for Lower Shelf Prices**

LARCH HOLLOW — Members of the Larch Hollow Food Cooperative voted 280-90 Wednesday to end its member rebate program and use the money instead to lower shelf prices on selected staple foods, a change the co-op says will apply to members and nonmembers alike.

The vote, held Aug. 26, authorizes the cooperative to stop the rebate on purchases made after Oct. 1. Rebate credits members have already earned will still be redeemable under their existing expiration dates.

Of the cooperative's roughly 6,800 members, 370 cast ballots.

Last year's rebate returned $54,000 in credits to members based on their eligible purchases. The board plans to put the same amount — $54,000 — toward reduced shelf prices in the coming year, though it has not yet chosen which items will be discounted. The board said the new budget is not additional money on top of an unchanged rebate program; it replaces the rebate.

The cooperative reported an operating surplus of $61,000 last year before rebate credits were distributed.

Manager Edda Pike said the co-op will track item-level sales and gross margins on the initial round of price cuts before deciding whether to expand the list.

The cooperative has not calculated how the change will affect individual households and said it has no estimate of how many shoppers will end up saving more than they received under the former rebate. Whether a given shopper comes out ahead will depend on how much they had been earning under the rebate and whether they buy the specific items chosen for the new discounts, according to the co-op.

Member Oren Lusk said his rebate credit had helped cover a large winter grocery trip in past years. "A little off a shelf price does not arrive in one envelope when the heating bill does," he said.

Cira Venn, a nonmember who shops at the co-op for basic ingredients, said she welcomed the change. Venn said she has not been able to spend enough there regularly to justify paying for membership just to collect rebates. "I can use a lower price today without waiting to become somebody's loyal customer," she said.

The cooperative's $20 annual membership fee is unchanged, and members will keep their voting rights and discounts on workshops.

The board's authorization includes a required review after 12 months and does not commit the cooperative to keeping the price-reduction program beyond that point.

Board members said the vote does not reflect any decision to reduce wages, change how the cooperative pays its suppliers, or sell the business.

The cooperative began in 1989 as a bulk-buying club and still keeps a community noticeboard for member recipes and classes.

The price cuts are set to take effect alongside the rebate's end on Oct. 1. The co-op said it expects to announce the initial list of discounted items before that date.
