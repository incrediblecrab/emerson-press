**Larch Hollow Co-op Members Vote to Swap Rebate for Lower Shelf Prices**

LARCH HOLLOW — Members of the Larch Hollow Food Cooperative voted Wednesday to scrap the store's member rebate program in favor of targeted price cuts on staple foods, a change that will apply to members and nonmembers alike.

The measure passed 280-90 in balloting completed Aug. 26. It ends the rebate for purchases made after Oct. 1, redirecting the money that would have funded it toward reduced prices on a still-undetermined list of items. Rebate credits already earned will remain redeemable under their existing expiration dates.

About 6,800 people belong to the cooperative, and 370 cast ballots in the vote.

Last year's rebate returned $54,000 in credits to members based on their eligible purchases. The board has budgeted the same amount, $54,000, for the coming year's price reductions, but has not finalized which products will see lower shelf tags. Board members stressed the new spending is not additional money layered on top of an unchanged rebate — it replaces it.

Manager Edda Pike said the cooperative will monitor item-level sales and gross margins before deciding whether to expand the discounted list. "We want to see how shoppers respond to the first round of price changes before committing to more," she said.

The cooperative reported an operating surplus of $61,000 last year before rebate credits were distributed.

Officials acknowledged they have no household-level estimate showing which shoppers will end up saving more money under the new system than they did under the rebate. Whether an individual shopper comes out ahead will depend on how much they used to spend on rebate-eligible purchases and whether they buy the newly discounted items going forward.

That uncertainty divided shoppers interviewed outside the store this week.

Member Oren Lusk said his rebate check had helped cover a large winter grocery trip in past years. "A little off a shelf price does not arrive in one envelope when the heating bill does," he said.

Cira Venn, who is not a member, said she buys basic ingredients at the co-op but doesn't spend enough regularly to justify paying for membership just to earn a rebate. "I can use a lower price today without waiting to become somebody's loyal customer," she said.

The cooperative's annual membership fee will stay at $20. Members will keep their voting rights and discounts on in-store workshops regardless of the pricing change.

The vote includes a requirement that members review the new pricing approach after 12 months; it does not guarantee the program will continue beyond that point.

Cooperative officials said no decisions have been made to reduce employee wages, change how quickly the store pays suppliers, or sell the business — steps that have circulated as rumors among some shoppers since the vote was announced.

Founded in 1989 as a bulk-buying club, the cooperative still keeps a community noticeboard where members post recipes and sign up for classes.

The board is expected to announce the initial list of discounted items before the Oct. 1 start date.
