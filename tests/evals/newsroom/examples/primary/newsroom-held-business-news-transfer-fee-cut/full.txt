**Wayfern cuts counter transfer fee to Keldra in half, but payout amount stays the same**

Wayfern Transfer said Tuesday that it will lower its fee on qualifying cash transfers to Keldra from $4 to $2 starting Sept. 15, though the change does not affect the exchange rate the company applies or the amount a recipient receives.

The lower fee applies to cash-funded transfers of $100 to $500, sent in person at one of eight local Wayfern agency counters. The fee is charged on top of that amount rather than subtracted from it, and it is not counted toward the $100-to-$500 range. Transfers made through Wayfern's separate online product keep their existing fee schedule and are not part of this change.

Using the exchange rate Wayfern quoted Sept. 1 — 9.8 Keldran luma per dollar, against a reference rate of 10 — a $200 transfer would pay 1,960 luma to the recipient and cost the sender $202 under the new fee, compared with $204 under the current one. The luma figure would not change between the two fees; only the sender's total cost drops. Wayfern said that exchange quote was current as of Sept. 1 and is not guaranteed to be available Sept. 15 or afterward.

Wayfern manager Iona Pell said a renegotiated agency contract made the lower fee possible. She did not disclose the commercial terms of that agreement.

"The transfer fee is lower," Pell said. "We are not saying every part of the exchange has become cheaper."

That distinction is the one Wayfern is drawing: the company's currency-conversion margin — the gap between the reference rate and the rate it offers customers — is unchanged by Tuesday's announcement. Wayfern said recipients pay no additional Wayfern collection fee through the payout partners covered by the offer, though it did not name those partners. The company's reporting packet does not address whether banks or other intermediaries outside those partners might impose their own charges.

Reni Moss, who sends money regularly to cover a relative's household expenses and uses a counter because she wants a printed receipt, said the sender's fee is not the only number she checks.

"The fee on my side matters, but I also ask what number appears on the other side," Moss said.

Wayfern said it will continue to show customers both the amount they are paying and the amount that will be paid out before they confirm a transfer. Payout timing is expected to remain the next business day, subject to the identity and transaction checks Wayfern already applies.

The company processed 12,400 transfers on this route last month, a count of transactions rather than of individual senders, meaning the figure could include repeat transfers by the same customer.

Wayfern's announcement sets no end date for the $2 fee but does not commit the company to holding future exchange rates or other fees at current levels. The reporting packet does not include data comparing Wayfern's pricing with that of other transfer providers on this route.

Wayfern opened its first local counter, inside a travel shop, in 2010.
