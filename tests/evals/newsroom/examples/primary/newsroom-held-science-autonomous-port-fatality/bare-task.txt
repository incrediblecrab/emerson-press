Worker Killed by Autonomous Vehicle in First U.S. Fatality at Fully Automated Port

A maintenance worker was struck and killed by an autonomous straddle carrier Sunday at the Portside Container Terminal, in what federal officials say is the first fatality at a fully automated container terminal in the United States.

The victim, identified by family as David Osei, 41, was performing a scheduled inspection of a carrier guide rail when the vehicle entered the zone, according to terminal officials.

Terminal records show the carrier's proximity detection system had been running in a degraded-mode status for about 90 minutes before the incident, the result of a software update deployed that morning. The degraded status was flagged by the terminal's central management system but was not escalated to a stop-work decision. Standard protocol allows carriers to keep operating in degraded mode at reduced speed limits, according to terminal records.

Portside Terminal director Melissa Ward said the facility has halted all automated operations.

"We have suspended all autonomous vehicle operations pending a full safety review," Ward said. "Our deepest condolences go to the family of our colleague."

Ward said the terminal will not resume autonomous operations until an independent safety audit is complete.

The Occupational Safety and Health Administration opened an inspection within hours of the incident and has jurisdiction over the investigation into workplace conditions. The agency has six months to complete its inspection and issue any citations.

Federal investigators are also examining whether the software update that triggered the degraded mode should have forced a mandatory stop-work hold under existing automated equipment regulations, and whether Osei followed a required zone clearance procedure that technicians must use before entering active carrier areas.

Paul Kimura, director of the federal Maritime Automation Safety Task Force, said the incident marks a turning point for an industry that has largely operated without a fatal accident in the U.S.

"We have not had a fatality at a U.S. automated terminal before," Kimura said. "This event will force a reassessment of degraded-mode operating permissions at facilities across the country."

The task force was established in 2024 but has not yet issued binding standards for automated terminal operations.

Portside became fully automated in 2022 and uses 28 autonomous straddle carriers — each weighing about 42 tonnes unladen — along with automated guided vehicles and remote-operated ship-to-shore cranes. The carrier involved in Sunday's incident was manufactured by Helix Automation, which said it is fully cooperating with investigators.

The terminal handles roughly 1.4 million container units a year, and the suspension of autonomous operations has halted about 70% of its throughput.

A union representing workers at adjacent non-automated berths said it had raised safety concerns about the Portside expansion with OSHA in 2023. Those concerns were addressed in a compliance audit that year, the union said.

Automated terminals have operated in Europe and Asia since the 1990s. European industry data shows two prior fatalities at such facilities, both involving temporary workers who entered active zones.

An attorney for Osei's family said the family intends to pursue all available legal remedies.

A trade group representing terminal operators said the industry is committed to working with regulators to update safety standards in light of the incident.

The investigation into the exact circumstances of Osei's death, including whether proper safety procedures were followed, remains ongoing.
