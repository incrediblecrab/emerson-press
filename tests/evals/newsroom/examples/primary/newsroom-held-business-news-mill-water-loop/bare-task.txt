# Sedge Row Paper approves $5.6 million water-reuse project

Sedge Row Paper’s board approved a $5.6 million water-reuse installation Friday that its equipment supplier projects will cut the Dovewick mill’s fresh-water use by 40%, though the project will add electricity demand and its financial payback has not been verified.

Construction is scheduled to begin in November and take eight months. The system will filter process water so it can be used again, reducing — but not eliminating — the mill’s need for fresh water. It also will retain a controlled discharge stream.

The mill currently draws an average of 900,000 gallons of fresh water per production day, according to its metered annual report. The supplier projects that the new equipment will reduce that to 540,000 gallons at the same paper output, a difference of 360,000 gallons per production day.

Those figures describe a target for an installation that has not yet been built, not a measured reduction. Comparing water use at equivalent production levels will be central to assessing whether the equipment achieves the projected savings.

Company engineer Teri Holt said dissolved salts limit how often the same water can circulate through the mill’s processes.

“Reusing more water is not the same as never needing another gallon,” Holt said.

The project’s costs extend beyond the installation budget. New pumps will increase electricity use, and the company has not published a calculation of the system’s net energy use or emissions. The expected reduction in fresh-water demand therefore does not establish an overall energy or emissions benefit.

The company expects its fresh-water purchase bill to fall but has not disclosed a verified payback period. The $5.6 million budget includes a $1.2 million manufacturing-efficiency grant and $4.4 million in company borrowing.

For households, the investment does not itself require higher water charges, the Dovewick Water Authority has confirmed. But the authority has not promised that savings for the industrial customer will translate into lower residential rates.

River-users association chair Inna Cole supports reducing withdrawals but wants public comparisons that account for how much paper the mill produces.

“A quieter mill uses less water too. We need to know what it took to make the same amount of paper,” Cole said.

The company’s plan provides for monthly water-use reporting during the first operating year. Those reports would provide a record of water use once the system is operating; the supplier’s current estimate remains a projection tied to unchanged paper output.

Mill employees will receive training for three new water-treatment operator assignments. The assignments will be filled from existing staff rather than through new hires.

The mill’s existing water authorization remains in place, and the environmental office has requested updated discharge-design details. The reuse system will still need both incoming fresh water and a stream through which water leaves the process.

The Dovewick mill makes packaging paper from recovered fiber and last replaced its pulping equipment in 2016. As of Friday’s approval, the reporting record contains no measured change in river wildlife, water quality or neighborhood health attributable to the water-reuse project.
