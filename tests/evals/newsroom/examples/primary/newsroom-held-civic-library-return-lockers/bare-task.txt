Bramble Quay Library to Launch After-Hours Tool-Return Lockers Oct. 5

BRAMBLE QUAY — The Bramble Quay Library Board on Sept. 3 approved a system of after-hours lockers that will let borrowers return household tools without waiting for a staffed service desk, with the new service set to begin Oct. 5.

The library's tool-lending collection includes items such as drills and sewing machines, which currently must be handed back to library staff during regular hours. Under the new system, four of the library's six branches will each install 16 compartments, for a total of 64 across the system. Installation work has begun, though the lockers are not yet available for use.

The $128,000 project, funded by a designated foundation grant, covers installation and the first year of maintenance. The grant does not cover ongoing costs beyond that period; the library estimates annual maintenance afterward at $12,000 and says a future operating allocation will be needed to sustain the service.

To use the lockers, borrowers will reserve a compartment sized for their item through their library account or by asking a branch employee to do so. A borrower may return an item to any of the four equipped branches, regardless of where it was checked out. The lockers will accept returns only; borrowing a tool will still require the existing in-branch checkout process. Oversized equipment and items with leaking containers are excluded and must still be returned during staffed hours.

Library director Osa Merrin said the change is meant to ease scheduling burdens on borrowers. "Returning something should not require taking time off work," Merrin said in the announcement.

The system will record the exact time an item is deposited, which will determine whether the return met its deadline even if staff do not inspect the item until the following day. However, depositing a tool will not immediately clear it from a borrower's account, since an employee must still check its parts and condition before the loan is closed out. Staff will empty and inspect the compartments before each participating branch opens on its next staffed day.

Two branches were left out of the initial installation because they lack suitable space under their current building leases. A tenants association near one of the excluded branches has asked the board to publish options for residents who cannot easily travel to another location. "An extra option across town is not the same option for someone carrying a sewing machine," said Devra Senn, the association's secretary. Printed instructions for using the lockers will be available at all six branches, including the two without them.

The board has asked library staff to conduct a review in January 2027 covering compartment availability, incomplete returns and requests made through branch employees. Library officials said the review will not begin with any target for reducing staff positions, and the approved plan includes no staffing cuts.

The library's tool-lending program began with donated garden equipment, though the library now purchases replacement items through its regular collection budget.

Library officials said they have not yet collected usage data on the new service and have not estimated how many borrowers might change their return habits once the lockers open.
