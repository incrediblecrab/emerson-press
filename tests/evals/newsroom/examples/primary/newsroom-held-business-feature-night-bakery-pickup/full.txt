**Before Dawn, a Bakery Reshapes Its Day — and Its Workers' Lives — to Beat the Breakfast Rush**

The first delivery van at Low Ember Bakery pulled out at 5:45 a.m. on July 28, according to a production note, while the finishing crew was still packing bread for the second route.

By then, the overnight shift had already been at work for more than five hours.

Low Ember supplies breakfast rolls and sandwich bread to 18 cafes and three workplace canteens. Five employees work the overnight production shift, which runs from 12:30 a.m. to 8:30 a.m. and includes eight paid hours under the bakery's roster. The overnight hourly rate is $25, including a $3 premium above the $22 daytime base rate.

The schedule is not the one the bakery started with. Owner Oren Moss moved production earlier in February after several cafe customers asked for deliveries before their own opening times.

For baker Ceri Dain, the shift solved one problem and created another. She now finishes in time to collect her child from an afternoon school activity, but relies on her partner for morning arrangements. She said she sleeps during part of the daytime; the bakery's records contain no measured health or sleep outcome for the crew.

"Finishing early gives me one part of the family day and takes me out of another," Dain said.

Crew members can request roster swaps. The supervisor recorded four approved swaps in August. Those four approvals do not show every request that was made, and they do not establish that every employee can obtain a preferred schedule.

The bakery makes about 3,000 rolls on a normal weekday, based on orders customers place before noon the preceding day. Changes after that cutoff can leave the bakery with surplus bread or with an addition to an order it cannot fill. Moss sells some surplus at a reduced price from a morning counter, but he has no guaranteed buyer for every extra tray.

Downstream, the timing shows up on cafe counters. Nessa Orlen, who owns one of the 18 cafes Low Ember supplies, said a late bread delivery can delay assembled lunch orders even when her coffee service opens on time.

"The morning rush in my shop starts with work somebody else did while our shutters were down," Orlen said.

A delivery log covering August lists 120 completed stops, three of which arrived after the promised window. No comparable log from before the February schedule change was supplied, so the record does not show whether the new shift improved delivery reliability overall.

The overnight bakers are not covering every task at Low Ember. A separate daytime crew handles cleaning and preparation work.

The bakery itself carries traces of an earlier business: Low Ember occupies a former restaurant kitchen, and one wall still carries an unused menu rail from that previous tenant.

Moss has not published a profit comparison or a net energy-saving estimate weighing the current schedule against the one it replaced. What exists instead is the daily arithmetic the crew and their customers work out on their own — a van that has to leave by quarter to 6, a counter of discounted trays, and a cafe owner counting on bread that was baked while she was still asleep.
