# How to weigh in on Spurwick’s proposed street-tree removals

Spurwick residents have until Oct. 6 to comment on recommendations to remove 12 city-managed street trees, with an opportunity to provide information that could lead to another inspection or a revised proposal.

The parks office posted the notices Sept. 15 after its latest scheduled inspections. As of Sept. 16, all 12 recommendations are in the city’s public review process. An objection requires consideration of the information submitted, but it does not automatically stop a removal.

Here is how the review works and what residents can contribute.

## Which trees are under review?

The parks office published recommendations concerning 14 street trees. Two with storm-split trunks were assigned immediate work under the city’s emergency-work procedure and are outside the 21-day comment timetable. The remaining 12 entered public review.

The process covers city-managed street trees, not every privately owned tree visible from the road. Each notice identifies the tree, the recorded reason for recommending removal and the city office receiving comments.

Reasons in the current notices include conflicting root and pavement conditions, damage recorded by inspectors and insufficient suitable space for a tree’s mature form. The reporting packet does not establish that all 12 trees are unsafe or that every recommendation will remain unchanged.

Several notice tags are attached to trees planted in a municipal street scheme more than 40 years ago.

## How can residents comment?

Residents can use the city form at https://example.org/spurwick/tree-review or make a paper submission at the parks office. Comments are due Oct. 6. Residents do not need to hire a specialist to participate.

Submissions may include photographs, local observations or questions about alternatives. The notice’s recorded reason for removal provides a starting point for a response: Residents can address that reason and identify relevant information the initial report may not cover.

City arborist Tera Venn said, “A useful response tells us what we have missed, not simply whether a tree is loved.”

When a comment supplies relevant information not addressed in the first report, the parks office can request another inspection or revise its proposal.

The office also has arranged a drop-in session to explain the notices. That session is not an on-site tree-safety assessment.

## Does an objection save a tree?

Not automatically. An objection triggers consideration of the information provided, rather than cancellation of the recommendation.

The process also is not a binding vote. The number of comments does not determine the outcome, so residents should not confuse support for retaining a tree with a guarantee that it will remain.

After considering submissions, the parks office will publish a decision record for the 12 recommendations. That record will state the final action and the main reasons.

## What about replacement trees and shade?

A street association has asked the city to explain replacement planting alongside removal decisions, rather than after work is finished.

Association member Orrin Saye said, “We are looking at the shade over a whole street, not only at the space around one trunk.”

Replacement planting is considered separately for site suitability. There is no promise that a new tree can go in the identical position, and the current notices do not include a total forecast of future canopy loss or growth.

Residents can raise questions about alternatives in their submissions, but a removal recommendation and a replacement planting decision are distinct matters.

The review procedure is Spurwick’s current local policy, not a statement of tree-removal law elsewhere.
