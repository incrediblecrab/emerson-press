**Slateford Approves $132,000 Plan for Permanent Power at Public Market**

SLATEFORD — The town council has approved a $132,000 budget to install permanent electrical supply points at the outdoor public market, replacing a patchwork of generators and borrowed outlets that stallholders have relied on for years.

The plan calls for 14 shared pedestals, each with two separately booked outlets, for a total of 28 bookable connections. The market currently has 52 registered stallholders, though not all trade every market day or require power.

Construction is planned for the gap between the end of the winter market season and the market's scheduled March 2027 reopening, subject to final scheduling with contractors. Licensed contractors will install and inspect the equipment before any outlet becomes available for use.

The approved budget covers installation and inspection. It does not cover the electricity itself or ongoing maintenance, which will remain separate operating expenses. The council has not announced what it will charge stallholders to book an outlet or how it will divide electricity costs among users. Council members said those decisions will come later, tied to the market's next fee schedule.

"Power will become something we book and maintain together, rather than something each stall improvises separately," market manager Elva Rane said.

Powered stall positions will be assigned through a published booking process rather than claimed by traders arriving first, according to the plan. The market office will hold equipment-registration sessions before assigning powered spaces, and the outlet plan caps the equipment load allowed at each pedestal — approval to run one appliance will not automatically extend to every appliance a trader owns.

Some stall locations will shift to align with the new supply layout, though the council has not yet issued a final assignment map. The pedestals themselves will sit along existing service edges of the market square rather than in the middle of pedestrian aisles, which the plan is designed to keep clear.

Stallholders who currently power equipment through permitted generators or arrangements with nearby businesses will not lose that ability immediately. The council said existing generator permissions remain in effect until replacement arrangements are announced, and the vote to fund the pedestals does not by itself revoke them.

Reaction among traders was mixed. Food trader Neri Vale welcomed the move but said cost details remain the open question.

"A reliable socket would help, but the running charge matters as much as the installation announcement," Vale said.

Some stallholders who sell goods without powered equipment have asked the council to spell out, in the eventual fee system, which costs will be shared by the whole market rather than billed only to traders who book outlets. The council said it will consult stallholders on operating charges before adopting the next fee schedule.

The council has not supplied measurements of current generator emissions at the market or predicted a specific reduction in noise once permanent power is in place. The project also does not include vehicle-charging facilities for customers or unrestricted public access to the market's outlets, and the council has not approved any increase in the market's total number of stalls as part of the work.

Further details on booking procedures and charges are expected before the market's March reopening.
