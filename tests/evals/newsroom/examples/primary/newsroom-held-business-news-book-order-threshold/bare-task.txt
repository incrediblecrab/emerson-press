# Wrenstack to combine publishers' books for free freight, set 50-copy minimum

Wrenstack Distribution will let bookstores combine books from different publishers to qualify for free shipping starting Oct. 1, a change that could simplify deliveries for some independent shops while leaving smaller orders subject to a flat $15 charge.

The distributor notified bookstore customers Friday, Aug. 21, of the new terms. Stores will need at least 50 copies in a shipment to receive free freight, but those books can come from any of the 38 small publishers Wrenstack represents. Previously, stores needed 25 copies from each individual publisher for that publisher's shipment to qualify.

The change shifts the shipping calculation from purchases from each publisher to the size of a combined shipment. For Wrenstack's 214 independent bookstore customers in its region, the practical benefit will depend on how many books they order, how widely those purchases are spread and how long they can wait for delivery.

Shipments containing fewer than 50 copies will cost $15, regardless of the books' combined retail value. Book trade discounts and the existing return window will not change. The policy covers only books from Wrenstack's represented publishers, not every title a participating bookstore sells.

Elbow Lane Books offers one example of a shop positioned to use the combined threshold. Owner Teri Moss said the store currently orders about 60 copies a week across six Wrenstack publishers. If gathered into one shipment, that weekly volume would clear the new free-freight minimum.

Moss expects a mixed shipment to reduce the number of small deliveries she must receive and unpack. Combining purchases could thus change not just the store's freight calculation but also the work of handling incoming books.

For shops that buy in smaller quantities, the choice may be less straightforward. Dune Pocket Books often orders 10 or fewer copies to fill customer requests and lacks room for large spare stocks. Owner Ivo Keld said waiting to accumulate 50 copies could slow special orders.

"The shelf space is real money too, even when the delivery line says free," Keld said.

Wrenstack will allow shops to keep a combined order open for up to seven calendar days before dispatch, giving them time to add books. Shops may request an earlier dispatch; the $15 charge applies only if the shipment contains fewer than 50 copies.

That option lets stores choose between sending a small order promptly and waiting to combine it with other purchases. It does not eliminate the space constraints or customer deadlines that can make a larger order impractical.

For publishers, combining shipments could help titles move in quantities too small to meet a publisher-specific freight threshold. Small publisher Asha Vale said a mixed order could place her slower-selling poetry titles alongside releases from larger publishers.

"I want the book to travel with the order, not wait until it can fill a box by itself," Vale said.

Distribution director Fenna Sore said a new shared warehouse inventory system made mixed-publisher packing possible. Wrenstack's warehouse employs 31 people and occupies a former wholesale-fabric building.

The company has scheduled two customer webinars and is not requiring stores to use automated reordering. It has not released estimates of aggregate bookstore savings or additional warehouse labor costs, leaving the overall financial effect uncertain ahead of the October rollout.
