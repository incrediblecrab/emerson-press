# Foldline Maps keeps paper walking routes under revision

A path connection came off the next edition of the original Asterfen Footpath Map because mapmaker Neri Saven could not confirm it. The June 2026 revision note records a small subtraction with a practical purpose: keeping an uncertain connection off a printed walking map.

For Foldline Maps, a two-person business selling local walking maps through shops and visitor centers, deciding what to leave out is part of keeping the product useful. Once an edition is printed, changes to routes and access information must be addressed on a separate corrections sheet.

The seasonal business depends on selling paper that remains useful even as the information on it changes. Copies printed are not copies sold, and an unsold edition can become outdated before its paper wears out. Fieldwork, revision decisions and the handling of unsold stock all sit behind a $3.50 retail price.

“Paper does not need a signal, but it still needs a date,” Saven said.

Foldline checks proposed revisions against land-manager notices, direct permissions and its own recorded site observations. Those checks inform what goes into an edition; they do not give the business authority to settle disputed public-access rights or guarantee a route’s future condition. Omitting a location because access is unconfirmed does not declare it permanently closed or legally private.

As of June 30, one planned revision concerns a footbridge under repair. The land manager has not announced a reopening date, so Foldline is holding space for an update rather than printing a guessed reopening promise.

The printed map identifies its revision month and directs readers to a dated corrections sheet available from stockists. That gives the business a way to update information after printing, but the sheet cannot alter a copy already in a customer’s bag.

The cost of that limitation is part of the arithmetic of a print run. The current run is 2,000 copies at $1.20 each, a $2,400 print bill before fieldwork, design and other costs.

Participating retailers retain 40% of the $3.50 listed retail price, or $1.40 per sale. Foldline receives $2.10, leaving 90 cents after the stated unit print cost and before other expenses. That 90 cents is not profit: it must still contribute toward the work and costs beyond printing.

Retailers can also return unopened bundles under negotiated terms, transferring some risk of unsold stock back to Foldline. Printing a bundle does not ensure that customers will buy it while its information is current.

The appeal of paper does not necessarily rest on customers abandoning screens. Visitor-center worker Omi Harlen said some customers prefer a paper overview while using a phone for other travel information.

“People are not always choosing paper instead of a phone. Sometimes they want the two things to do different jobs,” Harlen said.

Foldline has not measured a general decline in phone navigation or a universal preference for printed maps. Its business began with a hand-drawn festival-area leaflet in 2014 and now uses digital cartographic files for printing.

A larger regional edition remains a proposal, with no completed survey or committed print order. For the next edition of the original Asterfen map, the June revision note records a narrower decision: leave out the connection that could not be confirmed.
