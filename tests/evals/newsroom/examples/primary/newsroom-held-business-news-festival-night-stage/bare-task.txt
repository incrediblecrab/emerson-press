# Marrow Quay festival cancels evening stage; refunds to begin Aug. 17

The Marrow Quay Lantern Festival canceled its ticketed evening stage Friday, Aug. 14, three weeks before the event, after a revised production quote increased costs by $34,000. Ticket holders will receive automatic refunds, while food vendors face decisions about whether to keep their weekend pitches.

Free daytime workshops and the lantern walk remain scheduled for Sept. 4-6. The daytime program uses existing indoor rooms and does not require the temporary stage that would have served the evening concerts.

The production contractor’s revised quote for the stage, lighting and power was $112,000, up from a provisional estimate of $78,000. Festival treasurer Una Vale said the earlier quote had not been a fixed-price contract.

Vale said the board chose to cancel rather than take a short-term loan it could not repay from current ticket sales. Festival accounts show that 2,400 evening tickets had been sold at a single face price of $25, generating $60,000 in face-value receipts.

A $40,000 municipal grant cannot be used to cover the evening stage under its award terms. That money is restricted to free daytime activities, leaving it unavailable to offset the revised production costs.

The festival says it will refund ticket face values and its booking fees automatically to the original payment method. Refunds are scheduled to begin Aug. 17 and be completed by Aug. 28. The $60,000 recorded in face-value receipts does not include the booking fees that the festival also says it will return.

For vendors, the cancellation removes an evening concert program that some had counted on as their main selling period. Twenty-two food vendors booked pitches for the full weekend, including nine that planned to operate mainly during the concerts.

Vendors can cancel without losing their pitch deposits through Aug. 21. The festival will not, however, reimburse food purchases or travel costs.

Food-stall owner Ceri Holt has ordered frozen ingredients that can be used at later events but has already paid for festival signage. The option to recover a pitch deposit does not account for all the work already done, Holt said.

“Getting the pitch money back is not the same as getting the whole week of preparation back.”

The festival has paid $19,000 in nonrefundable planning and artist deposits associated with the canceled stage. Four musical acts had contracts to perform, and their representatives are discussing cancellation payments beyond those deposits with the festival.

Those negotiations, along with unresolved vendor cancellations, mean the supplied accounts do not establish a final festival-wide loss. The production quote increase and the deposits already paid are known costs, but they do not settle the festival’s overall financial position.

Festival director Rian Quell said the decision preserves the portion of the event organizers can still deliver.

“Keeping the free program open is a smaller promise, but it is one we can still make.”

The cancellation scales back a festival that began as a school lantern display in 2009 and added paid concerts in 2018. For now, the remaining program consists of the scheduled free daytime workshops and lantern walk. The board has not approved an evening replacement event or a date for next year’s festival.
