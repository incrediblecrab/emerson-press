Temporary Moves Begin as Hearth Court Starts Phased Pipe and Wiring Retrofit

Hearth Court housing association is moving forward with a retrofit of shared pipe runs and wiring across its six stairwells, starting with a first construction phase covering two of the six stairwells — 40 of the complex's 120 apartments — rather than relocating all households at once, according to reporting notes reviewed as of September 18, 2026.

The work involves replacing shared infrastructure in sections of the building that cannot remain in normal use while they are opened up, the notes say. Because of that, the association's written resident plan sets out temporary accommodation for households whose apartments become unavailable during construction.

Under the plan, a temporary move does not end a resident's existing tenancy with the association, and the vacated apartment is not allocated to another household in the meantime, according to the notes. Moving dates are set individually, after the association assesses each household's access needs alongside the contractor's construction sequence — meaning that even though one phase covers 40 apartments, not every household within that phase will be displaced for the same number of days.

"We need a building schedule and a household plan; neither one can simply stand in for the other," housing manager Selin Marr said, according to the notes.

The resident plan covers agreed moving transport and temporary accommodation costs but does not provide unlimited reimbursement of every expense a resident might incur, the notes indicate. Before each move, the association will give the household a written record of the agreed arrangements. Residents may raise school, work, mobility and caregiving arrangements that bear on whether a proposed temporary location works for them, and the association has not promised that temporary housing will be within walking distance of Hearth Court.

Residents committee member Tera Vale said the offer of temporary housing is only a starting point for discussion. "Being offered a roof is the beginning of the conversation when your ordinary week is built around this address," she said, per the notes.

Returning home will also follow a defined process rather than a fixed calendar date, the notes say. The construction contract requires a check of completed services before any household is told its apartment is available again, and the association will issue return arrangements after that check — meaning the contractor's target completion date is not by itself permission to move back.

Rent during the temporary period will be handled according to the resident plan and explained in each household's written arrangement, the notes say. The materials reviewed do not establish any rent increase tied to the completed retrofit, nor do they set out a general rule applicable to other landlords.

For the first phase, a resident liaison contact has been named separately from the contractor's site delivery contact, according to the notes. The association plans to hold meetings in the ground-floor common room, with individual conversations available for residents who prefer not to raise issues in public.

The building's shared laundry facility is not part of the first construction phase, though its access route may change temporarily, the notes say. Later phases covering the remaining four stairwells will be scheduled only after the association reviews how the first round of moves went and evaluates the contractor's progress, according to the notes.

The reporting notes reviewed contain no personal details of any actual household and no final placement list.
