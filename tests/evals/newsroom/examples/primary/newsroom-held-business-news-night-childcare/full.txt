Lantern Shift Child Care Center Set to Open Oct. 12, With Room for Only 20 Children a Night

EAST VARN — A consortium of three employers announced Thursday that a new evening child care center serving overnight shift workers is scheduled to open Oct. 12, offering 20 places a night for children ages 3 through 10 — a fraction of what officials say the town's night-shift workforce may need.

The consortium behind the Lantern Shift center includes a parcel depot, a food-packing plant and a hotel laundry in East Varn. Together the three employers have 1,180 workers, though they have not published how many of those employees need child care.

The center, to be housed in a former community training building with separate quiet and active areas, will run sessions from 4 p.m. to 2 a.m. Monday through Friday. It will not operate on weekends.

Fourteen families have reserved regular bookings, though the number of children present on any given evening will depend on parents' shifts, according to the consortium.

Families will pay $5 per child for each booked hour. A combined $240,000 employer subsidy is intended to cover the center's remaining budgeted operating costs for its first year — not unlimited cost overruns, the consortium said. A separate $160,000 refit budget has no payer breakdown in the announcement. The subsidy commitment expires after one year, and fees beyond that period have not been set.

Parents employed by the three consortium members will get booking priority; other local families may use any remaining places.

The center has hired a director and four qualified care workers and is recruiting two relief staff. A final premises inspection is booked for September, and the Oct. 12 opening remains conditional on that approval.

Center director Olla Reed acknowledged the limits of the new capacity.

"Twenty places are a start, but they are not a solution for every night worker in town," Reed said.

For laundry worker Miri Gant, the center's appeal lies less in its size than in its flexibility around shift changes. Gant currently relies on her sister to collect her child when late shifts run long.

"I need to book care for the shift I actually work, not the one printed a month ago," Gant said.

Under the consortium's announced booking policy, families can change reservations without a fee until noon on the day before care is needed. Changes made later will be charged unless the center is able to fill the vacated place.

The center is not the town's first attempt at evening child care. A previous town-funded pilot program offering short evening care ended in 2022 after its grant expired.

Because the Lantern Shift center has not yet opened, there are no results yet on whether it will affect worker retention, absences or productivity at the three participating employers.

With capacity capped at 20 places against a combined workforce topping 1,000, and next year's fees still undetermined, questions remain about how many families the center can ultimately serve — and for how long its funding will hold once the first-year subsidy runs out.
