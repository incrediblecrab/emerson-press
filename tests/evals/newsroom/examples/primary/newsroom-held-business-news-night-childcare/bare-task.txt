**East Varn, Aug. 27, 2026** — A three-employer consortium here announced Thursday that its planned evening child care center, known as Lantern Shift, is scheduled to open Oct. 12, offering families working overnight and late-shift jobs an alternative to informal arrangements — though the center's limited hours, capacity and one-year funding commitment mean it will not meet all local demand.

The consortium — a parcel depot, a food-packing plant and a hotel laundry — will operate the center in a former community training building, with separate quiet and active areas for children ages 3 through 10. It will offer 20 places at any one time, with sessions running from 4 p.m. to 2 a.m. Monday through Friday. No sessions will begin on Saturday or Sunday.

Fourteen families have made regular bookings so far, though the number of children present on any given evening will vary with parents' shifts. Families employed by the three consortium members will get booking priority, with remaining places open to other local families.

The center's hours illustrate a limit for some workers: care is unavailable before 4 p.m. or after 2 a.m., and there is no weekend coverage. The three employers together have 1,180 workers, but have not said how many need child care, leaving the gap between 20 places and total demand unclear.

Cost is also bounded in scope. Families will pay $5 per child per booked hour, with a first-year, $240,000 combined employer subsidy intended to cover the center's remaining budgeted operating costs — not unlimited overruns. That subsidy commitment expires after one year, and fees for families beyond that period have not been set. A separate $160,000 refit budget has been announced, though the record does not show which employer is paying what share.

The opening also remains conditional: a final premises inspection is booked for September, and the Oct. 12 date depends on the building winning approval.

Center director Olla Reed said the facility should be understood as a partial step rather than a full fix. "Twenty places are a start, but they are not a solution for every night worker in town," Reed said.

The center has hired a director and four qualified care workers and is recruiting two relief staff, according to the consortium.

Under the announced booking policy, families can change bookings without a fee until noon the day before care is needed. Changes made later will be charged unless the center is able to fill the vacated place.

That policy drew attention from Miri Gant, a laundry worker whose current child care depends on her sister picking up her child when late shifts run long. Gant said the shift-based charge structure needs to reflect how real schedules work.

"I need to book care for the shift I actually work, not the one printed a month ago," Gant said.

East Varn previously funded a short-lived evening care pilot that ended in 2022 after its grant expired, a history that underscores questions about the durability of the new center's funding once the one-year subsidy lapses.

No data yet exists on how the unopened center might affect worker retention, absences or productivity, since it has not begun operating.

If the September inspection is approved, Lantern Shift will open Oct. 12 as one of the only evening-care options in East Varn — a limited but closely watched test of how employers can address off-hours child care needs.
