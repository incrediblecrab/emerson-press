# Why Fernwick Playhouse’s online ticket price now starts at $35

A ticket to Fernwick Playhouse’s autumn production of “The Narrow Orchard” now appears to cost more online, but the mandatory checkout total has not changed. The website is showing the fees earlier.

The playhouse changed its display Aug. 10. Previously, customers first saw a $30 base price, then encountered a $3 booking fee and a $2 facility charge. Now the first price is $35 — the same mandatory total an online buyer paid before the change.

That distinction explains the higher opening number: The playhouse has changed when customers see the full mandatory cost, rather than raising that cost. Optional purchases remain separate.

Playhouse manager Leda Voss said the aim is to make the first number closer to what a customer must pay.

“The price has not gone up at checkout. We have stopped making people reach checkout to discover it,” Voss said.

## What does the $35 cover?

The online total includes the $30 base ticket, the $3 booking fee and the $2 facility charge. Those are all the mandatory charges in the playhouse’s price schedule.

The fees have different destinations. The facility charge goes into the playhouse’s building fund, which is paying for roof work. The booking fee pays the contracted ticketing provider and does not pass directly to performers.

Customers can still spend more than $35. Checkout offers an optional $5 donation, but it is not automatically selected. Digital delivery is free. Postal delivery costs an additional $2 per order, not per ticket.

A customer choosing digital delivery and no donation therefore pays $35 for one online ticket. Choosing postal delivery adds a charge to the order without changing the ticket’s mandatory price.

## Is the counter cheaper?

Yes. A counter ticket costs $32: the $30 base price plus the $2 facility charge. Buying there avoids the $3 online booking fee, but it does not eliminate the facility charge.

That saving requires buying during counter hours, Tuesday through Saturday from noon to 5 p.m. Those hours are not convenient for every customer. The playhouse also uses numbered paper tickets for customers without email.

Refunds depend on the reason for cancellation. Under the playhouse’s stated policy, mandatory ticket charges are returned when the playhouse cancels a performance. Ordinary customer cancellations follow separate terms; that policy is not a promise of a refund whenever a buyer’s plans change.

## What does the change tell customers?

It gives online buyers the mandatory total at the start, which addresses the comparison problem described by customer Toma Reed. Reed said comparing shows was difficult when one venue advertised totals and another advertised base prices.

“I cannot compare two nights out if one number is a ticket and the other is only the start of one,” Reed said.

Whether the display change has affected purchasing is not established. As of Aug. 17, the playhouse had not published evidence that it increased sales or reduced abandoned orders.

The Aug. 10-16 sales report records 300 online tickets and 180 counter tickets across multiple performance dates for the production. They generated $16,260 in mandatory ticket receipts, excluding optional donations and postal delivery. Those figures describe sales during that period, but do not establish an effect from the new display.

For buyers, the immediate comparison is $35 online with free digital delivery or $32 at the counter, before optional purchases. Those prices and the stated refund policy describe Fernwick’s practices, not rules that necessarily apply at other venues.
