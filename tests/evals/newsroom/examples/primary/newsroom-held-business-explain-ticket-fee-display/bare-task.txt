# Why Fernwick Playhouse’s online ticket price now starts at $35

An online ticket for Fernwick Playhouse’s autumn production of “The Narrow Orchard” now appears to cost more at first glance. But the change is in when customers see the mandatory charges, not how much they must pay at checkout.

On Aug. 10, the playhouse began displaying $35 instead of a $30 base price. Previously, customers saw the $30 figure before a $3 booking fee and a $2 facility charge were added. The new opening price includes both charges, matching the mandatory online total before the change.

Playhouse manager Leda Voss said the aim is to make the first number closer to what a customer must pay.

“The price has not gone up at checkout. We have stopped making people reach checkout to discover it,” Voss said.

## What does the $35 cover?

The displayed price covers the ticket and all mandatory charges in the playhouse’s price schedule. It does not mean the fees have disappeared. They are included earlier in the shopping process rather than added to the initially advertised amount.

The $2 facility charge goes into the playhouse’s building fund, which is paying for roof work. The $3 booking fee pays the contracted ticketing provider; it does not pass directly to performers.

Optional purchases remain separate. Checkout offers a $5 donation, but it is not automatically selected. Digital delivery is free. Customers who choose postal delivery pay another $2 per order, not per ticket.

A customer buying one online ticket, using digital delivery and declining the donation therefore pays $35. Choosing optional extras can raise the final bill without changing the mandatory ticket total.

## Is there a cheaper way to buy?

Buying at the playhouse counter avoids the $3 online booking fee. The $2 facility charge still applies, making the counter total $32 per ticket.

That option comes with a practical limitation: The counter opens Tuesday through Saturday from noon to 5 p.m., hours that are not convenient for every customer. The lower counter price requires buying there rather than online.

The playhouse also uses numbered paper tickets for customers without email.

## Why does the first price matter?

Customer Toma Reed said comparing shows was difficult when one venue advertised totals while another advertised base prices.

“I cannot compare two nights out if one number is a ticket and the other is only the start of one,” Reed said.

Fernwick’s revised display puts its mandatory online total at the beginning of that comparison. It does not establish that other venues include the same charges or follow the same practices.

Nor is there published evidence yet that the change has increased sales or reduced abandoned orders.

The Aug. 10-16 sales report records 300 online tickets and 180 counter tickets across multiple performance dates for the production. Those sales generated $16,260 in mandatory ticket receipts, excluding donations and postal delivery. The figures document sales during the first week of the display change, but do not establish its effect on buying decisions.

## What happens if a performance is canceled?

The playhouse’s stated policy returns mandatory ticket charges when the playhouse cancels a performance. Ordinary customer cancellations follow separate terms.

Those are Fernwick’s policies, not universal rules for ticket sellers. For this production, the central distinction is straightforward: The online price customers see first has risen by $5, while the mandatory amount they pay has stayed at $35.
