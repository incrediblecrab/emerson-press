# What stays free under Vellum Step’s revised returns policy?

Vellum Step Shoes still offers free returns, but its revised policy makes the route a customer chooses — and the form of repayment — important. Returning an online purchase at one of its six stores remains free. Mailing back an ordinary change-of-mind return means choosing between a $6 deduction from a refund to the original payment method and full eligible value in store credit.

The online policy changed July 1. As of Aug. 3, the central trade-off is straightforward: Store credit avoids the label deduction, but keeps the money with Vellum Step and comes with an expiration date.

## What remains free?

Returns at the retailer’s six stores remain free, including returns of online purchases. A branch can accept an online return even if it does not carry that product in its own inventory.

For ordinary change-of-mind returns by mail, customers who choose store credit receive the item’s full eligible price without the $6 label deduction. That makes the label free under this option, but does not make store credit equivalent to money returned to the original payment method.

The retailer also separately provides prepaid labels for items it accepts as faulty or sent in error. The $6 change-of-mind charge does not apply to those returns. The reporting record does not establish how every disputed fault claim is resolved, so that exception should not be read as a guarantee that every claimed fault will be accepted.

## How does the refund choice change the result?

For an eligible $60 item returned by mail because the customer changed their mind, the merchandise portion of the refund is $54 to the original payment method or $60 in store credit.

Those figures exclude separately handled tax adjustments and assume there was no original delivery charge or discount allocation.

The extra $6 retained through store credit has limits: The credit can be spent only with Vellum Step and expires 12 months after issuance. A refund to the original payment method is smaller, but does not require another purchase from the retailer to use the returned value.

Online sales director Mara Tern says customers taking store credit often combine it with a later purchase.

“A credit keeps the next transaction with us. Cash goes back to whatever the customer needs next,” Tern said.

Neither choice changes the policy’s 40-day return window or its requirement that items be unworn.

## Is a store return a practical alternative?

A free store return avoids the mailed-return deduction, but reaching a branch can be a separate consideration.

Customer Iven Sore ordered two sizes because neither was stocked at his nearest branch. That branch could nevertheless accept his online return.

“Free in a shop is a different offer when the shop is two buses away,” Sore said.

## Why charge, and what has changed so far?

Vellum Step estimates that mailing and handling an ordinary posted return costs it an average of $9.50, excluding any resale loss on the item. That estimate provides context for the $6 charge, but does not establish the cost or profit on an individual return.

The company’s first four-week report shows a lower return rate than in the previous four weeks. However, the product and promotion mix also changed. The company has not isolated the fee’s effect on return behavior or published the value of unredeemed new credits.

For customers, the immediate distinction is clearer than the business results: Store returns remain free, while ordinary mailed returns offer a smaller original-payment refund or full eligible value tied to future Vellum Step spending. These are the retailer’s stated options, not legal advice about all retailers’ return obligations.
