# What stays free under Vellum Step Shoes' revised returns policy?

Vellum Step Shoes still offers free returns at its six stores, but customers mailing back an ordinary change-of-mind purchase now face a choice: pay a $6 label charge for a refund to their original payment method or take store credit without that deduction.

The online returns policy changed July 1. As of Aug. 3, the retailer's 40-day return window and requirement that items be unworn remain unchanged. The main distinction is how customers send an item back and where they want the refund to go.

## What remains free?

Returns at Vellum Step's stores remain free, including returns of online purchases. A branch can accept an online return even if it does not carry that product in its own inventory.

For an ordinary return by mail, choosing store credit avoids the $6 label deduction. The customer receives the item's full eligible price as credit to spend with Vellum Step.

Faulty items and items sent in error are handled separately. The retailer provides prepaid labels for returns it accepts in those categories, and the $6 change-of-mind charge does not apply. The reporting record does not establish how every disputed fault claim is resolved.

## What does the refund choice mean in dollars?

For an eligible $60 item returned by mail, the merchandise portion of the refund is $54 to the original payment method or $60 in store credit.

Those amounts exclude separately handled tax adjustments and assume there was no original delivery charge or discount allocation. They illustrate the label deduction, rather than every possible component of a customer's final refund.

The extra $6 retained through store credit comes with restrictions. Credit can be used only with Vellum Step and expires 12 months after issuance under the stated terms. A refund to the original payment method is smaller, but does not tie that refunded amount to another Vellum Step purchase.

Online sales director Mara Tern said store-credit customers often combine the credit with a later purchase.

"A credit keeps the next transaction with us. Cash goes back to whatever the customer needs next," Tern said.

## Is a store return a practical alternative?

A store return avoids the mailing charge, but getting to a branch may affect whether that option suits a customer.

Customer Iven Sore ordered two sizes because neither was stocked at his nearest branch. Although that branch can accept the online return, he described the travel involved as a drawback.

"Free in a shop is a different offer when the shop is two buses away," Sore said.

Customers deciding between the options therefore have more to weigh than the label charge: access to a store, whether they expect to buy from Vellum Step again and whether they can use credit before it expires.

## Why charge, and has it changed returns?

The company estimates that mailing and handling an ordinary posted return costs an average of $9.50, excluding the item's resale loss. That estimate is above the $6 charge, but it does not establish the cost or profit on any individual return.

The company's first four-week report shows a lower return rate than in the preceding four weeks. The product and promotion mix also differed, however, and the company has not isolated the fee's effect on return behavior. It has not published the value of unredeemed new credits.

For customers, the established trade-off is narrower: a mailed change-of-mind return preserves the full eligible merchandise price as time-limited store credit, while a refund to the original payment method carries a $6 deduction. These are Vellum Step's stated options, not a guide to every retailer's legal return obligations.
