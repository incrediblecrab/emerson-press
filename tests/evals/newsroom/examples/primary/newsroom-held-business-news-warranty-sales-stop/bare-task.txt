Hearthline Appliances to Stop Selling Extended Service Plan, Says Existing Contracts Unaffected

Hearthline Appliances said Tuesday, Aug. 18, 2026, that it will stop selling its Extra Years service plan after Sept. 30, while stressing that the change does not end coverage for customers who already hold one.

The retailer's 14 stores will continue selling appliances, parts and separately priced repair visits, the company said. Written manufacturer warranties that come with appliances are not affected by the decision.

According to Hearthline's account records, roughly 14,000 Extra Years contracts are currently active. The company cautioned that the figure counts contracts, not distinct customers, since some plan holders have coverage on more than one appliance.

Those existing contracts will continue to be administered by Cinder Service Network, the plan's current provider, through each contract's stated expiration date, Hearthline said. No blanket cancellation refund is being offered, and no automatic extension of existing plans is part of the announced change.

Hearthline said its renewed agreement with Cinder raised the one-time administration charge on each newly sold plan from $22 to $31. The company described this as a cost it pays the provider on new sales, not a new charge to holders of existing plans. Hearthline has not disclosed the average retail price of the plan or its profit margin on plan sales, so the fee change does not by itself indicate whether the company's overall profit from the plans will rise or fall.

Chief commercial officer Nessa Flint said sales staff will no longer earn commissions for new service-plan sales once the Sept. 30 cutoff takes effect. Staff will receive a temporary three-month commission supplement, though Hearthline said the amount will vary based on each employee's prior sales volume.

Flint said the end of new sales should not be read as a change in how existing repair requests are handled. "Stopping new sales does not move an existing repair request to the back of a different queue," she said.

Cinder's process for existing claims begins with a telephone assessment before an in-home appointment is scheduled, according to the provider. A Cinder report covering July 2026 found a median of eight calendar days between a completed telephone assessment and the first home visit, among visits completed that month in this service region. Cinder said that figure excludes visits still pending and any later parts orders, and it is not a maximum wait time or a measure of how long a full repair takes to complete.

Dane Voss, a Hearthline customer whose refrigerator plan runs through 2028, said he initially thought Tuesday's announcement meant his coverage had ended. "I wanted to know who would answer the phone, not whether they would sell me another leaflet," Voss said.

Hearthline said it plans to mail active contract holders a notice identifying their service provider and listing their plan's expiration date, a step the company said is intended to address confusion like Voss's.

The reporting record contains no regulator assessment of the value of the service plans and no finding, by a regulator or otherwise, that every claim under the plans has been handled correctly.

Hearthline began selling service plans in 2014, after previously offering appliance repairs only on a per-visit basis.
