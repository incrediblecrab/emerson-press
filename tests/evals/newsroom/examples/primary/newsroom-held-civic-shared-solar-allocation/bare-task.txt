# What a share of Rookmere’s community solar project delivers

Members of the Rookmere Energy Circle receive an accounting share of eligible electricity exported from the solar installation on the community hall — not a particular panel, a private electricity supply or a guarantee that their household bills will disappear.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the circle’s first household group has 20 equal allocations. The grid-connected installation operates under a roof-use agreement, and the supplier applies members’ allocations through its agreed billing method. Here is what that means for participating households.

## What is being shared?

The shared quantity is eligible exported electricity, measured after the hall’s own electricity use is accounted for under the supply arrangement. That is different from the total electricity the panels generate.

The hall’s daytime activities, including a playgroup and community lunches, affect when electricity is used on site before export. Its public display shows generation and export separately rather than treating them as the same quantity.

In the circle’s illustration, 1,000 kilowatt-hours of eligible export in a period would produce an allocation of 50 kilowatt-hours for each of the 20 household shares. That is a simple accounting example, not reported production for an actual month. Weather changes production, and the circle has not promised identical output in every billing period.

## Does a member receive electricity from a particular panel?

No. An allocation does not mean ownership of an individually identified panel or a private wire carrying its output to a member’s home.

“The roof keeps working while most of us are somewhere else. The allocation is how we account for the shared output afterward,” circle engineer Dera Moss said.

Generation and household consumption also occur at different times. A member can use grid electricity at night even if the project’s panels generated electricity during the day. The allocation accounts for shared output; it does not establish a continuous household supply from the roof.

## How does the share appear on a bill?

The supplier applies the allocation under its agreed billing method. That arrangement is separate from a promise to eliminate every line on a household’s electricity bill.

The circle’s packet gives neither a universal bill-saving percentage nor a guaranteed cash return for joining.

“I joined for a share in a local project. I still wanted someone to show me exactly where that share appears on the statement,” member Arin Cole said.

The circle provides a statement showing eligible export, the member’s allocation and the supplier period to which it relates. Meter reconciliation can delay an allocation until a later statement. Such a delay does not, by itself, prove that the panels stopped generating.

## What about outages, costs and repairs?

The current installation has no battery-backed household supply and is not a promised source of electricity during a grid outage.

The circle manages maintenance costs and replacement reserves before proposing additional distributions to members. Responsibility is also divided: The hall remains responsible for the building, while the roof-use agreement assigns the circle defined responsibilities for its equipment.

## Can a share move with a household?

A household moving away must use the circle’s membership-transfer process. Members should not assume their allocation automatically follows a new electricity account.

The circle has not approved additional household shares beyond the first 20. These arrangements describe Rookmere’s agreement, not every shared-solar scheme. For its members, the central benefit is an allocated share of eligible exports applied through the supplier’s billing method, with statements documenting that share — not dedicated panels or guaranteed uninterrupted power.
