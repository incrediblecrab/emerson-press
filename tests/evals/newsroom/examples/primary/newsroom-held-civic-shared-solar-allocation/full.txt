# What Rookmere's shared solar members receive — and what they don't

Members of the Rookmere Energy Circle receive an accounting allocation of eligible electricity exported from the solar installation on their community hall. They do not receive ownership of a particular panel or a private electricity supply running from the roof to their homes.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the circle's first household group has 20 equal allocations. The supplier applies those allocations under an agreed billing method. That arrangement does not promise to eliminate every line on a household electricity bill.

The distinction matters when members read their statements, compare daytime generation with electricity used at home or consider what happens during a power outage. This explanation concerns Rookmere's agreement, not every shared-solar scheme.

## How the electricity is counted

The installation is connected to the grid. Its meter records actual eligible export after the hall's own electricity use is accounted for under the supply arrangement. Daytime activities, including a playgroup and community lunches, affect when electricity is used in the building before export.

Generation and export are therefore different quantities. The public display in the hall shows them separately.

The circle illustrates the allocation with a period in which eligible export totals 1,000 kilowatt-hours. Divided equally among 20 household shares, that would give each share an allocation of 50 kilowatt-hours. This is a simple accounting example, not reported production for a real month.

Weather changes production, and the circle has not promised the same output in every billing period. Equal shares describe how eligible export is divided, not a fixed amount of electricity each household will receive.

"The roof keeps working while most of us are somewhere else. The allocation is how we account for the shared output afterward," circle engineer Dera Moss said.

## What appears on a statement

The circle provides a statement showing eligible export, the member's allocation and the supplier period to which it relates. The supplier then applies the allocation using its agreed billing method.

An allocation measured in kilowatt-hours is not, by itself, a stated cash return. The supplied record gives no universal bill-saving percentage or guaranteed cash return for joining.

Member Arin Cole said, "I joined for a share in a local project. I still wanted someone to show me exactly where that share appears on the statement."

Timing can also affect what members see. A delay in reconciling meter records can put an allocation on a later statement. That delay alone does not prove the panels stopped generating.

## What the arrangement does not supply

The allocation does not mean a household uses the roof's electricity at the moment it is generated. Generation and home consumption occur at different times, so a member can still use grid electricity at night after the panels generated power during the day.

The current installation has no battery-backed household supply. Membership is not a promise of power during a grid outage.

## Costs, responsibilities and moving away

The circle manages maintenance costs and replacement reserves before proposing additional distributions to members. The hall remains responsible for the building, while the roof-use agreement gives the circle defined responsibilities for its equipment.

Members who move away must use the circle's membership-transfer process. They cannot assume their allocation automatically follows a new electricity account.

The circle has not approved household shares beyond the first 20. For those members, the statement connects their equal allocation to the installation's eligible export and the relevant supplier period; it does not establish ownership of a panel or guarantee a particular level of savings.
