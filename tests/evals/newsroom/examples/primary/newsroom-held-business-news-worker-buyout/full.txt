# Ospen Toolworks employees sign purchase agreement; membership optional for workers

Ospen Toolworks' retiring owner, Halen Pike, signed an agreement Monday to sell the shop to an employee cooperative for $1 million, with an Oct. 1 ownership transfer planned.

The transfer remains subject to the lender's final equipment valuation. Eighteen of the shop's 26 employees, a count that excludes Pike, have agreed to become founding members, each contributing $5,000 toward the purchase.

The eight employees who have not joined can keep their current jobs at the same base pay and apply for membership later. Remaining employed will not require buying a share, and becoming a member will not guarantee permanent employment, dividends or a future resale value for that share.

Membership will bring a role in the cooperative's governance. Each member will have one vote, regardless of job title or years of service. The cooperative's five-member board will appoint a general manager, and daily production decisions will not require votes by all members.

For some employees, the distinction between keeping a job and purchasing an ownership stake is a matter of what they can afford. Nonmember machinist Iri Bell said the $5,000 contribution is beyond her current savings, although she wants the shop to remain open.

"I can be glad the shop survives without being able to buy a piece of it this month," Bell said.

The cooperative is considering installment payments for employees who join later, but it has not adopted a policy. The agreed contribution for each founding member remains $5,000.

Those contributions will provide $90,000 in member capital. A $510,000 community-lender loan and $400,000 in seller financing will cover the rest of the $1 million purchase price.

A separate $250,000 working-capital facility has been approved. It is not part of the purchase price, and the available record does not state that any of it has been drawn. The lender requires quarterly financial reports and has not promised to cover future operating losses.

The shop makes small batches of replacement parts for food-processing equipment and water pumps. Its largest customer accounted for 28% of last year's revenue and has not committed to a new long-term contract.

Founding member Toma Wren said the purchase leaves employees responsible for securing future business.

"We are buying the responsibility for the next order, not a guarantee that it will come," Wren said.

The agreement retains Pike for six months as a paid adviser on customer relationships and quoting work. That gives him a continuing role during the transition, while the cooperative's board will be responsible for appointing the general manager.

Before signing the employee purchase agreement, Pike had explored a sale to a competitor that wanted the customer list. The competitor had made no written offer for the plant.

Ospen Toolworks opened in 1987. Its equipment includes computer-controlled lathes and an older manual milling machine. The lender's final equipment valuation remains a condition of the planned Oct. 1 transfer.
