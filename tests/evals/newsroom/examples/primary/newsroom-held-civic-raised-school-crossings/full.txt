Terncross accepts $410,000 crossing plan, but guard vacancy leaves gap

Terncross Council has accepted a $310,000 county grant toward three raised pedestrian crossings near primary schools, adding $100,000 in city funds to bring the approved project budget to $410,000, city officials said.

The crossings will be built outside Willow Gate School, at the west approach to Bell Lane School and between Ash Court School and its playing field. The transport team chose the sites because they connect existing walking routes, not because they drew the most complaints, officials said.

Each design raises the crossing surface to the level of the adjoining footway and includes revised drainage. Construction is planned for the February 2027 school break, though the city has not yet selected a contractor or finalized the work sequence. Officials said they expect short lane restrictions during construction and will publish detailed arrangements once a contractor's plan is approved. The existing marked crossings will stay in place until each replacement is ready for use.

The grant covers construction and design only. It does not pay for crossing-guard wages. Two of the three sites currently have staffed crossing guards, and the approved project does not eliminate either position. A guard vacancy at the third site remains unfilled; the city said recruitment for that post is continuing separately from the construction procurement.

Transport engineer Sela Dorn said the two issues are distinct. "The new surface and the person helping children across are different parts of the route," Dorn said.

A parents group welcomed the plan to connect the walking route but pressed the city to fill the vacant guard position before the new crossings open. Parent representative Evin Rell said a construction timeline does not resolve the immediate question of staffing. "A construction date is not an answer to who will be standing there next Monday," Rell said.

The project packet contains no completed safety evaluation and no prediction of a specific reduction in collisions. The grant application includes written accounts of difficult crossings at the three sites, city officials said, not a verified count of crashes or near misses.

Bus operators have asked that the raised surfaces be reviewed with their vehicles in mind as the detailed design work proceeds.

The county has required a post-opening assessment of access and drainage at each site before it releases the final portion of its grant. That assessment will take place after construction is finished and is not, officials said, evidence that the crossings already meet every intended outcome.

Schools will distribute the city's work notices through printed newsletters and their existing electronic mailing lists, the city said.

The $410,000 covers only the three locations named. Other crossings requested by residents remain part of the city's wider transport program, officials said.

Willow Gate School's older wooden crossing signs, which will come down as part of the work, have been offered to the school's drama department, according to the city.
