# At Relay Yard, salvaged doors wait on records and room

Before a builder collected a reclaimed door from Relay Yard, employee Neri Havell matched its tag to its dimensions and removal record. The check, recorded in a July yard note, was part of the work between taking a door out of one building and sending it toward another.

Relay Yard buys or receives materials from authorized building strip-outs and sells reusable stock to builders and household customers. It occupies a former seed warehouse, with separate aisles for measured items and stock still awaiting inspection.

The business depends on more than finding something worth keeping. Dimensions and origins must be recorded, suitability considered for the proposed use and storage found while a buyer's construction schedule catches up. As of Sept. 8, another offered batch of doors remains unaccepted because covered storage is nearly full.

The latest intake ledger shows how accepted stock moves through the yard: Of 160 doors, 90 have sold, 30 are reserved and 40 remain available. The reservations are not completed sales. A canceled order can put a door back among the available stock.

Relay Yard's standard reservation lasts 14 days. After that, storage arrangements must be agreed separately. That gives a buyer a defined period to hold an item, but a reservation does not settle where it will wait if the project is not ready.

Owner Tavin Lorne said arriving materials and outgoing construction schedules rarely match exactly.

"The bargain has to wait somewhere between the building coming down and the next one being ready," Lorne said.

A common interior door is listed at $40, compared with one local supplier's $140 quote for a new door of similar size. Those prices offer a starting comparison, not a universal installed saving. Condition, hardware, refinishing and fitting costs differ.

Nor does a recorded history establish that a salvaged product is suitable for every purpose. Relay Yard records known dimensions and origins but does not certify every product for structural or fire-rated use. Suitability is assessed for the proposed use rather than inferred solely from age or appearance.

Architect Cera Mossen said specified performance records can be necessary before her clients can use an item for a particular purpose.

"A beam can look sound in the yard and still need evidence before it belongs in the design," Mossen said.

Unidentified coatings and missing test records can limit where materials are offered. The supplied reporting notes include no chemical test results for those items, leaving their composition unresolved.

The recordkeeping also takes paid time. Relay Yard employs four people, including one who spends most working hours measuring, photographing and listing stock. Even donated material carries storage and handling costs despite having no purchase price.

The business has not released a net-profit figure or a verified carbon-saving total for its stock. Neither measure can be established from the number of doors accepted or their listed prices.

For now, space limits what can enter the warehouse. A demolition contractor has offered another batch of doors, but Relay Yard has not accepted it. Lorne has no signed agreement to expand into the neighboring building. Inside the existing warehouse, the aisles still divide the measured items from those awaiting inspection.
