**Small Publisher Redesigns Board Game Tokens So Players Can Tell Them Apart More Than One Way**

By [Staff Writer]

Five Lantern Games, a small board-game publisher run by three co-founders, is testing a revised set of components for its 2023 title "Bridge of Five Markets" that would let players distinguish the four in-game groups by shape and symbol as well as by color, according to reporting notes current as of Sept. 8, 2026.

The original edition relied mainly on token color to separate the four player groups. The prototype under review adds a distinct outline shape and a printed symbol to each color, giving players more than one way to identify which pieces belong to which group. The core rules and scoring remain unchanged, according to the notes.

The idea traces to an August 2026 prototype session in which designer Nera Selven compared tokens with different outlines during an internal trial of the original game. That led to a broader test with 12 players, some of whom said they had found the old color-only scheme difficult to distinguish and others who reported no such difficulty. The session produced comments about how easily players could recognize and handle the pieces; the notes describing it do not characterize the trial as a scientific measure of accessibility or of any particular learning difference.

The redesign process has already produced at least one revision. Some testers found one of the new token shapes awkward to pick up, and the design team altered its shape in response. "Making pieces easier to tell apart is not enough if one of them is awkward to move," Selven said.

Five Lantern Games does not manufacture its own components; like many small publishers, it contracts the work out. A manufacturer has quoted $2,300 for new cutting dies plus $2 per box, but that price assumes a production volume the publisher has not disclosed publicly. The company has not signed a production order, and no final retail price has been set.

The publisher has collected 180 refundable preorder deposits of $10 each, totaling $1,800 — a figure the notes distinguish from 180 completed full-price sales. Those deposits remain refundable until Five Lantern Games confirms a final component specification and a delivery schedule.

Retailer Omi Valgren, who sells the game, said any revised edition needs to be clearly labeled so shoppers know which version of the components they are buying before they open the box. "The useful change has to be something a customer can identify before opening the box," Valgren said.

The company is also weighing a separate, component-only pack aimed at people who already own the 2023 edition, though it has not set a price for that pack or promised it would work with every prior printing of the game. Existing copies of the game are not being recalled, and current owners can keep playing under the original rules if they choose.

Much of the early design work took place informally: Five Lantern Games' office shares space with a community arts print room, where the first paper prototypes of the new tokens were cut by hand before any manufacturer was contacted.

The founders have not yet released a cost-recovery forecast for the redesign, and the notes contain no evidence so far that the changes have increased retail sales. Whether the new components reach production depends on manufacturing costs, order volume and a specification the company has yet to finalize.
