Headline: Five Lantern Games Redesigns Tokens for Bridge of Five Markets, Testing Shape and Symbol Alongside Color

MEDFORD, Ore. — Five Lantern Games, a small publisher run by three co-founders that contracts out its manufacturing rather than operating its own factory, is testing a revised set of components for its board game Bridge of Five Markets, adding printed symbols and distinct outlines to tokens that had previously been distinguished mainly by color.

The 2023 edition of the game marks its four player groups largely through token color. In an August 2026 prototype note, designer Nera Selven recorded comparing tokens with different outlines during a trial of the original game. The proposed edition would give each color group its own outline shape and a printed symbol, so a player could identify a token’s group in more than one way. The game’s rules and scoring remain unchanged in the current prototype, according to the company.

The change followed a 12-person trial that included players who said the old colors were difficult for them to distinguish, along with others who reported no such difficulty. The company said the trial gathered comments about recognition and handling of the pieces rather than a population-wide measure of accessibility or learning ability.

That trial also produced an unplanned finding: some testers said one of the new tokens was harder to pick up than the others, prompting a further revision to its shape.

"Making pieces easier to tell apart is not enough if one of them is awkward to move," Selven said.

The company’s office shares space with a community arts print room, where early paper prototypes of the redesigned tokens were cut by hand before the current manufacturing quote was sought.

That quote, from the publisher’s contract manufacturer, is $2,300 for new cutting dies plus $2 per box, a figure the manufacturer said assumes a production quantity the publisher has not disclosed. Five Lantern Games has not signed a production order or fixed a final retail price for the revised edition.

The publisher has collected 180 refundable preorder deposits of $10 each, totaling $1,800. Those deposits are not completed full-price sales, and the company said they remain refundable until it confirms a final component specification and delivery schedule.

Retailer Omi Valgren, who has carried the original edition, said the packaging for any revised edition needs to make clear to shoppers which version they are getting.

"The useful change has to be something a customer can identify before opening the box," Valgren said.

Five Lantern Games said it is considering a component-only pack that would let existing owners obtain the new tokens without buying a full new copy, but it has not set a price for that pack or promised it would be compatible with every earlier printing of the game. Existing copies of Bridge of Five Markets are not being recalled, and owners can continue playing under the original rules and components.

The company has not released a completed cost-recovery forecast for the redesign or evidence that it has increased retail sales. Nothing in the company’s account of the redesign describes it as treating a medical condition or as suitable for every access need; the stated aim, according to Selven’s notes, is giving players more than one way to tell the pieces apart.

The publisher has not set a date for finalizing the edition or beginning full production.
