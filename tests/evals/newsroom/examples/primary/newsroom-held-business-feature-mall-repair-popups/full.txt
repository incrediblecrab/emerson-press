# Repair shops try a short stay in Ambercourt mall's former department store

Where luggage once went on display, upholsterer Rena Selt showed a customer two replacement chair fabrics. The August scene at Ambercourt mall put a repair decision inside a space built for selling new goods.

Some display rails and old fitting-room mirrors remain from the department store, which closed in 2024. Part of the vacant space has been divided into eight temporary workshop-retail units. Five were occupied at the end of July, with three still available.

The arrangement gives repair businesses a place to meet customers while the landlord looks for a long-term tenant for the former anchor space. But the agreements renew in 28-day periods for up to six months, and the landlord can end the arrangement with 14 days' notice. The workshops have no promise of a permanent home.

For Selt, that means deciding both what work belongs in the mall and how much to leave there.

"A shop window lets people bring a question in. A four-week lease makes me careful about what I leave behind," she said.

Selt uses the unit for customer meetings, fabric cutting and small repairs. Dusty stripping work stays at a separate workspace. The mall's temporary-use rules exclude large saws and spray finishing, so the unit cannot accommodate every stage of a repair.

That split also matters to the cost of doing business. A unit costs $150 a week in rent and $35 for shared utilities, or $185 before materials, labor and a tenant's other expenses. For Selt, those expenses include maintaining the separate workspace.

She completed eight paid repair jobs in her first month and made three additional quick adjustments without charging. Those counts show work coming through the business, but they do not establish whether she made a profit after expenses. No tenant profit survey has been announced.

Customer Oren Bell said seeing fabric samples in person helped him decide whether to repair chairs rather than replace them. His question about a later repair, though, reached beyond the current lease: How would he contact Selt if the mall unit closed?

Selt's written work orders list her business contact details separately from the temporary unit address. That gives customers a way to reach the business without relying on the workshop remaining in the mall.

For the landlord, the arrangement generates some rent and occupied frontage while negotiations with prospective anchor tenants continue, mall manager Ceri Marel said.

"We can offer a beginning here without pretending we have already found a permanent ending for the building," Marel said.

The mall counted 320 visits to the workshop area during one observed week. It has no comparable count for the former department store, and visits are not repair purchases. The figure does not establish that the mall as a whole has recovered commercially.

As of Sept. 8, no permanent anchor lease or mall-wide employment recovery had been announced. The repair units remain a temporary use of part of the vacancy, with the landlord still seeking a longer-term occupant.

For now, customers can bring repair questions into the former luggage display area. On Selt's work orders, the business contact details remain separate from the address.
