# Campus bus-pass deal opens local routes to students, leaves schedules unchanged

Sable Crossing Transit signed agreements Thursday that will give students at Nerith College and West Sable Technical Institute unlimited local bus travel beginning Sept. 14, without a new student fee.

Enrolled students at either institution will be able to activate a pass for the system's 11 local routes. The agreements do not cover the separately operated regional express service or travel by faculty and other employees.

The deal changes how students pay for local bus travel, but it does not immediately add buses or extend operating hours. Other riders will continue to pay the existing fares, including $48 for a standard monthly adult pass.

Nerith will pay the transit system $180,000 for the academic year, and West Sable will pay $120,000. Both institutions will use their existing transport budgets rather than add a student fee for the 2026-27 academic year.

The institutions have a combined enrollment of approximately 6,000, but students must separately request activation of their passes. That enrollment figure is not a count of expected riders: The agreements do not establish how many students will activate passes or how often they will travel.

Students can activate a physical card at campus service desks without owning a smartphone. Those already receiving a separate travel allowance should ask their campus office how the pass will affect that allowance. The agreements do not set a uniform rule for those students.

Both campuses are already on existing routes. At West Sable, however, the last weekday bus leaves campus at 9:10 p.m., 20 minutes before some workshops finish at 9:30 p.m. The institute has requested a later departure, but the transit board has not funded it.

Student council member Sora Penn, who works at a supermarket between morning and evening classes, described the difference between help with fares and a schedule that accommodates classes.

"The pass removes a bill. It does not make the last bus wait for my class," Penn said.

The immediate benefit is therefore access to unlimited rides on the current local network, with its existing schedules. For students whose classes end after the final departure, the pass agreement leaves that timing problem unresolved.

Transit director Daven Reed said ridership will be counted by boardings, with no academic records shared with the transit system. The institutions and transit system will review route-level boarding totals in January 2027.

"We need to see which trips fill up before we decide where another bus belongs," Reed said.

That review will give the institutions and transit system a record of boardings by route, rather than treating every enrolled student as a rider. It is not a commitment to add service; the agreements provide no immediate increase in buses or operating hours.

The contracts run through Aug. 31, 2027. Funding for renewal has not been approved.
