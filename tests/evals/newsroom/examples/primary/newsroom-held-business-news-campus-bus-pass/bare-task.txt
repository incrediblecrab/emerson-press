# Sable Crossing signs campus bus-pass deals; service hours stay unchanged

SABLE CROSSING — Students at Nerith College and West Sable Technical Institute will be able to activate passes for unlimited local bus travel beginning Sept. 14 under agreements signed Thursday, but the deals do not immediately add buses or extend service hours.

Sable Crossing Transit signed the agreements with both institutions Aug. 13. The campuses will pay a combined $300,000 for the academic year from existing transportation budgets, rather than add a student fee for the 2026-27 academic year.

The program covers enrolled students who separately request activation. Combined enrollment at the two institutions is approximately 6,000, but that figure does not establish how many students will use the passes or how often they will ride.

For students, the agreements remove the need to buy a separate pass for covered local trips once their campus pass is activated. For other riders, the standard monthly adult pass will remain $48. The agreements do not cover faculty or other employees, and they exclude the separately operated regional express service.

Students will not need a smartphone to participate. They can activate a physical card at campus service desks. Those already receiving a separate travel allowance should ask their campus office how the new pass affects that allowance: The two agreements do not establish a uniform rule.

Nerith will pay the transit system $180,000, and West Sable will contribute $120,000. Those institutional payments provide access to the local network, but they do not resolve an existing scheduling gap at West Sable.

The last weekday bus serving that campus leaves at 9:10 p.m., 20 minutes before some workshops finish at 9:30 p.m. West Sable has requested a later departure, but the transit board has not funded it.

Student council member Sora Penn, who works at a supermarket between morning and evening classes, drew a distinction between the pass’s financial benefit and the limits of the bus schedule.

“The pass removes a bill. It does not make the last bus wait for my class,” Penn said.

Both campuses already sit on existing routes. Sable Crossing Transit operates 11 local routes and has used the same central transfer square since 2001. The new agreements change students’ access to passes, rather than immediately changing the network or its operating hours.

Transit director Daven Reed said the system will count ridership by boardings, with no academic records shared with the transit system. The institutions and transit system will review route-level boarding totals in January 2027.

“We need to see which trips fill up before we decide where another bus belongs,” Reed said.

That review will provide route-level information after the passes become available, but the agreements themselves do not establish future student use or commit the system to additional service. For now, students planning trips will still need to account for existing departure times, including West Sable’s last weekday bus.

The contracts run through Aug. 31, 2027. Funding for renewal has not been approved.
