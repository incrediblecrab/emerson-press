# Dunveil Museum drops admission increase, turns to gift and savings

Dunveil Museum's trustees withdrew a planned admission increase Thursday, keeping the standard ticket at $8 while turning to a new gift and spending cuts to narrow a projected operating gap.

Admission for visitors age 16 and older had been scheduled to rise to $11 on Oct. 1. The increase, announced July 23, never took effect. Younger visitors will continue to enter free.

A newly confirmed $70,000 unrestricted operating gift and $30,000 in expected savings will cover most of the museum's projected $120,000 gap for the coming financial year. That leaves $20,000 for which the museum has not identified a complete funding plan.

The savings will come from closing the temporary-exhibition gallery on Wednesdays and reducing outside catalog printing. The Wednesday closure begins Oct. 7 and will be reviewed after six months. The permanent collection, reading room and cafe will remain open that day.

Director Elna Reed said the savings plan would not eliminate permanent posts, although casual gallery shifts would be reduced. The museum has not yet published the number of casual hours affected.

"The gift lets us keep the entry price where it is. It does not remove every difficult choice in the budget," Reed said.

The $120,000 operating gap was projected at the unchanged $8 admission price, before the gift and savings. The revised plan therefore leaves a smaller shortfall at the existing ticket price, rather than relying on the proposed increase to balance the budget. The $30,000 in savings remains an expectation, and the remaining $20,000 is still a projected gap.

The donor requested anonymity. The supplied gift agreement imposes no exhibition-theme requirement, and the money is unrestricted operating support.

For some visitors, the reversal on price comes with a change in what they can see. Local tour organizer Pella Sorn said some prebooked Wednesday groups had expected access to a temporary textile exhibition.

"An unchanged ticket price is useful news, but my visitors also need to know which doors will be open," Sorn said.

The museum will offer those groups an alternative date or a refund of their group-booking payment. The temporary gallery's closure will thus affect bookings even though the rest of the museum's Wednesday opening arrangements remain in place.

The museum counted 42,600 visits last year, but that total includes free school visits and repeat admissions. The record does not specify how many visits were paid at the standard rate. Multiplying the full attendance count by the proposed $3 increase would not establish how much revenue the museum is forgoing by withdrawing it.

Dunveil Museum, which occupies a former customs building, has charged the same adult admission price since 2021. The trustees have not committed to keeping admission at $8 beyond the next financial year.
