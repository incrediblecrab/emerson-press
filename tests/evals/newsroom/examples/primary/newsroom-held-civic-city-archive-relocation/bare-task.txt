# Larkhaven archive to close Nov. 2 for move to former school annex

LARKHAVEN — The public archive will close its reading room Nov. 2 as the Larkhaven records office begins moving about 3,700 cataloged boxes from the old market basement to a former school annex, with reopening scheduled for Jan. 11.

The move follows recurring water entry into a basement corridor, although the office has not reported water damage to stored documents. The annex will provide storage on one level and a reading room with step-free access, replacing the basement’s stair-only public entrance.

The archive holds planning files, council minutes, property photographs and donated community records, not just material used in historical exhibitions. While its online catalogue will remain searchable throughout the closure, most documents listed there are not available as digital images.

“A box being on a truck is a real interruption, even if its catalogue entry is still online,” records manager Pella Norn said.

Staff will continue answering research inquiries during the relocation, but physical retrieval will pause while each numbered batch of boxes is being moved. Movers will transfer the boxes in batches so staff can account for each one before shelving it.

The records office has asked regular researchers to identify essential files before Oct. 16 so staff can discuss access during the move. That does not guarantee advance access to every requested file, particularly oversized plans and material awaiting conservation work.

Requests tied to an existing city administrative deadline will be referred to the relevant city department rather than automatically delayed until the reading room reopens.

A temporary scanning station will allow staff to provide selected requested pages when handling restrictions and reproduction permissions permit. It will not make the full collection available online. The $186,000 relocation budget covers transport, new shelving and fitting out the reading room, not digitizing the entire collection.

For researchers, the distinction between a searchable catalogue and access to the records themselves can be important.

“A catalogue tells us what might be there; sometimes the answer is a pencil mark that was never entered in it,” local-history group coordinator Brin Sayer said.

The records office plans to publish weekly relocation updates identifying which collection sections are temporarily unavailable. The Jan. 11 reopening date remains dependent on completing shelving inspections and reconciling the box inventory.

Access at the new site will change in several ways. In addition to the step-free reading room entrance, the annex is closer to two neighborhood bus routes, according to the city’s access plan. It is, however, farther from the central bus interchange.

The city will retain appointment booking and staff assistance in the new reading room. The relocation will not give visitors open access to the storage shelves.

No records are scheduled for disposal as part of the move. Ordinary decisions about how long records should be retained will remain a separate process.

The future use of the old basement has not been decided. The space is not included in the current market-renovation contract.
