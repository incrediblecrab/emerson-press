# Larkhaven archive to close Nov. 2 for move to former school annex

Larkhaven's public archive will close its reading room Nov. 2 for a move from the old market basement to a former school annex, with reopening scheduled for Jan. 11.

The records office will transfer approximately 3,700 cataloged boxes to the annex, which offers storage on one level and a reading room with step-free access. The move follows recurring water entry into a basement corridor, though the office has not reported water damage to stored documents.

Staff will continue answering research inquiries during the closure, but physical retrieval will pause while each numbered batch of boxes is being moved. The Jan. 11, 2027, reopening date depends on completing shelving inspections and reconciling the box inventory.

The archive has asked regular researchers to identify essential files before Oct. 16 so staff can discuss access during the move. It has not promised advance access to every requested file, particularly oversized plans and material awaiting conservation work.

Requests tied to an existing city administrative deadline will be referred to the relevant city department rather than automatically delayed with the reading room.

The collection includes planning files, council minutes, property photographs and donated community records. Its online catalogue will remain searchable throughout the closure, but most listed documents are not available as digital images.

“A box being on a truck is a real interruption, even if its catalogue entry is still online,” records manager Pella Norn said.

A temporary scanning station will allow staff to provide selected requested pages when handling restrictions and reproduction permissions allow. That service will not make the full collection available online: The $186,000 relocation budget covers transport, new shelving and fitting out the reading room, not digitizing the entire collection.

Local-history group coordinator Brin Sayer said catalogue descriptions cannot always supply the detail researchers need.

“A catalogue tells us what might be there; sometimes the answer is a pencil mark that was never entered in it,” Sayer said.

The records office plans to publish weekly relocation updates listing which collection sections are temporarily unavailable. Movers will transfer the boxes in numbered batches so staff can account for each batch before shelving it. No records are scheduled for disposal as part of the move; ordinary retention decisions remain a separate process.

The annex will replace the basement's stair-only public entrance with step-free access to the reading room. Travel arrangements will differ, however. According to the city's access plan, the annex is farther from the central bus interchange but closer to two neighborhood routes.

The city will retain appointment booking and staff assistance in the new reading room. Researchers will not have open access to the storage shelves.

The old basement's future use has not been decided. It is not included in the current market-renovation contract.
