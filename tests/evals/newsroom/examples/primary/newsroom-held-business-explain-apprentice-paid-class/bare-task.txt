# Corven apprentices earn pay for class time, but still face out-of-pocket costs

The Corven Trades Partnership pays maintenance apprentices for scheduled college classes as well as workplace hours and covers their annual tuition. But participants still need to budget for commuting, ordinary meals and the upfront cost of a required tool kit.

The partnership enrolled its first 24 apprentices in August under agreements with eight local employers. As of Aug. 31, 2026, it offers a two-year apprenticeship, with a potential pay increase tied to demonstrated progress rather than time served alone.

Here is what the current agreement covers — and what it does not.

## What does a paid training week include?

A normal full week consists of four eight-hour workplace days and one eight-hour college day. Both workplace and scheduled classroom hours are paid at an initial rate of $16 an hour.

That produces $640 in gross pay for a full 40-hour week: $512 for workplace hours and $128 for the college day. The total is before individual taxes or other deductions. Those deductions have not been supplied, so the gross figure should not be treated as take-home pay.

Paid classroom time was essential for apprentice Sora Reed, who previously worked retail shifts. She said losing a day’s wages to attend college would have prevented her from joining.

“I can plan around a paid class day. I could not replace that day’s wages with a certificate,” Reed said.

The arrangement is not an unlimited entitlement to paid study. Attendance requirements and arrangements for missed class days are set out in each apprentice’s supplied program agreement.

## Who pays tuition?

Annual tuition is $3,600 per apprentice. The partnership grant contributes $2,400, and the employer pays the remaining $1,200. Apprentices are not billed for that tuition under the current agreement.

Training takes place in a renovated college workshop that also serves existing evening courses. The first cohort includes adults changing careers and recent school leavers, with previous jobs in retail, warehousing and office administration.

Employers also provide supervision at work. Employer representative Deni Holt said apprentices are productive workers but need time from experienced staff.

“Paying the learner is one cost. Making time for the person teaching them is another,” Holt said.

## What must apprentices pay themselves?

Commuting and ordinary meals are not covered. Apprentices need to account for those expenses alongside deductions from their wages. The supplied figures do not establish how much any individual will spend on travel or food.

The required personal tool kit costs $240. Reimbursement is offered after six months of continued participation, but that money is not available in advance of the purchase.

The distinction matters for an initial budget: Apprentices must have a way to pay for the kit upfront rather than count on reimbursement cash being available when they buy it.

## Is higher pay or a continuing job guaranteed?

A rise to $18 an hour requires both completion of specified assessments and employer confirmation of listed workplace competencies. Eighteen months is the planned review point, not an automatic raise based solely on reaching that date.

The apprenticeship runs for two years. Employment afterward depends on the individual employer’s plans and agreement, so participants should not treat a post-program job as guaranteed.

The grant covers the enrolled cohort but does not yet fund another intake. And because these are the partnership’s first apprentices, there are no completion or post-program earnings outcomes yet to show what participants can expect after training.
