Lenwick to Merge Ceramics, Furniture Programs Into New Materials-Design Department

Lenwick University announced Friday that its ceramics and furniture-design programs will be combined into a new materials-design department, a move officials say will streamline coursework but that will also cut annual admissions and reduce evening workshop hours for students who rely on the studios outside daytime classes.

The administrative merger takes effect in January 2027, but the first students will not be admitted under a single combined degree until autumn 2027, the university said. Current enrollment across the two programs totals 120 students — 74 in ceramics and 46 in furniture design.

Students now enrolled in either program will be allowed to finish their existing degrees under the university's published completion plan, and their tuition will not change because of the merger, the university said. Tuition for the incoming autumn 2027 class has not yet been set.

Annual admission to the two programs has totaled 40 new students combined. Under the new structure, the combined degree will admit 32 — a reduction of eight seats a year.

The university said it will retain all nine full-time instructors in the merged department but will not renew three part-time teaching contracts after the 2026-27 academic year. It has not said how many individuals hold those contracts.

Provost Iona Brill said she projects the merger will save about $310,000 annually once the new curriculum is fully running, a figure that accounts for reduced administrative costs and the part-time teaching reductions. That estimate does not include a separate one-time workshop refit, budgeted at $180,000 from an existing capital allocation. Final refit costs have not yet been determined.

Under the new department, students will share drawing, materials-history and business-practice courses, while ceramics and furniture design will retain separate kiln and machine workshops, the university said.

One immediate change for current students: evening workshop access will end at 9 p.m. rather than 11 p.m., part of a broader reduction in staffed access hours.

Milo Fenn, a third-year ceramics student who works afternoons at a grocery store, said he currently relies on the workshop after 9 p.m. to make up studio time.

"Finishing my degree is not just a promise about the name on the certificate," Fenn said. "I need time at the kiln."

Brill said the department will solicit student scheduling requests before it publishes a January workshop timetable, though she did not say whether that process could restore later access for students in situations like Fenn's.

Local furniture maker Tavi Reed said a shared materials curriculum could benefit graduates but cautioned that employers still expect proficiency on specific equipment.

"A broader course helps if there is still enough time to get good at a difficult tool," Reed said.

The university has not released separate employment-outcome data for the existing ceramics and furniture-design programs, making it difficult to assess how graduates of either track have fared in hiring to date.

The ceramics studio, housed in a former railway goods office, includes a student-run shop open one afternoon a week. The university's published plan retains that shop, which operates as a supervised sales placement rather than a separate degree requirement.

Further details on the January 2027 workshop schedule and autumn 2027 tuition are expected before the end of the year, the university said.
