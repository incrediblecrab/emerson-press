**Ashcombe's new water tariff will raise some business bills and lower others — the dividing line is 30,000 gallons a month**

ASHCOMBE — Starting Oct. 1, commercial customers here will pay water bills built on a different formula, and the Ashcombe Water Board says the change will not move every bill the same direction.

The board approved the new commercial tariff in its August budget decision. It raises the monthly fixed charge on commercial accounts from $35 to $50 and lowers the usage charge from $3 to $2.50 per 1,000 gallons. Residential accounts are not affected and keep their current rates.

The two changes work against each other, and which one wins depends on how much water an account uses.

At 30,000 gallons a month, the math is a wash: both the old and new formulas produce a $125 water-only bill. Below that usage level, the higher fixed charge outweighs the lower usage rate, and bills rise. Above it, the lower usage rate outweighs the higher fixed charge, and bills fall. The board's budget documents do not project the same percentage change for every commercial customer.

Two examples from the board's rate tables illustrate the split. An account using 20,000 gallons a month would see its water-only bill rise from $95 to $100. An account using 200,000 gallons a month would see its water-only bill fall from $635 to $550. Both figures hold usage constant and exclude sewer charges, which are billed separately and are not changing under this decision.

Deni Rook, owner of Mallow Street Bakery, said the shop uses about 20,000 gallons a month and cannot lower its fixed charge by cutting water use. "The first fifty dollars arrives before we turn on a tap," Rook said. She said she still plans to keep repairing leaks, since the usage portion of the bill remains and adds up on top of the fixed charge.

Vale Washworks, a linen processor that uses about 200,000 gallons in a typical month, would fall on the other side of the 30,000-gallon line under steady use. But the business has substantial seasonal swings in demand, and its manager cautioned that a lower tariff at constant use does not guarantee a lower bill in a busier month, when total gallons used would be higher.

Tera Linn, the water board's finance officer, said the redesign reflects a shift in how the system recovers its costs. "We changed the balance between keeping the system ready and charging for each gallon," Linn said. The board says the larger fixed charge is meant to collect more revenue for pipes and treatment capacity that must be maintained regardless of how much water customers use in a given month.

A few other details apply across the change. Businesses with more than one meter are billed a fixed charge on each account, under both the old and new schedules. The board is offering customers account-specific estimates based on the previous year's monthly meter readings, though it says those estimates are not a guarantee of future bills. The tariff decision does not include a new mandatory meter installation fee and does not change water quality or treatment standards. Ashcombe's oldest pumping station remains in service as a backup during maintenance, unrelated to the rate change.

The new rates take effect with October billing.
