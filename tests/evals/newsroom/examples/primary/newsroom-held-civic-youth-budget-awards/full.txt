# Greywillow approves $40,000 for youth panel's three projects

Greywillow Council approved $40,000 on Sept. 14 for skate-area seating, shared rehearsal equipment and a community garden water connection, ratifying recommendations from its first youth-budget panel.

The package provides $18,000 for the seating, $12,000 for rehearsal equipment and $10,000 for the water connection. The 18-member panel recommended the combined package by a 15-3 vote after considering 11 eligible proposals.

The council retained responsibility for authorizing spending and checking whether projects could be delivered on city-controlled sites. The city has not announced installation dates for all three projects or said that any is already operating. The garden work still requires a final utility estimate before it can be ordered.

The awards have limits on what they will provide. Seating at the skate area will include spaces for people watching rather than taking part, but the grant will not rebuild the skating surface.

The rehearsal equipment will be stored and booked through the youth center rather than transferred permanently to one band. The center has agreed to manage bookings and routine equipment checks within its existing program.

For the garden, an estimate above the $10,000 allocation would require the proposal to return for a funding decision. It would not automatically draw money from the other two awards.

"We had to decide what could be shared, not just what each of us wanted for our own group," the panel's youth chair said in a supplied statement.

Staff screened submissions against the program's budget and location rules before the panel considered the 11 eligible proposals. Some panel members wanted a transport project, but a transport submission was excluded because its funding request exceeded the entire $40,000 budget.

Staff will publish the reasons for eligibility decisions so rejected applicants can distinguish exclusions based on program rules from choices reflecting the panel's preferences. The transport submission, for example, was screened out before the panel chose among eligible projects.

Youth-services director Amra Vale said the recommendations leave purchasing and installation work for the city.

"The panel made real choices, and the city still has to carry out the unglamorous work of buying and installing things," Vale said.

The grant is capital funding and does not pay for a permanent new youth-worker position. Panel members will be invited to review procurement updates and inspect completed projects with city staff.

Members were recruited through schools and youth organizations, not elected in a citywide vote. The city covered their travel costs and did not require them to donate money to any selected project. Meetings rotated between the youth center and a library meeting room to avoid making one school the permanent host.

The council will decide whether to repeat the program after reviewing project delivery and who took part in the first panel. The funding decision is not accompanied by a citywide measure of young residents' preferences or evidence of long-term participation outcomes.
