**South Nacre schools weigh laptop bids: cheapest sticker price isn't the whole story**

By [Staff Writer]

SOUTH NACRE — When South Nacre School District's board meets in September, it will choose between two bids to replace 300 aging student laptops — but district officials say the lowest number on the page doesn't settle the question.

The purchase bid comes in at $450 per laptop, or $135,000 for all 300 devices, paid at delivery. The lease bid costs $54,000 a year for three years — $162,000 total before any optional charges. On paper, buying looks like the cheaper route by $27,000.

But that comparison leaves out maintenance. The lease includes repairs for ordinary hardware faults and battery replacements once a battery's tested capacity drops below a set threshold, though it does not cover accidental damage or lost devices. District technicians estimate that maintaining purchased laptops would cost about $12,000 a year, or $36,000 over three years — though officials caution that figure is an internal estimate, not a fixed vendor price. Add it to the purchase bid, and the three-year cost climbs to $171,000, about $9,000 more than leasing.

Ownership also cuts the other way at the end of the term. Laptops bought outright stay district property indefinitely. Leased laptops must be returned unless the district pays an end-of-lease option of $90 per device — $27,000 for the full fleet — to keep them. Whether that option is worth exercising isn't yet clear: the board has no reliable estimate of what the purchased laptops would be worth in resale after three years of use.

Wear and tear adds another layer of uncertainty. The district's current fleet, now six years old, needed 71 battery replacements in the 2025-26 school year alone. Officials caution that figure can't be used to predict how the new devices will hold up, since the old fleet's total device count and repeat-repair figures weren't tracked in a way that yields a failure rate — and newer hardware may behave differently regardless.

Finance director Sora Brill said the choice is as much about cash flow as total cost. A lease spreads payments evenly over three years, while an outright purchase draws more heavily on the district's current capital budget in a single year. "A lower bill in September can come with a different decision waiting three years later," Brill said, referring to the return-or-buy choice built into the lease.

Staffing costs don't disappear under either option, either. Technician Peren Moss said someone still has to manage repairs and keep loaner devices ready for students whose machines are out of service. "Someone still has to get a working machine onto the student's desk," Moss said.

Some elements of the decision are simpler. Both bids cover the same laptop model and include setup, so those factors don't distinguish the offers. Neither bid includes the district's teaching-software subscriptions, which are purchased separately and unaffected by which hardware option is chosen.

The district needs 260 laptops for regular classroom assignment and plans to keep 40 as spares for repairs and new enrollment, across its two secondary schools and shared technology workshop.

No bid has been selected. The board is expected to take up the decision in September.
