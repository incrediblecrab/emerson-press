# Bellharrow to open multilingual service desk Oct. 19

Bellharrow's civic center will open a multilingual navigation desk Oct. 19 to help residents find the right city office, understand required documents and arrange appointments.

The desk, beside the main reception area, will initially serve residents in English, Spanish and Somali. Interpreters will work on a schedule, so residents should not expect every language to be available at every hour. The city will display a weekly language schedule at reception and provide it by telephone.

Residents can visit without an appointment, though a specialist interpreter may need to be booked for a later conversation. The desk will help people navigate city services, but employees will not decide housing applications, issue permits or give legal advice about an individual's case.

Service manager Arla Deyn said the desk will address questions about where residents should take their inquiries.

"The first question is often not how to complete a form, but which counter should receive it," Deyn said.

A community survey supplied examples of residents visiting several offices for the same inquiry. It was not designed to estimate a citywide error rate, so those examples do not establish how often residents across Bellharrow encounter that problem.

The city approved two coordinator positions and a $48,000 contracted interpreting budget for the first year. The coordinators' salaries are funded separately through the civic center staffing budget and are not included in the interpreting allocation.

The neighborhood language forum has asked the city to track requests it cannot meet for languages outside the initial three. Forum member Iman Varek said residents also need to know when language help will be available.

"Publishing the hours matters as much as publishing the list of languages," Varek said.

Staff will record inquiry topics and requests they cannot meet, without retaining copies of visitors' personal application documents. A side room will be used for conversations that should not take place in the public reception queue.

The city has not promised that using the desk will shorten the processing time of an underlying application. Existing departments will remain responsible for decisions and for telling applicants what further evidence they need.

A review after three months will examine the language schedule, unmet requests and whether visitors reached the relevant department. The city has made no commitment to add a particular fourth language.

The opening follows six months of planning involving reception staff and local organizations that already help residents navigate services. Those organizations will continue operating independently rather than becoming part of a city-run advice service.

The civic center's original reception window will remain available for routine inquiries alongside the new desk. The building's large wall directory is also being replaced because its department names no longer match several current office signs.
