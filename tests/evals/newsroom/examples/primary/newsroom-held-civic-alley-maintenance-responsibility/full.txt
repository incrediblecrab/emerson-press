# Who repairs Bellfen's shared alleys? Responsibility can change along the route

In Bellfen, the city maintains alley segments formally accepted into its maintenance network. For other stretches, repair responsibility depends on the agreements and property records that apply to that segment. An alley's public use alone does not settle the question.

That distinction matters for residents on three connected blocks who requested resurfacing of an alley used for waste collection and deliveries. Parts of the route appear in the city's maintenance register, but an intervening segment does not.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the works and property offices have begun a joint review of the request but have not issued a final responsibility decision. The waste collection schedule for those blocks remains unchanged.

## What does the city maintain?

Bellfen's works register lists 238 alley segments. Of those, 142 have been formally accepted into the city's maintenance network. The remaining 96 include private shared accesses and segments whose maintenance status still requires document review.

The register records maintenance status. It is not, by itself, a final determination of who owns the land or who has the right to use it. Nor does absence from the maintained network necessarily mean staff have settled which private party is responsible.

"The route feels continuous when you walk it, but the records can assign different responsibilities along it," works manager Orin Vell said.

Under city policy, regular public use or passage by a city waste truck does not automatically bring an alley into the maintenance network.

## How is responsibility checked?

Staff first compare the location in a repair request with the register, earlier agreements and plans held by the city's property office. If maintenance status remains unresolved, the works department refers the document question to the property office rather than inferring responsibility from the pavement's condition.

Some older alley plans identify buildings by former business names that no longer appear on current street signs. Residents may submit copies of agreements or older plans, but staff must assess those documents before changing the register.

"We need one answer about the paperwork before we can have a useful argument about the potholes," resident organizer Ena Marr said.

## Does reporting a pothole guarantee repairs?

No. A request can lead to a records inquiry, temporary work or a scheduled repair, depending on the segment and the problem identified. The city does not guarantee city-funded resurfacing of an entire shared route simply because a defect has been reported.

The ordinary resurfacing budget is allocated to maintained segments. Work on other land requires a separate approved arrangement. Under the city's procedure, an urgent temporary measure to keep an access open does not automatically transfer permanent maintenance responsibility to the city.

Private owners can propose a maintenance agreement. That possibility should not be read as an automatic offer of city acceptance.

## Will the planned map settle the question?

The city plans a public map showing maintained segments, with a notice directing residents to check unresolved boundaries with staff. It will not display private residents' contact details or replace advice about an individual property dispute.

For the three-block request, the outstanding question is responsibility for the intervening segment missing from the register. The joint review has not yet settled it. Bellfen's process concerns its own records and policies; it does not establish maintenance law for alleys elsewhere.
