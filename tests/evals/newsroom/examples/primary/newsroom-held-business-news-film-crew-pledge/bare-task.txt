# Thornhaven studio proposes paid training, but funding and production remain unsettled

THORNHAVEN — Thornhaven Screenworks published a proposed local-hiring agreement Tuesday alongside an application for a $600,000 county production grant, outlining paid training opportunities that depend on the opening of two sound stages and a signed production contract.

The Aug. 25 proposal sets aside 12 trainee placements, but it does not establish that production work is funded or ready to begin. No grant has been awarded, conversion of the warehouse space has not started and letters of interest from two producers are not binding contracts.

The company is seeking county money to help convert two warehouse bays into sound stages. The grant board will consider applications in October. The public application does not disclose the founders’ full private financing commitments or establish a final opening date.

The proposed placements would each last 16 weeks, with 30 scheduled paid hours a week at a gross hourly rate of $18. That amounts to $540 a week and $8,640 over a full placement before deductions, which were not supplied. If all 12 placements run for their full scheduled terms, combined gross trainee pay would total $103,680.

Those positions would cover lighting, set construction and production administration, not acting. Applicants would have to live in the county when they apply, though the proposal sets no minimum length of residence.

The application estimates a peak crew of 70 people during a full shoot. That figure includes the 12 trainees if all placements are filled; it is not 70 jobs plus the trainees, nor is it a count of permanent, year-round positions.

Studio founder Iven Tal said production schedules could leave gaps between shoots when no freelance crew members are engaged.

“A stage can be a permanent building while the work inside it comes in blocks,” Tal said.

For set carpenter Rena Doss, who travels outside the county for most jobs, local production would offer the prospect of shorter trips. But Doss said trainees also need safe storage for their tools and a way to get home after late calls.

“Training gets somebody onto the first call sheet. The next call is the harder promise,” Doss said.

The proposal includes a county-college training partner, but it guarantees no job after a placement ends. The college has agreed to help design instruction; its governing board has not approved a new degree program.

The property arrangements also remain conditional. The warehouse landlord has signed a lease that takes effect if project financing closes by February 2027. One bay previously held theatrical scenery, while the other remains occupied by a furniture wholesaler until December.

The county grant program scores applications on paid training, local purchasing and evidence of financing, rather than solely on headline job estimates. The proposed training schedule supplies specific hours and pay rates, but the placements remain contingent on the stages opening and a first production contract being signed.

As of Tuesday, the project had a proposed hiring framework, a conditional lease and producer interest. It did not yet have a county grant award, a binding production contract or a final stage-opening date — the distinction between announced opportunities and work that crews can count on starting.
