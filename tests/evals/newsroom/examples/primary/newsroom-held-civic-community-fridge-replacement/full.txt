**Oatbridge food circle to swap 24-hour fridge for staffed pantry hours**

OATBRIDGE — The Oatbridge Food Circle will close its outdoor community fridge Sept. 30 and replace it with staffed pantry sessions at the neighborhood hall starting Oct. 6, trading round-the-clock access for a system some shift workers say will shut them out.

The fridge sits on a host business's forecourt, and the closure comes as that business renovates its entrance. The circle has not described the closure as the result of a food-safety violation.

Rather than move the fridge to another outdoor location, the circle will open a pantry inside the hall on Tuesday evenings and Saturday mornings. The current fridge can be approached at any hour.

"We can offer a person to ask, but we are losing the freedom to come at three in the morning," said coordinator Una Fenn.

A group of shift workers has asked the circle to assess access outside the two weekly sessions before treating the new arrangement as a full replacement for the fridge. Resident representative Cal Roven said the hall setup narrows who can reach food when they need it.

"A staffed door can be welcoming and still be a closed door when your shift ends," Roven said.

The circle plans to collect feedback during the pantry's first month before deciding whether it has enough volunteers to add another session.

The hall has offered the circle a lockable food-storage room under a one-year agreement; renewal has not been negotiated. An $18,600 community grant will cover the arrangement's first year, with $9,300 going toward the room charge and $9,300 toward equipment, transport and volunteer training. The grant does not establish a permanent municipal food service.

Under the circle's participation policy, visitors will not need a referral or proof of income to use the pantry. But the circle has not promised a fixed parcel for every visit; what is available will depend on donations and storage capacity. Four local food businesses will keep donating, though their written commitments do not specify a guaranteed daily quantity.

Donations will now be handed to volunteers during arranged delivery windows rather than left anonymously outside the hall, and the storage room will not be generally accessible to the hall's other users.

Notices posted at the existing fridge will list the closure date, the new session times and a contact point for the transition. The circle has arranged a final collection of remaining food before the forecourt closes, following its usual donation-handling procedures.

The fridge door has for months carried handwritten recipe cards and spare shopping bags left by visitors. Volunteers hope to continue that exchange at the pantry, though recipe cards will not be a condition of receiving food.

The circle's new records will track visits and stock movements rather than assess individual visitors' health or nutritional outcomes. The reporting packet on the transition contains no estimate of how many distinct households used the unstaffed fridge.
