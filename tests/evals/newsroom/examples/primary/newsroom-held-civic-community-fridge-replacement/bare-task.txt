Oatbridge Community Fridge to Close, Replaced by Staffed Pantry Sessions

OATBRIDGE — The Oatbridge Food Circle will shut down its outdoor community fridge Sept. 30, when its permission to use a host business's forecourt expires, and will replace it Oct. 6 with staffed pantry sessions at the neighborhood hall.

The host business is renovating its entrance, and the circle said the closure is not the result of any food-safety violation.

Rather than relocate the same refrigerator to another outdoor site, the circle plans to move its operation indoors. The hall has offered the group a lockable food-storage room under a one-year agreement; renewal terms have not yet been negotiated. An $18,600 community grant will fund the change, with $9,300 covering a year of room charges and $9,300 going toward equipment, transport and volunteer training. The grant covers one year only and does not establish a permanent municipal food service.

The shift in location comes with a shift in hours. The outdoor fridge could be approached at any hour, but the pantry will operate only during staffed sessions on Tuesday evenings and Saturday mornings.

"We can offer a person to ask, but we are losing the freedom to come at three in the morning," said coordinator Una Fenn.

A group of shift workers has asked the circle to evaluate access outside the two weekly sessions before treating the new setup as a full replacement for the fridge. Resident representative Cal Roven said the change carries a tradeoff. "A staffed door can be welcoming and still be a closed door when your shift ends," Roven said.

The circle said it will collect feedback during the pantry's first month before deciding whether it has enough volunteers to add another session.

Under the circle's participation policy, visitors will not need a referral or proof of income to use the pantry. But the group is not promising a fixed parcel of food at every visit; availability will depend on donations and how much the storage room can hold. Four local food businesses have agreed to continue donating, though their written commitments do not guarantee a set daily quantity.

The mechanics of receiving donations will also change. Instead of items being left anonymously at the hall's doorstep, volunteers will accept donations from businesses during arranged delivery windows. The locked storage room will not be generally accessible to the hall's other users.

Notices posted on the existing fridge will inform visitors of the Sept. 30 closure date, the new pantry sessions and a contact point for the circle during the transition. Before the forecourt closes, the circle plans a final collection of any food remaining in the fridge, using its standard donation-handling procedures.

The fridge door has served as an informal community bulletin board, holding handwritten recipe cards and spare shopping bags left by visitors. Volunteers hope to preserve that recipe-card exchange at the new pantry, though sharing a recipe will not be a condition of receiving food.

The circle does not have an estimate of how many distinct households relied on the unstaffed fridge. Going forward, the group said its records will track visits and stock movements rather than assess individual visitors' health or nutritional needs.

The pantry is set to open for its first session Oct. 6 at the neighborhood hall.
