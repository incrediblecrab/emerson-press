# What Wrenbar's broadband map means for your address

A street marked for Wrenbar's municipal broadband expansion is not necessarily ready for service. The network office's map separates planned streets, completed street works and addresses ready to order — distinctions that matter before residents make decisions about their existing internet service.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the current expansion area contains 650 premises, including businesses and multiunit buildings. That is the project's scope, not a count of 650 new household customers.

"The map follows the work toward the front door. It does not make every street-level milestone a connection inside the building," network manager Irena Pell said.

## What do the map's stages show?

A planned street is included in the expansion, but that label does not mean construction is complete. Completed street works mark a later step, though a cable route passing a property does not by itself mean the individual connection, equipment and service checks are finished.

An address marked ready to order is distinct from those street-level categories. It should not be confused with an already activated service: Street construction, property permission, an appointment for the final connection and service activation are recorded as separate stages.

The office's older map used one color for all included streets. The current distinctions separate those stages so readers can tell more about where the work stands.

## Why can buildings on the same block differ?

Street construction can be complete while access arrangements for a particular building remain unresolved. In multiunit properties, building management may need to coordinate shared routes before individual apartments can be connected.

The office has not promised that every apartment in a building passed by the cable route will become ready to order on the same day.

The public map is updated weekly. Individual appointment confirmations, however, are sent directly through the ordering process. A planned community demonstration will explain ordering and map terminology; it will not serve as an installation appointment.

## What do interest and reservation payments mean?

The office has received 430 expressions of interest. Those are neither completed installations nor paid service subscriptions. The office also has not linked or removed duplicate expressions of interest by premise, so the figure cannot establish what share of the 650 premises has expressed interest.

Under the published terms, a resident may make a $25 reservation payment. It does not guarantee an installation date. The payment is refundable before a service order is finalized and will be credited against the first service bill if the order proceeds.

"People are making decisions about their existing service, so the words ready and planned need to mean different things," resident representative Olan Marr said.

The network tells prospective customers not to treat an expression of interest as notice that they can discontinue their current service.

## What about schedule changes and neighboring roads?

The office says it will publish changes when a construction dependency affects the schedule, rather than convert a planned date into a completed milestone without notice.

The expansion budget covers the stated build area. It does not authorize automatic service to every neighboring road. Requests from outside the area are logged for future planning, but they do not have the same construction status as included streets. No decision has been made on the next expansion area after the current 650-premise project.

For residents with limited current internet access, paper copies of the expansion map are available at the civic center.
