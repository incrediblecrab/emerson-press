# Rookmeadow hangar delayed at least three months as tenant holds off on hiring

Construction of Rookmeadow Airport's proposed maintenance hangar will start at least three months late, the airport authority said Thursday, leaving a prospective tenant unable to set recruitment plans for the new site.

The authority had planned to begin site work in September and open the hangar in May 2027. An incomplete drainage permit has delayed that schedule: The engineering submission omitted runoff calculations for an adjacent service apron, and the permit office requested them July 29.

The delay leaves business and training decisions waiting on a revised construction timetable. Prospective tenant Finchward Aviation plans to continue operating at its current workshop at another airport, while a technical college seeks alternatives for six trainee rotations it had expected to place at the new hangar next summer.

The permit office has not rejected the entire project. The authority said it will publish a revised schedule after receiving the drainage engineer's submission, but it has promised no specific publication date. As of Aug. 20, there is no revised opening date to replace the May target.

Finchward has paid a refundable $120,000 lease deposit. Its agreement allows it to withdraw if the hangar is not available by Sept. 1, 2027, but the company has not exercised that option. The construction delay does not establish whether the building will miss that deadline; the revised schedule remains pending.

“We can keep repairing aircraft where we are. We cannot recruit against a building date that keeps moving,” Finchward manager Rella Vint said.

Finchward projected 40 jobs at the new site, conditional on the hangar opening. That total includes 24 transfers and 16 additional positions, rather than 40 new regional jobs. No hiring notices for the new site have been issued.

The college's search for alternative placements concerns the six expected trainee rotations. It has not canceled the trainees' course. Whether those rotations can take place at the new hangar next summer remains unresolved while the authority awaits the engineering submission and prepares a new timetable.

The project's approved capital budget is $7.2 million, including an estimated $420,000 for site drainage. The authority has not priced the additional drainage design or revised the final construction budget, so the cost consequences of completing the drainage work are not yet established.

Neighboring garden-center owner Tavin Lark said he supports the jobs but wants drainage settled before more hard surface is built.

“A permit question can be paperwork at the airport and water at my fence,” Lark said.

His concern is about drainage from the proposed development. The reporting record does not establish that the proposed hangar has caused flooding at the garden center.

The hangar site has been used for equipment storage since a taxiway realignment in 2011. The delay concerns the new maintenance facility; the airport's existing passenger and freight services will continue.
