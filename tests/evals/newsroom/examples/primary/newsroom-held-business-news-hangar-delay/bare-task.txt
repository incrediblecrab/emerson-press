# Rookmeadow hangar delayed at least 3 months, leaving hiring plans uncertain

Construction of a proposed maintenance hangar at Rookmeadow Airport will be delayed at least three months, the airport authority said Thursday, leaving a prospective tenant’s hiring plans and college trainee placements waiting on a revised building schedule.

The authority had planned to begin site work in September and open the hangar in May 2027. But its drainage permit remains incomplete because an engineering submission omitted runoff calculations for an adjacent service apron. The permit office requested those calculations July 29; it has not rejected the project.

The delay announced Aug. 20 concerns only the new maintenance facility. The airport’s existing passenger and freight services will continue.

For prospective tenant Finchward Aviation, the uncertainty affects when it can recruit for the planned operation, though the company intends to keep repairing aircraft at its current workshop at another airport during the delay.

“We can keep repairing aircraft where we are. We cannot recruit against a building date that keeps moving,” Finchward manager Rella Vint said.

Finchward has paid a refundable $120,000 lease deposit. Its agreement allows it to withdraw if the hangar is not available by Sept. 1, 2027, but the company has not exercised that option.

The original May 2027 opening target would have put the facility into service before that contractual deadline. With no revised opening date announced, the effect of the construction delay on that timing remains unresolved.

Finchward projected 40 jobs at the hangar, contingent on its opening. That figure includes 24 transfers and 16 additional positions, rather than 40 new regional jobs. The company has issued no hiring notices for the new site.

The uncertain schedule also affects a technical-college placement program that had expected six trainee rotations at the hangar next summer. The college is seeking alternative placements, but it has not canceled the trainees’ course. Those arrangements, like Finchward’s recruitment plans, had anticipated a new facility that now lacks a revised opening schedule.

The project has an approved capital budget of $7.2 million, including an estimated $420,000 for site drainage. The authority has not priced the additional drainage design or revised the final construction budget, leaving the cost implications of the missing calculations unsettled.

Drainage is also a concern for neighboring garden-center owner Tavin Lark, who said he supports the jobs but wants the issue resolved before more hard surface is built.

“A permit question can be paperwork at the airport and water at my fence,” Lark said.

The reporting record does not establish that the proposed hangar has caused flooding at the garden center. The outstanding permit issue is the missing runoff analysis for the service apron.

The proposed hangar site has been used for equipment storage since a taxiway realignment in 2011. Its planned conversion to a maintenance facility now awaits the drainage submission and an updated construction timetable.

The authority said it will publish a revised schedule after receiving the drainage engineer’s submission. It promised no specific publication date, leaving Finchward and the college without a new timetable for decisions tied to the hangar’s opening.
