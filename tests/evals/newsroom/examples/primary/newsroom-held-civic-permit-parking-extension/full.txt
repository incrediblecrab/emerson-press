# Reedharbor to expand residential parking zone to 14 blocks Nov. 9

Reedharbor Council will add eight blocks to its residential parking zone Nov. 9, extending weekday permit requirements while keeping the annual resident permit fee at $35.

The extension will bring the existing six-block zone to 14 blocks after signs and curb markings are installed. Permits will be required from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays, not around the clock.

Eligible households in the added blocks may begin applying Oct. 12 through the city's existing address-verification process. A permit allows parking in eligible spaces within the zone but does not reserve a particular space or guarantee that one will be available.

Residents petitioned for the extension after a nearby office complex expanded its occupied floor area without a change to the city's parking-zone boundary. The approved extension changes access to existing curb parking rather than adding spaces.

"The permit changes who can use a space at certain times; it does not create a space," transport manager Reva Linn said.

The added area contains 176 ordinary curb spaces, according to the city's marking plan. The city has not supplied a count of how many are occupied by residents rather than commuters on a typical day. That leaves unclear how much of the existing parking use in the added blocks involves commuters.

Loading bays and marked accessible spaces will retain their existing arrangements. They will not become ordinary permit spaces. Guest permits will also remain available under the current visitor scheme, which has not been expanded to cover all-day commuter parking.

A group of nearby shop workers has asked the city to examine where displaced employee parking might move. No new off-street parking facility or employee shuttle is included in the approved package.

"A boundary can solve a problem on one side and move it to the next street," shop worker representative Dena Wren said.

The council plans to survey streets immediately outside the expanded zone before and after implementation. That work is intended to examine parking outside the boundary; the city has not yet concluded whether displacement will occur. The planned survey is not a finding that nearby streets will absorb more vehicles or that they will remain unaffected.

Under the rollout plan, drivers will receive warning notices during the extension's first 30 calendar days before routine penalty notices begin. The warning period does not suspend existing restrictions on blocking entrances, loading bays or accessible spaces.

The new street signs will use a different border color for the zone but retain the existing permit symbol. The city budget includes $28,400 for signs, road markings and processing the first round of new applications.

The council has also scheduled a six-month review. It can consider changes to the boundary, but it does not automatically authorize another expansion.
