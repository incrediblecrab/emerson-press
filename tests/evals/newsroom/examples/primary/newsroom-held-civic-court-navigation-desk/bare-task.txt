# Harven County court to open free navigation desk Nov. 3

Harven County court administration will open a free public navigation desk Nov. 3 in the court building's main entrance hall to help visitors find rooms, understand appointment arrangements and locate existing public forms.

The service will be available without an appointment during the building's public counter hours. It will provide practical information about navigating the court, not legal representation or advice about which argument to make in a case.

The first-year allocation of $82,000 will pay for two part-time staff members, training and revised building signs. It does not establish a legal-aid fund or cover visitors' court fees.

“Finding the right room should not depend on having been through the building before,” court administrator Edda Voss said.

The project followed staff reports of visitors joining the wrong queues and missing check-in instructions posted in separate corridors. Those accounts were examples collected during planning, not a measured rate of missed hearings or incorrect filings.

Revised signs will use the same room names as appointment letters, replacing several older departmental labels still displayed in the building. The desk will replace a freestanding rack that held mixed forms and leaflets. The entrance hall's wooden benches will remain.

The desk's role will be limited. Staff will not represent visitors, select a legal argument or predict how a judge will decide a case. The court's ordinary filing and hearing procedures will remain unchanged when the service opens.

A local advice partnership will train navigation staff on when to refer someone to an independent legal adviser. The partnership will remain a separate organization, and its advisers will not automatically be available to everyone who visits the desk.

“Directions can remove one obstacle, but they cannot stand in for advice about a person's choices,” said Rana Pell, the partnership's coordinator.

The desk will offer printed information in large type and arrange use of the court's existing language-assistance booking process. The administration has not promised that an interpreter in every language will be immediately available at the desk.

Visitors who need to discuss private details can ask to use a side counter away from the main waiting line. Navigation staff will not retain copies of visitors' case papers as part of their inquiry log.

Instead, the log will count the kinds of practical inquiries the desk receives. The administration will use that information to identify signs or instructions that visitors find confusing.

A public review after six months will consider the service's hours and recurring navigation problems. The opening announcement includes no evidence of changed case outcomes or shorter court processing times.

For visitors, the new service will provide a place to ask practical questions about where to go and how to find court information. Its opening will not change filing or hearing requirements, provide money for fees or guarantee access to an independent legal adviser.
