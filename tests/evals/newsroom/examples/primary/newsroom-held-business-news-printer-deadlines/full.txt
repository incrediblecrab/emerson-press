# Pine Estuary Journal to move printing Oct. 5, cut six pressroom jobs

The Pine Estuary Journal will move weekday printing to Oriole Pressworks on Oct. 5, eliminating six pressroom positions and moving its nightly print deadline two hours earlier, the newspaper announced Wednesday.

Publisher Veda Quill said the Journal will continue publishing five print editions a week and will not change its subscription price at the move. The newspaper will keep its newsroom, advertising staff and public office in Pine Estuary.

The change will put printing 62 miles from the current pressroom. Readers will face an earlier cutoff for news in each day's paper, while local shops that order commercial leaflets are still waiting for deadlines under the new arrangement.

The weekday print deadline will move from 10:30 p.m. to 8:30 p.m. to allow time for transportation. Late council meetings and evening sports results may appear online first, then in the next print edition whose deadline has not passed.

Editor Milo Reeve said the newsroom plans an online late-news page but has not announced a change in paid access.

"An earlier press time changes when a reader gets a fact, not whether we should report it," Reeve said.

Delivery contractors have been given the same target doorstep times as under the current arrangement. But as of Wednesday, Aug. 12, the company had not completed a trial run of the new weekday transport route. The unchanged targets have therefore not yet been tested in a completed trial of that route.

For the nine people employed in the current pressroom, the move will mean either different work or the loss of their positions. Three have accepted packaging jobs at the Journal. The other six positions will be eliminated, and the affected employees have been offered severance based on their length of service. Individual amounts were not disclosed.

Press operator Deni Vale described the knowledge workers have developed while running the equipment.

"We know which rollers complain before the machine stops. That knowledge does not fit in a moving box," Vale said.

Quill estimates the move will save $380,000 a year in recurring printing and maintenance costs once it is complete. One-time transition expenses are separate, and their total has not been disclosed.

The current press also needs a replacement control unit that Quill quoted at $240,000. That is a one-time expense, not an annual maintenance cost. The Journal installed the press in 1993.

Commercial leaflets now printed for local shops will also move to Oriole Pressworks, but order deadlines for those jobs remain under negotiation. Hardware advertiser Kira Stenn said she needs confirmation of the leaflet deadline before booking her November sale.

Although the Journal's advertising staff will stay in Pine Estuary, the unresolved production schedule leaves Stenn without the deadline she needs to make that booking.

The Journal reports current print circulation of 11,200 in its latest publisher statement. That figure is publisher-reported, not independently audited.

The newspaper still prints a weekly tide table on its back page. No decision has been announced about selling the pressroom property or changing the Journal's ownership.
