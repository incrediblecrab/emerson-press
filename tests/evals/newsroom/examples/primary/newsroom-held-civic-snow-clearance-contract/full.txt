# Northrill splits winter snow clearing between two contractors

Northrill Council has awarded its next winter snow-clearing work to two contractors, separating roads from pedestrian routes at a combined base price of $312,000.

The road-clearing contract is $188,000, and the pedestrian-route contract is $124,000. Together, they are $22,000, or about 7.6%, above the previous season's combined base contract of $290,000. The contracts begin Nov. 1 and run through March 31, 2027.

One city duty officer will coordinate the contractors' deployments and resolve gaps where their responsibilities meet. Both contractors must report completed sections to that officer rather than assume the other has cleared connecting crossings.

The road contractor will handle the priority vehicle network. The second contractor will clear designated footways and public-building approaches, including school approaches, the civic center and the main bus interchange. The pedestrian contract does not cover every sidewalk in the city.

Operations manager Elen Marr said coordination matters because clearing one part of a route can still leave people unable to reach their destinations.

"A cleared road with an uncleared crossing can still leave someone unable to finish the journey," Marr said.

A mobility group has asked for crossing points and bus-stop approaches to appear explicitly on the pedestrian route maps. The final maps will be published before the contracts begin.

"The last few yards are not a small detail when they are the yards you cannot get through," said Pera Voss, a representative of the mobility group.

The council has not promised that every listed route will be clear by the same hour after every snowfall. Deployment order will depend on conditions reported to the duty officer and the priority network described in the contracts.

The base prices cover standby capacity and specified routine deployments. Exceptional additional work will be billed at separate agreed rates, so the $312,000 figure is not a fixed total for all work the winter may require.

Likewise, the previous season's $290,000 base contract is distinct from the amount ultimately paid for all weather-related callouts. The increase in base prices therefore does not establish how total spending will compare between the two seasons. The approved award report contains no forecast of how many major snow events the coming season will bring.

The former combined contractor did not submit a bid this year. The council has not described the change as a dismissal for misconduct.

Residents will be able to report blocked designated routes through a public contact line. Those reports will enter the coordinated work queue, but they will not guarantee an immediate visit.

City crews will continue maintaining grit bins and carrying out smaller tasks outside the contractors' main routes. Before winter operations begin, the depot will hold a joint route familiarization session for the two contractors.

The council will compare completed work records with public reports at a midseason review. Any extension beyond March 31 will require approval.
