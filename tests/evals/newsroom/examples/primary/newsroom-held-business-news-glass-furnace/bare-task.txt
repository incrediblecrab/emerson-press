**Brindle Kiln Glass to Swap Gas Furnace for Electric Model at Dunmere Plant; Shutdown Set for January**

DUNMERE — Brindle Kiln Glass announced Monday that it will replace the gas-fired furnace at its Dunmere bottle plant with an electric furnace, a $16 million project that will halt production for six weeks starting Jan. 11, 2027.

The project includes a $4 million grant from the Fen County Industrial Fund, with the remaining $12 million coming from company funds and a bank loan. The company did not disclose the loan's interest rate.

All 96 production employees have received written offers for work during the shutdown. Under the plan, 68 workers would be assigned to training, 16 to maintenance duties, and 12 to a company plant 34 miles away. Base hourly pay would continue in all three cases, but the offers do not include normal production overtime. Employees sent to the other plant would be given a company shuttle rather than mileage reimbursement.

Union steward Kori Bell said the loss of overtime pay is a serious concern for workers who depend on it to cover household bills. The union has asked the company for a temporary income supplement during the shutdown, but Brindle has not agreed to the request. The company also has not said how many employees have accepted their assignments.

"Keeping a job and keeping the same paycheck are not quite the same promise," Bell said.

Plant manager Ansel Pike said the new furnace is expected to make smaller bottle production runs more efficient, something the current equipment handles poorly. He did not provide figures on the new furnace's energy cost per bottle or its emissions, which the company has not yet calculated.

The furnace being replaced began operating in 2004 and has undergone two scheduled rebuilds since then.

The Fen County Industrial Fund grant requires Brindle to file annual employment reports for three years but does not guarantee that overtime levels will remain unchanged or that emissions will be reduced by any specific amount.

The project also depends on infrastructure outside the company's control. Dunmere Power Cooperative has approved the electrical connection needed for the new furnace, but a required substation transformer has not yet arrived; it is still on order. The cooperative said the transformer must be delivered by December for the announced installation schedule to remain on track.

Ahead of the shutdown, Brindle is building up its bottle inventory for its 23 beverage-company customers and has offered many of them earlier delivery dates to help bridge the gap in production.

Not all customers see that as a full solution. Leda Marr, owner of Thistle Run Cider, said the offer of early shipments only helps if she has somewhere to store the extra bottles.

"An early delivery helps only if I can find somewhere to put it," Marr said.

Marr said her company has rented a storage unit to hold inventory through the shutdown period, though she declined to disclose the rental cost.

Brindle has not released a timeline for when the transformer delivery or employee acceptance numbers will be finalized, and company officials did not say Monday how they would respond if the shutdown or connection work is delayed beyond the current schedule.

The company said further updates on staffing, energy performance and construction progress would be provided as the project moves forward.
