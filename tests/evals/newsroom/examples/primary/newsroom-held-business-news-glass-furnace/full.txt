Let me check in with the advisor before finalizing given the fact-density and quote-integrity requirements.Brindle Kiln Glass to swap gas furnace for electric, with six-week shutdown planned

Brindle Kiln Glass will replace the gas furnace at its Dunmere bottle plant with an electric one, the company said Monday, and expects to halt production for six weeks starting Jan. 11, 2027, while the old furnace is removed.

The project carries an announced budget of $16 million, including a $4 million grant from the Fen County Industrial Fund. The remaining $12 million will come from company funds and a bank loan; the company did not disclose the loan's interest rate.

The plant employs 96 production workers, all of whom received written offers for work during the shutdown, the company said. The offers assign 68 employees to training, 16 to maintenance work and 12 to a company plant 34 miles away. Base hourly pay would continue under all three assignments, but normal production overtime would not be available. Employees assigned to the other plant would receive a company shuttle rather than mileage reimbursement.

Union steward Kori Bell said several employees rely on overtime pay for household bills, and the union has asked the company for a temporary income supplement during the shutdown. The company has not agreed to the supplement and has not reported how many employees have accepted their assignments.

"Keeping a job and keeping the same paycheck are not quite the same promise," Bell said.

Plant manager Ansel Pike said the new furnace should allow smaller bottle runs, which he said the current equipment handles inefficiently. The company has not released a calculation of the new furnace's energy cost per bottle or its emissions.

Dunmere Power Cooperative has approved an electrical connection for the new furnace, but a required substation transformer is still on order, the cooperative said. The cooperative said the transformer must arrive by December for the announced installation schedule to hold.

The existing furnace began operating in 2004 and has undergone two scheduled rebuilds, according to the company.

Brindle is building up bottle inventory for its 23 beverage-company customers and has offered them earlier delivery dates ahead of the shutdown.

Leda Marr, owner of Thistle Run Cider, said an earlier delivery only helps if she has somewhere to store the extra stock. "An early delivery helps only if I can find somewhere to put it," Marr said. Her business has rented a storage unit for the shutdown period; she would not disclose the rental cost.

The Fen County Industrial Fund grant requires Brindle to file annual employment reports for three years. The grant does not guarantee that overtime hours will continue unchanged or set a required reduction in emissions.
