**Choir adds side room for parents, and rehearsal picks up where it left off**

A parent set a music folder down beside a bag of crayons, then walked into the next room to join the warm-up.

That small handoff is the point of a new room the Bracken Bell community choir has rented next to its rehearsal hall, where two volunteers organize quiet activities for children while their parent or guardian rehearses. The parent or guardian must stay on the premises; the choir describes the setup as an accommodation limited to rehearsal night, not an independent drop-off childcare service.

The idea grew out of members talking about missed rehearsals, choir officials said, not a survey of childcare demand around the city. Some singers had been skipping sessions because they had nowhere to bring a child for a few hours midweek.

"I used to do the whole calculation before answering the rehearsal message," said Rena Dall, a choir member. "This gives me another way to say yes."

The choir is paying $25 a week for the extra room during its initial 12-week term, a total of $300, covered by a members' collection kept separate from the hall's normal charge for the main rehearsal room. The side room holds donated books, drawing materials and a washable floor mat, rather than equipment bought through a grant.

The volunteers keep attendance and track parent-contact arrangements for the session; children do not move freely between the side room and other activities in the hall, which is also used by a craft club. Choir members pack away the activity materials after each rehearsal so the room is clear for the next group.

Choir director Oren Vell said the schedule now allows for interruption. He sometimes pauses while a parent responds to a child in the next room, then resumes the same passage once the group is ready again.

"We are adjusting the rehearsal we have, with the people who are actually in it," Vell said.

Some members asked for a clear line between the side-room activities and the quieter stretches of rehearsal, and the choir and hall staff agreed on which door stays available for family movement and where the activities can take place, to keep noise from crossing into the main room.

Using the side room is not required. The choir said it still welcomes members who arrange child care elsewhere, and the new room does not replace any existing arrangement.

Choir officials said they have no measured count of how attendance has changed since the room opened, and the packet on the arrangement does not claim it meets every family's need or that all interested parents will be able to take part. The initial 12-week plan will be reviewed afterward, including the workload on volunteers and whether the choir can keep paying for the second room. No permanent funding, and no expansion of the room beyond rehearsal night, has been agreed.

For now, the arrangement runs one rehearsal at a time. The folder stays by the crayons until warm-up ends.
