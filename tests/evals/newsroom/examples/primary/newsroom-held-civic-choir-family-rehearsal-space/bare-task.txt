Bracken Bell Choir Adds Side Room So Parents Can Keep Singing

A community choir here has found a modest fix for an old problem: members who kept missing rehearsal because they couldn't find someone to watch their kids for a few hours.

The Bracken Bell community choir has rented a second room beside its regular rehearsal hall, giving parents a place to bring children while they sing. Two volunteers run quiet activities in the side room during the weekly session, though a parent or guardian must stay on the premises rather than dropping children off and leaving.

Choir member Rena Dall said the option has changed how she thinks about the weekly invitation to rehearse.

"I used to do the whole calculation before answering the rehearsal message," Dall said. "This gives me another way to say yes."

On a recent evening, a parent set a music folder down beside a bag of crayons before heading into the next room for warm-up exercises, one small illustration of the routine now built into rehearsal night.

The side room holds donated books, drawing materials and a washable floor mat — not new equipment bought through any grant. The choir is paying $25 a week for the space during a 12-week initial term, a total of $300, covered by a members' collection kept separate from the hall's regular charge for the main rehearsal room.

Organizers are careful to describe the arrangement in limited terms. It applies only during rehearsal and is not offered as standalone drop-off child care. The choir does not require any parent to use the room, and members who arrange care elsewhere are still welcome. Volunteers keep attendance and parent-contact information for the session, and children are not free to move between other activities happening in the hall, which is also used by a local craft club. The choir packs away its activity materials after each rehearsal so the space can be used for other purposes.

The idea grew out of conversations among members about missed rehearsals, choir officials said, not any broader survey of childcare demand in the community. Choir director Oren Vell said the goal was practical rather than sweeping.

"We are adjusting the rehearsal we have, with the people who are actually in it," Vell said.

That adjustment shows up in small ways. Vell said he sometimes pauses while a parent responds to a child in the next room, then resumes the same passage once the group is ready again.

Not every detail has been settled without friction. Some members asked for a clearer boundary between the side-room activities and the quieter portions of rehearsal, and the choir and hall staff have agreed on which door stays available for family movement and where activities can take place.

The choir's repertoire for the term is unaffected by the new arrangement. Adults can still join the group without an audition, under the choir's existing membership rules.

Organizers acknowledge the limits of what they've built. There's no measured before-and-after count of membership tied to the room, and no guarantee the setup meets every family's needs or that all interested parents can attend. No permanent funding has been secured, and there are no plans to expand the room's use beyond rehearsal nights.

The initial 12-week term will be reviewed afterward, choir officials said, weighing volunteer workload against the cost of keeping the second room.
