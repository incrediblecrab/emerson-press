# New warehouse roles do not mean more jobs at North Loom

North Loom Fulfillment is advertising warehouse jobs after installing automated carts, but its permanent workforce has shrunk: New roles and seasonal openings do not necessarily mean more employees overall.

The Fenstone warehouse had 96 employees before the transition and 94 at the end of July. It eliminated 12 picking positions left vacant by departures while adding four maintenance and six returns-processing positions. That changed the work available but left two fewer permanent jobs.

The two additional returns roles advertised on its careers page are seasonal, not confirmed additions to permanent staffing. The warehouse uses temporary staff during its winter peak, and those placements are outside the 96-to-94 comparison.

## How hiring and a smaller workforce coexist

Job openings, hires and total employment measure different things. An opening can replace someone who leaves, fill a post vacated by an internal transfer or add a position. Only looking at the number of advertisements cannot show which is happening.

North Loom's payroll reconciliation records 12 departures and 10 external hires. Five of those hires filled posts vacated by internal transfers rather than the 12 eliminated picking positions. Moving an employee to another role changes where that person works without adding someone to the payroll; hiring a replacement fills the resulting vacancy.

The position count and payroll count therefore reach the same result by different routes: Twelve picking positions disappeared and 10 other positions were added, while 12 people departed and 10 people joined.

The company says no employee was dismissed as part of the transition. That describes how this reduction happened, not a guarantee about future employment.

"Counting job titles alone misses the work that moved between them," operations director Kalen Orr said.

## What the carts changed

North Loom installed 17 automated carrying carts during the first half of 2026. They move bins between workstations. Employees still select items, inspect orders and pack parcels at the warehouse, which handles clothing and household accessories.

Worker Iri Dain moved from picking to returns and now spends more time examining damaged packaging than walking between shelves.

"The walking went down, but deciding what can go back into stock is still somebody's job," Dain said.

Access to the new roles also differs. Maintenance jobs require an equipment certificate and pay a higher base rate than entry-level picking jobs.

The company paid for eight current employees to take a certification course. Seven completed it, and four obtained the new maintenance jobs. The other participants remained eligible for their existing roles; taking the course did not automatically deliver a technician post.

## What the output figures cannot settle

Average dispatched order lines rose from 10,000 to 12,000 per operating day during the transition, a 20% increase. An order line is one product entry within an order, not necessarily a whole parcel or a unique customer.

That increase does not establish how much additional output the carts produced. Product mix and customer order sizes also changed, and the company has not isolated the carts' effect.

Nor do the figures establish whether the $1.8 million equipment investment has paid for itself. North Loom has not published a full unit-cost or payback calculation. The comparison also supplies no matched employee injury, turnover or satisfaction measurements, so it cannot establish changes in those outcomes.

What the staffing records do show is narrower: North Loom added some kinds of work, stopped refilling others and hired people while its permanent headcount fell. The seasonal advertisements describe further openings, not a reversal of that decline.
