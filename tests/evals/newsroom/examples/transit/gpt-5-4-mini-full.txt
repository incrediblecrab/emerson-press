Bay Cross Transit’s advisory board has recommended cutting Saturday Loop departures from six to four, but the existing timetable stays in place for now and no start date has been announced.

The board voted 5-3 to recommend that the route keep its 8 a.m., 10 a.m., 4 p.m. and 6 p.m. departures from the station on Saturdays. Under the current schedule, the bus also leaves at noon and 2 p.m. The proposed change would remove only those middle departures. It would not change the route, the stops or fares, and it would apply to Saturday service only, not weekday or Sunday service.

The Saturday Loop runs from the station through Yard Square and Cliff Terrace and then returns to the station. The midday gap in the recommendation means there would be no station departure at noon or 2 p.m., leaving a six-hour span between the 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. trips. That gap is about spacing between departures, not how long the bus takes to complete a trip.

In the board paper, the stated reason for the proposal is driver availability for the middle shift. The packet does not include boarding counts or any estimate of financial savings, and it does not propose an alternative midday shuttle.

Board chair said in the minutes: “This is a recommendation, not a timetable.”

Before any change can take effect, the funding committee must approve it. The operating team also must supply a staffing plan before the committee can act on the proposal. The committee is scheduled to consider the item on 22 May 2026, but no outcome has been recorded.

Public notice remains part of the process. Bay Cross Transit’s materials say approved timetable changes require at least 14 days of public notice before they begin. Stop panels will continue to show the current timetable until an authorized replacement is issued.

Public comments may be submitted through 15 May 2026 at https://example.org/bay-cross/saturday-comments. Those submissions can be considered, but they do not decide the timetable.

The access group Bay Cross Access Circle said in writing: “A six-hour gap would change whether a same-day visit is practical.” The group asked planners to consider passengers who need a midday return, not only the first and last departures.

For passengers, the practical bottom line is simple: nothing has changed yet. The current six-departure Saturday schedule remains in force unless and until the proposal clears the remaining approvals, notice requirements and any final authorization.
