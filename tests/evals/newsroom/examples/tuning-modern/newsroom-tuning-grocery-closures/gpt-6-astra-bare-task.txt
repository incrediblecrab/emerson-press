# Vantage to close 23 supermarkets, putting 1,800 jobs at risk

Vantage Market Group will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across four states after sustained financial losses, putting about 1,800 jobs at risk and leaving two communities without a nearby full-service grocery store.

The closures in Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia are scheduled to begin April 30 and finish by June 15. The company, which says it can no longer compete on price in the affected markets, faces a threatened union lawsuit over whether workers received legally required notice.

Vantage reported a $47 million net loss in the fourth quarter of 2024, its third consecutive quarterly loss. Its stock fell 9.4% Thursday on news of the closures.

“We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability,” CEO Gregory Patton said.

Patton said Vantage had lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years.

About 1,100 of the workers at the closing stores are full-time employees. Workers at 14 of the 23 locations are represented by United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880.

“Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed,” Local 880 President David Osei said.

Osei said the union planned to file a federal court complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act. No complaint had been filed as of Thursday.

The federal law generally requires covered employers to provide 60 days’ advance written notice of qualifying plant closings or mass layoffs. Coverage depends on employment thresholds and other conditions, and exceptions can apply. The union contends the affected stores qualify and that no exception excuses shorter notice. No court has ruled on coverage or liability.

Workers interviewed outside the Millfield, Ohio, store said they learned of the closure during an all-hands meeting that morning. None said they had received written notice.

At nonunion stores, interviewed workers said Thursday that they had received no information about severance. Their eligibility for severance was not established.

The closures also threaten grocery access in Millfield and Dunning, Kentucky. The Vantage stores in those towns are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their respective communities.

Local officials in both towns said they had not been notified before the announcement. Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan called the closure “a gut punch to a town that's already struggling.”

Vantage plans to sell or sublease its store properties to help reduce approximately $210 million in long-term debt. It has not identified any buyers.

The company reported revenue of $1.42 billion in fiscal 2024, down from $1.61 billion in 2022. Founded in 1978, Vantage once operated 140 stores across six states.

Patton said he expects the remaining 64 stores to return the company to profitability by the end of fiscal 2026, but he did not provide specific projections.
