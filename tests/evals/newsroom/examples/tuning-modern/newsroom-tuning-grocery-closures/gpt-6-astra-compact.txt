# Vantage to close 23 supermarkets, putting about 1,800 jobs at risk

Vantage Market Group will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across four states, putting about 1,800 jobs at risk as the company struggles with sustained losses and competition from lower-priced rivals.

The closures in Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia are scheduled to begin April 30 and end June 15. A union representing workers at 14 of the closing stores says Vantage failed to provide legally required advance notice and plans to seek remedies in federal court.

The retreat will also leave two communities without their only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles. Vantage's stock fell 9.4% Thursday on news of the closures.

Vantage reported a net loss of $47 million in the fourth quarter of 2024, its third consecutive quarterly loss. Revenue totaled $1.42 billion for fiscal 2024, down from $1.61 billion in 2022.

"We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability," CEO Gregory Patton said.

Patton said Vantage had lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years. The company cited an inability to compete on price as a reason for the closures.

About 1,100 of the workers employed at the affected stores are full-time employees. United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880 represents workers at 14 of the locations.

"Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed," Local 880 President David Osei said.

Osei said the union planned to file a federal court complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, known as WARN. No complaint had been filed as of April 24.

The law generally requires covered employers to provide 60 days' advance written notice of qualifying plant closings or mass layoffs. Coverage thresholds and exceptions determine whether that requirement applies. The union asserts that the affected stores qualify and that no exception excuses shorter notice; no court has ruled on coverage or liability.

Workers who spoke with reporters outside the Millfield, Ohio, store said they learned of its closure at an all-hands meeting that morning. None said they had received written notice. Interviewed workers at nonunion stores said Thursday that they had received no severance information.

The Millfield store and another in Dunning, Kentucky, are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their respective communities. Local officials in both places said they had not been notified before the announcement.

Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan called the closure "a gut punch to a town that's already struggling."

Vantage carries approximately $210 million in long-term debt and plans to sell or sublease store properties to reduce that burden. The company has not identified any buyers.

Patton said Vantage expects to return to profitability with its remaining 64 stores by the end of fiscal 2026, but he did not provide specific projections.

Founded in 1978, Vantage at its peak operated 140 stores across six states.
