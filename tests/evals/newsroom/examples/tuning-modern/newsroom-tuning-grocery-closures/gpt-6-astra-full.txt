# Vantage to close 23 supermarkets as losses mount; union plans notice lawsuit

Vantage Market Group will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across four states after sustained financial losses, putting about 1,800 workers’ jobs at risk and prompting a union to threaten a lawsuit over advance notice.

The closures in Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia are scheduled to begin April 30 and end June 15. Vantage said it could no longer compete on price in the affected markets. Its stock fell 9.4% Thursday on news of the closures.

The company reported a $47 million net loss in the fourth quarter of 2024, its third consecutive quarterly loss. The stores slated to close employ about 1,100 full-time workers.

“We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability,” CEO Gregory Patton said.

Workers at 14 of the closing stores are represented by United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880. Union President David Osei said the union planned to file a federal court complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, known as WARN. No complaint had been filed as of Thursday.

“Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed,” Osei said.

WARN generally requires covered employers to provide 60 days’ advance written notice for qualifying plant closings or mass layoffs. Whether the law applies depends on coverage thresholds and exceptions. The union asserts that the affected stores qualify and that no exception excuses shorter notice; there has been no court finding on coverage or liability.

Workers who spoke with reporters outside the Millfield, Ohio, store described learning of the closure at an all-hands meeting that morning. None said they had received written notice. Interviewed workers at nonunion stores said Thursday that they had received no severance information.

The closures will also leave two communities without a nearby full-service supermarket. The stores in Millfield and Dunning, Kentucky, are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their respective communities.

Local officials in both communities said they had not been notified before the announcement. Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan called the closure “a gut punch to a town that’s already struggling.”

Patton said Vantage had lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years.

Vantage’s revenue fell to $1.42 billion in fiscal 2024 from $1.61 billion in 2022. The company carries about $210 million in long-term debt and plans to sell or sublease its store properties to reduce that debt. It has not identified any buyers.

Patton said he expects the remaining 64 stores to return the company to profitability by the end of fiscal 2026, but he did not provide specific projections.

Founded in 1978, Vantage at its peak operated 140 stores across six states.
