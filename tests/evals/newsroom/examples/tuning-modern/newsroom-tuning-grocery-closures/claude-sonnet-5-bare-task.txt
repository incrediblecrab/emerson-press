**Vantage Market Group to Close 23 Stores in Four-State Region, Cutting 1,800 Jobs**

Vantage Market Group announced Thursday it will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia, citing sustained financial losses and an inability to compete on price with larger rivals.

The closures, scheduled to begin April 30 and conclude by June 15, will affect roughly 1,800 workers, including about 1,100 full-time employees. Workers at 14 of the 23 stores are represented by United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880.

The announcement follows a fourth-quarter net loss of $47 million, the company's third consecutive quarterly loss. Vantage's revenue fell to $1.42 billion in fiscal year 2024, down from $1.61 billion in 2022.

"We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability," Vantage CEO Gregory Patton said.

Patton said the company had lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years. He said Vantage expects to return to profitability with its remaining 64 stores by the end of fiscal year 2026 but did not offer specific projections.

Local 880 President David Osei criticized the timing and manner of the announcement. "Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed," he said.

Osei said the union plans to file a federal complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, alleging Vantage failed to give required advance notice. No complaint had been filed as of Thursday. The WARN Act generally requires covered employers to give 60 days' written notice ahead of qualifying plant closings or mass layoffs, though coverage thresholds and exceptions can apply. Whether the affected stores qualify has not been determined by any court.

Workers interviewed outside the Millfield, Ohio, store said they learned of the closure during an all-hands meeting Thursday morning and had not received written notice. Employees at non-unionized stores said they had received no information about severance.

Two of the closing stores — in Millfield and in Dunning, Kentucky — are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their communities. Local officials in both towns said they were not notified before the announcement.

"This is a gut punch to a town that's already struggling," Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan said.

Vantage carries about $210 million in long-term debt and plans to sell or sublease the closing stores' properties to help pay it down, though the company has not identified any buyers.

Founded in 1978, Vantage once operated 140 stores across six states at its peak.

Investors reacted swiftly to the news. Vantage shares fell 9.4% Thursday.

The closures come amid broader consolidation pressures in the grocery industry, as regional chains face growing competition from national discount retailers and rapidly expanding supermarket subsidiaries. For towns like Millfield and Dunning, the stakes extend beyond jobs: Residents there could be left without easy access to groceries once the stores shut their doors.

Vantage has not detailed severance or transition assistance for affected workers. Union and company officials did not immediately respond to questions about further negotiations.

The company said additional details about the closure timeline and property dispositions would be released in the coming weeks as it works to stabilize its finances and reassure shareholders that the reduced footprint can return the business to sustained profitability.
