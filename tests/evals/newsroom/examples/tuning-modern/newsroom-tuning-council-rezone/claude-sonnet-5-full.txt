Council Approves Rezoning of Former Rail Yard for 1,200-Unit Development

MERIDIAN HEIGHTS — The City Council voted 6-3 Tuesday night to rezone a 28-acre former rail yard in the northeast corridor, clearing the way for a mixed-use development that could bring up to 1,200 housing units to a site that has sat vacant since 2019.

The rezoning of the Northline Site, proposed by Anchor Point Development Corp., allows buildings up to 14 stories and includes both residential and commercial space. Under the city's inclusionary housing ordinance, at least 20% of the units — about 240 — must be priced for households earning 80% or less of the area median income.

The vote followed nearly four hours of debate and public comment at the regular council session. Forty-seven residents spoke, 31 in favor and 16 opposed, before the council took up the measure.

Council President Diane Vasquez, who has represented the northeast district since 2014, voted for the plan and called it the culmination of years of work.

"This is a decade of negotiation finally bearing fruit," Vasquez said.

The three dissenting votes came from council members Marcus Okafor, Rhonda Tran and Eli Suárez, who each cited concerns about whether the corridor's infrastructure could support the added density.

"We are approving density without a clear answer on whether the pipes can handle it," Okafor said.

Those concerns stem partly from a water main capacity study completed in January. City planning director Rebecca Lim told the council the study found the corridor would need $14 million in infrastructure upgrades to accommodate the planned development. That cost is not included in the rezoning approval and would have to be addressed separately, through the city's capital budget process.

A representative for Anchor Point told the council the company would fund the water system upgrades if the city granted a 10-year tax abatement for the project. The council did not act on that request Tuesday, voting instead to table the abatement question until the June budget cycle.

The Meridian Residents Coalition, a group that has organized against the project, submitted a petition opposing the rezoning signed by 2,300 residents.

The council's approval reverses the outcome of a previous attempt to rezone the site, which failed on a 4-5 vote in 2021.

Anchor Point has 18 months from Tuesday's vote to submit a final site plan to the city.

If built as proposed, the development is expected to add an estimated 2,000 to 2,400 residents to a corridor currently served by a single bus line, raising questions about transit capacity that were not resolved Tuesday.

City officials have pointed to the region's broader housing shortage in discussing the project. The regional planning authority estimates Meridian Heights needs 8,500 additional housing units to meet current demand.

The council's vote on the rezoning itself was final. The tax abatement request, and the $14 million question of who ultimately pays for the water infrastructure needed to serve the new development, remain open and are expected to return to the council in June.
