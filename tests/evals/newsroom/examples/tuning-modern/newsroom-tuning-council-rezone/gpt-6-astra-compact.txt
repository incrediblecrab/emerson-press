# Meridian Heights approves rail yard rezoning; water funding unresolved

The Meridian Heights City Council voted 6-3 Tuesday night to allow redevelopment of a former rail yard with about 1,200 homes, leaving funding for needed water infrastructure upgrades unresolved.

The rezoning permits mixed-use residential and commercial development up to 14 stories on the 28-acre Northline Site in the city's northeast corridor. Anchor Point Development Corp. proposed the plan for the property, which Meridian Central Railroad closed in 2019.

A water main capacity study found that the corridor would need $14 million in infrastructure upgrades to support the planned density, city planning director Rebecca Lim told the council. That cost is not included in the rezoning approval and must be addressed through a separate capital budget process.

An Anchor Point representative said the company would fund the water infrastructure upgrades if the city agreed to a 10-year tax abatement on the project. The council voted separately to table the tax abatement discussion until the June budget cycle. Tuesday's rezoning vote did not settle that proposed exchange.

Council President Diane Vasquez, who supported the rezoning, said the approval followed years of negotiations over the site.

"This is a decade of negotiation finally bearing fruit," said Vasquez, who has represented the northeast district, including the Northline Site, since 2014.

Council members Marcus Okafor, Rhonda Tran and Eli Suárez voted against the plan, all citing concerns about infrastructure readiness.

"We are approving density without a clear answer on whether the pipes can handle it," Okafor said.

Lim said the water main capacity study was completed in January. The findings put a price on the upgrades needed to support the development, but the question of how to pay for them remains separate from the council's decision to allow the new uses and building heights.

The housing proposal comes as Meridian Heights faces a citywide shortage officially estimated at 8,500 units by the regional planning authority. Under the city's inclusionary housing ordinance, at least 20% of the project's units must be priced as affordable to households earning 80% or less of area median income. For the approximately 1,200 units proposed, that requirement would cover about 240 homes.

The nearly four-hour council meeting drew 47 public speakers. Of those, 31 spoke in favor of the plan and 16 opposed it. The Meridian Residents Coalition also submitted a petition opposing the plan signed by 2,300 residents.

If built as proposed, the development would add an estimated 2,000 to 2,400 residents to a corridor currently served by one bus line.

Tuesday's decision reverses the outcome of an earlier effort to rezone the Northline Site. A prior proposal failed in 2021, with four council members voting in favor and five against.

Anchor Point must submit a final site plan within 18 months of the rezoning approval. The proposed tax abatement is due to return for discussion during the June budget cycle, when the council will face the developer's request to link tax relief to funding the water upgrades.
