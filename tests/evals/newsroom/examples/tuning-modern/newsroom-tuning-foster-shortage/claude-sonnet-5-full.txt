**Alcott County shelters foster children in hotels as state orders fix**

ALCOTT COUNTY, Texas — Eighty-three children in this county's foster care system are living in hotel rooms, county office suites and, in one case, a staff member's private home, because the county has run out of family placements for them.

"We are running a de facto shelter system inside a child welfare agency, and that is not what any of these children need," said Beverly Chen, director of Alcott County Child Welfare Services.

One of them, a 9-year-old referred to in county records as Marcus, has lived in a hotel for four months. Staff say they have not found a family placement that meets his needs.

The Texas Department of State Health and Family Services cited the county in March for maintaining non-compliant placements and ordered it to cut the share of children in non-family settings to below 10% of its total caseload within 90 days. Raymond Park, the state regional director who issued the citation, said the county could face a state receivership of its foster care operations if it does not comply.

With the county's caseload fixed at 847 children, that threshold works out to no more than 84 children in non-family settings. The 83 children currently in hotels, offices and other temporary arrangements already put the county close to that limit, at about 9.8%. But meeting the numerical target alone would not resolve the citation, which also cited the placement conditions themselves.

The agency lists 312 licensed foster homes for its caseload, though the reporting does not include how many beds those homes could still take. It also has 98 funded caseworker positions, 34 of them vacant — a vacancy rate of roughly 35%. Starting caseworkers in Alcott County make $41,000 a year, well below the roughly $54,000 average in neighboring states. Foster families are reimbursed $29 per day per child, compared with a $38 state average, and turn over at a rate of about 28% a year.

Chen said she had told the county commissioners' court about the agency's financial and operational strain since mid-2023 but received no additional funding until after the state's citation. Commissioners have since allocated an emergency $750,000, which Chen called "helpful but not sufficient to resolve the structural problem." The agency's foster care budget was cut 12% in the last fiscal year amid a broader county shortfall tied to declining property tax revenue.

In January, the county signed an 18-month, $2.1 million contract with Bright Futures Partners, a private placement firm, to manage the hotel and office placements. Chen called the contract "a stopgap," saying the agency has no long-term solution for children it cannot place in family homes.

A February recruitment campaign using paid social media ads has drawn 67 inquiries and led to 11 newly approved foster families as of April. Elena Bell, a child welfare researcher at South Plains College, said recruitment alone will not resolve the county's problems and pointed to reimbursement rates and caseworker support as areas needing attention.

State lawmakers are considering House Bill 1402, which would raise the statewide foster parent reimbursement rate to $52 a day. The bill has cleared committee but has not been scheduled for a floor vote.

The Foster Care Reform Alliance, an advocacy group, called for a state takeover of the county's foster care system in a statement issued after the March citation.

Alcott County, with a population of about 210,000, has no homeless shelter. The hotels housing its foster children are commercial properties billed to the county at market rates.
