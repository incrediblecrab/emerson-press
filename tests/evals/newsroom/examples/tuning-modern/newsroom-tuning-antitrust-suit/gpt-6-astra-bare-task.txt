# Harwick sues Nexum over exclusive app payment contracts, seeks $500 million

Harwick State Attorney General Carla Muñoz sued Nexum Technologies in federal court Monday, alleging the company used exclusive payment-processing contracts with app stores to block competitors and seeking $500 million in monetary relief for affected consumers.

The antitrust complaint, filed April 7 in U.S. District Court for the District of Harwick, alleges violations of Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Antitrust Act. It challenges contracts requiring app stores to process all in-app purchases exclusively through Nexum’s payment system.

The state also is seeking an injunction to void the exclusive dealing contracts. No court has ruled on the requested remedies, and Nexum disputed the allegations.

“Our investigation found a deliberate strategy to block smaller processors from competing on the merits,” Muñoz said.

Nexum spokesperson Paul Sterling said the claims “misread our contracts and misunderstand how competitive payment markets actually work.” He said the company would defend the case vigorously.

According to the complaint’s market-share analysis, Nexum controls approximately 67% of the mobile payment processing market in Harwick. The company processed about $4.1 billion in mobile payment transactions in the state in 2024, the complaint says.

The state’s expert economist calculated approximately $380 million in “supracompetitive” payment-processing fees — fees above competitive levels — between 2020 and 2024. That estimate is distinct from the $500 million in monetary relief sought in the lawsuit.

The complaint identifies six app store operators as parties to the exclusive contracts, but none is named as a defendant. The operators include large national platforms and regional businesses, according to the complaint. Muñoz’s office declined to identify them by name.

A complaint from ClearPay Inc., a small Harwick-based payment processor, helped trigger the lawsuit. ClearPay CEO Amanda Jin said her company had “lost five signed partnership agreements after Nexum applied pressure on our distribution partners.”

Muñoz’s office developed the case during an 18-month investigation. The office said investigators reviewed more than 400,000 pages of internal company documents produced under a civil investigative demand.

Harwick is bringing the case on its own. A U.S. Department of Justice spokesperson said the department is “monitoring the litigation,” but it has not joined the lawsuit.

The Federal Trade Commission closed a 2022 investigation into Nexum’s app store practices without bringing an enforcement action. The closure, on its own, does not establish that the conduct challenged in the state’s lawsuit was lawful.

Daniel Trent, an antitrust professor at Fairhaven Law School who is not involved in the case, described the state’s legal theory as plausible. But he cautioned that exclusive dealing claims in digital markets can be difficult to prove.

The case is assigned to U.S. District Judge Harriet Fontaine.

Nexum’s stock fell 3.8% Monday following news of the lawsuit. The company employs approximately 2,400 people, including roughly 300 at its Harwick office.
