# Broadreach buys Tribune Valley for $380 million, plans 85 newsroom cuts

Broadreach Capital Partners has completed its $380 million acquisition of Tribune Valley Media Group, putting 14 rural Midwestern newspapers under private equity ownership as the buyer plans to eliminate about a fifth of their editorial staff.

The deal ends more than six decades of control by the Larkin family, which founded Tribune Valley in 1964. The chain, headquartered in Clayfield, Iowa, has a combined paid circulation of approximately 340,000 across its daily and Sunday editions.

Broadreach announced plans to eliminate 85 editorial positions, approximately 22% of Tribune Valley’s 390-person editorial staff. The cuts have drawn a union objection and a pointed response from the company’s chief executive, who will remain in her position.

“Sustainable local journalism requires operating discipline that a family ownership structure can no longer afford in this environment,” Broadreach managing partner Jeffrey Crane said.

Tribune Valley CEO Sarah Holloway pushed back in an internal memo obtained by reporters.

“I will not sign off on cuts that destroy the editorial capacity of these papers,” she wrote.

Holloway stopped short of threatening to resign. Her statement nevertheless highlights a divide between the new owner’s cost-cutting plans and the executive who will continue to lead the newspaper group.

The Newspaper Guild Local 203, which represents journalists at six of Tribune Valley’s 14 papers, filed an objection with the National Labor Relations Board in February. The union argued that Broadreach had failed to bargain over the announced job cuts.

Several Tribune Valley journalists reached by reporters said they would seek other positions if the reductions proceeded as announced, raising the possibility of additional departures beyond the planned layoffs.

The acquisition comes as Tribune Valley’s advertising business shifts toward digital products, though growth online has accompanied a steep decline in print advertising.

Digital advertising revenue reached $42 million in 2024, up 18% from 2023. Print advertising revenue fell 31% over the same period. The figures provided do not establish whether the digital gains offset the print losses in dollar terms.

The purchase price is approximately 2.7 times Tribune Valley’s total 2024 revenue of $141 million. The Department of Justice reviewed the acquisition before it closed and did not challenge it.

Crane said Broadreach intends to invest in digital infrastructure for the newspapers but declined to specify an amount. The firm also declined to answer questions about editorial policies or whether the papers would share content across titles.

Broadreach previously acquired two other regional newspaper groups. Both experienced editorial staff reductions of more than 30% within 18 months of acquisition, according to journalism industry records.

The Local News Alliance, an advocacy organization, called the Tribune Valley deal “the latest chapter in a pattern of private equity consolidation that has hollowed out local newsrooms.”

The alliance estimates that Tribune Valley’s papers are the primary source of local news for approximately 510,000 residents in communities with no other local news outlet.

The newspapers’ recent work underscores the stakes for those communities. Three Tribune Valley papers have won regional journalism awards in the past two years, and two investigations resulted in county-level officials’ resignations.
