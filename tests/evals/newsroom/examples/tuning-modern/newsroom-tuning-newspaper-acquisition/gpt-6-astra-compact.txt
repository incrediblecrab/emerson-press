# Broadreach buys Tribune Valley for $380 million, plans 85 newsroom cuts

Broadreach Capital Partners has completed its $380 million acquisition of Tribune Valley Media Group, taking ownership of 14 regional newspapers across the rural Midwest while announcing plans to eliminate 85 editorial positions.

The cuts would remove approximately 22% of Tribune Valley's 390-person editorial staff. The deal ends the Larkin family's controlling ownership of the newspaper group it founded in 1964 and puts its newsrooms under a private equity firm whose previous newspaper acquisitions were followed by substantial staff reductions.

The planned cuts have drawn a union objection and a warning from Tribune Valley CEO Sarah Holloway, who will remain as chief executive. In an internal memo obtained by reporters, Holloway said: "I will not sign off on cuts that destroy the editorial capacity of these papers."

Her statement stopped short of threatening resignation. It nonetheless raises a question about how the company's chief executive and its new owner will resolve decisions about newsroom staffing.

Broadreach managing partner Jeffrey Crane defended the need to change the company's operations.

"Sustainable local journalism requires operating discipline that a family ownership structure can no longer afford in this environment," Crane said.

Tribune Valley, headquartered in Clayfield, Iowa, has a combined paid circulation of approximately 340,000 across its 14 papers, including daily and Sunday editions. The purchase price is about 2.7 times the company's 2024 revenue of $141 million.

The company's advertising results show a shift between its print and digital businesses. Digital advertising revenue totaled $42 million in 2024, up 18% from 2023, while print advertising revenue fell 31% over the same period. Those figures alone do not establish whether the digital gains offset the dollar value of the print decline.

Crane said Broadreach intends to invest in digital infrastructure for the papers but declined to specify an amount. Broadreach also declined to answer questions about editorial policies or whether Tribune Valley's newspapers would share content across titles after the acquisition.

The Newspaper Guild Local 203, which represents journalists at six of Tribune Valley's 14 papers, filed an objection with the National Labor Relations Board in February. The union argued that Broadreach failed to bargain over the announced job cuts.

The Department of Justice reviewed the acquisition before it closed and did not challenge it.

Broadreach previously acquired two other regional newspaper groups. Both experienced editorial staff reductions of more than 30% within 18 months of acquisition, according to journalism industry records. That history provides context for the current cuts, though it does not establish whether Broadreach will make further reductions at Tribune Valley.

The Local News Alliance, an advocacy organization, called the acquisition "the latest chapter in a pattern of private equity consolidation that has hollowed out local newsrooms." The organization estimates that Tribune Valley papers are the primary source of local news for approximately 510,000 residents in communities with no other local news outlet.

Three of the group's newspapers have won regional journalism awards in the past two years, and two investigations resulted in county-level official resignations.

Several Tribune Valley journalists reached by reporters said they would seek other positions if the cuts proceeded as announced, raising the possibility of departures beyond the 85 jobs Broadreach plans to eliminate.
