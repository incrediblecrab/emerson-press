**Port Calloway Strike Enters Third Day as $180 Million in Cargo Sits Idle**

CALLOWAY — Dockworkers at Port Calloway remained off the job Wednesday for a third day, leaving an estimated $180 million in cargo idle as talks between the union and port management showed no sign of a breakthrough.

The strike began Monday when International Longshore Workers Union Local 14 rejected a contract offer from the Port Calloway Authority and walked off the job. About 890 dockworkers are involved.

"We move every container that keeps this economy running, and we have been offered less than inflation in real terms," Local 14 President Maria Reyes said.

Port Calloway Authority chief Donald Hsu said management wants a deal. "We remain committed to reaching a fair agreement, but this disruption is costing everyone," he said.

The union is seeking a 28% wage increase over four years. The port authority's offer stands at 18% over the same period. The union's previous four-year contract, which expired Dec. 31, provided a 12% raise.

A second dispute involves automation. The union wants the port to halt new automation investment while negotiations continue. The port authority plans to install six automated container cranes that officials say would cut labor needs by about 18% per shift once running.

Reyes called automation a "red line" for the union. Hsu said the cranes are necessary for the port to stay competitive with neighboring facilities.

About 2,100 containers are sitting at the port that would normally have been loaded or unloaded, port records show. Roughly 12% of those containers hold perishable goods, including strawberry and lettuce shipments.

Port Calloway handles about $4.2 billion in cargo annually and is a major entry point for electronics, auto parts and refrigerated agricultural products. The Calloway Regional Chamber of Commerce estimated Wednesday that the strike is costing the regional economy about $60 million a day in delayed commerce and downstream supply disruptions.

Three electronics importers and two auto parts distributors said they have missed delivery deadlines because of the stoppage. None agreed to be named.

The port's terminal operator has redirected four arriving cargo ships to Port Westfield, about 80 miles up the coast, adding an estimated $2,300 per container in handling and trucking costs.

The Federal Mediation and Conciliation Service sent a team to Calloway on Tuesday. Team leader Gerald Watts said the two sides have agreed to talk but have not entered formal mediated sessions.

California Gov. Gavin Newsom's office offered to assign a state mediator. The union said it was open to the idea, but port management said it preferred to continue with the federal process.

ILWU national leadership has not declared a broader strike; Local 14 is acting independently under its own collective bargaining agreement.

The port authority reported a $28 million operating surplus for fiscal year 2024, a figure the union has cited in pressing its wage demands.

The last major work stoppage at Port Calloway came in 2011 and lasted nine days before it was resolved through federal mediation.

No further talks were scheduled as of Wednesday evening.
