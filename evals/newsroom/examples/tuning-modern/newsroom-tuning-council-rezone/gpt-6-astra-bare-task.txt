# Meridian Heights approves rail yard rezoning; water upgrade funding unresolved

The Meridian Heights City Council voted 6-3 Tuesday night to allow approximately 1,200 homes and commercial development at a former rail yard, leaving unresolved how to pay for $14 million in water infrastructure upgrades needed to support the project.

The rezoning permits mixed-use development up to 14 stories on the 28-acre Northline Site in the city's northeast corridor. Meridian Central Railroad closed the yard in 2019, and a previous attempt to rezone the property failed in 2021.

The approval at the council's April 15 meeting advances a proposal by Anchor Point Development Corp. in a city with a housing shortage officially estimated at 8,500 units by the regional planning authority. But the vote does not cover the infrastructure costs, which must be addressed through a separate capital budget process.

Under the city's inclusionary housing ordinance, at least 20% of the development's homes — approximately 240 units — must be priced as affordable to households earning 80% or less of the area median income.

Council President Diane Vasquez, who supported the rezoning, described the vote as the culmination of years of work.

"This is a decade of negotiation finally bearing fruit," said Vasquez, who has represented the northeast district, including the Northline Site, since 2014.

Council members Marcus Okafor, Rhonda Tran and Eli Suárez cast the three dissenting votes, all citing concerns about infrastructure readiness.

"We are approving density without a clear answer on whether the pipes can handle it," Okafor said.

City planning director Rebecca Lim told the council that a water main capacity study completed in January found the corridor would need $14 million in infrastructure upgrades to accommodate the planned density. That funding question remains separate from the council's decision to allow taller buildings and a mix of residential and commercial uses at the site.

An Anchor Point representative said the company would pay for the water infrastructure upgrades if the city agreed to a 10-year tax abatement for the project. The council voted separately to table discussion of the tax abatement until the June budget cycle, leaving the company's offer contingent on a future city decision.

If built as proposed, the development would bring an estimated 2,000 to 2,400 additional residents to a corridor now served by one bus line.

The council meeting lasted nearly four hours and drew 47 public speakers. Of those, 31 spoke in favor of the plan and 16 opposed it. The Meridian Residents Coalition also submitted a petition opposing the proposal with signatures from 2,300 residents.

Tuesday's approval reversed the outcome of the 2021 rezoning effort, which failed on a 4-5 vote. It establishes the permitted uses and building height for the property, but Anchor Point still must submit a final site plan within 18 months of the approval. The next scheduled discussion of the proposed tax abatement is in June, as the city takes up its budget.
