Council Approves Rezoning for Former Rail Yard After Years of Debate

MERIDIAN HEIGHTS — The City Council voted 6-3 Tuesday night to approve a long-debated rezoning plan for a 28-acre former rail yard, clearing the way for a mixed-use development that could bring roughly 1,200 housing units to the city's northeast corridor.

The vote, taken during the council's regular Tuesday session, ends more than a decade of negotiations over the Northline Site, which Meridian Central Railroad closed in 2019. A previous rezoning attempt for the property failed on a 4-5 vote in 2021.

The approved plan, proposed by Anchor Point Development Corp., allows residential and commercial construction up to 14 stories. Under the city's inclusionary housing ordinance, at least 20 percent of the units — about 240 — must be priced as affordable for households earning 80 percent or less of the area median income.

"This is a decade of negotiation finally bearing fruit," said Council President Diane Vasquez, who has represented the northeast district since 2014 and voted in favor.

The three dissenting votes came from council members Marcus Okafor, Rhonda Tran and Eli Suárez, who cited concerns about whether the corridor's infrastructure can support the added density.

"We are approving density without a clear answer on whether the pipes can handle it," Okafor said.

Those concerns stem from a water main capacity study completed in January by the city planning department. Planning Director Rebecca Lim told the council the study found the corridor would need $14 million in infrastructure upgrades to accommodate the proposed development. That cost is not included in Tuesday's approval and must be addressed separately through the city's capital budget process.

A representative for Anchor Point said the company would be willing to fund the water infrastructure upgrades if the city agreed to a 10-year tax abatement for the project. The council voted separately to table that discussion until the June budget cycle, leaving the funding question unresolved for now.

Tuesday's meeting stretched nearly four hours and drew 47 public speakers, 31 of whom spoke in favor of the rezoning and 16 in opposition. The Meridian Residents Coalition, a local advocacy group, submitted a petition opposing the plan signed by 2,300 residents, citing many of the same infrastructure and growth concerns raised by the dissenting council members.

If built as proposed, the development is expected to add an estimated 2,000 to 2,400 residents to a corridor currently served by a single bus line, raising questions about transit capacity alongside the water infrastructure concerns.

Supporters of the project have pointed to the city's broader housing shortage, which the regional planning authority estimates at 8,500 units, as justification for moving forward despite the unresolved infrastructure questions. Backers argued Tuesday that further delay would only deepen that shortfall.

Under the terms of the approval, Anchor Point must submit a final site plan within 18 months.

The unresolved tax abatement request means the project's financing — and the timeline for the infrastructure upgrades Lim's study identified as necessary — will not be settled until at least June, when the council takes up its next budget cycle. City officials did not say Tuesday whether construction could begin before the infrastructure and funding questions are resolved.

For now, Tuesday's vote marks the furthest the long-stalled Northline Site proposal has advanced, giving Anchor Point Development Corp. the zoning authority to move forward with design work even as key financial and infrastructure details remain unsettled.
