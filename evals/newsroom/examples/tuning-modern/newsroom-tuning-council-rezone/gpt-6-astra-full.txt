# Meridian Heights approves rail yard rezoning; water funding unresolved

The Meridian Heights City Council voted 6-3 Tuesday night to rezone a former rail yard for roughly 1,200 homes, leaving funding for $14 million in needed water infrastructure upgrades unresolved.

The approval allows mixed-use residential and commercial development up to 14 stories on the 28-acre Northline Site in the city's northeast corridor. Anchor Point Development Corp. proposed the plan for the yard, which Meridian Central Railroad closed in 2019.

Under the city's inclusionary housing ordinance, at least 20% of the homes, or approximately 240 units, must be priced as affordable to households earning 80% or less of area median income. The regional planning authority estimates Meridian Heights has a citywide shortage of 8,500 housing units.

The council approved the rezoning despite concerns from three members about whether infrastructure would be ready to support the development. City planning director Rebecca Lim told the council that a water main capacity study completed in January found the corridor would need $14 million in upgrades to support the planned density.

That expense is not included in the rezoning approval and must be addressed through a separate capital budget process.

An Anchor Point representative said the company would fund the water infrastructure upgrades if the city agreed to a 10-year tax abatement on the project. The council voted separately to table discussion of the tax abatement until the June budget cycle, leaving the company's proposed funding arrangement undecided.

Council member Marcus Okafor, who voted against the rezoning, questioned the decision to allow the development before resolving the infrastructure issue.

"We are approving density without a clear answer on whether the pipes can handle it," Okafor said.

Council members Rhonda Tran and Eli Suárez also voted no. All three dissenting members cited concerns about infrastructure readiness.

Council President Diane Vasquez voted for the plan, describing the approval as the result of years of work.

"This is a decade of negotiation finally bearing fruit," Vasquez said.

Vasquez has represented the northeast district, which includes the Northline Site, since 2014. A previous attempt to rezone the property failed in 2021 on a 4-5 vote.

Tuesday's regular council session ran nearly four hours and included 47 public speakers. Of those, 31 spoke in favor of the plan and 16 opposed it. The Meridian Residents Coalition submitted a petition opposing the plan signed by 2,300 residents.

If built as proposed, the development would add an estimated 2,000 to 2,400 residents to a corridor currently served by one bus line. The rezoning permits the proposed mix of housing and commercial uses, but Anchor Point still must submit a final site plan within 18 months of the approval.

The next scheduled discussion of the requested tax abatement is in the June budget cycle. Tuesday's vote settled the rezoning question while leaving the water infrastructure funding to the separate budget process.
