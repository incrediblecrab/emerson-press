**Vantage Market Group to close 23 supermarkets, cut about 1,800 jobs**

Vantage Market Group announced Thursday it will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia, citing sustained financial losses and an inability to compete on price.

The closures will affect about 1,800 workers, including roughly 1,100 full-time employees. Workers at 14 of the 23 stores are represented by United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880.

Vantage reported a net loss of $47 million in the fourth quarter of 2024, its third consecutive quarterly loss. Revenue fell to $1.42 billion in fiscal year 2024, down from $1.61 billion in 2022.

CEO Gregory Patton said the company could no longer sustain the losses in the markets it is exiting.

"We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability," Patton said.

Patton said Vantage has lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years. He said the company expects to return to profitability with its remaining 64 stores by the end of fiscal year 2026 but did not provide specific financial projections.

Closures are scheduled to begin April 30 and conclude by June 15.

Local 880 President David Osei said the union plans to file a federal court complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, alleging Vantage failed to give the 60 days' advance written notice the law generally requires for covered plant closings and mass layoffs. As of Thursday, no complaint had been filed. The law's coverage depends on factors including the size of the layoff and the employer, and no court has ruled on whether the closures qualify or whether Vantage is liable.

"Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed," Osei said.

Workers who spoke with reporters outside the Millfield, Ohio, store said they learned of the closure that morning during an all-hands meeting. None said they had received written notice. Interviewed workers at non-unionized stores said Thursday they had not been given any severance information; Vantage's severance policy for those employees was not immediately available.

Two of the closing stores — in Millfield and in Dunning, Kentucky — are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their communities. Local officials in both towns said they were not notified before Thursday's announcement.

"A gut punch to a town that's already struggling," Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan said of the closure.

Vantage carries about $210 million in long-term debt and plans to sell or sublease the closing stores' properties to help reduce it, though the company has not identified any buyers.

Founded in 1978, Vantage once operated 140 stores across six states.

Vantage's stock fell 9.4% Thursday after the closures were announced.
