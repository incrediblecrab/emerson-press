Vantage Market Group to close 23 stores across four states, cutting 1,800 jobs

COLUMBUS, Ohio — Vantage Market Group said Thursday it will close 23 of its 87 supermarkets across Ohio, Indiana, Kentucky and West Virginia, citing sustained financial losses and an inability to compete on price.

The closures will eliminate approximately 1,800 jobs, including about 1,100 full-time positions. Workers at 14 of the 23 stores are represented by United Food and Commercial Workers Local 880.

The closures are scheduled to begin April 30 and conclude by June 15.

Vantage reported a net loss of $47 million in the fourth quarter of 2024, its third consecutive quarterly loss. Fiscal year 2024 revenue was $1.42 billion, down from $1.61 billion in 2022.

"We cannot ask our shareholders to fund losses indefinitely in markets where we no longer have a viable path to profitability," Vantage CEO Gregory Patton said.

Patton said the company had lost market share in rural and small-city markets to Walmart, Aldi and a Kroger subsidiary that has expanded aggressively in the region over the past four years. He said Vantage expects to return to profitability with its remaining 64 stores by the end of fiscal year 2026 but did not provide specific projections.

Vantage carries about $210 million in long-term debt. The company said it plans to sell or sublease the closing stores' properties to reduce that debt. No buyers have been identified.

Local 880 President David Osei said the union plans to file a federal complaint seeking remedies under the Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act, alleging Vantage failed to give legally required advance notice of the closures. No complaint had been filed as of Thursday.

"Our members are losing jobs right before summer, without the notice they are legally owed," Osei said.

The WARN Act generally requires covered employers to give 60 days' written notice before a qualifying plant closing or mass layoff, though coverage thresholds and exceptions can apply. Osei said the union believes the affected stores qualify and that no exception excuses the shorter notice Vantage gave. No court has ruled on coverage or liability.

Workers who spoke with reporters outside the Millfield, Ohio, store said they learned of the closure at an all-hands meeting Thursday morning and that none had received written notice beforehand. Workers at non-unionized stores said they had not been given information about severance.

Two of the closing stores — in Millfield, Ohio, and Dunning, Kentucky — are the only full-service grocery stores within 12 miles of their communities. Local officials in both towns said they were not notified before Thursday's announcement.

"A gut punch to a town that's already struggling," Millfield Mayor Roger Callahan said of the closure.

Vantage's stock fell 9.4% Thursday following the announcement.

Founded in 1978, Vantage once operated 140 stores across six states at its peak.
