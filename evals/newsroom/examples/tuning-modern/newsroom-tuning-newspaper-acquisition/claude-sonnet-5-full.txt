Private Equity Firm Completes $380 Million Purchase of Tribune Valley Newspapers, Plans 85 Job Cuts

CLAYFIELD, Iowa — Broadreach Capital Partners has completed a $380 million acquisition of Tribune Valley Media Group, taking ownership of 14 regional newspapers across the rural Midwest and announcing plans to eliminate 85 editorial positions.

The cuts represent about 22% of Tribune Valley's 390-person editorial staff, the company said. The deal closed after review by the Department of Justice, which did not challenge it.

Tribune Valley's papers have a combined paid circulation of approximately 340,000 for daily and Sunday editions. The company was founded in 1964 and had been owned by the Larkin family since then; the family sold its controlling stake as part of the sale.

Broadreach managing partner Jeffrey Crane said the acquisition reflects financial pressures facing family-owned newspaper chains.

"Sustainable local journalism requires operating discipline that a family ownership structure can no longer afford in this environment," Crane said.

Tribune Valley CEO Sarah Holloway, who will remain in her role, pushed back against the scale of the cuts in an internal memo obtained by reporters.

"I will not sign off on cuts that destroy the editorial capacity of these papers," Holloway wrote. Her statement stopped short of threatening resignation.

The Newspaper Guild Local 203, which represents journalists at six of the 14 papers, filed an objection with the National Labor Relations Board in February, arguing Broadreach failed to bargain over the announced job cuts before the deal closed.

Several Tribune Valley journalists reached by reporters said they would look for other jobs if the cuts proceed as announced.

The financial picture behind the sale shows a company in transition. Tribune Valley's digital advertising revenue reached $42 million in 2024, up 18% from 2023, while print advertising revenue fell 31% over the same period. The $380 million purchase price amounts to about 2.7 times the company's 2024 revenue of $141 million.

Crane said Broadreach intends to invest in digital infrastructure at the papers but declined to specify an amount. The firm also declined to answer questions about editorial policies or whether Tribune Valley papers would share content across titles going forward.

Broadreach has acquired two other regional newspaper groups in past deals. Both saw editorial staff reductions of more than 30% within 18 months of acquisition, according to journalism industry records.

The Local News Alliance, an advocacy group that tracks local news ownership, criticized the deal.

"The latest chapter in a pattern of private equity consolidation that has hollowed out local newsrooms," the organization said in a statement.

The group estimates that Tribune Valley's papers are the primary source of local news for about 510,000 residents in communities that have no other local news outlet.

Despite the financial strain, Tribune Valley's newsrooms have produced award-winning work in recent years. Three of the company's papers have won regional journalism awards over the past two years, and two investigations led to the resignations of county-level officials.

It remains unclear how the coming staff reductions will affect that reporting capacity, or how quickly the promised digital investment will materialize. Broadreach has not said when the job cuts will take effect.
