# Broadreach buys Tribune Valley for $380 million, announces 85 editorial job cuts

Broadreach Capital Partners has completed its $380 million acquisition of Tribune Valley Media Group and announced plans to eliminate 85 editorial positions at the chain's 14 regional newspapers across the rural Midwest.

The planned cuts represent approximately 22% of Tribune Valley's 390-person editorial staff. The purchase ends the Larkin family's ownership of the newspaper group it founded in 1964 and puts its newsrooms under a private equity firm whose previous newspaper acquisitions were followed by substantial staff reductions.

Tribune Valley CEO Sarah Holloway, who will remain as chief executive, challenged the scope of potential cuts in an internal memo obtained by reporters. A union representing journalists at six of the papers has also contested the announced job losses.

"I will not sign off on cuts that destroy the editorial capacity of these papers," Holloway wrote. Her statement stopped short of threatening resignation.

Broadreach managing partner Jeffrey Crane defended the firm's approach.

"Sustainable local journalism requires operating discipline that a family ownership structure can no longer afford in this environment," Crane said.

Tribune Valley, based in Clayfield, Iowa, has a combined paid circulation of approximately 340,000, including daily and Sunday editions. The Larkin family sold its controlling stake as part of the acquisition.

The company's advertising results show growth in digital revenue alongside a steep decline in print. Digital advertising revenue totaled $42 million in 2024, up 18% from 2023, while print advertising revenue fell 31% over the same period. The figures do not establish whether digital gains offset print losses because the dollar amount of print advertising revenue was not provided.

The purchase price is approximately 2.7 times Tribune Valley's total 2024 revenue of $141 million.

Crane said Broadreach intends to invest in digital infrastructure for the papers but declined to specify an amount. Broadreach also declined to answer questions about editorial policies or whether the papers would share content across titles after the acquisition.

The Newspaper Guild Local 203 filed an objection with the National Labor Relations Board in February, arguing that Broadreach failed to bargain over the announced job cuts. The objection concerns labor practices, separate from the federal review of the acquisition. The Department of Justice reviewed the purchase before closing and did not challenge it.

Broadreach previously acquired two other regional newspaper groups. Both experienced editorial staff reductions of more than 30% within 18 months of acquisition, according to journalism industry records.

The Local News Alliance, an advocacy organization, called the Tribune Valley acquisition "the latest chapter in a pattern of private equity consolidation that has hollowed out local newsrooms."

The alliance estimates that Tribune Valley's papers are the primary source of local news for approximately 510,000 residents in communities with no other local news outlet. That estimate measures residents served, rather than paid circulation.

Three of the group's papers have won regional journalism awards in the past two years, and two investigations resulted in county-level official resignations. Several Tribune Valley journalists reached by reporters said they would seek other positions if the cuts proceeded as announced.
