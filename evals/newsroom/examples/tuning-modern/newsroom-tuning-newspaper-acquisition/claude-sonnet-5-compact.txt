Private equity firm buys 14 Midwest papers, cuts 85 newsroom jobs

CLAYFIELD, Iowa — Broadreach Capital Partners has completed a $380 million acquisition of Tribune Valley Media Group, taking ownership of 14 regional newspapers across the rural Midwest and immediately announcing plans to eliminate 85 editorial positions.

The cuts represent about 22% of Tribune Valley's 390-person editorial staff. The deal closed after the Department of Justice reviewed the acquisition and did not challenge it.

Tribune Valley, founded in 1964, had been owned by the Larkin family since its founding. The family sold its controlling stake as part of the sale. The company's 14 papers have a combined paid circulation of about 340,000, including daily and Sunday editions, and are headquartered in Clayfield.

Broadreach managing partner Jeffrey Crane defended the job cuts as a financial necessity.

"Sustainable local journalism requires operating discipline that a family ownership structure can no longer afford in this environment," Crane said.

Tribune Valley CEO Sarah Holloway, who will remain in her role, pushed back against the scale of the cuts in an internal memo obtained by reporters.

"I will not sign off on cuts that destroy the editorial capacity of these papers," Holloway wrote. Her statement stopped short of threatening to resign.

The Newspaper Guild Local 203, which represents journalists at six of the 14 papers, filed an objection with the National Labor Relations Board in February, arguing that Broadreach failed to bargain over the announced job cuts before the deal closed.

Several Tribune Valley journalists told reporters they would look for other jobs if the cuts proceed as announced.

The sale price of $380 million amounts to about 2.7 times Tribune Valley's 2024 revenue of $141 million. The company's digital advertising revenue grew 18% last year, to $42 million, while print advertising revenue fell 31% over the same period.

Crane said Broadreach intends to invest in digital infrastructure at the papers but declined to specify an amount. The firm also declined to answer questions about editorial policies or whether Tribune Valley's papers would begin sharing content across titles.

Broadreach has acquired two other regional newspaper groups in prior deals. Both experienced editorial staff reductions of more than 30% within 18 months of acquisition, according to journalism industry records.

The Local News Alliance, an advocacy organization, criticized the deal as "the latest chapter in a pattern of private equity consolidation that has hollowed out local newsrooms." The group estimates that Tribune Valley's papers are the primary source of local news for about 510,000 residents in communities that have no other local news outlet.

Three of Tribune Valley's papers have won regional journalism awards in the past two years, and two investigations by its reporters led to the resignations of county officials.
