# Pine Estuary Journal moves printing off-site, cuts six jobs

The Pine Estuary Journal will move printing of its weekday editions to Oriole Pressworks beginning Oct. 5, shifting production 62 miles from its current pressroom, eliminating six jobs and moving its nightly print deadline two hours earlier.

Publisher Veda Quill announced the change Wednesday, Aug. 12, saying the newspaper will continue publishing five print editions a week and will not change its subscription price at the move. The Journal will retain its newsroom, advertising staff and public office in Pine Estuary.

For readers, the most immediate change will be the cutoff for news in the weekday paper. The print deadline will move from 10:30 p.m. to 8:30 p.m. to allow time to transport the newspapers from the contracted printer.

That means coverage of late council meetings and evening sports results may appear online first, then in the next print edition whose deadline has not passed. Editor Milo Reeve said the newsroom plans an online late-news page but has not announced a change in paid access.

“An earlier press time changes when a reader gets a fact, not whether we should report it,” Reeve said.

Delivery contractors have been given the same target doorstep times as under the current printing arrangement. The company, however, has not completed a trial run of the new weekday transport route, leaving the delivery plan untested as of Wednesday.

The move will also affect commercial printing for local businesses. Leaflets now printed for local shops will shift to Oriole Pressworks along with the newspaper, but order deadlines for those jobs are still being negotiated.

Hardware advertiser Kira Stenn said she needs confirmation of the leaflet deadline before booking her November sale. While the Journal’s advertising staff will remain in Pine Estuary, the unresolved commercial printing schedule leaves advertisers such as Stenn waiting for information needed to plan their orders.

For the pressroom’s nine employees, the change means either a different job or the elimination of their positions. Three have accepted packaging jobs at the Journal. The other six positions will be eliminated, and the affected employees have been offered severance based on length of service. Individual severance amounts were not disclosed.

Press operator Deni Vale described the experience workers have accumulated running the equipment.

“We know which rollers complain before the machine stops. That knowledge does not fit in a moving box,” Vale said.

Quill estimates the move will produce $380,000 in recurring annual printing and maintenance savings once it is complete. One-time transition expenses are separate, and their total has not been disclosed.

The existing press also needs a replacement control unit that Quill quoted at $240,000. That figure is a one-time equipment expense, not an annual maintenance cost.

The Journal installed its current press in 1993 and still prints a weekly tide table on the back page. Its current print circulation is 11,200, according to its latest publisher statement; that figure has not been independently audited in the supplied reporting.

No decision has been announced about selling the pressroom property or changing the newspaper’s ownership.
