# Thornhaven studio proposes 12 trainee placements, but production work remains conditional

Thornhaven Screenworks proposed 12 paid local trainee placements Tuesday, but the work depends on opening two planned sound stages and signing a first production contract. Neither milestone has been reached.

The proposed local-hiring agreement, published Aug. 25 alongside an application for a county production grant, accompanies a request for $600,000 to convert two warehouse bays. The grant board will consider applications in October. No award has been made, and the company has not begun the conversion.

The application estimates a peak crew of 70 during a full shoot, including the 12 trainees if all placements are filled. That figure is not a count of permanent, year-round jobs. The company has letters of interest from two producers, but neither is a binding production contract.

Studio founder Iven Tal said production schedules may leave gaps between shoots when no freelance crew are engaged.

“A stage can be a permanent building while the work inside it comes in blocks,” Tal said.

Under the proposal, each trainee placement would last 16 weeks, with 30 scheduled paid hours a week at a gross hourly rate of $18. That amounts to $540 a week, or $8,640 over the full placement before deductions. Individual deductions were not supplied.

The placements would cover lighting, set construction and production administration, rather than acting roles. Applicants would have to live in the county when applying, but the proposal sets no minimum length of residence.

The agreement includes a county-college training partner, though it offers no guaranteed job after a placement ends. The college has agreed to help design instruction; its governing board has not approved a new degree program.

For set carpenter Rena Doss, who currently travels outside the county for most jobs, work closer to home would mean welcome shorter trips. But Doss said trainees also need safe storage for their tools and a way to get home after late calls.

“Training gets somebody onto the first call sheet. The next call is the harder promise,” Doss said.

Financing and access to the proposed stages remain conditional. The warehouse landlord has signed a lease that takes effect if project financing closes by February 2027. One bay previously held theatrical scenery. The other remains occupied by a furniture wholesaler until December.

The public application does not disclose the founders’ full private financing commitments or establish a final opening date for the stages. The proposed grant would go toward the warehouse conversion; the trainee placements would still depend on the stages opening and a first production contract being signed.

The county grant program scores applications on paid training, local purchasing and evidence of financing, rather than solely on headline job estimates. Its October consideration will address the grant request. The proposal’s stated conditions for trainee work remain separate from that decision, and the two producers’ letters of interest do not yet provide a binding production contract.
