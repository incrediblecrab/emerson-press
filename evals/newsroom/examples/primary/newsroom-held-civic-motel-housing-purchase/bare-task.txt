Nonprofit Buys Shuttered Motel to Create 18 Apartments for Homeless Households

The Alder Key Housing Trust completed its $1.8 million purchase of the closed Brook Lantern Motel on Sept. 16, part of a plan to convert the vacant building into housing for people leaving temporary accommodation.

The purchase was financed with a $1.2 million housing grant and $600,000 from the trust's designated acquisition fund, according to the trust.

The motel, which has been empty since its operator closed it in 2024, has 24 former guest rooms. Under the trust's current concept, some rooms would be combined to create 18 self-contained apartments. No apartments are ready for occupancy, and the purchase itself does not approve the conversion's final design, the trust said. Because the motel has been vacant for two years, the sale does not displace any current guests.

The apartments are intended for households moving out of temporary accommodation under the trust's locally funded allocation program. Final eligibility rules, rents and application dates have not been published.

"A purchased building is an opportunity for homes, not eighteen keys we can hand out tomorrow," trust director Dera Quill said.

The trust's design brief calls for adding kitchens to the units, improving insulation and revising fire-separation arrangements throughout the building. The trust has commissioned an assessment of that work but has not fixed a renovation budget or selected a contractor. Officials cautioned that the acquisition grant does not automatically cover every renovation expense.

A neighboring residents group has voiced support for reusing the empty building but has asked how the trust will manage parking and support services at the site.

"We want to understand the daily arrangements, not argue about an empty building in the abstract," said Olin Sare, the group's secretary.

The trust plans to operate an on-site management office during staffed hours, but it has not proposed round-the-clock staffing. The motel's former parking court will remain part of the site, though its final layout depends on the conversion design.

The project will use the existing building footprint rather than add a separate apartment block in this first phase, the trust said. Officials also said the motel's former breakfast room is being considered for a shared laundry and meeting space, not a restaurant or other commercial use.

The property's roadside lantern sign, a longtime landmark, remains in place; the trust has not decided whether to keep it as part of the redevelopment.

Under the completed purchase agreement, the previous owner has no continuing role in selecting future residents.

The trust said it plans to hold a public design meeting once the building assessment is finished, and that information on the project — including eligibility and applications when ready — will be distributed through local advice organizations as well as the trust's own communication channels.

The trust has not provided a target completion date for the conversion, and no information was available on what effect, if any, the project might have on the city's overall housing waiting list.

The purchase marks an early step in a process the trust described as still subject to design review, budgeting and further public input before any of the planned apartments become available.
