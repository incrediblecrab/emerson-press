# Wood fiber or rubber? What Maple Strand is weighing for its playground

Maple Strand's parks committee is comparing renewed engineered wood fiber with bonded rubber for its main playground, with preliminary estimates putting the rubber option $80,000 higher.

The choice is part of a refurbishment plan that also addresses drainage and routes between play activities. As of Sept. 18, 2026, the committee has not selected a surface. Its recommendation will precede a funding decision, so the higher-priced option is not an approved spending commitment.

For visitors, the decision involves more than what goes beneath the equipment. The proposed design considers how children and the people accompanying them would move from the gate to several activities, along with requests to assess shade and places to rest.

## What do the prices cover?

The committee's preliminary design estimates are $160,000 for renewed engineered wood fiber and $240,000 for bonded rubber. Both include associated drainage work and use the same stated project scope.

Neither estimate is a signed construction contract. They provide a comparison of the proposed work, but they do not contain a complete lifetime cost comparison.

Supplier information describes different maintenance tasks for the materials, including replenishing loose material and repairing damaged bonded sections. The estimates do not settle how those costs would compare over the surfaces' lifetimes. The lower preliminary price therefore does not establish which option would cost less over time.

## Why are drainage and routes part of the choice?

City maintenance inspections found that the existing loose surface has shifted at busy entry points. The inspections identify maintenance and drainage problems at this playground; they do not establish that every wood-fiber playground is inaccessible.

The committee's papers retain records of previous repairs so recurring problem areas can inform the decision. The committee plans to compare installation cost, expected maintenance, drainage and route usability before making its recommendation.

The proposed connected route extends from the gate to several play activities, rather than focusing only on the surface immediately beneath equipment.

"The drawing has to show where a child and the person with them can go together," parks designer Nella Voss said.

An access group will review the proposed routes with the design team before procurement. The refurbishment will not replace every piece of play equipment or guarantee that every visitor can use each activity in the same way.

## Where do shade and seating fit?

A parents group has asked the committee to assess shade and resting places along the route, rather than treat the surface material as the only access issue.

"There are days when the bench and the shaded path decide how long we can stay," group member Evin Tare said.

Some donated benches can be moved within the site. Others would need new foundations if relocated, a distinction that matters when considering where resting places could go.

The city has not supplied comparable temperature measurements for the two proposed surfaces at this playground. The available information therefore does not establish which would be cooler at the site. It also contains no material-specific health finding or claim that either option eliminates injury risk.

## What happens next?

The committee will make its recommendation after comparing the options, with a funding decision to follow.

The playground will need to close during surface installation, but no final closure dates have been announced. The adjacent grass field is outside the surface contract and is not an equivalent replacement for every playground activity.
