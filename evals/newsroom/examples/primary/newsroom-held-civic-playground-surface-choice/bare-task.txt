# Wood fiber or rubber? Maple Strand weighs playground surface options

Maple Strand's parks committee is comparing two surface options for the main playground: renewed engineered wood fiber and bonded rubber. The choice is part of a refurbishment plan that also considers drainage, maintenance and how children and their companions move between play activities.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the committee has not selected an option. Preliminary design estimates put wood fiber at $160,000 and rubber at $240,000 for the same stated project scope, including associated drainage work. Neither estimate is a signed construction contract.

Here is what the proposals would address — and what remains undecided.

## What is driving the work?

City maintenance inspections found that the existing loose surface has shifted at busy entry points. The inspections identify maintenance and drainage problems at this playground; they do not establish that every wood-fiber playground is inaccessible.

The committee's papers retain records of previous repairs so recurring trouble spots can be considered in the decision. Both options include drainage work, making the comparison broader than simply choosing a new surface material.

## How does access figure into the design?

The proposed design includes a connected route from the gate to several play activities, rather than evaluating only the surface directly beneath equipment.

“The drawing has to show where a child and the person with them can go together,” parks designer Nella Voss said.

An access group will review the proposed routes with the design team before procurement. The planned refurbishment does not replace every piece of play equipment or guarantee that every visitor can use each activity in the same way.

A parents group also wants shade and places to rest assessed as part of the route, rather than treating surface material as the only access issue.

“There are days when the bench and the shaded path decide how long we can stay,” group member Evin Tare said.

Some donated benches can be moved within the site. Others would need new foundations if relocated.

## What do the prices tell residents?

The rubber option's preliminary estimate is $80,000 higher than the wood-fiber estimate. But the figures do not provide a complete lifetime cost comparison.

Supplier information describes different maintenance tasks, including replenishing loose material and repairing damaged bonded sections. The estimates do not fully account for the lifetime costs of those tasks, so the initial prices alone do not establish which option would cost less over its useful life.

The committee will compare installation cost, expected maintenance, drainage and route usability before making a recommendation. A funding decision will follow; the larger preliminary amount has not already been committed.

## What is not established?

The city has not supplied comparable temperature measurements for the two proposed surfaces at this playground. The record therefore does not establish how their temperatures would compare at the site.

The committee packet also contains no material-specific health finding or claim that either option eliminates injury risk.

## Will the playground close?

The playground will need to close during surface installation, but no final closure dates have been announced.

The adjacent grass field is outside the surface contract. It is not an equivalent replacement for every playground activity during the closure.

For now, the surface choice, funding and closure schedule remain unresolved as the committee weighs the proposals and the planned routes await review.
