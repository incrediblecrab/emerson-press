**Caldwell Motors Recalls 94,000 Electric Vehicles After Battery Fires**

Caldwell Motors on Wednesday announced a voluntary recall of 94,000 Apex electric vehicles from model years 2022 through 2024, after seven thermal runaway incidents and two fires linked to a battery defect.

The recall follows incidents reported over a 14-month period. No fatalities have been reported, and three injuries from the incidents were minor, the company said.

Caldwell and its battery supplier, NovaChem Battery Solutions, said they traced the problem to a manufacturing defect in the anode coating applied at NovaChem's Plant 4 facility. The defect reduces the anode's resistance to lithium plating during rapid charging, which raises the risk of thermal runaway — a process in which an overheating battery cell triggers a chain reaction in neighboring cells, releasing energy that can lead to fire.

Plant 4 produced battery modules for Apex vehicles assembled between January 2022 and August 2024. Six of the seven thermal incidents occurred during or immediately after high-speed DC fast charging, according to the company.

Caldwell CEO Miriam Foster said the company caught the problem before regulators did. "We identified this issue through our own field data monitoring and brought it to regulators before any agency contacted us," Foster said. "Customer safety is not negotiable."

Raymond Dahl, chair of the National Vehicle Safety Board, confirmed the agency received Caldwell's preliminary findings three weeks before Wednesday's public announcement and is conducting its own parallel investigation.

Caldwell said affected owners will be notified by mail and can schedule a free battery module inspection at any authorized dealer. The recall applies only to vehicles with battery serial numbers in a specific range that Caldwell says it can identify through its production database.

Until vehicles are inspected, Caldwell is imposing a software-enforced charging limit of 80% of battery capacity. The company said the limit reduces peak battery stress but also cuts the vehicles' maximum driving range by roughly 20% in the meantime.

If an inspection finds a defective module, Caldwell will replace the entire battery pack at no cost to the owner, a process the company said takes about four hours.

NovaChem has halted production at Plant 4 pending a full quality audit, the company said. NovaChem declined to comment on the financial impact of the shutdown.

Caldwell's stock fell 7.4% in morning trading Wednesday. The Apex accounts for about 18% of Caldwell's global vehicle sales. The company sold approximately 31,000 Apex vehicles in 2022, 38,000 in 2023, and 25,000 in the first eight months of 2024 before the model was updated.

Diana Reyes, a consumer safety attorney not affiliated with any pending lawsuit against Caldwell, said the company's decision to initiate the recall itself, along with the proactive charging limit, would likely influence how regulators assess its conduct.

The National Vehicle Safety Board's parallel investigation is ongoing, and Dahl's agency has not said when it expects to complete its review.
