# How anonymous review works at the Briar Coast Arts Fund

The Briar Coast Arts Fund removes applicants' names from project descriptions before its first-stage artistic review. But the process does not keep identities hidden from everyone, guarantee that panelists will not recognize a project or assure eligible applicants of an award.

As of Sept. 18, 2026, the fund's current round has received 46 applications, with 34 passing an initial eligibility check. The fund plans up to 10 awards from a $100,000 allocation. The round has not reached final award acceptance, so there is no confirmed list of recipients.

Here is how applications move through the process and where anonymity has limits.

## What happens before the panel review?

Staff check applications against the fund's published eligibility and completeness rules. The 12 applications that did not proceed were screened under those rules, rather than ranked by the artistic panel.

That distinction matters when interpreting the results: An application that fails the initial check has not received a panel judgment on its artistic proposal. Conversely, passing the check means an application can proceed to review, not that it will receive funding.

Staff retain identifying information to check eligibility and communicate with applicants. The anonymity applies to the papers presented to the panel at the first stage, not to all of the fund's work.

## What does the panel see and assess?

A five-member panel reviews eligible project descriptions with names and direct organizational identifiers removed. The papers use reference numbers so panel comments can be linked to the correct application without displaying a name.

The panel's criteria include the proposed public activity, feasibility, artistic purpose and the relationship between the budget and the work described. Applicants may propose workshops, performances, exhibitions or other public arts activities within the same round.

When checking a proposed budget, the fund separates project grant spending from an applicant's unrelated organizational income. Projects are not chosen through public popularity voting or by counting the supporters an applicant recruits.

## What if a panelist recognizes an applicant?

Removing a name does not necessarily make a proposal unrecognizable. A distinctive earlier project or a proposed venue may identify the applicant to a panelist.

"We can remove the name on the page. We cannot promise that a small town makes every project unrecognizable," fund director Elva Norr said.

Panelists must disclose a relevant connection when they recognize one and follow the fund's recusal procedure. The procedure records recusals and brings the remaining panel members into the relevant decision.

"The useful question for me is how a connection is handled after someone notices it," artist Rena Vale said.

The fund has not supplied evidence that removing names eliminates every form of bias or changes the award rate for a particular group. The use of anonymous papers alone does not establish either result.

## What happens before and after an award?

Before final awards, staff check an applicant's capacity to accept the grant and the practical conditions attached to the proposed activity. A strong first-stage proposal can still require clarification before an award is made.

Unsuccessful applicants will receive feedback distinguishing eligibility issues from panel judgments on eligible proposals. That should make clear which part of the process determined the outcome.

Anonymity is not permanent. Final recipients and funded activities will be published after acceptance. The fund also plans to review the process after the round using feedback from staff, panelists and applicants, rather than assessing it only through the eventual list of recipients.
