Drafting an AP-style update from the notes, keeping the employment-status question open and attributing all figures to named sources.Reedmile Parcel to Shift Three Rural Delivery Zones to Contractor Amid Pay-Model Uncertainty

Reedmile Parcel announced Wednesday that three rural delivery zones will move to contractor Brack Lane Logistics on Oct. 19, offering the 14 drivers who currently cover those routes what Brack Lane describes as self-employed contracts rather than transferred employment.

The three zones cover 1,460 delivery addresses, a figure that reflects address count rather than daily parcel volume, Reedmile said. The affected drivers work within a wider Reedmile workforce of 48.

Under Reedmile, the drivers are paid $19 an hour and use company-supplied vehicles. Brack Lane's offer sets a route fee of $168 for a completed route day, with drivers supplying their own vehicle, fuel and maintenance, and does not guarantee a fixed number of route days each week. The reporting record does not include comparable route-hour or vehicle-cost figures needed to calculate what a driver's net income would be under the new arrangement, and no such comparison is offered here.

Brack Lane has not published a route-by-route schedule or a guaranteed fuel adjustment in the offers it has supplied to drivers, according to the record reviewed. Whether the drivers are properly classified as self-employed contractors has not been determined by any court or regulator; the record contains only Brack Lane's own characterization of the arrangement.

Driver Tavi Holt, who currently borrows a family car outside of work and does not own a vehicle suited to parcel delivery, said the fee looks different once vehicle costs are counted.

"A route fee starts looking different when the van has to come out of it," Holt said.

Reedmile has also advertised two internal warehouse vacancies to the affected drivers, with applications still open. The company has not announced how many of the 14 drivers have accepted Brack Lane's contracts, applied for the warehouse positions or intend to leave.

Reedmile operations director Mara Venn said the change is driven by cost. Lower parcel density on the rural routes makes them more expensive to run per stop, she said.

"We are changing who runs the route, not asking customers to find a new parcel company," Venn said.

Reedmile said retail delivery charges and its promised delivery-day window will not change when Brack Lane takes over the routes. Customer complaints and compensation requests will continue to go through Reedmile rather than directly to individual drivers, the company said.

The company has not released an independently assessed comparison of service quality between its current delivery crews and Brack Lane, and no such assessment appears in the record.

Reedmile began serving the rural zones in 2019, after acquiring a smaller carrier.
