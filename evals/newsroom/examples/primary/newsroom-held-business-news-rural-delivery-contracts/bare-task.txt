Reedmile to Shift Rural Delivery Routes to Contractor, Leaving Drivers to Weigh New Terms

Reedmile Parcel said Wednesday that it will hand off three rural delivery zones to Brack Lane Logistics on Oct. 19, a change that will require 14 current drivers to decide whether to accept new contractor terms, seek other work inside the company or move on.

The zones cover 1,460 delivery addresses, though the company said that figure does not reflect daily parcel volume. The 14 affected drivers are part of a broader 48-driver workforce at Reedmile.

Brack Lane has offered each of the 14 drivers a route contract structured as a self-employed arrangement rather than a transfer of employment. Under the offer, drivers would be paid $168 for a completed route day and would supply their own vehicle, fuel and maintenance. The offer does not guarantee a fixed number of route days each week.

That marks a shift from drivers' current pay of $19 an hour as Reedmile employees using company vehicles. The reporting record does not include comparable data on route hours or vehicle costs under the new arrangement, making it unclear what any driver's net income would look like after expenses.

Reedmile has also posted two internal warehouse vacancies open to the affected drivers, with applications still being accepted. The company has not said how many drivers have accepted Brack Lane's contracts, applied for the warehouse jobs or plan to leave altogether.

Tavi Holt, one of the drivers whose route is affected, said the math depends heavily on vehicle costs he does not yet have to spend. Holt currently borrows a family car outside of work and does not own a vehicle suited to parcel delivery.

"A route fee starts looking different when the van has to come out of it," Holt said.

Brack Lane has not published a route-by-route schedule for the zones, nor has it guaranteed any fuel-cost adjustment in the offers provided to drivers.

Reedmile operations director Mara Venn said the rural routes cost more per stop because of lower parcel density in those areas, one reason the company said it opted to bring in a contractor.

"We are changing who runs the route, not asking customers to find a new parcel company," Venn said.

The company said retail delivery charges and its promised delivery-day window will not change once the contracting arrangement takes effect. Customer complaints and requests for compensation will continue to go through Reedmile directly rather than to individual drivers, the company said.

Reedmile began serving the three rural zones in 2019, after acquiring a smaller carrier that had operated there.

The company has not released any independently assessed comparison of service quality between its current driving crews and Brack Lane, and the reporting record contains no determination from a court or regulator on the employment status of the drivers being offered contractor terms — a question that has drawn scrutiny in other delivery-industry disputes over classification of contract drivers.

With the Oct. 19 transition date about six weeks away, the outstanding questions — how many drivers will take the contractor offer, what their actual costs and hours will look like, and how the change affects service in the three zones — remain unresolved as of this reporting.
