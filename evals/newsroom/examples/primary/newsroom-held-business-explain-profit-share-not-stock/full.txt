**Elmward Components pays first profit-sharing bonus, but no ownership changes hands**

Elmward Components will pay its first annual profit-sharing bonus to eligible employees in November, the company said, splitting a $40,000 pool calculated from the fiscal year that ended June 30.

The payment is separate from base wages and gives recipients no shares, votes or other ownership stake in the company, according to plan details provided by Elmward.

Under the plan, Elmward sets aside 10% of eligible operating profit before the profit-sharing expense for employee payments. The company's supplied calculation put eligible operating profit at $400,000 for the year, producing the $40,000 pool. That figure is not described as after-tax net profit, and the company has not provided every adjustment from its full accounts.

The pool is divided by allocation units. Thirty eligible full-time employees each receive one unit, and 20 eligible half-time employees each receive half a unit, for a total of 40 units. That works out to $1,000 per full unit and $500 per half unit, before individual deductions.

Of Elmward's 57 employees, seven had not completed six months of service by the plan's June 30, 2026, eligibility cutoff and will not share in this payout.

Finance manager Rella Gant said eligibility and allocation amounts were checked against payroll records for the covered year. She said the payment does not alter who controls the company.

"Sharing a result is different from changing who owns the company," Gant said.

The payment will appear as a separate line item in November payroll and will not change hourly wage rates, the company said. Employees will receive individual calculation notices and can ask payroll to review their recorded allocation unit.

The distinction between a profit-sharing payment and stock ownership matters because the two carry different rights. Owning shares typically includes a claim on the company's assets, a vote on some company decisions, and the right to sell that stake. Elmward's plan includes none of those features: it does not let employees demand a sale of the business or cash out an ownership stake, and the founders retain ownership under the announcement. No worker buyout or outside acquisition is proposed.

The payment also is not guaranteed to repeat. Because the formula is tied to eligible operating profit, a future year with no eligible profit would produce no pool, the company said. That makes this year's payout a one-time result rather than a recurring addition to annual wages.

Assembly worker Omi Vale said the payment is welcome but does not change how she budgets for ordinary bills, which she said depend on her regular wage rate.

"I can use a bonus when it arrives," Vale said. "I cannot put next year's unknown profit on a monthly payment plan."

Elmward has not published an independent audit of the full plan calculation or any measured effect on productivity.

The company makes fittings for industrial shelving and has operated from the same estate since 2003. It employs 57 people.
