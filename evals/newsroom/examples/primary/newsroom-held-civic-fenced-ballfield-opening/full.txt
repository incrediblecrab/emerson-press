# Public access planned for Coppermead ballfield in April 2027

Coppermead's parks department plans to open the city-owned Mill Acre ballfield for general recreation in April 2027, after an exclusive club lease expires and access and drainage work is completed.

The Coppermead Athletics Club's 30-year lease ends Sept. 30 without renewal, but the field will not open to the public immediately. The fence will remain closed during the work. The city has owned the 11-acre property throughout the lease, so the transition does not involve buying land from the club.

The council has approved a $275,000 works allowance for the transition, though final contractor prices have not yet been obtained. Initial work includes a step-free entrance, repairs to the perimeter path and improvements to the wet corner nearest the pavilion.

Public access will replace the club's exclusive control, but the club will continue to train at Mill Acre. A new booking agreement gives it 12 scheduled training hours a week. Other organized groups will use the city's booking system, with casual recreation possible outside reserved sessions.

"Opening a gate is simple; making room for different uses is the work that comes after it," parks director Soren Vale said.

The proposed public opening hours are 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. The council will confirm them with the final management plan. The current plan does not add floodlights or authorize late-night events.

Nearby residents have asked for seating and informal play space rather than turning every available hour into another organized booking. A dog-access policy remains unsettled and will be considered alongside maintenance and sports-use requirements.

For the club, the new agreement preserves scheduled time on the field while ending its control over the whole property.

"We lose the certainty of having the whole place, but we keep a place to train," club secretary Mara Keel said.

The club has agreed to transfer ownership of the small equipment shed to the city and will remove its privately owned training equipment. The city will also take over routine grounds maintenance, now the club's responsibility under the lease.

The operating budget for that maintenance will be considered separately from the $275,000 capital works allowance. The approved transition plan includes no attendance forecast or estimate of additional spending at local businesses.

Access to the pavilion toilets is also unresolved. They will remain closed until their condition assessment and repair scope are complete.

The club's noticeboard displays handwritten match results dating to its first season on the ground. The local history society has offered to photograph it before the pavilion is altered.

The parks department will publish a works timetable after procurement and hold a management-plan meeting before the public opening. April 2027 remains the planned opening month, with the final hours and dog-access rules still to be settled.
