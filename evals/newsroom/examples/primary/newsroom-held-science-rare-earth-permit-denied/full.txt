# Agency denies Granite Ridge rare earth mine permit over river contamination risk

The Environmental Resources Agency denied Pacific Minerals Corporation's application to mine rare earth minerals at Granite Ridge in Cascadia County on Monday, citing an inadequate plan to prevent acid drainage.

The June 30 decision followed a 34-month environmental review. Uncontrolled drainage from the ore body could acidify a 38-kilometer stretch of the Coldwater River, which supports a protected steelhead trout population.

Pacific Minerals President Grant Thomas said the company will appeal and submit a revised drainage management plan within 90 days. The agency's decision allows Pacific to reapply within 24 months with a revised environmental management plan.

"The ore body is economically significant, but the applicant has not demonstrated that it can prevent contamination of the river to a scientifically defensible standard," agency Director Helen Waters said.

The Granite Ridge deposit contains an estimated 480,000 metric tons of rare earth oxides, including concentrations of neodymium and dysprosium. Those materials are used in permanent magnets for electric vehicle motors and wind turbine generators. Granite Ridge would be the second commercial rare earth extraction site in the United States if permitted.

The agency's scientific review panel found that Pacific's proposed passive treatment system would be effective for 40 to 60 years but would require perpetual active maintenance after the mine closes. The panel found that the company had not fully addressed the resulting cost and liability.

Acid rock drainage occurs when sulfide minerals exposed during excavation react with water and air to produce sulfuric acid. It is a well-documented challenge in mining sulfide-bearing ores and has contaminated rivers at multiple historic mining sites in the western United States.

A coalition of fishing and conservation organizations had argued that Pacific's drainage plan relied on models calibrated for ores with lower sulfide content and was inappropriate for the Granite Ridge formation.

Thomas said Pacific had offered to post a $120 million environmental performance bond and fund a long-term treatment trust. The agency panel found those terms insufficient.

Pacific has been exploring Granite Ridge since 2017 and has invested approximately $340 million in exploration and permitting activities. State legislators from Cascadia County said the denial would cost the region an estimated 1,200 construction jobs and 380 permanent positions. Pacific's stock fell 14% on the day of the decision.

The proposed mine comes as the United States imports more than 80% of its processed rare earth materials, primarily from China. A critical minerals advisory board designated rare earth deposits as national priority extraction targets in a 2022 report, but that designation does not override environmental permitting authority.

Waters acknowledged the strategic importance of domestic rare earth supplies but said the agency's mandate was environmental protection, not supply chain policy.

A related federal bill that would streamline rare earth permitting on federal lands has passed the Senate but has not advanced in the House.
