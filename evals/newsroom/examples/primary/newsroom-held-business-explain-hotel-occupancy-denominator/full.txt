**Occupancy rose, but Wren Coast hotels sold fewer rooms and earned less revenue**

Five hotels that report to the Wren Coast Lodging Association posted a higher occupancy rate in April 2026 than a year earlier, even as they sold fewer room nights and took in less room revenue, according to the association's August 2026 report. The explanation lies in the report's denominator: available room nights fell faster than the number the hotels actually sold.

The five hotels offered 3,000 available room nights in April 2025 and sold 2,400 of them, an occupancy rate of 80%. Renovations took 20 rooms out of service for all 30 days of April 2026, cutting available room nights to 2,400 — a drop of 600, or 20%. The hotels sold 2,040 room nights that month, a drop of 360, or 15%, from the year before. Because sold room nights fell by a smaller percentage than available room nights did, the occupancy rate rose five percentage points, to 85%.

Room revenue moved the opposite direction. The average daily rate across the five hotels — room revenue divided by room nights sold — rose from $100 in April 2025 to $110 in April 2026, the report said. That 10% rate increase did not make up for the 15% drop in room nights sold. Total room revenue fell from $240,000 to $224,400, a decrease of 6.5%.

A separate figure, revenue per available room night, rose from $80 to $93.50. The report attributed part of that increase to the shrunken pool of available rooms rather than to stronger business; with fewer rooms to divide revenue across, the same or lower total revenue produces a higher figure per available room.

Association analyst Ceri Venn said occupancy percentages can describe fuller remaining capacity without describing more total business. "A smaller hotel can look fuller on the percentage line while its reception desk sells fewer nights," Venn said.

The report cautioned against reading its average rate as a uniform price increase. The renovations affected room types unevenly, so the 10% rise in the average daily rate does not establish that every room's listed price went up by that amount, the report said. It also does not isolate how much of the change came from renovations, demand, prices or which rooms were sold, as opposed to some combination of the four.

The figures describe room nights, not people. A room can hold more than one guest, and a single stay can span several nights, so the report's numbers do not translate directly into a visitor count. Restaurant owner Tavin Holt said hotel guests are one source of his customers, alongside local residents and day visitors. "I cannot turn a hotel percentage into a count of people coming through my door," Holt said.

The revenue totals in the report cover rooms only, excluding food, parking and event income, and do not measure profit.

The report's five hotels are voluntary reporting members of the association, not a complete accounting of coastal lodging; short-term rentals and campsites are not included. Two of the five occupy converted boardinghouses, and three were purpose-built, though the report did not attribute the occupancy change to that mix.

The association said it expects the renovated rooms to return to service this autumn but has not published confirmed reopening dates.
