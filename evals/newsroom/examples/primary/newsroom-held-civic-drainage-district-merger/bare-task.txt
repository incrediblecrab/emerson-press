# County approves East Fen, Lower Rill drainage district merger

The county has approved a merger of the East Fen and Lower Rill drainage districts effective Jan. 1, 2027, bringing their maintenance operations under one administrative board while leaving existing assessment schedules in place for the first year.

The districts now maintain adjoining but separately managed channels and pumping equipment. The merger changes how that work is administered, but it does not physically connect every channel or immediately change the equipment serving a particular neighborhood. No new pump purchase or guarantee of reduced flooding accompanies the approval.

A joint maintenance survey found that work near the districts’ shared boundary was sometimes deferred while they clarified which district was responsible. The survey documented those administrative delays but did not attribute a measured amount of flood damage to them.

“One board can settle a boundary question faster, but the water will still follow the ground we have,” county infrastructure director Halen Sore said.

East Fen’s current operating budget is $230,000, and Lower Rill’s is $170,000, for a total of $400,000 before transition expenses. That total is not a final budget for the merged district: The county has not adopted a combined budget or forecast a specific reduction in household assessments.

Existing assessment schedules will remain in place during the first year as the new board reviews costs and service boundaries. The approval therefore does not establish lower household charges when the merger takes effect.

An interim board will include three representatives from each existing district and an independent chair for the transition year. The merger agreement leaves arrangements for permanent representation to a later county decision.

Maintenance employees will transfer to the combined district without a reduction in posts under the approved transition plan. Both existing district offices will initially remain open. A later decision about office accommodation is not part of the approved merger package.

A residents group in the lower-lying district has asked for published work priorities so that its needs remain visible within the larger administration.

“A single letterhead will help only if we can still see which jobs are waiting and why,” said Mera Clove, the group’s secretary.

The new district will publish a consolidated list of maintenance projects and unresolved responsibility questions. That list will bring work from both districts into one record while identifying questions about responsibility that still need to be settled.

Other transition work includes reconciling the districts’ different channel-numbering systems. Staff will cross-reference the numbers rather than erase them from old records. The transition plan also requires an inventory of equipment condition before the board considers shared replacement purchases.

Emergency reporting numbers will remain active during the transition, with calls routed to the same duty team. The merger does not replace the county’s separate emergency-management responsibilities.

The approved changes thus establish a common administration and a process for reviewing maintenance needs, while decisions about the final combined budget, permanent board representation and future office accommodation remain unresolved.
