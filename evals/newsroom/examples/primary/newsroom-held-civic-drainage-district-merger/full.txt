# County approves East Fen-Lower Rill drainage merger for 2027

The county has approved a merger of the East Fen and Lower Rill drainage districts effective Jan. 1, 2027, putting their adjoining channels and pumping equipment under one administrative board.

Existing assessment schedules will remain in place for the first year while the new board reviews costs and service boundaries. The county has not adopted a final combined budget or forecast a specific reduction in household assessments.

The approval changes how the districts are managed but does not physically connect every channel or immediately change the equipment serving a particular neighborhood. No new pump purchase or guarantee of reduced flooding accompanies the merger.

A joint maintenance survey found that work near the districts' shared boundary was sometimes deferred while they clarified which district was responsible. The survey records those administrative delays but does not attribute a measured amount of flood damage to them.

"One board can settle a boundary question faster, but the water will still follow the ground we have," county infrastructure director Halen Sore said.

East Fen's current operating budget is $230,000 and Lower Rill's is $170,000. Together, those budgets total $400,000 before transition expenses. That total reflects the districts' current operating budgets, not a final spending plan for the combined district.

An interim board will include three representatives from each existing district and an independent chair for the transition year. The merger agreement leaves the arrangements for permanent representation to a later county decision.

Maintenance employees will transfer to the combined district without a reduction in posts under the approved transition plan. District offices will initially remain open at both existing locations. A later decision about office accommodation is outside the approved merger package.

A residents group in the lower-lying district has asked for published work priorities so that its needs remain visible within the larger administration.

"A single letterhead will help only if we can still see which jobs are waiting and why," said Mera Clove, the group's secretary.

The new district will publish a consolidated list of maintenance projects and unresolved questions about responsibility. The list will bring those items together, while the board's review of costs and service boundaries proceeds during the first year.

The transition plan also requires an inventory of equipment condition before the board considers shared replacement purchases. Although both districts currently maintain pumping equipment, the administrative approval does not include a new pump purchase.

Staff will cross-reference the districts' different channel-numbering systems rather than erase the existing numbers from old records.

Emergency reporting numbers will remain active during the transition, with calls routed to the same duty team. The merger does not replace the county's separate emergency-management responsibilities.
