**What 'below-market' means in Stoneharbor's new rental program**

STONEHARBOR — City officials this month began enforcing a new rental program that caps participating apartments at 80% of an annually published reference rent, but the label does not mean every tenant will pay a fixed share of income.

The program's benchmark applies to a defined type of home, not to each household's actual earnings. In an illustration the city has circulated, a $1,000 monthly reference rent produces an $800 rent for a participating unit. For a hypothetical household earning $2,000 a month before taxes, that $800 alone would equal 40% of its gross income. City officials say the example is instructional only; it does not disclose any real tenant's income and does not establish what a typical household in the program will actually pay as a share of earnings.

The comparison also leaves out utilities and any separately listed service charges, which are not included when the city measures a unit's rent against the benchmark.

The first agreement under the program covers 70 apartments spread across three existing buildings, which have different layouts and service-charge arrangements. The units remain under their private owners' management and are not becoming city-owned public housing. The agreement does not apply to all new rental housing in Stoneharbor.

Housing director Vera Dall said the discount figure alone does not describe affordability. "The label needs its number beside it," Dall said. "A reduction from a benchmark does not tell us what every household can manage."

Applicants must meet the program's published eligibility conditions, but the city has not released a universal income cutoff, and meeting the conditions does not guarantee an offer of housing.

A housing advice group has asked the city to list total expected housing charges in its advertising, not just the discounted base rent. "People need the amount they will actually have to find each month, including the lines below the headline rent," said advice worker Perrin Moss.

The rent restriction in the initial agreements runs for five years; extending it requires a further agreement between the city and a participating owner. City officials have not promised that every property will stay in the program indefinitely. The benchmark itself is revised annually, which can change the rent cap, though individual lease terms and notice requirements still apply as set out in the program's documents. The city said it plans to publish the source of the benchmark and the date it applies to, so applicants can compare figures from the same period.

The program does not give tenants a right to buy their apartments. The advice group also said the rental program is distinct from the city's separate emergency accommodation and direct rent-assistance programs.

The city said it will check rent schedules submitted by participating landlords against the program's cap to confirm compliance.

As of this month, the city has not reported occupancy results, average incomes among participating tenants or any effect on citywide rents from the new agreement.

City officials also cautioned that the discount definition is specific to Stoneharbor's program and does not establish a general meaning of "affordable housing" beyond it.
