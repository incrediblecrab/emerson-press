Vesper Quay Terminal to End Routine Sunday Loading Amid Smaller Autumn Order Book

VESPER QUAY — The Vesper Quay Grain Terminal said Monday it will stop routine Sunday loading in October, as a smaller autumn order book prompts a leaner seasonal staffing plan that will shorten contracts for a dozen workers.

The terminal has signed autumn export orders for 180,000 tons so far, down 25% from the 240,000 tons booked at the same point last year, according to terminal figures. The 60,000-ton gap reflects booked orders at this point in the season, not a measure of the region's overall grain output.

About two-thirds of that decline traces to a single customer. Pelden Grain Trading, which had booked 40,000 tons for autumn last year, canceled its order after changing its storage arrangements, terminal officials said. That accounts for 40,000 tons of the 60,000-ton drop; the terminal has not explained the remaining 20,000 tons.

The staffing changes are less severe than the drop in bookings. The new seasonal roster allocates 4,800 paid hours, a 20% reduction from last autumn's 6,000-hour roster. Operations director Mara Keld said the hours cut is smaller than the volume decline because inspection and maintenance work continues regardless of loading volume.

"We are matching the loading roster to signed ships, not predicting the size of the harvest," Keld said.

Nineteen permanent jobs will remain unchanged. But 12 seasonal employees will see shorter contracts this year. The terminal has offered those workers first consideration for unscheduled vessel work, though it is not guaranteeing additional hours.

The reduced schedule already has consequences for workers who plan their year around seasonal terminal work. Evin Moss, a seasonal loader, also works at a winter warehouse but does not begin that job until November.

"A shorter season leaves a space in the calendar that the next job does not automatically fill," Moss said.

For growers, the immediate effects are limited. Weekday truck-receiving hours will remain unchanged, and farmers with existing delivery contracts may still deliver their contracted quantities. The terminal has not announced a new farmgate price for the coming season.

Lora Flint, chair of the local grower association, said the impact will vary by farm. Some members can store grain themselves and wait out any bottlenecks, she said, while others depend on prompt delivery slots and could be more exposed if scheduling tightens later in the season.

The terminal handles grain from 63 contracted farms and also stores grain for two food manufacturers under separate contracts; that storage business is not included in the 180,000-ton export figure.

The port authority said Monday it has not changed the terminal's berth allocation or handling charges in response to the reduced booking volume.

The terminal, whose oldest silo dates to 1981 alongside a covered conveyor that opened in 2018, has not released a regional harvest estimate, and there is no indication the drop in bookings is linked to broader trade policy shifts.

Terminal officials said they will continue evaluating the roster as additional autumn orders are booked, but offered no timeline for when a fuller picture of the season's volume might emerge.
