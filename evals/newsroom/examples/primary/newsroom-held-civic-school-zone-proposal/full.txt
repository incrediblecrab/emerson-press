Merrowfield weighs new school boundary lines, with sibling question still unresolved

An explainer on what the proposal would and wouldn't change for families

MERROWFIELD — The Merrowfield School Board has published a proposed attendance-zone map for new enrollments in the 2027-28 school year, opening a public comment period on changes that would shift some future students from North Meadow School toward River Step School.

Here is what the proposal covers, and what it does not yet settle.

**What's being proposed**

The map is a draft for public comment, not an adopted assignment notice for individual children. Under the proposal, currently enrolled pupils could remain at their present schools; the draft does not require every household in a changed block to move schools.

The plan would shift several future-enrollment blocks from North Meadow toward River Step. District planning officer Dena Orr said the boundaries were drawn with walking routes in mind, not just proximity on a map.

"We are drawing a route to a school, not just a line around a number of houses," Orr said.

The district says a short distance on the map can translate into a longer walk if a household has to reach the nearest bridge over the river, which has limited crossing points. Older printed maps show a former footbridge that is no longer part of the designated school walking route.

**Why the district says change is needed**

North Meadow currently has 398 pupils against a planning capacity of 360. River Step has 332 pupils against a planning capacity of 400. The district cautions that whole-school totals do not show every year group's available places or the suitability of particular rooms, and that the capacity figures reflect current room-use assumptions rather than a guarantee that every spare place fits every grade.

One housing development included in the district's projections has planning approval but no confirmed occupancy schedule. The district said it will revise its projected pupil totals if that development's timetable changes before the board adopts a final plan.

The district has not proposed closing either school or selling either site. The figures in the proposal concern only these two schools and this planning exercise, not a broader measure of educational quality.

**What hasn't been decided**

The proposed treatment of younger siblings of currently enrolled students has not been settled and is listed as a question for the consultation period. Lora Senn, a parent group representative, said families need that question answered before the map is treated as final.

"Please answer the sibling question before asking families to treat the map as a settled plan," Senn said.

The draft boundary document contains no final transport entitlement for any particular address. Any revised transport arrangements will be published with the final adopted enrollment policy, the district said, rather than inferred from the draft map.

The board has not taken a final vote on the proposal and has not announced implementation instructions for current pupils.

**How to weigh in**

The district will accept comments identifying route barriers, anticipated housing changes, and family circumstances not represented in the draft data. Officials said the comments will inform a revised proposal to bring back to the board, rather than function as a household-by-household referendum on individual boundary segments.

Public sessions are scheduled at both North Meadow and River Step schools. Written comments will be accepted from residents unable to attend in person. The district had not, as of Sept. 18, announced the comment deadline or the date of a revised proposal.
