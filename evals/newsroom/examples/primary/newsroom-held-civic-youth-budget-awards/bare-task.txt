# Greywillow approves $40,000 for first youth-budget panel’s picks

Greywillow Council has approved $40,000 for skate-area seating, shared rehearsal equipment and a community garden water connection, ratifying the recommendations of the city’s first youth-budget panel.

The council’s Sept. 14 decision allocates $18,000 for the seating, $12,000 for rehearsal equipment and $10,000 for the water connection. The 18-member panel recommended the combined package on a 15-3 vote after considering 11 eligible proposals.

The approval sets the projects’ funding allocations, but delivery work remains. The city has not announced installation dates for all three projects or said any is already operating. The garden connection also requires a final utility estimate before work can be ordered.

“We had to decide what could be shared, not just what each of us wanted for our own group,” the panel’s youth chair said in a supplied statement.

The skate-area award will provide seating that includes spaces for people watching rather than taking part. It does not fund a rebuilding of the skating surface.

The rehearsal equipment will be stored and booked through the youth center, rather than transferred permanently to one band. The center has agreed to handle bookings and routine equipment checks within its existing program.

For the community garden, the final utility estimate will determine whether the water connection can proceed within its $10,000 allocation. If the estimate exceeds that amount, the proposal must return for a funding decision. It cannot automatically draw money from the seating or rehearsal equipment awards.

Youth-services director Amra Vale said the vote was one step in getting the projects delivered.

“The panel made real choices, and the city still has to carry out the unglamorous work of buying and installing things,” Vale said.

The council retained responsibility for authorizing spending and checking whether each proposal could be delivered on city-controlled sites. The grant is capital funding and does not pay for a permanent new youth-worker position.

Staff screened proposals for compliance with the program’s budget and location rules before the panel considered them. Some panel members wanted a transport project, but a transport submission was excluded because its funding request exceeded the entire $40,000 budget.

Staff will publish reasons for eligibility decisions so rejected applicants can distinguish exclusions based on program rules from choices reflecting the panel’s preferences.

Panel members were recruited through schools and youth organizations, not elected in a citywide vote. The city paid their travel costs, and members were not required to donate money to any selected project. Meetings rotated between the youth center and a library meeting room to avoid making one school the permanent host.

Members will be invited to review procurement updates and inspect completed projects with city staff, giving them a role in following the awards through delivery.

The council will decide whether to repeat the program after reviewing project delivery and who took part in the first panel. The funding decision is not accompanied by a citywide measure of young residents’ preferences or evidence of long-term participation outcomes.
