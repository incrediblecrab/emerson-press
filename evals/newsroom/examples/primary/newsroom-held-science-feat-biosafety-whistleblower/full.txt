Whistleblower's Complaint Puts Biosafety Lab's Inspection Records Under Federal Review

Dr. Rosa Mensah has not worked in biocontainment since October 2023, the month she was placed on administrative leave, two months after she filed a complaint accusing colleagues at Northgate Biocontainment Research Center of logging inspections of critical safety equipment as complete when the personnel who signed off were not qualified to conduct them.

"I tried to raise these internally three times," Mensah said. "Each time I was told it was an administrative matter and would be addressed. It wasn't addressed. That's when I went to the board."

Northgate, a biosafety level 4 facility, has been under internal review for 18 months following Mensah's August 2023 complaint, which was reviewed by this reporter. It described 14 instances between 2021 and 2023 in which containment equipment inspections were logged by staff Mensah said lacked the qualifications for those specific checks.

The facility conducted its own review and cleared all 14 instances. Mensah said that review was carried out by personnel who reported to the same division head she had reported to, raising questions about its independence. Facility records reviewed by the reporter show none of the 14 instances resulted in a confirmed containment failure.

Biosafety experts not affiliated with the case said that absence of a confirmed failure does not resolve the underlying concern. Dr. Adaora Nwachukwu of the Pacific Biosafety Institute said BSL-4 safety depends on a documented chain of verified steps, not on the absence of an incident. "Missing links in that chain are not trivial even if nothing bad happened yet," she said. Nwachukwu, who was not involved in the Northgate case, said the risk in incomplete inspection logging is that it disables the surveillance system meant to catch early failures before they become confirmed ones.

Northgate said the October 2023 leave was unrelated to Mensah's complaint, saying she had been considered for reassignment before she filed it. Mensah filed a federal whistleblower complaint in December 2023; the case remains open.

The facility's director, Dr. Pieter Halvorsen, declined interview requests. A statement from the facility said it takes biosafety compliance seriously and cooperates fully with regulatory reviews.

The National Biocontainment Safety Board confirmed it is reviewing Northgate's inspection documentation practices but declined to characterize the status of that review. Northgate's operating license comes up for renewal in 2027, and the outcome of the regulatory review could affect that process.

Three former Northgate employees who spoke on background said biosafety concerns were often discussed informally at the facility but that there was cultural resistance to raising them through formal complaint channels. Nwachukwu said BSL-4 facilities are often risk-averse about public disclosure, a posture that can produce the same reluctance internally, discouraging staff from putting concerns on the record.

Federal law requires BSL-4 facilities to keep inspection records accessible to auditors, but it does not specify how disputes over inspectors' qualifications should be resolved. The whistleblower statute covering research facilities protects against retaliation but does not provide for reinstatement while a complaint is under investigation, according to Mensah's lawyer, who said that gap leaves complainants without income during a review that can take months or years.

Mensah said she does not regret filing the complaint, though she acknowledged its cost. She was unable to obtain a reference from Northgate for subsequent job applications and has accepted a position at a university laboratory outside biosafety work, a change she called voluntary but not entirely freely chosen.

"I knew what I was doing," she said. "The question is whether the system I was protecting is protecting me. The answer is taking a long time to arrive."

Mensah said she wants people in similar positions to know the formal complaint process exists, even when it is slow. "Not because it is easy or fast," she said. "Because it is the only thing that creates a record."
