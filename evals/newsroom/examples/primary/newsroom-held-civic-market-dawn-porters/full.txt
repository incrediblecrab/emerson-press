# Before Harrow Quay opens, five porters get the market moving

At 4:45 a.m., the first vegetable truck reaches Harrow Quay's loading edge. Crew leader Daro Venn checks the chalked pitch numbers that tell the porters where deliveries belong. Ahead lies the work of moving the produce from the truck's arrival point to the traders' stalls.

Fruit trader Selma Rill lays out empty trays while her delivered crates wait at the marked unloading point. Preparing a stall and getting its stock across the market are separate jobs, carried out on the same morning schedule.

The link between them is a five-person paid porter crew. Employed through the traders' joint service agreement, the porters move deliveries within the market before customers enter. Traders have retained the shared service for the coming season rather than shifting all unloading responsibility to individual stallholders.

"If I spend the first hour chasing boxes, I have not spent it getting ready for the people who buy from me," Rill said.

Under the current arrangement, routine vehicle unloading ends at 7:30 a.m., ahead of the market's 9 a.m. public opening. The porters use wheeled carts through lanes that must be clear of delivery vehicles before customers arrive.

Those carts and lanes are shared. A late truck can affect several stalls, rather than just the trader awaiting its load, because the crew does not operate as five independent delivery services. Breaks follow the delivery sequence, too. The crew keeps a kettle in a small room beside the cart store.

Knowing the route takes more than reading a number. Older pitch names remain in everyday speech even where the current market plan uses numbered positions. New porters spend their first shifts alongside an experienced worker, learning the pitch locations and the routine for sharing the lanes.

The equipment has its own signals.

"You learn the sound of the carts. A loose wheel announces itself before the trader comes looking for it," Venn said.

The crew records damaged carts and broken paving through the market manager's maintenance log. The manager has also asked suppliers to give notice of unusual loads: A standard cart is not suitable for every delivery.

Traders contribute to the service through the market's published operating charge. The agreement covers movement within the market, not the condition of produce before it arrives or every trader's road transport. Its shared responsibility begins with the work of getting deliveries across the market.

A planned paving repair will require a temporary unloading arrangement, though the manager has not announced any change in public opening hours. The lanes also serve another purpose after closing, when waste removal takes place under a separately timed part of the daily operation.

For now, the morning's task is to prepare those lanes for customers. Stacks of closed crates gradually give way to signs, price cards and open aisles. At Rill's pitch, the empty trays are laid out, ready for the fruit.
