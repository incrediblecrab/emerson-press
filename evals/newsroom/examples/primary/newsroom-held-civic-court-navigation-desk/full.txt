# Harven County court to open free navigation desk Nov. 3

Harven County court administration will open a free public navigation desk Nov. 3 to help visitors find rooms, understand appointment arrangements and locate the court's existing public forms.

The desk will be in the court building's main entrance hall and available without an appointment during public counter hours. Staff will provide practical guidance but will not represent visitors, select a legal argument or predict how a judge will decide a case.

The first-year allocation of $82,000 will pay for two part-time staff members, training and revised building signs. It does not establish a legal-aid fund or cover visitors' court fees. Ordinary filing and hearing procedures will remain unchanged.

“Finding the right room should not depend on having been through the building before,” court administrator Edda Voss said.

The project followed staff reports of visitors joining the wrong queues and missing check-in instructions posted in separate corridors. Those examples were gathered during planning; they are not a measured rate of missed hearings or incorrect filings.

The revised signs will use the same room names as appointment letters, replacing several older departmental labels still displayed in the building. The signs and desk are intended to address confusion about where visitors need to go, rather than change how cases are handled.

A local advice partnership will train desk staff on when to refer a visitor to an independent legal adviser. The partnership will remain a separate organization, and its advisers will not automatically be available to everyone who visits the desk.

“Directions can remove one obstacle, but they cannot stand in for advice about a person's choices,” advice partnership coordinator Rana Pell said.

That distinction limits what visitors can expect from the free service: Staff can explain where to obtain an existing public form, but they cannot choose a legal argument for the person requesting it. A referral also does not guarantee that an adviser will be available.

For visitors who need accessible information, the desk will offer printed material in large type and arrange use of the court's existing language-assistance booking process. The administration has not promised an interpreter in every language immediately at the desk.

Visitors discussing private details can ask to use a side counter away from the main waiting line. Navigation staff will not retain copies of visitors' case papers as part of their inquiry log.

The log will count the kinds of practical inquiries received so the administration can identify confusing signs or instructions. A public review after six months will consider the service's hours and recurring navigation problems. The opening announcement includes no evidence of changed case outcomes or shorter court processing times.

The desk will replace a freestanding rack that previously held mixed forms and leaflets in the entrance hall. The hall's wooden benches will remain.
