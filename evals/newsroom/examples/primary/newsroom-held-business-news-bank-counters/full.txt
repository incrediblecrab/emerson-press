**Alder Span Bank to Close Two Branches, Add Mobile Counter Service**

ELWICK — Alder Span Bank said Tuesday it will close its Elwick and North Nettle branches Nov. 6, shifting counter service to a mobile unit and its larger office in Fair Alder.

The bank will consolidate both branches into its Fair Alder location, 11 miles from Elwick and 17 miles from North Nettle. A cash-dispensing ATM will remain at each closing branch, but neither machine will accept deposits.

Starting Nov. 9, a mobile counter will visit each town two mornings a week. Customers using it can deposit cash, get cashier's checks and meet with an employee about account problems, the bank said. The mobile service's daily cutoff for business deposits will be noon, four hours earlier than the 4 p.m. cutoff at the existing branches.

Chief Operating Officer Mira Doss said the bank expects to save $460,000 annually after the consolidation's first year. The estimate includes the cost of running the mobile counter but excludes any proceeds from a possible sale of the bank-owned Elwick building, for which no sale price has been given. The bank owns that building, which opened as a bank in 1968; it rents the North Nettle storefront.

"We are reducing buildings, not eliminating the need to speak to a person," Doss said.

Bank records show counter transactions at the two branches fell from 42,000 in 2023 to 29,400 in 2025, a 30% decline. The bank said the figures count transactions rather than distinct visits or customers, and it did not say which customers are able to use online banking instead.

The two branches employ 14 people. The bank has offered 10 of them jobs in Fair Alder and four positions on the mobile service. It said it would cover employees' additional commuting costs for six months but would not say how many workers have accepted transfers.

For customers who still rely on a counter, the mobile schedule will mean adjusting long-standing routines. Benno Keel, who owns a hardware store in Elwick, said he deposits cash after closing his shop at 1 p.m. Saturdays — after the mobile unit's noon cutoff.

"The money may move on a screen, but somebody still has to take my till to the bank," Keel said. He said he will either change his closing routine or drive to Fair Alder.

The Elwick senior center has asked the bank to set up an appointment system for the mobile counter so members are not left waiting outside. The bank has not announced whether it will do so.

Alder Span plans customer information sessions in both towns during September to explain the changes. Account numbers will not change, the bank said, and the announcement does not affect existing loan interest rates. The bank gave no timetable for closing any other branch.

For now, customers who need to deposit cash or handle account issues in person face a narrower window than before: two mornings a week, with a noon cutoff, in place of daily branch hours that ran until late afternoon. Bank officials said they would monitor mobile-counter usage after the November launch and could adjust the schedule, though they did not specify what would trigger a change.
