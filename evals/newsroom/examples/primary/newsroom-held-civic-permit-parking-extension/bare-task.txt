# Reedharbor to expand residential parking zone to 14 blocks Nov. 9

Reedharbor Council will more than double the size of its residential parking zone Nov. 9, adding eight blocks while keeping the annual resident permit fee at $35.

The extension will bring the existing six-block zone to 14 blocks after signs and curb markings are installed. The permit requirement will apply from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays, not around the clock.

Residents petitioned for the extension after a nearby office complex expanded its occupied floor area without a corresponding change to the city's parking-zone boundary. The decision changes who may use eligible curb spaces during permit hours but does not add parking capacity.

The added area contains 176 ordinary curb spaces, according to the city's marking plan. The city has not supplied a count showing how many of those spaces are occupied by residents rather than commuters on a typical day.

“The permit changes who can use a space at certain times; it does not create a space,” transport manager Reva Linn said.

A resident permit allows a driver to use eligible spaces within the zone. It does not reserve a particular space or guarantee that parking will be available.

Eligible households on the newly included blocks may begin applying Oct. 12 through the city's existing address-verification process. Guest permits will remain available under the current visitor scheme, but that scheme has not been expanded to cover all-day commuter parking.

Loading bays and marked accessible spaces will keep their existing arrangements. They will not become ordinary permit spaces as part of the extension.

The change has also prompted questions about where employees who park in the area might go. A group of nearby shop workers asked the city to examine where displaced employee parking could move.

“A boundary can solve a problem on one side and move it to the next street,” shop worker representative Dena Wren said.

The council will survey streets immediately outside the expanded zone before and after implementation. That work is still planned, and the city has not concluded whether parking displacement will occur. The approved package includes neither a new off-street parking facility nor an employee shuttle.

Under the rollout plan, drivers will receive warning notices during the first 30 calendar days of the extension before routine penalty notices begin. The warning period does not suspend existing restrictions on blocking entrances or on the use of loading bays and accessible spaces.

Street signs will have a different border color for the zone while retaining the existing permit symbol. The city budget includes $28,400 for signs, road markings and processing the first round of new applications.

The council has scheduled a six-month review of the expanded zone. That review can consider boundary changes, but it does not automatically extend the zone to additional streets.
