Rivermoth Library Ends Overdue Fines, Erases $15,200 in Balances — But Books Still Must Come Back

RIVERMOTH — Rivermoth Library's board voted unanimously Monday to stop charging daily fines on overdue books starting Oct. 1 and to erase existing book-overdue fines already on the ledger, a balance that totals $15,200.

Library officials stressed the change does not touch every kind of charge, and it does not remove the obligation to return borrowed materials. Charges for lost or damaged items, printing and equipment rental will not be erased. Reminders will still be sent to borrowers before an item is classified as lost, and under the library's unchanged lost-item policy, a book still unreturned 35 calendar days after its due date becomes subject to a replacement charge. Returning an undamaged book removes that book's replacement charge, but any other charges on the account remain.

About 620 accounts are currently blocked solely by book-overdue fines, and those would regain borrowing access once the fines are erased — provided no other block applies to the account. Circulation manager Leda Sorn said other blocked accounts may still owe replacement charges or have long-overdue items outstanding, so not everyone with a blocked account will automatically be able to borrow again.

The financial picture is mixed. Last year's overdue-fine receipts totaled $9,400, and a staff-time review estimated the labor used to administer those fines at about $18,000. Library officials said that $18,000 figure is not cash the library will actually receive after the change; the staff time will instead be reassigned to other circulation work, not banked as savings. The library's budget assumes the loss of the $9,400 in fine receipts going forward, and officials said the $15,200 in erased ledger balances is not being counted as a new cash expense, since it was never collected.

"We want the book back on the shelf, not a small debt that keeps its borrower outside," Sorn said.

Some patrons welcomed the change. Neri Wren, a parent whose family has borrowed from the library, said a past overdue balance had made her reluctant to ask staff about another missing book. "I can come in with the book instead of coming in ready to explain the bill," Wren said.

The library has not yet measured how many of the newly restored accounts will actually resume borrowing after Oct. 1. Board member Torin Vail asked for quarterly reporting on return times and missing-item rates going forward, and the board approved that reporting request, though it did not set any target for increasing circulation as a result of the policy.

The policy applies at all four Rivermoth branches and the mobile library, but not to materials borrowed through a separate university library system, officials said.

The change is broader than previous efforts. Rivermoth Library has waived children's book fines during occasional summer-return campaigns since 2013, but those were time-limited. Monday's vote establishes a continuing policy rather than another temporary campaign, meaning the elimination of daily overdue fines will remain in place beyond this year rather than expiring at a set date.

Library officials said borrowers with blocked accounts can check their status now, but urged patrons who still have books out to return them promptly rather than wait for the Oct. 1 change, since the 35-day clock toward replacement charges continues to run regardless of the fine policy.
