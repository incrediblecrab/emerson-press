**Rivermoth Library board votes to end overdue book fines, erase $15,200 in existing charges**

RIVERMOTH — The Rivermoth Library board voted unanimously Monday to eliminate daily overdue fines on borrowed books starting Oct. 1 and to erase $15,200 in existing book-overdue fines currently on the library's account ledger.

Charges for lost or damaged materials, printing and equipment rental will not be erased under the new policy, and borrowers must still return books they have checked out. The library will continue sending reminders before an item is classified as lost.

About 620 accounts are blocked solely by book-overdue fines in the current ledger, and those accounts would regain borrowing access once the fines are erased, provided no other block applies to them, according to library records. Other blocked accounts may still owe replacement charges or have long-overdue items, circulation manager Leda Sorn said.

The library's lost-item policy is unchanged: a book still unreturned 35 calendar days after its due date becomes subject to a replacement charge. Returning an undamaged book removes that book's replacement charge, but unrelated charges on an account remain.

"We want the book back on the shelf, not a small debt that keeps its borrower outside," Sorn said.

Neri Wren, a parent who uses the library, said a past overdue balance had made her reluctant to ask staff about another missing book.

"I can come in with the book instead of coming in ready to explain the bill," Wren said.

Last year's overdue-fine receipts totaled $9,400. A staff-time review valued the labor spent administering those fines at an estimated $18,000, but that figure is not cash the library will receive after the change. Staff who handled fine collection will be reassigned to other circulation work, Sorn said. The library's budget assumes the loss of the $9,400 in annual fine receipts and does not count the erased ledger balances as a new cash expense.

The policy applies to all four Rivermoth Library branches and its mobile library, but not to materials borrowed through a separate university library system.

Board member Torin Vail asked for quarterly reporting on return times and missing-item rates. The board approved that reporting request but did not set a target for increased circulation.

The library has not measured how many of the roughly 620 restored accounts will resume borrowing after Oct. 1.

Rivermoth Library has waived children's book fines during occasional summer-return campaigns since 2013. Library officials said Monday's vote establishes a continuing policy rather than another time-limited summer campaign.
