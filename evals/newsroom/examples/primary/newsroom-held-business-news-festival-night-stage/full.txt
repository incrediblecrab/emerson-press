# Marrow Quay festival cancels evening stage; refunds to begin Aug. 17

The Marrow Quay Lantern Festival canceled its ticketed evening stage Friday, Aug. 14, three weeks before the event, after a revised production quote rose $34,000 above its provisional estimate.

Free daytime workshops and the lantern walk remain scheduled for Sept. 4-6. The festival says it will automatically refund evening ticket face values and its booking fees to the original payment method, with refunds scheduled to begin Aug. 17 and be completed by Aug. 28.

Vendors have until Aug. 21 to cancel without losing their pitch deposits, but the festival will not reimburse food purchases or travel. Nine of the 22 food vendors booked for the full weekend had planned to operate mainly during the evening concerts.

Festival accounts show 2,400 evening tickets sold at a single face price of $25, producing $60,000 in face-value receipts. Booking fees will be refunded in addition to that amount.

The production contractor's revised quote for the stage, lighting and power was $112,000, up from a provisional $78,000. Festival treasurer Una Vale said the earlier quote had not been a fixed-price contract.

Vale said the board chose cancellation rather than taking a short-term loan it could not repay from current ticket sales. The revised production quote alone was $52,000 more than the evening ticket face-value receipts.

A $40,000 municipal grant cannot cover the evening stage under its award terms, which restrict the money to free daytime activities. The grant therefore does not provide money the board can shift to the canceled concerts under those terms.

The festival has already paid $19,000 in nonrefundable planning and artist deposits associated with the stage. Representatives of the four musical acts contracted to perform are discussing cancellation payments beyond deposits with the festival.

Those discussions leave part of the financial outcome unsettled. The supplied accounts do not establish a final festival-wide loss because artist settlements and vendor cancellations remain unresolved. The increase in the production quote is not a measure of that loss.

For food-stall owner Ceri Holt, the option to recover a pitch deposit does not cover all the work and spending already committed to the weekend. Holt has ordered frozen ingredients that can be used at later events but has already paid for festival signage.

"Getting the pitch money back is not the same as getting the whole week of preparation back," Holt said.

The remaining daytime program uses existing indoor rooms and does not require the canceled temporary stage.

"Keeping the free program open is a smaller promise, but it is one we can still make," festival director Rian Quell said.

The board has not approved an evening replacement event or a date for next year's festival. The festival began as a school lantern display in 2009 and added paid concerts in 2018.
