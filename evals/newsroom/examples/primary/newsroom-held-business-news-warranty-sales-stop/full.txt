**Hearthline Appliances to stop selling Extra Years service plan after Sept. 30**

Hearthline Appliances said Tuesday it will stop selling its Extra Years extended service plan after Sept. 30, though the roughly 14,000 contracts already in force will keep running under their current terms.

The retailer, which operates 14 stores, will keep selling appliances, parts and separately priced repair visits. Chief Commercial Officer Nessa Flint said the change affects only new plan sales, not appliances or existing coverage.

"Stopping new sales does not move an existing repair request to the back of a different queue," Flint said.

Hearthline's account records show about 14,000 active Extra Years contracts, a figure that does not represent 14,000 separate households because some customers hold plans covering multiple appliances, the company said.

Those contracts will continue to be administered by Cinder Service Network, the plan's existing provider, until each contract's stated expiration date. Hearthline said the sales change does not alter the written manufacturer warranties that come with its appliances.

Hearthline said its renewed agreement with Cinder raised the one-time administration charge on each newly sold plan from $22 to $31. The company described the increase as a cost to the retailer, not a new charge passed to people who already hold plans. Hearthline has not disclosed the average retail price of the plan or its profit margin on plan sales, so the fee increase alone does not show how the change affects the company's overall earnings from the product.

Flint said sales staff will stop earning commissions on new service-plan sales after the Sept. 30 cutoff. Staff will receive a temporary three-month commission supplement, though the amount will vary based on each employee's prior sales, she said.

For customers with active contracts, Cinder's claims process starts with a telephone assessment before an in-home appointment is scheduled. According to Cinder's July 2026 report, the median time from a completed telephone assessment to the first home visit was eight calendar days among visits completed that month in this service region. The company said that figure excludes visits still pending and any later parts orders, and it does not represent a maximum wait time or the time needed to finish a repair.

Dane Voss, who holds a refrigerator plan that runs through 2028, said he initially thought the announcement meant his coverage was ending.

"I wanted to know who would answer the phone, not whether they would sell me another leaflet," Voss said.

Hearthline said it plans to mail active contract holders a notice listing their service provider and contract expiration date. The company said no blanket cancellation refunds or automatic plan extensions are part of the announced change.

Hearthline began selling service plans in 2014, after previously offering appliance repairs only on a per-visit basis, according to the company.

The reporting record for this story does not include any regulatory assessment of the value of Hearthline's service plans or any finding on whether all claims under the plans have been handled correctly.
