# Bellharrow civic center to open multilingual help desk Oct. 19

BELLHARROW — A multilingual navigation desk opening Oct. 19 at Bellharrow’s civic center will help residents find the right city office, understand which documents they need and arrange appointments.

The desk, beside the main reception area, will initially offer service in English, Spanish and Somali. Interpreters will work on a schedule, so residents should not expect every language to be available at every hour. A weekly language schedule will be displayed at reception and available by telephone.

Residents may visit without an appointment, though a specialist interpreter may need to be booked for a later conversation.

Service manager Arla Deyn said the desk is intended to address questions about where residents should take their inquiries.

“The first question is often not how to complete a form, but which counter should receive it,” Deyn said.

The city approved two coordinator positions and $48,000 for contracted interpreting during the first year. The coordinators’ salaries are funded separately through the civic center staffing budget and are not included in the interpreting allocation.

The service will focus on helping residents navigate city processes, not deciding their cases. Desk employees will not decide housing applications, issue permits or give legal advice about an individual’s case. Existing departments will remain responsible for decisions and for telling applicants what additional evidence they need.

The city has not promised that a visit to the desk will shorten the processing time for an underlying application. Its role is to help residents reach the appropriate department and understand the steps required, rather than take over that department’s work.

A community survey provided examples of residents visiting several offices about the same inquiry. It was not designed to estimate how often such problems occur across the city.

The neighborhood language forum has asked the city to track requests it cannot meet for languages beyond the initial three. Forum member Iman Varek emphasized the importance of making interpreter availability clear.

“Publishing the hours matters as much as publishing the list of languages,” Varek said.

Staff will record inquiry topics and requests they cannot meet, but will not retain copies of visitors’ personal application documents. A side room will be used for conversations that should not take place in the public reception queue.

The opening follows six months of planning involving reception staff and local organizations that already help residents navigate services. Those organizations will continue to operate independently and are not being absorbed into a city-run advice service.

The civic center’s existing reception window will also remain available for routine inquiries. Separately, the building’s large wall directory is being replaced because its department names no longer match several current office signs.

A review after three months will examine the language schedule, unmet requests and whether visitors reached the relevant department. The city has made no commitment to add a particular fourth language.
