# Fenharbor approves $460,000 creekside lot purchase for floodwater storage

FENHARBOR — The Fenharbor Council voted 6-1 Tuesday to buy a 2.4-acre parking lot beside Narrow Creek for a future floodwater-storage project, committing $460,000 to acquire the land while leaving construction funding and parking alternatives unresolved.

The seller has accepted the city's offer for the privately owned Mill Bend lot, but closing is scheduled for November and ownership has not transferred. The purchase will use $240,000 from a city reserve and a $220,000 regional land-acquisition grant.

The city intends to remove pavement and create an area that can hold water when creek levels rise. Tuesday's vote authorizes the land purchase, not a final engineering plan. Design work and an environmental assessment must precede construction, and the grant does not cover demolition or construction. Neither expense has a confirmed funding allocation.

“Buying room for water is the first step, not a promise that every nearby property will stay dry,” city engineer Hollis Vare said.

City records document flooding on the parking lot during storms in 2019 and 2024. Those records do not measure how much the proposed project would affect flooding.

The lot contains 87 marked spaces a short walk from Fenharbor's shopping streets. Under terms described in the council report, the current parking operator will continue running it until the sale closes. The council has not announced when parking would end after closing.

A merchants group says the lot is important to workers whose shifts begin before nearby short-stay spaces become available. Its concern centers on access to workday parking as the city moves toward a different use for the property.

“We support fixing the flooding, but the replacement for a workday parking space cannot be a two-hour space,” merchants group chair Rima Doss said.

The lone dissenting council member wanted parking alternatives identified before the city committed reserve money to the purchase.

The city also will examine converting six curb spaces to loading bays. That is a separate proposal and has not been approved.

The draft floodwater-storage concept calls for a planted basin rather than a permanent pond. Plantings and public access arrangements remain undecided, and the council has not announced when any public recreation area might open.

An existing public path lies outside the purchase boundary and will remain under the parks department's management. The regional grant requires the purchased land to remain available for floodwater storage and prohibits later commercial development.

The environmental assessment will include soil sampling because the parcel previously served as a vehicle storage yard. The council packet contains no finding that the soil is contaminated and no estimate of any cleanup expense.

A public design meeting will be scheduled after the assessment contract is arranged. As of Wednesday, no meeting date had been published.
