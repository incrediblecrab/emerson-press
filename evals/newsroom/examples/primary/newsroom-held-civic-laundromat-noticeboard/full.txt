# Between dryer loads, a place to catch up on local notices

Above the folding table inside the Turnwater laundry, a cork noticeboard offers a neighborhood lunch, a secondhand sewing machine and a volunteer gardening session. Customers waiting for the dryers can read the notices without leaving their laundry behind.

Neri Pell reads the board during a quiet weekday visit while folding bedding for a household she helps care for. One notice supplied information she needed alongside the work she had come to do.

“I came for clean sheets and found out the hall lunch had moved. Both things were useful that morning,” Pell said.

The board gives local messages a place in an ordinary errand. As of Sept. 18, it is free to post there, subject to the laundry’s rules about space, contact details and keeping notices current. Its usefulness rests on small exchanges like Pell’s, rather than any measured tally of jobs, sales or friendships.

Owner Ada Venn began allowing notices after customers repeatedly asked to leave handwritten messages with staff. The cork board put those messages where people waiting for their laundry could see them.

“The dryer gives people a few minutes to read. That is more time than a notice gets when it flashes past on a phone,” Venn said.

Turnwater has six dryers, and some customers come primarily to dry bulky items rather than complete an entire wash cycle. Time for reading and conversation varies with the ordinary work of sharing the machines. The folding table also gives notice writers somewhere to straighten a page and borrow a pen before pinning it up.

Keeping that space useful takes some tending. Venn stamps an expiry date on event notices and removes them after the event, so an old invitation does not appear current. A dated notice from the lunch organizer confirmed the move Pell mentioned, though the reporting record provides no broader attendance result.

Staff check that notices have a contact method and a date when relevant. They do not certify the quality of every offer. Customers can ask them to remove an outdated or disputed notice, which is set aside for its writer to clarify. Removal alone is not a finding of fraud or proof that an offered service never existed.

Space imposes another limit: Each poster gets one current sheet unless Venn agrees to a larger community announcement. The board is not reserved for paying advertisers.

A volunteer sometimes helps writers prepare a second version in another language. The original writer checks the details before it goes up. That help does not amount to a promise of translation into every language, and the laundry has no professional interpretation service.

The notices also remain distinct from the laundry’s electronic page. They are not automatically photographed and republished online. Venn keeps removed community notices in a folder in case their writers want them back, not as a publicly searchable archive. Access to the board follows laundry opening hours.

For Pell, the useful information was there beside the task already in hand: bedding to fold, and a dated notice about where to go for lunch.
