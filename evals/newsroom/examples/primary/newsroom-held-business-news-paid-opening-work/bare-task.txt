# Threadgate to pay for preopening work; earlier time records remain under review

Threadgate Stores said Tuesday that employees assigned to opening preparation will begin paid shifts 15 minutes before stores open, while a separate review of earlier time records could result in an estimated $84,000 in additional pay.

The scheduling change takes effect Sept. 7 at the clothing chain’s 18 stores. It covers preparation such as unlocking stock cages, checking tills and taking delivery racks onto the sales floor. Base hourly wage rates and public opening hours will not change.

The company’s internal review found that its old scheduling system commonly placed the start of paid shifts at the same time stores opened to customers. The new schedule moves the paid start time earlier for employees assigned to prepare stores for opening.

“Opening the shop is work, even when there are no customers in it yet,” payroll director Oren Tal said.

Tal said managers will receive additional labor-budget hours to accommodate the change rather than being told to cut later shifts to cover it.

The review of earlier records covers opening shifts from January 2024 through June 2026 and approximately 260 current and former workers. Threadgate estimates that correcting those entries could produce $84,000 in additional pay, but individual calculations remain unfinished.

That figure is an estimate, not a completed tally of what each worker will receive. As of Tuesday, Aug. 11, the company had not announced a date for completing every payment calculation or published a store-by-store breakdown of the estimated additional pay.

Employees began raising the scheduling issue after a new till system recorded the time of the first morning check. Those timestamps show when till checks occurred, but they do not capture every task performed before opening. They therefore provide a record of one part of opening preparation, rather than a complete account of the work involved.

Employee representative Anya Bell said workers want access to the records used to calculate their individual additional pay. Threadgate said employees will receive an itemized calculation and can request a payroll review if they disagree.

“Starting the clock earlier fixes tomorrow. People still need to see what happened to yesterday,” Bell said.

The prospective scheduling change and the review of earlier time entries address distinct periods. Paid preopening shifts are scheduled to begin Sept. 7, while the review concerns records through June 2026. The announcement does not mean the individual calculations for that earlier period have been completed.

Threadgate said former employees will be contacted at their last recorded address. They may submit updated contact details through the company’s payroll office.

The review is limited to opening preparation and does not purport to settle every possible time-record issue across the chain. The reporting record contains no court judgment or regulator finding about the old scheduling system.

Threadgate began as a single workwear shop in 1996 and now sells school uniforms as well as adult clothing.
