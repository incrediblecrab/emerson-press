# Threadgate to start paid opening shifts earlier; past time records remain under review

Threadgate Stores said Tuesday that employees assigned to opening preparation will start paid shifts 15 minutes before doors open, beginning Sept. 7 at its 18 clothing stores.

The schedule change is separate from a review of earlier opening shifts that the company estimates could result in $84,000 in additional pay. Individual calculations remain unfinished, and Threadgate has not announced when it will complete all of them.

The review covers opening shifts from January 2024 through June 2026 and approximately 260 current and former workers. The $84,000 figure is an estimate, rather than a completed tally of individual payments. The company has not published a store-by-store breakdown.

Opening preparation includes unlocking stock cages, checking tills and taking delivery racks onto the sales floor. The old scheduling system commonly placed employees' paid start time at the time stores opened to the public, according to the company's internal review.

"Opening the shop is work, even when there are no customers in it yet," payroll director Oren Tal said.

The new schedule will not change base hourly wage rates or stores' public opening hours. Tal said managers will receive additional labor-budget hours rather than being told to cut later shifts to cover the earlier starts.

That change addresses how opening work will be scheduled beginning in September. The separate review concerns whether earlier time entries need to be corrected and how much additional pay those corrections would produce for individual workers.

Employees began raising the scheduling issue after a new till system recorded the time of the first morning check. Those timestamps show when till checks occurred, but they do not capture every task performed before opening. They therefore provide a record of one part of opening preparation, rather than a complete account of all work before the doors opened.

Employee representative Anya Bell said workers want access to the records used in their individual calculations.

"Starting the clock earlier fixes tomorrow. People still need to see what happened to yesterday," Bell said.

Threadgate said employees will receive an itemized calculation and can request a payroll review if they disagree. That promise describes how the company plans to present the results and handle disputes; it does not specify whether workers will receive access to the underlying records Bell is seeking.

Former employees will be contacted at their last recorded address, the company said. They may submit updated contact details through Threadgate's payroll office. No date has been announced for completing every payment calculation, leaving the timing of the review's completion unresolved.

The review is limited to opening preparation and does not purport to settle every possible time-record issue across the chain. The available reporting record contains no court judgment or regulator finding about the old scheduling system.

Threadgate began as a single workwear shop in 1996 and now sells school uniforms as well as adult clothing.
