# Kestrel Bend market adopts sales-based fees, drawing mixed vendor views

Kestrel Bend's market board voted 7-2 Wednesday to replace its flat weekly stall fee with a sales-based charge that will lower bills for some regular vendors and raise them for others.

Beginning Sept. 1, regular vendors will pay $18 a week plus 5% of gross market sales instead of a flat $30. The nine-member board adopted the arrangement without a cap, rejecting the approach sought by two dissenting members.

The change comes after the public market recorded a $28,000 operating deficit in the last fiscal year, largely reflecting a roof repair and higher cleaning costs. The city covered the deficit with a one-time appropriation but has not promised another subsidy. The board will review six months of fee receipts before deciding whether to keep the sales-based system.

For vendors, the effect will depend on how much they sell at the market. A vendor with $100 in weekly sales will pay $23, or $7 less than the current fee. A vendor with $300 in weekly sales will pay $33, an increase of $3.

The new fee equals the current $30 charge at $240 in weekly sales. Below that amount, vendors will pay less; above it, they will pay more. With no cap in the adopted measure, the percentage charge will continue to rise with sales.

Flower seller Oona Drel said her weekly sales vary sharply with weather and event bookings. She favors a fee that changes with the amount of business she does.

"A quieter week should not cost me the same as a full table of orders," Drel said.

Hot-food vendor Silas Gant raised a different concern: Gross sales do not account for his ingredient and refrigeration costs.

"The board sees the sale, but it does not see what I paid to put that meal together," Gant said.

Under the board's definition, the percentage applies to sales made at this market before business expenses, excluding separately collected sales tax. Sales elsewhere are not included. Vendors with the same amount of market sales will therefore owe the same fee even if their business expenses differ.

How those calculations will affect the market's 46 regular vendors, who sell produce, prepared food, flowers and household crafts, remains uncertain. Market director Tessa Rook said existing records show only stall occupancy, so the board cannot yet predict each vendor's new bill.

Vendors will report their own sales on a weekly form. The board has not adopted an electronic payment requirement. It also approved free bookkeeping help through a local nonprofit, starting with four sessions in September.

The separate $15 daily fee for occasional guest stalls will remain unchanged. The vote also leaves the market's Saturday opening hours and winter calendar intact.

As of Wednesday, no vendor had given the board a formal departure notice since the vote. That does not establish that all vendors intend to stay.
