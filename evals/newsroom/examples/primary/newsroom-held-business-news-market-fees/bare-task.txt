# Kestrel Bend market adopts sales-based fees, with uneven effects for vendors

KESTREL BEND — Regular vendors at Kestrel Bend’s public market will pay a lower base stall fee plus a share of their sales beginning Sept. 1, under a measure approved Wednesday that will reduce bills for slower-selling stalls and raise them for those with higher sales.

The nine-member market board voted 7-2 on Aug. 5 to replace the flat $30 weekly charge with an $18 fee plus 5% of gross market sales. The change comes after a $28,000 operating deficit in the last fiscal year and without a promise of further city support.

The market’s 46 regular vendors sell produce, prepared food, flowers and household crafts. Their new bills will depend on sales made at the market, not sales elsewhere. The board’s definition excludes separately collected sales tax but does not deduct business expenses.

A vendor selling $100 in a week will pay $23, or $7 less than under the current arrangement. A vendor with $300 in sales will pay $33, an increase of $3. The two formulas produce the same $30 bill at $240 in weekly sales; above that amount, the new charge will be higher.

That makes the effects different not only from stall to stall, but potentially from week to week.

Flower seller Oona Drel said her weekly sales vary sharply with the weather and event bookings.

“A quieter week should not cost me the same as a full table of orders,” Drel said.

The lower base fee means a smaller charge during weeks when sales fall below the break-even point. But for vendors with substantial costs, a sales-based bill does not necessarily track what they have left after paying expenses.

Hot-food vendor Silas Gant said gross sales do not account for his ingredient and refrigeration costs.

“The board sees the sale, but it does not see what I paid to put that meal together,” Gant said.

Two dissenting board members sought a fee cap. The adopted measure contains none, so the percentage portion of a regular vendor’s bill will continue to rise with market sales.

How much the arrangement will collect overall remains uncertain. Market director Tessa Rook said existing records show only stall occupancy, leaving the board unable to predict each vendor’s new bill.

Vendors will report their own sales on a weekly form. The board has not adopted a requirement to use electronic payments. It also approved free bookkeeping help through a local nonprofit, starting with four sessions in September.

The market’s deficit largely reflected a roof repair and higher cleaning costs. The city covered the shortfall with a one-time appropriation but has not promised another subsidy. The board will review six months of fee receipts before deciding whether to keep the sales-based system.

The separate $15 daily fee for occasional guest stalls will remain unchanged. Wednesday’s vote also left Saturday opening hours and the winter calendar intact.

The market opened in a former tram shed in 1998 and continues to use movable stalls rather than permanent shop units.

As of Wednesday, no vendor had given the board a formal departure notice since the vote. That does not establish that all vendors intend to stay as they assess what the new formula will mean for their businesses.
