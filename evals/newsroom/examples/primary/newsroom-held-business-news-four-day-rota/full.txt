Wholesaler to Reopen Friday Counter, Keep Four-Day Weeks for Many Workers

Hallow Reed Wholesale said Monday it will reopen its service counter on Fridays starting Sept. 14, using a rotating schedule designed to restore five-day customer access while many employees keep four-day workweeks.

The plan follows a 12-week trial that ran from March 16 through June 5, during which the counter was closed on Fridays. Eighty participating employees worked four 10-hour days instead of five eight-hour days, with no change to base weekly pay.

Under the new arrangement, employees will rotate Monday or Friday as their nonworking day so the counter can operate five days a week. Workers who cannot manage 10-hour shifts may request five eight-hour days instead; the company has not published how many such requests have been approved.

The wholesaler will also add a second supervisor to the rotating counter roster. It did not announce any new warehouse positions.

Operations chief Torin Brack said the goal was to preserve the shorter workweek without cutting customer access. "Four days for a worker does not have to mean four days for the customer," Brack said.

The new arrangement is scheduled for review after six months. No permanent companywide workweek policy has been adopted beyond that six-month period.

Company figures from the trial show mixed results. Average weekly order volume rose to 5,400 from 5,000 in the preceding 12 weeks, an 8% increase. But product mix and seasonal demand differed between the two periods, so the comparison does not isolate the effect of the schedule change.

The company's order log also showed the average elapsed time from order receipt to dispatch rose to 1.8 calendar days during the trial, up from 1.2 days beforehand. Customer-service logs recorded 116 Friday collection requests that had to be redirected to another day during the closure. The logs do not show how many different customers were affected or how many took their business elsewhere.

Hallow Reed has not released comparable figures on absences, overtime, profit or employee retention for the trial period.

Reaction from workers and customers was mixed. Warehouse worker Neri Gall said the longer shifts suited his commute but created conflicts for some colleagues with evening commitments. "A day off is useful," Gall said. "So is being able to get home before the evening is gone."

Plumbing contractor Osa Keene, a customer, said Friday collections often supply jobs that cannot wait until Monday.

The trial covered the company's wholesale depot, which distributes plumbing and electrical supplies from a building expanded in 2017. It did not affect Hallow Reed's separate weekend retail outlet.

The new rotation begins Sept. 14, with management set to reassess the arrangement after six months.
