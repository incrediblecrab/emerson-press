Hallow Reed Wholesale to Reopen Fridays, Keeping Four-Day Weeks for Most Staff

By staff report

Hallow Reed Wholesale announced Monday that its service counter will reopen on Fridays beginning Sept. 14, even as most employees continue working four-day weeks, under a new rotating schedule set for review after six months.

The move follows a 12-week trial that ran from March 16 through June 5, during which the counter was closed on Fridays. Eighty participating employees worked four ten-hour days instead of five eight-hour days, with no change to base weekly pay.

Under the arrangement announced this week, employees will rotate Monday or Friday as their nonworking day, allowing the counter to operate all five weekdays. Workers unable to manage ten-hour shifts may request a return to five eight-hour days; the company has not released figures on how many such requests have been approved.

"Four days for a worker does not have to mean four days for the customer," said Torin Brack, the company's operations chief, in announcing the plan.

The company also said it will add a second supervisor to the rotating counter roster, though it has not announced any new warehouse positions.

Results from the spring trial were mixed. Average weekly order volume rose to 5,400 during the four-day schedule, up 8% from 5,000 in the preceding 12 weeks. But the company cautioned that product mix and seasonal demand differed between the two periods, meaning the comparison does not isolate the effect of the schedule change.

Other metrics pointed to strain. The average time from order receipt to dispatch rose to 1.8 calendar days during the trial, up from 1.2 days beforehand, according to the company's order log. Customer-service logs also recorded 116 Friday collection requests that had to be redirected to another day. The logs do not indicate how many individual customers were affected or how many took their business elsewhere as a result.

Reaction among workers and customers has been mixed. Warehouse worker Neri Gall said the longer daily shifts suited his commuting arrangement but created friction for colleagues with evening obligations.

"A day off is useful," Gall said. "So is being able to get home before the evening is gone."

Plumbing contractor Osa Keene, a customer of the depot, said the Friday closure had been a persistent inconvenience. "Friday collections often supply jobs that cannot wait until Monday," Keene said.

The company has not released comparable data on employee absences, overtime costs, profitability or staff retention during the trial period, making it difficult to independently assess the experiment's overall impact.

The trial and the new arrangement apply only to the wholesale depot, which distributes plumbing and electrical supplies from a building expanded in 2017. The company's separate weekend retail outlet was not part of the trial.

Hallow Reed has not adopted a permanent companywide workweek policy. Monday's announcement covers only the six-month arrangement beginning next month, after which the rotating schedule will again be reviewed.

It remains unclear whether the new system will resolve the delivery delays and redirected collection requests observed during the spring trial, or whether reopening the counter five days a week will fully offset the disruptions reported by customers during the Friday closures.
