**HEADLINE:** After the Last Bus Stops, an Evening Class Begins at the Depot

MOSSFIELD — Every week, after the daytime dispatch briefing ends and staff clear the operational papers from the room, tutor Rhea Noll arranges the tables into a horseshoe. Within the hour, adults arrive for a two-hour class in reading, writing and basic number tasks, held in a meeting room at the Mossfield transport depot.

The class, run by the Mossfield adult learning service, is open to residents as well as depot employees. It is not a required employer training session, and the depot provides the room at no charge while the learning service pays Noll and supplies the materials.

The current course runs 10 weeks and is built around tasks the learners themselves identified as useful. A recent session had participants compare a printed timetable, draft a short work message and check quantities on an order form. The exercises use forms invented for the class rather than participants' own bills or workplace paperwork, so no one has to show private documents to the group.

"People arrive with a whole working day behind them," Noll said. "We begin with the thing they need to do, not with a label for what they cannot do."

Eighteen adults registered for the course, though registration does not mean everyone attends every session. Benra Vale, a learner who comes around a shift change, sometimes leaves before the class ends and instead takes home a paper exercise to finish later.

"I wanted to write a clear message without asking someone else to finish it for me," Vale said.

The class does not ask learners to describe their personal histories in front of the group at the first meeting. Instead, goals are discussed privately with Noll, who has taught adult community classes before and adjusts exercises for learners working toward different immediate goals. The course plan does not assume that a person's age or current job determines how familiar they are with any given task.

The depot was chosen partly because it sits on an evening bus route, making it easier for some learners to reach after work. But the route cuts both ways: the last bus still limits how long some participants can stay, and organizers are clear the class itself is not a transportation service.

At the end of the table sits a kettle and a small supply of donated biscuits — offered, Noll said, without any expectation tied to attendance or a fee.

The learning service has not released test results, completion figures or any measured employment outcome connected to the course. Officials said they will discuss whether to offer a follow-on class after reviewing attendance patterns and requests from learners, but no funding for a continuation beyond the current 10 weeks has been announced.

For now, the sessions continue on their weekly schedule, structured around the ordinary tasks — a timetable, a message, an order form — that brought each learner into the room in the first place.
