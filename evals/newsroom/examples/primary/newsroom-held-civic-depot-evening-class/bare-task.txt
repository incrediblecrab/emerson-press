Evening Class Meets Workers Where the Shift Ends, at Mossfield Transport Depot

MOSSFIELD — After the daytime dispatch briefing clears out of the meeting room, tutor Rhea Noll rearranges the tables into a horseshoe. By early evening, the space that hours earlier held operational schedules for the Mossfield transport depot is set for something else: a weekly adult learning class.

The class, run by the Mossfield adult learning service, is open to adult residents as well as depot workers, and organizers stress it is not a compulsory employer training session. The depot supplies the room without a hire charge; the adult learning service pays the tutor and provides materials.

The current 10-week course focuses on practical reading, writing and number tasks chosen with the learners themselves. A recent session included comparing a printed timetable, drafting a short work message and checking quantities on an order form. The exercises use invented forms rather than requiring learners to display their own bills or workplace documents in front of the group.

"People arrive with a whole working day behind them," Noll said. "We begin with the thing they need to do, not with a label for what they cannot do."

The course plan makes no assumption that a person's age or current job determines how familiar they are with any given task, and learners can discuss their goals privately with Noll rather than announce personal histories at the first meeting.

Benra Vale, a learner who attends around changing shifts, sometimes collects a paper exercise when unable to stay for the full session. "I wanted to write a clear message without asking someone else to finish it for me," Vale said.

Eighteen adults registered for the current course, though organizers note registration does not guarantee attendance at every session. The depot sits on an evening bus route, a factor in choosing the venue, but the last available bus still limits how late some learners can stay — the class itself is not a transport service.

Small touches mark the room as separate from its daytime use. A kettle and a small supply of donated biscuits sit at the end of the table, though organizers said refreshments are not a fee or a requirement for taking part. Depot staff remove operational papers from the room before each class, keeping the public session apart from private work records.

Noll has taught adult community classes before this course and said she adapts exercises as learners' immediate goals differ from week to week.

The learning service has not supplied test results, completion figures or any measured employment outcome tied to the course. Organizers said they plan to discuss a follow-on course after reviewing attendance patterns and hearing what learners request, but no continuation funding beyond the current 10 weeks has been announced.

For now, the arrangement remains what it has been since the course began: a room borrowed each evening from a transport depot, tables turned into a horseshoe, and a class built around whatever task a learner brings through the door that day.
