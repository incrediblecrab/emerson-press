Federal Engineers Flag Deterioration at Harlan Reservoir Dam, Order Remediation Plan

Federal dam safety engineers on Monday designated the Harlan Reservoir Dam a Level 2 structural concern, requiring accelerated monitoring and a remediation plan within 18 months, after a study found the concrete structure is deteriorating more than twice as fast as previously recorded.

The dam, built in 1958 and operated by the Cascade Valley Water Authority, supplies about 28% of drinking water for a metropolitan area of 800,000 residents and irrigates 180,000 acres of agricultural land in Harlan County. Its downstream inundation zone covers 140,000 people across three counties.

A structural assessment found the dam's concrete is deteriorating at a rate of 2.4 millimeters per year, up from 1.1 millimeters per year documented in a 2014 study. Engineers attributed the acceleration primarily to alkali-silica reaction, a chemical process in which silica in the concrete's aggregate reacts with the cement's alkaline compounds over time.

At the current rate, engineers estimate the dam would reach a threshold requiring emergency intervention within 12 to 18 years if left unaddressed.

Level 2 is the second of three concern levels under the federal dam safety framework, with Level 3 reserved for situations requiring immediate emergency action. The designation requires the authority to submit progress reports on its remediation plan to federal engineers every 90 days.

Cascade Valley Water Authority director Keith Obi said the authority accepts the designation and has commissioned a full remediation design study. He emphasized that a Level 2 designation does not indicate an imminent failure risk.

Dr. Pamela Johansson, the structural engineer who conducted the assessment, echoed that assurance while stressing the need for action.

"The dam is not in danger of failing today or next year," Johansson said. "But the trajectory of deterioration is clear, and addressing it will cost far less now than after further degradation."

Preliminary estimates put the cost of full remediation — including concrete resurfacing and drainage system upgrades — at $620 million to $740 million. The authority has $80 million in reserve funds available for immediate monitoring upgrades, but officials said the full remediation project would require bonding or federal assistance.

The dam passed its most recent comprehensive federal safety inspection in 2021 with a satisfactory rating. Engineers noted that assessment was based on conditions at the time and predates the updated deterioration data.

Harlan Dam is classified as a high-hazard potential structure, meaning its failure would likely result in loss of human life. Nationally, about 1,200 high-hazard-potential dams more than 60 years old are in service, according to the National Dam Safety Program's most recent data.

Federal emergency action plans for the dam call for evacuation of portions of the downstream zone within six hours of any determination that failure is imminent. Officials in two downstream counties said they would review and update their emergency evacuation plans in response to the new designation.

The water authority said it plans to hold public meetings in affected counties within 60 days to explain the designation and outline a remediation timeline for residents.

The remediation design study is expected to inform the plan the authority must submit to federal engineers within the 18-month window. No timeline for construction has been announced, and officials said funding decisions — including whether the authority will pursue bonding, federal assistance, or a combination of both — remain under discussion.
