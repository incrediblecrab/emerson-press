# Hollowmere Market to launch $5 purchase-matching vouchers Oct. 10

The Hollowmere Market Association will launch a grant-funded purchase-matching voucher program Oct. 10, offering shoppers who spend at least $10 during one qualifying market visit a $5 voucher for a later eligible purchase.

The offer will be limited to one voucher per shopper per market day, regardless of how much a shopper spends above the $10 threshold. First-round vouchers must be used by Dec. 19, the final scheduled market day of the round.

A $20,000 community-commerce grant will pay for the voucher value, while the association will cover administration separately. The allocation can fund 4,000 vouchers at $5 each if it is fully issued. That figure represents the program’s capacity, not the number of shoppers who have participated or vouchers that have been used.

The first round will cover produce, bread and pantry ingredients listed in the program rules, rather than every item sold at the market. Vouchers will be accepted only by participating traders and cannot be exchanged for cash. No cash change will be given.

Market coordinator Nella Orr said the offer is intended to encourage shoppers to come back.

“The extra five dollars is a reason to return, not a discount we can claim has already helped every household,” Orr said.

The program will be open without an income test and is not part of a government food-benefit system. Shoppers can continue making regular cash and card purchases whether or not they use the matching offer.

To receive a voucher, shoppers will need to have the market office check receipts from the same visit. A staffed alternative will be available for traders using handwritten receipts. Each voucher will carry a serial number and an expiration date, but will not have the shopper’s name printed on it.

Participating traders will display the association’s sign and receive reimbursement for eligible vouchers they submit. Participation is voluntary, and shoppers cannot require an unregistered stall to accept a voucher.

For small traders, the timing of those reimbursements has been a concern. A traders committee asked the association to make payments promptly enough that small stalls would not be financing the grant between market days.

“A token in the till still has to become money we can use to buy flour,” bread seller Paven Rill said.

The association plans to reimburse traders after each market week rather than have them wait until the program ends.

The association will track voucher issuance and redemption separately. Receiving a voucher does not establish that a shopper later used it, and the reporting available ahead of the launch contains no estimate of additional spending, dietary changes or household savings the program will produce.

The market office will display a running notice when the grant allocation is close to being fully issued. The association has not secured funding for a second round after December.
