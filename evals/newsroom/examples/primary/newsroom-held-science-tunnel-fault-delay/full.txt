Eastside Connector Tunnel Delayed 4 Years After Fault Discovery; Cost Rises to $7.4 Billion

MEDFORD — An unmapped geological fault discovered beneath a densely populated neighborhood will delay completion of Metro City's Eastside Connector tunnel by at least four years and add $1.9 billion to its cost, transit officials said Wednesday.

The Metro City Transit Authority said the tunnel, previously scheduled to open in 2027, will not be finished until 2031 at the earliest. The total project estimate now stands at $7.4 billion, up from an original budget of $4.4 billion.

Tunnel boring machines paused at the fault zone in August after geotechnical sensors detected fracturing and elevated groundwater pressure inconsistent with surveys done before construction began. The fault runs about 340 meters across the tunnel's path at a depth of 38 meters, authority officials said, and does not appear on any historical geological mapping of the city. The authority described it as a secondary splay from a larger regional fault last active more than 10,000 years ago.

"Pre-construction surveys do not capture every subsurface feature," Transit Authority Director Sandra Muñoz said. "We are dealing with a real geological constraint, not a project management failure."

Muñoz said no workers were injured and that boring was halted as a standard precaution.

Lead geotechnical engineer Dr. Chen Li said the authority is studying three options: reorienting the tunnel bore by 11 degrees, treating the soil across the fault zone to consolidate it, or slowing the boring machines through the zone at significant time cost. Li said ground treatment will be required regardless of which option is chosen. The authority said it will bring a preferred solution to its board in 90 days but has not indicated which option it favors, saying an engineering decision would come before any public announcement.

Three blocks of residential buildings above the fault zone are being monitored for surface settlement. The authority said no movement beyond normal tolerance has been detected.

Wednesday's announcement marks the project's third cost revision since construction began in 2019. Two earlier increases, totaling $1.1 billion, were attributed to COVID-19-related supply chain disruptions and rising steel prices.

Federal transit officials said the project's primary funding grant, which covers $2.2 billion of the original budget, would require renegotiation if the project's scope changes substantially. Bond rating agencies said they are reviewing the authority's debt rating in light of the revised cost profile.

City council members representing the eastern districts served by the line called for an independent audit of the geological survey process used before construction began.

The Eastside Connector is designed to carry 80,000 daily riders once complete. The eastern corridor it would serve is currently served only by bus lines, with average trip times 22 minutes longer than the tunnel would offer. Two stations at the western end of the line have been open since 2022.

The delay adds to scrutiny of the city's transit infrastructure, which received a D+ rating in the most recent national infrastructure report.
