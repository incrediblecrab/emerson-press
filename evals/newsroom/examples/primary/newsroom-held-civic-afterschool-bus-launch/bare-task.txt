Dunmere Schools to Launch First Late-Bus Service for After-School Activities

DUNMERE — The Dunmere School District will begin its first late-bus service on Oct. 27, offering students at four secondary schools a ride home after school-supervised clubs, study sessions and sports practices, district officials said.

The service will run on Tuesdays and Thursdays, giving students who stay for activities such as gardening club, drama, debate and supervised homework sessions — not just competitive sports teams — a way home without requiring a parent pickup.

The program is funded by a one-year, $96,000 education-access grant that covers a leased bus, driver hours and fuel under the district's approved plan. District officials said the grant does not establish funding for a continued service in future school years.

"An activity can be free to join and still be out of reach if the ride home is missing," said Transport Director Orla Venn.

Students will not be charged a fare, but they must register through their school before using a late-bus route. The bus will stop at designated neighborhood locations rather than at every student's home. School staff will confirm the passenger list before each departure and notify families which stop has been assigned to their child.

Under the initial route plan, the four schools are organized into two collection groups, with departure times set according to each group's differing activity schedules. The district has not published a single districtwide departure time, nor has it guaranteed how long any route will take.

Students leaving unsupervised private activities cannot assume a spot on the district service unless the required school arrangement is in place, officials said. Schools will keep their existing supervision arrangements in place for registered students while they wait for the bus.

The driver hours funded by the grant are additional paid hours, according to the district, not an expectation that current drivers extend their shifts without compensation. The district's regular morning and afternoon bus routes are unaffected by the new service.

Each participating school will distribute registration information both in print and electronically, the district said.

A local families group has asked the district to add a third service day, particularly for activities that meet on Wednesdays.

"The two days help, but a club does not move its whole calendar just because a bus is available," said Teren Moss, the group's organizer.

District officials said they will track registrations, actual boardings and unmet requests separately during the first term of the program. They said they have not yet gathered evidence on whether the new service has increased club participation or improved academic outcomes, and that such assessments will follow once the service is underway.

Officials said they will review demand data before deciding whether to seek funding for additional service days or another bus. No expansion of the program beyond the four participating schools has been approved.

The late-bus program marks a shift for the district, which has not previously offered transportation specifically tied to after-school activities. Officials said the first term will serve as a test period to determine whether — and how — the service might continue beyond the current grant.
