# Corven apprentices get paid class time, but tools and travel remain costs

The Corven Trades Partnership pays apprentices for scheduled college classes as well as workplace hours and covers their tuition, but participants still need to budget for commuting, meals and an upfront tool purchase.

The partnership enrolled its first 24 maintenance apprentices in August under agreements with eight local employers. For those joining the two-year program, paid training means an initial $16 an hour across a normal 40-hour week. It does not mean every expense is covered or that later pay increases and continued employment are guaranteed.

## What the paid week covers

A normal full week consists of four eight-hour workplace days and one eight-hour college day. Both workplace hours and scheduled classroom hours are paid at the same initial rate, producing $640 in gross weekly pay.

That is not a take-home figure. Individual taxes and other deductions have not been supplied, so apprentices cannot treat the quoted $640 as the amount available to spend each week.

For apprentice Sora Reed, who previously worked retail shifts, payment for the college day made joining possible. She said losing a day's wages to attend college would have prevented her from enrolling.

"I can plan around a paid class day. I could not replace that day's wages with a certificate," Reed said.

Paid classroom time also has limits. Attendance requirements and arrangements for missed class days are set out in each apprentice's program agreement. Payment for scheduled classes is not an unlimited entitlement to paid study time.

## Tuition is covered; everyday expenses are not

Annual tuition is $3,600 per apprentice. The partnership grant provides $2,400 and the employer contributes $1,200. Under the current agreement, apprentices are not billed for that tuition.

Commuting and ordinary meals are not covered. Those costs must come out of apprentices' own budgets, separate from the tuition funding. No amounts for travel or meals are supplied, so the program's quoted wage alone does not establish how much a participant will have left after those expenses.

The required personal tool kit adds a $240 upfront cost. Reimbursement is offered after six months of continued participation, but the money is not available in cash before the kit is purchased. Apprentices therefore need to account for the purchase even if they expect to qualify for reimbursement later.

Employers also commit staff time to training. Employer representative Deni Holt said apprentices are productive workers but need supervision from experienced staff.

"Paying the learner is one cost. Making time for the person teaching them is another," Holt said.

## A later raise depends on progress

The planned review point for a rise to $18 an hour is 18 months into the apprenticeship. Reaching that date alone does not trigger the increase.

Apprentices must complete specified assessments, and their employer must confirm that they have met the listed workplace competencies. A budget that assumes the higher rate will begin automatically at 18 months would overlook those requirements.

The apprenticeship runs for two years. Employment afterward depends on the individual employer's plans and agreement, rather than a program-wide promise of a continuing job.

As of Aug. 31, the partnership has no completion or post-program earnings outcomes because this is its first cohort. The grant covers the apprentices already enrolled but does not yet fund another intake.

For current participants, the budgeting distinction is clear: scheduled training hours are paid and tuition is covered under the agreement. Travel, ordinary meals and the initial $240 tool purchase remain expenses apprentices must plan to meet.
