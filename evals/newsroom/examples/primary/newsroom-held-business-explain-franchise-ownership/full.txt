**What "locally owned" means when the sign says something else**

CORMERE — When Omi and Rella Doss opened the Cormere branch of Ilex Pantry in July, the awning matched every other Ilex store in the region. The ownership did not.

The Dosses own the company that operates the store and lease the building it sits in, a former carpet dealership that still has its wide loading door at the rear. The national Ilex brand does not own this location. What the Dosses bought was a franchise agreement: the right to use the Ilex name, its purchasing system and its loyalty program, in exchange for fees and a set of operating requirements.

The math behind that arrangement shows why the distinction matters. The agreement charges a 4% royalty and a 1% advertising contribution on the store's gross sales, not its profit. On $100,000 in monthly sales, that comes to $5,000 before the Dosses pay rent, wages, stock and other expenses. They also paid a $28,000 initial franchise fee to open, a one-time cost separate from the recurring sales-based charges.

"The name gets us into a buying system," Omi Doss said. "It does not pay the electricity bill for us."

That buying system, Doss said, let the store arrange deliveries that would be difficult for a single independent shop to negotiate on its own.

The franchise agreement also sets terms for what the store looks like and sells. Ilex requires food-handling and shop-presentation standards, and a core list of 600 products the store must stock, though additional local products can be added through the brand's purchasing process. Day-to-day staff schedules are set locally, and the Dosses are responsible for hiring and paying the store's 16 employees.

Pricing works on a split system. The store can set most shelf prices on its own but must honor advertised national promotions during their stated periods. Ilex reimburses part of the cost of some of those promotions, though the reporting record does not include the formulas used to calculate that reimbursement.

The Dosses have not released net profit figures for the Cormere store, and no guaranteed-return projection is part of the public record. The franchise itself runs for a stated 10-year term, with renewal subject to conditions rather than automatic permanent ownership of the brand name.

Leda Vint, who owns an independent grocery near the new store, said the shared signage and advertising can blur a distinction customers may not think to look for.

"Two matching signs do not mean the same person is making the wage payment," Vint said. She added that shared advertising helps customers recognize a store but can make outlets appear more uniform than their ownership actually is.

That uniformity is not guaranteed across the brand. Ilex's regional network includes both company-owned and franchised stores, and the terms at the Cormere location cannot be assumed to apply at every branch carrying the same name.

The Dosses plan to add a display for local producers, though no sales results from that display are available yet.

For now, what "locally owned" means at the Cormere Ilex Pantry is specific: the Dosses hold the lease, hire the staff and absorb the risk, while paying the brand for its name, its supply network and its marketing — a percentage of what comes in the door, not a share of what's left over.
