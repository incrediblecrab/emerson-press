Let me check this approach before finalizing.Lenwick University merges ceramics, furniture-design programs into single department

MEDFORD, Ore. — Lenwick University announced Friday that it will merge its ceramics and furniture-design programs into a new materials-design department, a change that will reshape admissions, staffing and workshop access starting in 2027.

The administrative merger takes effect in January 2027. The first combined degree will admit its first class in autumn 2027, the university said.

The two programs currently enroll 74 students in ceramics and 46 in furniture design, for a combined 120. Students already enrolled may complete their current degrees under the university's published completion plan, and their tuition will not change as a result of the merger. Tuition for the new autumn 2027 combined degree has not yet been set.

Annual admission to the two existing programs has totaled 40 new students. The combined degree will admit 32, eight fewer seats — a 20% reduction.

The university will keep all nine full-time instructors on staff. It will not renew three part-time teaching contracts after the 2026-27 academic year and has not disclosed how many individuals hold those contracts.

The new department will share courses in drawing, materials history and business practices while keeping separate kiln and machine workshops for each discipline. Evening workshop access will end at 9 p.m. instead of 11 p.m., because the university is reducing staffed access hours.

Provost Iona Brill said the university projects $310,000 in annual savings once the new curriculum is fully running, from reduced administration and part-time teaching. That estimate does not include a one-time workshop refit, budgeted at $180,000 from an existing capital allocation; final expenditure on the refit is not yet known.

Brill said the department will seek student scheduling requests before it publishes its January workshop timetable.

The shortened evening hours concern Milo Fenn, a third-year ceramics student who works afternoons at a grocery store and relies on the workshop after 9 p.m. to complete his coursework.

"Finishing my degree is not just a promise about the name on the certificate. I need time at the kiln," Fenn said.

Tavi Reed, a local furniture maker, said shared materials training could benefit graduates but cautioned that employers still need workers skilled on specific equipment.

"A broader course helps if there is still enough time to get good at a difficult tool," Reed said.

The university has not released separate employment-outcome data for the existing ceramics and furniture-design programs.

The ceramics studio occupies a former railway goods office and includes a student-run shop open one afternoon a week. Under the university's published plan, the shop will continue operating as a supervised sales placement rather than a separate degree requirement.
