**South Nacre laptop bids show cheapest option up front isn't cheapest over three years**

SOUTH NACRE — A purchase bid of $450 per laptop is the lowest number on paper in South Nacre School District's search for 300 student devices, but district figures show it may not be the lowest cost by the time a three-year contract would end.

The district published purchase and lease bids in August for the laptops, and the school board plans to decide in September.

The purchase bid totals $135,000, due at delivery. The lease bid runs $54,000 a year for three years, or $162,000 before any optional charges — a higher number than the purchase price standing alone.

The gap narrows, and reverses, once maintenance is added in. District technicians estimate purchased-device upkeep at $12,000 a year, or $36,000 over three years. That is an estimate from district staff, not a fixed price from a vendor. Added to the $135,000 purchase bid, it brings the three-year total to $171,000 — above the $162,000 lease figure.

The lease includes coverage the purchase price does not. Under the lease, ordinary fault repairs and battery replacement are covered when a battery's tested capacity falls below a stated threshold. That coverage has limits: accidental damage and missing devices are excluded, meaning some repair costs would fall outside the lease regardless of which bid the board picks.

What happens after three years also differs. Laptops bought outright remain district property. Leased laptops must be returned unless the district exercises a purchase option, priced at $90 per device — $27,000 if applied to all 300. The board has not received a reliable estimate of what purchased laptops would be worth for resale or reuse at that point, so it isn't possible to say whether keeping owned devices affirmatively saves money for the district.

Finance director Sora Brill said the choice also affects how the district manages cash rather than only how much it spends in total. A lease spreads payments across three years; a purchase draws more heavily on the district's current capital budget in one hit.

"A lower bill in September can come with a different decision waiting three years later," Brill said.

The district needs 260 laptops for regular assignment and plans to keep 40 as spares for repairs and new enrollment. Its current fleet, six years old, needed 71 battery replacements in the 2025-26 school year. District officials did not supply the size of that older fleet or how many devices needed repeat repairs, so the number of replacements does not establish a failure rate for the newer model under consideration.

Neither bid resolves who handles the physical work of keeping devices in students' hands. Technician Peren Moss said staff still have to coordinate repairs and get loaner machines to students while devices are out of service, regardless of which bid wins.

"Someone still has to get a working machine onto the student's desk," Moss said.

Both bids specify the same laptop model and include initial setup, so those items don't distinguish the offers. Neither bid includes the district's teaching-software subscriptions, which are purchased separately.

The district operates two secondary schools and a shared technology workshop. The board has not indicated which bid it favors, and the comparison — hinging on an internal repair estimate, a coverage gap in the lease, and an unresolved resale question — does not point clearly to either option based on the figures released so far.
