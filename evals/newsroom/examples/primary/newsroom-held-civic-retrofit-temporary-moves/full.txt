Hearth Court Retrofit Sends 40 Households to Temporary Housing as Work Begins

Hearth Court's housing association has started moving residents out of two stairwells — 40 of the complex's 120 apartments — as the first phase of a building-wide retrofit begins, according to the association's written resident plan and first-phase notice.

The full project covers six stairwells of 20 apartments each. Rather than move every household at once, the association is proceeding stairwell by stairwell, starting with two. The work replaces shared pipe runs and wiring in locations that cannot stay in normal use while those sections are opened, the plan states.

The association will agree individual moving dates with each household after assessing access needs and the contractor's construction sequence, the plan says. Because those factors vary by apartment, a single published construction phase does not mean every unit will sit empty for the same number of days.

"We need a building schedule and a household plan; neither one can simply stand in for the other," said Selin Marr, the association's housing manager.

**Tenancy and moving costs**

A temporary move does not end a resident's existing tenancy with the association, and the vacated apartment is not allocated to another household, according to the plan. The plan covers agreed moving transport and temporary accommodation costs, though it does not provide unlimited reimbursement of every expense a resident might incur, the association said.

Residents can raise school, work, mobility and caring arrangements that bear on whether a proposed temporary location works for them, the plan states. The association has not promised that all temporary housing will be within walking distance of Hearth Court.

Before each move, the association will issue a written record of the arrangements it has agreed with that household.

Tera Vale, a member of the residents committee, said the offer of alternative housing does not settle the matter for residents whose routines depend on the address. "Being offered a roof is the beginning of the conversation when your ordinary week is built around this address," Vale said.

**Rent and returning home**

Rent treatment during the temporary period follows the resident plan and will be spelled out in each household's written arrangement, the association said. The supplied notice does not address rent after the retrofit is finished and makes no claim about landlords beyond Hearth Court.

Getting back into a renovated apartment is not automatic once the contractor reaches its target date. The construction contract requires a check of completed services before a household is told its apartment is available again, and the association said it will issue return arrangements only after that check — not treat the contractor's schedule as permission to move back on its own.

**Contacts and what's next**

The first-phase notice names a resident liaison contact for households affected by the move, separate from the contact for the construction site's delivery and contractor. The association plans meetings in the ground-floor common room, along with individual conversations for residents who prefer not to discuss their circumstances publicly, it said.

The buildings' shared laundry room falls outside the current construction phase, though the route residents use to reach it may change temporarily during the work, the association said.

Later phases covering the remaining four stairwells will be scheduled after the association reviews how the first moves went and assesses the contractor's progress, according to the plan.
