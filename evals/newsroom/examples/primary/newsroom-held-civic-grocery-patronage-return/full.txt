Headline: Rowan Yard Grocery Co-op Approves Its First Patronage Return to Members

MEDFORD — Members of the Rowan Yard grocery cooperative have approved the co-op's first patronage return, an $18,000 distribution drawn from surplus generated during the previous financial year, according to figures presented at the members' meeting.

The return is calculated at 2.5% of each member's qualifying recorded purchases during the completed year. Qualifying member purchases totaled $720,000, producing the $18,000 allocation at that rate.

Half of each member's calculated return will be paid in cash and half issued as store credit. As an illustration, a member with $800 in qualifying purchases would receive a $20 return, split into $10 cash and $10 credit. That figure is an example only — it does not represent a named member's account or a typical payout, since individual amounts depend on each member's own recorded purchases.

The calculation excludes purchases not recorded to a member account, along with categories excluded under the year's published purchasing rules. The cooperative said it cannot retroactively credit an unidentified cash purchase to a member's account simply because that person recalls shopping there.

Treasurer Fara Noll said the payment reflects results already on the books, not a promise made in advance. "This comes after we know what the year left us," Noll said. "It was never a discount promised on the shelf."

The patronage return is separate from the $30 ownership share members pay when joining. Members keep their ownership shares after receiving the payment; the distribution does not involve selling or redeeming those shares. The members' meeting voted to keep the remainder of the year's available surplus for equipment replacement and working capital. Nothing in the distribution guarantees a return in future years, or the same percentage rate if members approve another one later.

Member Oren Taye asked the meeting to clarify the cash-credit split, noting that members who move away may have trouble using store credit. "I can see why the shop wants money to come back through the till," Taye said. "People should know that before calling the whole amount cash." Under the terms approved, the credit portion expires after 12 months and cannot be exchanged for additional cash.

Members will receive individual statements showing their qualifying purchases, the calculation applied and the split between cash and credit. The cooperative has set a review period during which members can flag recording errors before payments are finalized. The distribution papers do not determine how any individual payment will be taxed; the cooperative has not offered members guidance on that question.

Nonmembers may continue shopping at the co-op's posted shelf prices but do not share in this distribution, which is limited to members.

Rowan Yard was founded by area residents after a privately owned food shop in the area closed, and its board is elected by the membership. The store carries local produce alongside standard wholesale goods; membership benefits are not limited to purchases from local suppliers.
