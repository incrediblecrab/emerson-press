**Rowan Yard Co-op Approves First Member Patronage Return, $18,000 Tied to Members' Purchases**

Members of the Rowan Yard grocery cooperative have approved the co-op's first patronage return, an $18,000 distribution drawn from surplus generated during the previous completed financial year.

The payment amounts to 2.5% of each member's qualifying recorded purchases during that year. Total qualifying purchases across the membership came to $720,000, which produces the $18,000 allocation at the approved rate.

Half of each member's calculated return will be paid in cash, and half will be issued as store credit usable at the cooperative. As an illustration, a member with $800 in qualifying purchases would receive a $20 return — $10 in cash and $10 in credit. Cooperative officials stressed that this example is not drawn from any named member's account and does not represent the amount a typical member will receive; individual returns will vary based on each member's recorded purchases.

The calculation counts only purchases recorded to a member's account and excludes categories the cooperative specified in its published purchasing rules for the year. Officials said the co-op cannot retroactively credit an unidentified cash purchase to a member's account simply because the person recalls having shopped there — the purchase must have been recorded under that member's account at the time.

Treasurer Fara Noll said the timing and size of the payment reflect the co-op's finances after the year closed, not a promise made in advance. "This comes after we know what the year left us," Noll said. "It was never a discount promised on the shelf."

The patronage return is separate from the $30 ownership share members purchase when they join the cooperative. Members keep their ownership shares after receiving the patronage payment; the distribution is not a sale or redemption of those shares.

Not all of the co-op's available surplus is going to members. The remainder was kept back at the members' meeting for equipment replacement and working capital. Cooperative materials note that neither a future return nor the same percentage rate is guaranteed if another distribution is approved down the line.

The credit-cash split drew questions at the members' meeting. Member Oren Taye asked officials to explain the reasoning, noting that members who move away could have difficulty using store credit. "I can see why the shop wants money to come back through the till," Taye said. "People should know that before calling the whole amount cash." Under the approved terms, the store credit expires after 12 months and cannot be exchanged for additional cash.

Members will receive individual statements detailing their qualifying purchases, how the return was calculated, and the breakdown between cash and credit. A review period has been set for members to flag possible recording errors before payments are finalized.

Nonmembers can continue shopping at the co-op's posted shelf prices, but they do not share in the patronage distribution, which is limited to members. The cooperative sells both locally grown produce and ordinary wholesale goods, and membership benefits are not restricted to purchases from local suppliers.

Rowan Yard was founded by area residents after a privately owned food shop in the area closed, and its board is elected by the membership.

Cooperative officials noted that the distribution paperwork does not determine how any individual member's payment will be treated for tax purposes, and members were encouraged to consult their own advisers on that question.
