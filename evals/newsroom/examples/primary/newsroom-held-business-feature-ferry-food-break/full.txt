# Before boarding, a breakfast counter works to the waterfront's clock

Ceri Lann was packing preordered breakfast rolls before the first ferry crew's boarding call at Selwick Ferry Terminal, according to an Aug. 18 terminal note. Completed orders go on an insulated collection shelf in a counter that occupies a former ticket-information booth.

For the workers collecting them, breakfast has to fit around the job. Deck worker Omi Darsen sometimes orders ahead because boarding checks can leave only a short window to pick up food.

"The useful part is knowing the food is ready when the gap in the work appears," Darsen said.

Lann moved the counter's opening time from 7 a.m. to 5:45 a.m. in May to serve terminal and vessel workers. The earlier start puts the small business closer to the waterfront's working timetable: The first staff shifts begin at 5:30 a.m., while most nearby shops open after 8 a.m. A six-week food-voucher pilot now adds another timetable for Lann to manage — the wait between serving a meal and receiving part of its price.

The ferry operator is offering 40 eligible early-shift employees a $5 food voucher for each worked shift. It can be used at Lann's counter before 9 a.m. The posted breakfast package costs $6.50, including any applicable tax, leaving a worker using one voucher to pay $1.50.

The voucher is not a cash payment added to wages. Unused vouchers expire on the day they are issued, and the pilot does not cover everyone working around the harbor. Its usefulness depends in part on whether an eligible worker can collect food before the cutoff.

Workers can bring their own food, but opportunities to leave the secure work area depend on their duties and sailing schedules. There is a staff break room at the terminal; its opening does not mean every job can pause at once. For Darsen, ordering ahead helps fit collection into the available gap.

For Lann, the voucher portion of a sale arrives on a different schedule. The operator reimburses redeemed vouchers every 14 days, while Lann pays suppliers weekly.

"A voucher is a sale I have made, but it is not cash I can hand to the bread supplier that morning," Lann said.

The kiosk's rent is $850 a month, with electricity billed separately. Lann said early-opening labor costs continue even when a sailing is canceled. Those expenses remain part of the calculation, but Lann has not provided a complete profit comparison for the early counter hours.

The operator has not published evidence that the pilot has improved attendance, productivity or health. A review is due after six weeks, with no commitment yet to extend the vouchers to later shifts or other vendors. The reporting record gives no change to sailing times, security access rules or workers' contractual break entitlements.

For now, the counter's arrangement works within those schedules. Before the first crew's boarding call, Lann packs the preordered rolls, with an insulated shelf ready to hold completed orders until workers can collect them.
