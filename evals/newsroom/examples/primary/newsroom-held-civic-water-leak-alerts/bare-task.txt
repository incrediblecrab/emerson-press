Moorhaven Water to Flag Unusual Usage With New Quarterly Notices

MOORHAVEN — Moorhaven Water will begin mailing notices to customers whose water use spikes sharply compared with a year earlier, starting with the utility's next quarterly meter-reading cycle in October, officials said.

Under the new screening rule, a property will trigger a notice only when consumption for a newly completed billing period is at least twice the consumption recorded for the same period a year earlier, provided that prior-year amount was greater than zero. Properties without a positive comparable reading from the previous year will not receive an alert under this first version of the rule.

Utility staff will check readings for obvious transcription errors before a notice goes out, but the process does not include an inspection of the property. The program relies on existing quarterly readings rather than new continuous-monitoring equipment installed in homes.

Utility director Enna Rook said the comparison is meant only as an initial screening tool, not a determination that something is actually wrong.

"The letter means something changed in the reading; it cannot tell us what changed inside a home," Rook said.

The notice will include a diagram showing how each period's consumption is calculated — by subtracting a valid opening cumulative meter reading from the closing cumulative reading — with both billing periods labeled. It will invite customers to review their reading history and discuss possible explanations with the utility. The utility's own materials cite an extended household stay or additional garden watering as examples of possible explanations, not findings tied to any specific customer.

Receiving a notice will not automatically change a customer's bill or establish eligibility for an adjustment, officials said. The utility's existing adjustment process, which predates the notification program, still requires a separate request along with supporting information, with decisions made case by case.

The city has allocated $19,500 for printing, staff training and evaluation covering the program's first two reading cycles. No estimate of water savings or number of repairs expected to result from the program has been provided.

A tenants group has asked that the notices explain how renters can raise a concern about high usage when they do not control repairs to the property they occupy. Organizer Sol Danner said the notices as planned may not address the position renters are often in.

"Knowing there may be a problem and being able to authorize a repair are different things," Danner said.

Notices will be sent only to the account holder, and the utility said it has not created a new system for automatically sending copies to other occupants of a property.

Officials said the ordinary channels for reporting an obvious leak or fault will remain in place, and customers are not being asked to wait for a quarterly notice if they already suspect a problem.

The utility's first evaluation of the program, expected after the initial two cycles, will track how many notices were issued and how many recipients contacted the utility — not assume that every notice reflects an actual leak. Staff said the screening threshold itself could be revised if it turns out to miss cases worth flagging or generates notices that confuse recipients.

The utility did not provide current household readings, account identifiers or confirmed examples of a customer discovering a leak through the program.

Moorhaven Water's customer-service office still displays a mechanical meter removed from the town's first pumping station.
