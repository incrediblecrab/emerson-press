# Why a $28 used textbook can cost more than a $96 bundle

A $28 used textbook for Fenwick College's autumn introductory accounting course costs $98 when paired with online homework access — $2 more than the campus bookstore's new book-and-platform bundle.

But neither print option is the least expensive route available. A $70 standalone platform code includes a digital textbook. Students can also arrange a no-charge offline assignment route through the course office, then obtain access to the assigned text separately.

As of Aug. 31, the choice depends on whether a student wants a print copy and plans to submit homework through the platform. The used book's lower sticker price covers only part of what a student needs for the online route.

The course uses "Ledgers in Practice" and an associated online problem platform. Instructor Omi Brack says the platform handles scheduled homework submissions, rather than serving merely as optional extra reading. Students who buy only the used book still need platform access unless they arrange the offline alternative.

The bookstore lists the new book-and-platform bundle at $96. Its $28 used print copy does not include an unused platform code, and the publisher charges $70 for a standalone semester code. That makes the used-book-and-code combination $98.

Student Rella Vale wants print because she annotates pages during her commute. For someone with that preference who chooses the online assignment route, the bookstore's new bundle costs less than its used copy paired with separate access.

"The used book is cheaper until I add the thing the assignment asks me to log into," Vale said.

Students comfortable reading digitally do not need to buy a print copy: The standalone code includes a digital version of the assigned textbook. At $70, that option costs $26 less than the new bundle and $28 less than the used-book-and-code combination.

Access also has limits that differ from ownership of a print book. Under the supplied terms, the platform code is assigned to one student and expires at the end of the semester. Buying a used book does not transfer its previous owner's already activated code.

Publisher representative Neri Doss says the separate platform price pays for hosted exercises and updates, rather than only a digital copy of printed pages.

"The book and the account travel together in a bundle, but they are not the same product," Doss said.

The reporting record does not include the publisher's platform costs or profit margin, so it does not establish how much of the $70 charge covers those expenses.

The offline route changes the calculation. Brack offers it at no charge to students who arrange it through the course office. It covers the same assessed topics but requires a different submission process and does not include a personal digital book license.

A student choosing that route could buy the $28 used print copy without adding the $70 platform charge. The college library also has six print reserve copies available for short loans. Those loans provide access to the text, but not platform accounts.

There are 150 students enrolled, and the college has not counted how many will choose each route. The six reserve copies therefore should not be read as a guarantee that a book will be available whenever a student needs one.

These comparisons use the bookstore's August 2026 prices, not prices from every secondhand seller. They also describe this course, not a requirement that every college course use paid access. Fenwick's bookstore runs an annual used-book exchange for courses without single-user access codes. Here, students need to compare the cost of their chosen assignment route and reading format, not the print book alone.
