# Why a $28 used textbook can cost more than Fenwick's $96 bundle

A $28 used textbook looks like the bargain option for students taking Fenwick College's introductory accounting course this fall. But for those who also buy access to its online homework platform, the total is $98 — $2 more than the campus bookstore's new book-and-platform bundle.

The difference comes from what the used book does not include: an unused code for the associated online platform. That distinction matters because instructor Omi Brack uses the platform for scheduled homework submissions, not merely optional extra reading.

Still, buying a bundle is not the only way to meet the course's requirements. Students can buy platform access without a print book or arrange a no-charge offline assignment route through the course office. The least expensive choice depends on how they plan to read and submit their work.

## What do the prices cover?

As of Aug. 31, the bookstore's August 2026 listings put a new copy of “Ledgers in Practice” bundled with platform access at $96. A used print copy costs $28, while the publisher's standalone semester platform code costs $70.

For a student who wants both print and online access, the bookstore's bundle therefore costs less than its used copy combined with a separate code.

The $70 standalone code also includes a digital version of the textbook. Students who are comfortable reading digitally do not need to purchase a print copy to read the assigned text. For them, standalone access costs $26 less than the bundle.

Print remains important to some students. Rella Vale wants a physical copy because she annotates pages during her commute.

“The used book is cheaper until I add the thing the assignment asks me to log into,” Vale said.

## Why doesn't access come with a used book?

Under the supplied terms, a platform code is assigned to one student and expires at the end of the semester. Buying that student's used textbook does not transfer an already activated code.

Publisher representative Neri Doss says the separate platform price pays for hosted exercises and updates, rather than only a digital reproduction of printed pages.

“The book and the account travel together in a bundle, but they are not the same product,” Doss said.

That explains the publisher's distinction between the products, but it does not establish how much providing the platform costs. The reporting record contains no figures for the publisher's platform expenses or profit margin.

## Can students avoid paying for access?

Yes, if they arrange the instructor's no-charge offline assignment route through the course office. It covers the same assessed topics but uses a different submission process.

That option does not include a personal digital textbook license. Students would still need a way to read the assigned text, such as a used print copy or a library reserve copy.

The library has six print reserve copies available for short loans. Those loans do not provide platform accounts, and short-term borrowing is different from having a personal copy available throughout the semester.

## How broadly does this apply?

The course has 150 enrolled students, but the college has not counted how many will choose bundles, standalone access or offline assignments. The bookstore's listed prices also do not establish what every secondhand seller charges.

Nor do these policies show that every college course requires paid online access. Fenwick's bookstore runs an annual used-book exchange for courses without single-user access codes. For this accounting course, the key comparison is the total cost of the reading materials and submission route a student chooses — not the print book's price alone.
