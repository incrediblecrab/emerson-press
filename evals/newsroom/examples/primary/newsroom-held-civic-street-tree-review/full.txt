# How to comment on Spurwick's proposed street-tree removals

Spurwick residents have until Oct. 6 to comment on 12 proposed street-tree removals, submitting photographs, local observations or questions about alternatives before the parks office makes its decisions.

The notices were posted Sept. 15 under the city's 21-day public review process. Residents can respond through the city form at https://example.org/spurwick/tree-review or make a paper submission at the parks office. They do not need to hire a specialist to comment.

An objection requires the city to consider the information submitted, but it does not automatically stop a removal. The parks office can request another inspection or revise a proposal if a comment provides relevant information that the initial report did not address.

## Which trees are under review?

The parks office published recommendations concerning 14 street trees after its latest scheduled inspections. Two trees with storm-split trunks were assigned immediate work under the city's emergency-work procedure. That work is outside the comment timetable for the other 12 trees.

The public review applies to city-managed street trees, not every privately owned tree visible from the road. Each notice identifies the tree, the recorded reason for recommending removal and the city office that will receive comments.

Reasons in the current notices include conflicting root and pavement conditions, damage recorded by inspectors and unsuitable space for a tree's mature form. A removal recommendation does not, by itself, establish that a tree is unsafe. The supplied record does not establish that all 12 trees are unsafe or that every proposal will remain unchanged.

## What makes a useful comment?

Start with the notice for the tree you want to discuss. A response can address its recorded reason for removal, add observations or photographs, or ask whether an alternative has been considered.

City arborist Tera Venn said, "A useful response tells us what we have missed, not simply whether a tree is loved."

The distinction matters because the review considers the information residents provide. A comment count is not a binding vote. Relevant information missing from the first report can lead to another inspection or a revised proposal, though neither result is automatic.

The parks office has also arranged a drop-in session to explain the notices. That session is not an on-site tree-safety assessment.

## Will removed trees be replaced?

Replacement planting is considered separately, based on whether a site is suitable. There is no promise that a new tree can occupy the same position as one removed.

A street association has asked the city to explain replacement planting alongside removal decisions rather than after the work is finished.

Association member Orrin Saye said, "We are looking at the shade over a whole street, not only at the space around one trunk."

Several notice tags are attached to trees planted in a municipal street scheme more than 40 years ago. The current notices do not include a total forecast of future tree-canopy loss or growth, so they do not settle the association's broader question about street-wide shade.

## What happens after comments close?

After considering submissions, the parks office will publish a decision record for the 12 recommendations under review. It will state the final action and the main reasons.

For residents who want information considered before those decisions, Oct. 6 is the deadline to respond. This process is Spurwick's current local policy, not a statement of tree-removal law elsewhere.
