Cinder Vale to subsidize three vacant shopfronts for one-year arts and repair leases

CINDER VALE — Three groups will open a printmaking studio, a clothing-repair workshop and a cooperative exhibition space on Foundry Street under the city's first subsidized short-term leases for empty storefronts, the development office said.

The leases, scheduled to begin Dec. 1 pending completion of agreed building work, are the outcome of a selection process that drew 36 applications for the three vacant premises, which have sat empty for periods ranging from nine months to nearly three years.

Under the arrangement, the city will pay each property owner $750 a month while each participating group pays the city $300 a month, a combined subsidy across all three sites of $16,200 over 12 months. The city separately set aside $24,000 for shared building work, including electrical checks and adjustments to make entrances accessible.

The selection panel weighed proposed public hours, the feasibility of each group's operating plan and how well each applicant fit its available space, the development office said. It did not rank proposals by projected visitor numbers, saying applicants' forecasts were too inconsistent to compare.

"A lit window is a start; the agreement also asks what people can actually do when they walk inside," said Nelda Venn, the development director.

Each group must open to the public at least 12 hours a week, though members may work inside at other times. The requirement covers public-facing activity generally and does not require every workshop or event to be free of charge.

The three property owners retain ownership of their buildings and are not committing to renew the leases after the first year.

Rovi Pell, who will run the printmaking studio, said the arrangement offers a start but not certainty. "An affordable room lets us start, but moving the presses again in a year is still part of the calculation," Pell said.

At least one unsuccessful applicant has asked the city to release feedback explaining how building suitability affected the decisions. The development office said it plans to give written feedback to all applicants and publish a summary of the selection process, without disclosing individual financial information.

The groups are responsible for arranging their own equipment, insurance and routine operating costs under the terms of the lease agreement. Existing shops on Foundry Street remain eligible to propose joint events with the new tenants, though the subsidy itself is limited to the three selected vacant premises.

At the exhibition space, a faded tile sign left over from a former shoe shop will remain above the entrance at the property owner's request.

The city said it will review actual opening hours, activities and building costs after six months. Officials said the program's effect on nearby retail sales or on whether any of the spaces lead to permanent commercial leases has not been established.

Any expansion beyond the three sites would require a separate budget decision, the city said; the current leases do not automatically authorize additional locations.
