Cinder Vale Picks Three Groups for Subsidized Shopfront Leases on Foundry Street

CINDER VALE — The city's development office has selected three groups to occupy long-vacant storefronts on Foundry Street under the city's first subsidized short-term lease program, officials said this week.

A printmaking studio, a clothing-repair workshop and a cooperative exhibition space were chosen from 36 applicants for one-year leases set to begin Dec. 1, pending completion of building work required under the agreements.

Under the arrangement, the city will pay each property owner $750 a month, while the participating groups pay the city $300 a month, according to the development office. The rent subsidy for all three premises totals $16,200 over the year. The city has separately allocated $24,000 for shared building work, including electrical checks and adjustments to make entrances accessible.

Development director Nelda Venn said the program is meant to bring more than foot traffic to the empty storefronts.

"A lit window is a start; the agreement also asks what people can actually do when they walk inside," Venn said.

The selection panel weighed public opening hours, the feasibility of each applicant's operating plan and how well a proposed use fit the available space, Venn's office said. The panel did not rank proposals by projected visitor counts, saying applicants' forecasts were too inconsistent to compare.

Each selected group must be open to the public at least 12 hours a week, though members may work inside outside those hours. The program requires a public-facing activity but does not require that admission to every workshop or event be free.

Rovi Pell, a printmaker whose studio was chosen, said the arrangement offers a chance to start operating, not a guarantee of permanence.

"An affordable room lets us start, but moving the presses again in a year is still part of the calculation," Pell said.

The three property owners retain ownership of their buildings and are not committing to renew the leases after the first year, the development office said. The storefronts have sat vacant for periods ranging from nine months to nearly three years.

At the exhibition space, a faded tile sign from a former shoe shop will remain above the entrance at the owner's request.

Groups selected for the program must arrange their own equipment, insurance and routine operating costs under the terms of the lease agreements supplied by the city.

At least one unsuccessful applicant has asked the city to release feedback explaining how building suitability affected the panel's decisions. The development office said it plans to give written feedback to all applicants and publish a summary of the selection process, without disclosing individual applicants' financial information.

Existing shops on Foundry Street remain eligible to propose joint events with the new tenants, though the city said the subsidy itself is limited to the three selected vacant premises.

City officials said they have not established that the program will raise sales at nearby businesses or lead to permanent commercial leases once the subsidized year ends. The development office plans to review actual opening hours, activity levels and building costs after six months.

Any expansion of the program to additional storefronts would require a new budget decision, officials said; the first three leases do not automatically authorize further sites.
