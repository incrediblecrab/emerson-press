# TeleLink says fiber rollout reached 78,000 Clearwater County homes ahead of schedule

TeleLink Rural Networks announced Aug. 22 that it had completed fiber-optic broadband connections to 78,000 previously unserved households in Clearwater County, eight months ahead of its contract schedule.

The $890 million project brings service with equal upload and download speeds of 1 gigabit per second at a monthly retail price of $65. A required low-income plan costs $30 a month and had enrolled 9,200 households as of August.

The rollout addresses a broadband gap documented since 2015, after two earlier proposals failed because of insufficient funding. Before the project, 74% of the county's rural households had access only to satellite or fixed wireless internet with speeds below 25 megabits per second.

Federal and state funding covered the project: a $440 million federal broadband equity grant and $450 million in state infrastructure bonds. The average project cost was approximately $11,400 per household served.

Crews installed 2,300 kilometers of fiber cable and 140 distribution nodes. For 84% of connections, they strung cable along existing utility poles, reducing permitting time and underground excavation costs.

"We finished early because we standardized the aerial installation process and trained crews to achieve 150 connections per day per team rather than the industry standard of 90," TeleLink CEO Rachel Auer said.

Workforce development officials said 140 of the 380 installation crew members hired for the project were from Clearwater County. Training was provided through a TeleLink partnership program.

Subscriptions exceeded the contract's target. Within the first 90 days, 61% of connected households had subscribed, compared with a goal of 50% within 18 months. The federal grant includes a repayment provision if fewer than 40% of connected households subscribe within five years.

Local education and healthcare officials reported changes as broadband became available.

The county school district said 4,200 students gained home broadband access through the project. It said that access eliminated a documented gap in homework completion rates between rural and town students.

Healthcare officials said telehealth visit rates among county residents in newly connected households increased threefold during the first six months of availability. The reporting notes did not provide the underlying visit rates or counts.

Clearwater County Board Chair James Moran said two agricultural businesses had relocated operations from neighboring counties in anticipation of the fiber project. He credited the connection with enabling precision agriculture monitoring.

TeleLink built the infrastructure under a 25-year indefeasible right-of-use agreement with the county. Ownership of the fiber network will transfer to the county when the agreement ends.

Auer said finishing early will allow TeleLink to redeploy installation teams to a second project in another state, improving the company's capital efficiency.

Congress authorized the federal broadband equity grant program in 2021. Clearwater County's project is among the first 40 completions nationally under the program. Two neighboring counties with similar broadband gaps are expected to begin their own federally funded projects in 2025.
