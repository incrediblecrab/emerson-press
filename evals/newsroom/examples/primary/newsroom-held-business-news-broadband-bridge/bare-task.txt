**Moorlace Broadband Signs First Construction Contract, but 170 Premises Left Off the Map**

MOORLACE — Moorlace Broadband Cooperative awarded its first fiber construction contract Thursday to Pella Trenchworks, marking the start of an $8.4 million buildout even as 170 farms and homes beyond the Greyfin road bridge remain outside the signed route.

The contract covers a path passing 620 premises — 240 businesses and 380 homes — and is financed by a $5 million rural-infrastructure grant combined with a $3.4 million cooperative loan, according to the cooperative.

An earlier proposed route had covered 790 premises, including the 170 farms and homes across the bridge. Those properties were dropped after the bridge's owner declined to approve attaching a cable conduit to the structure. The cooperative said it has requested an engineering study of an alternative river crossing but does not yet have a construction price for that option.

General Manager Daro Senn said the contract represents a starting point rather than a finish line for the project.

"Signing the trenching contract gives us a route to build, not a date for every front door," Senn said.

Construction is scheduled to begin Sept. 14, with the first customer connections expected in spring 2027. Senn cautioned that actual service dates will depend on road-opening permits and access to individual properties. Even where the cable passes, a property is not automatically connected — owners must order service and approve installation.

The cooperative's residential package is priced at $59 a month with a $149 standard installation charge. A separate business package, which includes priority fault response, costs $95 a month.

For businesses on the built side of the river, the contract offers the prospect of more reliable connections. Neri Voss, a farm-equipment dealer whose workshop sits within the contracted area, said unreliable uploads currently force the business to carry large diagnostic files to a supplier on a portable drive. But several of Voss's customers who rely on remote diagnostics are located beyond the bridge, outside the new route.

For residents on the far side of the river, Thursday's announcement offered less certainty. Farmer Yara Tern, who helped recruit members to the cooperative, said the outcome felt incomplete.

"I helped sign people up for this, and I am still on the far side of the map," Tern said.

The cooperative said members beyond the bridge retain full voting rights and will not be billed for broadband service until a connection is actually installed. The co-op has 412 members overall, a figure that does not match the premises count in either the contracted or the earlier proposed route, reflecting the difference between membership and individual serviceable locations.

Existing wireless providers in the area will continue operating, and officials said the new fiber contract does not require any resident to switch services.

Moorlace Broadband Cooperative formed in 2022 after a group of small employers surveyed local difficulties sending payroll and design files over existing connections, laying the groundwork for the cooperative model now being built out.

As of Thursday, no performance tests have been conducted on the still-unbuilt fiber network, and the cooperative has not released estimates of potential household savings from switching to the new service.

The unresolved status of the Greyfin crossing leaves the timeline for the 170 excluded premises undetermined. Cooperative officials did not offer a date by which the engineering study or a funding path for an alternative crossing might be completed.
