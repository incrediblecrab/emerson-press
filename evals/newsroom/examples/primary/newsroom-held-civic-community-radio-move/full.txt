# Mossbank Community Radio plans December transmitter move to water-tower site

Mossbank Community Radio plans to move its transmitter to the civic water-tower site in December, ahead of the expiration of its warehouse roof lease, while keeping its frequency and program schedule.

The cooperative's lease at the privately owned warehouse ends Dec. 31 because the owner is converting the roof area as part of a building redevelopment. The exact broadcast outage window will depend on commissioning the equipment at the new site.

Under the relocation plan, the studio will remain in the community arts building. The move affects the equipment that sends the broadcast signal, rather than the workplace where presenters produce programs. Listeners will not need to find a new frequency, though some may notice changes in reception.

The new mounting position is 40 feet higher than the current one. That does not guarantee better reception at every address, according to the engineering note. Hills and buildings between the transmitter and listeners mean the cooperative expects reception patterns to change.

"Keeping the same number on the dial does not mean every kitchen radio will hear us in exactly the same way," station coordinator Siva Dorne said.

The cooperative plans to conduct reception checks after commissioning and invite listeners to report persistent problems. As of Sept. 18, those checks had not been completed, and no new coverage map had been certified. The effect of the move on reception at individual addresses remains uncertain.

The station's internet stream will remain available during most transmitter work, subject to its ordinary studio connection. It is an alternative for listeners with internet access, but it will not serve everyone who relies on the broadcast.

"The stream is useful, but some of our listeners know us through a battery radio and nothing else," volunteer presenter Arin Pell said.

The station plans advance on-air notices about the changeover, along with printed notices at community centers for listeners who do not use its website. An exact outage window has not yet been set.

The city has approved a five-year site-use agreement for the water-tower location. The cooperative will remain responsible for its equipment and broadcast operations. The agreement does not make the station a city-owned outlet or give the city approval over program content.

A $57,000 relocation fund combines listener donations and a community-media grant. The cooperative has not published a forecast of future audience growth.

The station's local bulletin includes council notices, school events and interviews with neighborhood organizations. It has not announced staff reductions or a reduction in locally produced hours connected with the move.

The warehouse owner has agreed to keep power available to the old installation during the scheduled changeover period. If commissioning runs beyond the Dec. 31 lease deadline, however, a contingency extension at the warehouse would require another agreement.
