# Mossbank Community Radio plans December transmitter move to water tower

Mossbank Community Radio plans to move its transmitter to the civic water-tower site in December, ahead of the expiration of its warehouse rooftop lease, while keeping its broadcast frequency, program schedule and studio location unchanged.

The cooperative must leave the privately owned warehouse roof because the owner is converting the space as part of a building redevelopment. The lease ends Dec. 31. The exact window for a broadcast outage during the changeover will depend on equipment commissioning.

The city has approved a five-year site-use agreement for the water-tower location. The cooperative will remain responsible for its equipment and broadcast operations. The agreement does not make the station a city-owned outlet or give the city approval over program content.

For listeners, the move will not require finding a new frequency. But the higher transmitter position does not assure that reception will stay the same or improve at every home.

The new mounting position is 40 feet higher than the existing one. Hills and buildings between the transmitter and listeners mean some reception patterns are expected to change, and the engineering note does not guarantee better reception at every address.

“Keeping the same number on the dial does not mean every kitchen radio will hear us in exactly the same way,” station coordinator Siva Dorne said.

The cooperative plans to conduct reception checks after commissioning and invite listeners to report persistent problems. As of Sept. 18, those checks had not been completed, and no new coverage map had been certified.

A $57,000 relocation fund combines listener donations and a community-media grant. The cooperative has not published a forecast of future audience growth tied to the move.

The studio will remain in the community arts building, meaning the project relocates the transmission equipment rather than the presenters’ workplace. The station has not announced staff reductions or cuts in locally produced hours connected with the move. Its local bulletin includes council notices, school events and interviews with neighborhood organizations.

The station’s internet stream is expected to remain available during most transmitter work, subject to its ordinary studio connection. That offers an alternative for listeners with internet access, but it will not serve everyone who relies on the broadcast signal.

“The stream is useful, but some of our listeners know us through a battery radio and nothing else,” volunteer presenter Arin Pell said.

The cooperative plans advance on-air notices about the changeover, along with printed notices at community centers for listeners who do not use its website. The timing of the broadcast interruption remains unsettled while commissioning determines the outage window.

The warehouse owner has agreed to keep power available to the old installation during the scheduled changeover period. If commissioning runs beyond the Dec. 31 lease deadline, however, a contingency extension at the warehouse would require another agreement.
