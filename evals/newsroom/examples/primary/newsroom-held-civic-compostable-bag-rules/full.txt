Before drafting, let me quickly confirm structural approach given the AP explainer format and length target.# Why some 'compostable' bags aren't allowed in Elmharbor's food-scrap bins

Elmharbor's food-scrap collection service sends everything it picks up to a single contracted processing site, and that site accepts only what's on its approved list — a fact that has confused residents as stores begin stocking multiple bags labeled compostable alongside ordinary garbage bags.

The current list permits loose food scraps, plain paper liners and the uncoated paper bags distributed through the city program. It does not include plastic-like compostable bags, even when the packaging carries a general compostability claim.

According to the processor's operating note, its screening and processing schedule isn't set up to distinguish and handle every certified bag formulation that reaches its facility. The city says that limitation is the reason for the exclusion, not a finding that every rejected bag's label is false. "Our crews need a rule they can apply to the material in the bin, not a packaging claim they have time to research at the curb," said collection manager Enna Pell.

The clarification followed residents asking city officials to explain the mismatch after shops began selling several differently labeled liners next to standard trash bags, according to the city.

Starting with this update, the city and participating shops will post the local acceptance list side by side, so a bag's placement on a store shelf doesn't imply it belongs in the food-scrap bin.

The paper liners sold under the city program are optional. Households can skip them and use the city's loose-scrap collection method instead.

Bins found with unaccepted packaging may get a notice asking the resident to remove the material before the next regular collection. A rejected bin is not automatically a fine — the city has not announced any new financial penalty as part of this clarification.

Excluded liners should not go into the recycling bin instead. The food-scrap program is separate from ordinary recycling, and the city's waste guide, which is being updated alongside the bin notices, lays out where excluded packaging should go.

**Shared bins present a separate problem.** In buildings with communal collection, a notice left on a bin may not reach whoever actually added the contaminating bag. "A notice on the lid reaches the next person who opens it, not necessarily the person who put the bag there," said apartment manager Roven Dale. Apartment managers can request a demonstration session for shared-bin users to address that gap. The processor reports contamination by incoming load, not by individual household, so a flagged load doesn't identify a specific resident or establish sorting habits across the building.

The city has not released a citywide figure for how many bins are sorted correctly, and officials have not quantified how much this clarification is expected to improve that rate.

The list isn't necessarily permanent. The city says it will review what materials are accepted if the processor's equipment or its contract changes. A bag manufacturer can submit product information for that review, but a submission doesn't by itself change what collectors will take.

Finished compost from the program goes into contracted landscaping projects after standard quality checks — it is not returned as a finished product to individual households that contributed scraps.

Residents may also notice differing rules at community gardens, some of which post their own compost guidelines because they run smaller-scale processes with different tolerances. The city's clarification applies to its municipal collection contract; it does not require gardens to adopt the same list.
