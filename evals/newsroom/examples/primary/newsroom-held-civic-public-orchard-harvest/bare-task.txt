Wrenfold Authorizes First Organized Harvest of Public Orchard Fruit

WRENFOLD — The city parks department has authorized the Orchard Table cooperative to organize the first coordinated harvest of fruit from the East Fold public orchard this fall, under a one-season agreement covering 36 marked trees.

The arrangement, confirmed as of Thursday, does not extend to every tree or ornamental planting in the surrounding park, and it does not constitute a sale of the trees or a transfer of the park to the cooperative. Parks staff will identify which trees are included in each harvest session and will continue to be responsible for tree maintenance.

Harvest sessions are planned for October, though exact dates depend on the condition of the fruit and the availability of volunteers, according to the cooperative.

Under the agreement, harvested fruit by weight will be divided three ways: half will go to partner food pantries, one-quarter to the volunteers who take part in the harvest sessions as a group, and one-quarter to community cooking sessions. The cooperative said it will record harvested weight and how the fruit was distributed after each session, but no harvest total is yet available.

"The fruit is already part of the place; the new work is agreeing who gathers it and where it goes," said Vera Linn, an organizer with the Orchard Table cooperative.

The cooperative said picking will be limited to its registered volunteer sessions rather than open to unrestricted picking from every tree in the park. Any equipment use or work above ground level during harvest sessions will fall under the parks department's supervised-session rules.

A nearby residents group asked that the orchard remain available for ordinary walks while harvesting is underway. Under the plan, the orchard's main path will stay open, though short, marked restrictions may be placed around individual work areas during sessions.

"It is a harvest site for a few hours, but it is still our path to the rest of the park," said resident representative Darin Cole.

Partner pantries will decide on each delivery day what quantities of fruit they can accept, rather than receive a fixed consignment automatically, the cooperative said. It has set aside storage crates and transport funds for the effort, though the city has not provided a commercial value estimate for the fruit expected to be harvested.

The orchard includes several fruit varieties listed in an old planting record, and identification of some individual trees has not yet been completed, according to the cooperative.

Organizers noted the effort has not been certified organic, and no nutritional assessment of the distribution program has been conducted. Separately, the city's litter team has in the past removed fallen fruit from orchard paths, but its records do not establish how much of that fruit could otherwise have been recovered for use.

After the season concludes, the cooperative said it will report on distribution totals and any practical problems encountered to the parks committee. Any continuation of the harvest program next year would require a new agreement based on that review.

A local school has offered to make labels for the cooperative's storage crates but is not taking responsibility for organizing the harvest itself.

The city has not announced plans to open additional orchards to organized harvesting, nor has it promised that all partner pantries will receive fruit on a weekly basis.
