**Ashcombe Water Board Reshapes Commercial Rates, and Bills Won't All Move the Same Way**

ASHCOMBE — Starting Oct. 1, businesses across Ashcombe will see a new commercial water tariff take effect, but the change will not push every bill in the same direction. Whether a company pays more or less depends largely on how much water it uses each month, according to figures released after the Ashcombe Water Board's August budget decision.

Under the new schedule, the monthly fixed commercial charge rises from $35 to $50, while the usage charge drops from $3 to $2.50 per 1,000 gallons. The combination means smaller water users will generally see their water-only bills climb, while larger users will generally see theirs fall — a shift that pivots at 30,000 gallons a month, where the old and new formulas both produce a $125 water-only charge, board figures show.

Below that usage level, the new formula costs more; above it, the new formula costs less, before other charges are factored in. For an account using 20,000 gallons a month, holding usage constant, the change means a water-only bill of $100 instead of $95. For an account using 200,000 gallons, the same math produces $550 instead of $635. Both figures exclude sewer charges, which are billed separately under a schedule that is not changing.

The board's budget does not project the same percentage change for every commercial customer's bill, and businesses with more than one meter will continue to be charged the fixed fee separately on each account, as they are now.

Board finance officer Tera Linn said the redesign was meant to shift how the system recovers its costs. "We changed the balance between keeping the system ready and charging for each gallon," Linn said. The board says the larger fixed charge is intended to collect more revenue for pipes and treatment capacity that must be maintained regardless of how much water customers use in a given month.

For Deni Rook, owner of Mallow Street Bakery, the change lands on the side of the ledger that goes up. The bakery averages about 20,000 gallons a month and cannot lower its fixed charge simply by cutting back on water use. "The first fifty dollars arrives before we turn on a tap," Rook said. She added that she still sees value in fixing leaks, since the usage portion of the bill remains in place and continues to reward lower consumption.

Vale Washworks, a linen processor that typically uses around 200,000 gallons a month, sits on the other side of the crossover point on paper. But the company's manager cautioned that a lower tariff at constant usage does not guarantee a lower actual bill, given how much the business's water use swings from season to season. A slow month could land well under 200,000 gallons; a busy one could push well past it, changing the calculation each time.

The board is offering account-specific estimates based on customers' monthly readings from the previous year, though officials stressed those estimates are not a guarantee of future consumption or future bills.

Residential customers are not affected by the change and will remain on their current tariff. The board also said the update does not include any new mandatory meter installation fee and does not involve any change to water quality.

For now, Ashcombe businesses have a month to review their usage patterns before the new rates take hold Oct. 1 — and, as the board's own numbers show, no single answer for whether their bills will rise or fall.
