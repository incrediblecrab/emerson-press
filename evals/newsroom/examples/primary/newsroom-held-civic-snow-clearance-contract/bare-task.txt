# Northrill splits winter snow clearing between two contractors

Northrill Council has awarded its next winter snow-clearing work to two contractors, separating road clearance from designated pedestrian routes under contracts with a combined base price of $312,000.

The arrangement replaces the previous season's combined contract and puts coordination between the two operators under one city duty officer. The contracts begin Nov. 1 and run through March 31, 2027. Any extension will require approval.

The road-clearing contract is $188,000, while the pedestrian-route contract is $124,000. Together, their base prices are $22,000, or about 7.6%, higher than the previous season's $290,000 combined base contract.

Those figures are not a comparison of total winter spending. Last season's base contract differed from the amount ultimately paid for all weather-related callouts. The new base prices cover standby capacity and specified routine deployments, with exceptional additional work billed at separately agreed rates. The approved award report contains no forecast of how many major snow events the coming season will bring.

The road contractor will clear the priority vehicle network. The second contractor will handle designated footways and public-building approaches, including school approaches, the civic center and the main bus interchange. Its assignment does not cover every sidewalk in the city.

Operations manager Elen Marr highlighted the importance of connecting the two networks.

“A cleared road with an uncleared crossing can still leave someone unable to finish the journey,” Marr said.

The duty officer will coordinate deployments and resolve gaps where road and footway responsibilities meet. Both contractors must report completed sections to that officer rather than assume the other operator has cleared connecting crossings.

A mobility group has asked the council to show crossing points and bus-stop approaches explicitly on the pedestrian route maps. The final maps will be published before the contracts begin Nov. 1.

“The last few yards are not a small detail when they are the yards you cannot get through,” said Pera Voss, a representative of the group.

The council has not promised that every listed route will be clear by the same hour after every snowfall. Deployment order will depend on conditions reported to the duty officer and the priority network set out in the contracts.

A public contact line will accept reports of blocked designated routes. Those reports will enter the coordinated work queue, however, rather than guarantee an immediate visit.

The former combined contractor did not submit a bid this year. The council has not described the change as a dismissal for misconduct.

City crews will continue maintaining grit bins and carrying out smaller tasks outside the contractors' main routes. Before winter operations begin, the depot will hold a joint route familiarization session for the two contractors.

At a midseason review, the council will compare completed work records with public reports.
