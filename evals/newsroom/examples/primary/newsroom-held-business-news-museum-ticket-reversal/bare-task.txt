# Dunveil Museum drops admission increase, turns to gift and cuts to narrow budget gap

Dunveil Museum’s trustees withdrew a planned admission increase Thursday, keeping the standard ticket price at $8 while turning to a $70,000 gift and spending cuts to address most of a projected $120,000 operating gap.

The reversal means admission for visitors 16 and older will not rise to $11 on Oct. 1, as the museum announced July 23. Visitors younger than 16 will continue to enter free. The proposed increase never took effect.

The decision leaves the museum with difficult choices over access and staffing. Trustees expect to save $30,000 by closing the temporary-exhibition gallery on Wednesdays and reducing outside catalog printing. Together with the newly confirmed gift, those measures would leave a projected $20,000 gap for which the museum has not identified a complete funding plan.

“The gift lets us keep the entry price where it is. It does not remove every difficult choice in the budget,” Director Elna Reed said.

The $120,000 operating shortfall is the museum’s projection for the coming financial year at the unchanged $8 admission price, before the gift and savings are applied. The announced measures therefore narrow the gap but do not balance the projected budget.

The donor requested anonymity. The gift is unrestricted operating support, and the supplied agreement imposes no requirement concerning exhibition themes.

The Wednesday closure of the temporary gallery will begin Oct. 7 and be reviewed after six months. The permanent collection, reading room and cafe will remain open on Wednesdays, so the change will not amount to a weekly closure of the entire museum.

Still, the revised schedule will affect some visitors who have already made plans. Local tour organizer Pella Sorn said some prebooked Wednesday groups had expected access to a temporary textile exhibition. The museum will offer those groups an alternative date or a refund of their group-booking payment.

“An unchanged ticket price is useful news, but my visitors also need to know which doors will be open,” Sorn said.

Reed said no permanent posts would be cut under the announced savings plan, although casual gallery shifts would be reduced. The museum has not yet published the number of casual hours affected, leaving the extent of that staffing reduction unclear.

The museum counted 42,600 visits last year, but that figure includes free school visits and repeat admissions. Its record does not specify how many visitors paid the standard rate. Multiplying the total number of visits by the proposed $3 increase would therefore not establish how much revenue the museum is giving up by withdrawing it.

Housed in a former customs building, Dunveil Museum has charged the same adult admission price since 2021. Thursday’s decision preserves that price for the next financial year, but trustees have not committed to maintaining it beyond then.

For now, the revised plan keeps admission unchanged while reducing temporary-gallery access and some casual work. The six-month review will revisit the Wednesday gallery closure; the remaining $20,000 budget gap still requires a funding plan.
