# Wrenstack to combine publishers' books under new shipping terms

Wrenstack Distribution will let bookstores combine books from different publishers to qualify for free shipping starting Oct. 1, a change that could ease deliveries for some shops while leaving smaller orders subject to a $15 charge.

The company notified bookstore customers Friday, Aug. 21, that shipments totaling at least 50 copies will qualify for free freight. Orders below that threshold will carry the shipping charge regardless of the books' combined retail value.

The new terms replace an arrangement requiring stores to order 25 copies from each individual publisher for that publisher's shipment to qualify for free freight. Wrenstack represents 38 small publishers and supplies 214 independent shops in its region. The policy covers only books from those publishers, not every title a participating bookstore sells.

The practical effect will depend on how many copies a shop needs and how widely its orders are spread across publishers. Stores will no longer have to meet a separate minimum for each publisher, but an order of 25 copies from a single publisher will no longer qualify for free shipping on its own.

Book trade discounts and the existing return window will remain unchanged.

Distribution director Fenna Sore said a new shared warehouse inventory system made mixed-publisher packing possible. Wrenstack has not released an estimate of aggregate bookstore savings or additional warehouse labor costs.

For Elbow Lane Books, the combined threshold is below its current weekly volume. Owner Teri Moss said the shop orders about 60 copies a week across six Wrenstack publishers. Moss expects one mixed shipment to reduce the number of small deliveries she must receive and unpack.

That volume could qualify for free freight if combined in one shipment. Shops placing smaller orders face a different calculation: pay for dispatch, wait to gather more orders or buy additional stock to reach the threshold.

Dune Pocket Books often orders 10 or fewer copies for customer requests and does not have room to hold large spare stocks. Owner Ivo Keld said waiting to build a 50-copy order could slow special orders.

"The shelf space is real money too, even when the delivery line says free," Keld said.

Wrenstack will allow shops to keep a combined order open for up to seven calendar days before dispatch. Shops can request earlier dispatch, with the $15 charge applying only if the shipment contains fewer than 50 copies. That gives stores time to add books, though it does not remove the trade-off Keld described between prompt delivery and meeting the freight-free minimum.

For publishers, combining orders could give slower-selling books a route into shipments that would previously have depended on demand for one publisher's list. Small publisher Asha Vale said a mixed order could place her slower-selling poetry titles beside larger publishers' releases.

"I want the book to travel with the order, not wait until it can fill a box by itself," Vale said.

Wrenstack has scheduled two customer webinars on the changes. Shops will not be required to use automated reordering.
