Bay Cross Transit’s advisory board has recommended cutting the Saturday Loop from six departures each Saturday to four, but the change is not approved and the current timetable stays in place for now.

The board voted 5-3 for the proposal, which would keep the route, stops and fares the same while reducing only Saturday service. Under the plan, the loop would still run from the station through Yard Square and Cliff Terrace and back to the station, but station departures would shift from the current six trips at 8 a.m., 10 a.m., noon, 2 p.m., 4 p.m. and 6 p.m. to four trips at 8 a.m., 10 a.m., 4 p.m. and 6 p.m.

That would create a six-hour gap between the 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. departures from the station. The gap refers to the spacing between departures, not the time it takes the bus to complete the loop. The board paper says the reason for the proposed reduction is driver availability for the middle shift.

“This is a recommendation, not a timetable,” the board chair said in the minutes supplied with the packet.

The funding committee must approve the change before any new schedule can take effect. The operating team also must provide a staffing plan before the committee can act on the proposal. The committee is scheduled to consider it on May 22, 2026, and the packet does not record any outcome.

Even if approval comes, the service’s process requires at least 14 days of public notice before an approved timetable change begins. No start date has been announced, and stop panels are to keep showing the current timetable until an authorized replacement is issued.

Public comments are open until May 15 at https://example.org/bay-cross/saturday-comments. Those submissions can be considered by planners, but they are not ballots and do not determine the timetable. The packet also does not announce any alternative midday shuttle.

A written comment from the Bay Cross Access Circle said: “A six-hour gap would change whether a same-day visit is practical.” The group asked planners to think about passengers who need a midday return, not only the first and last departures.

The recommendation concerns Saturday service only and does not address weekday or Sunday schedules. The packet supplied for the board meeting does not include boarding counts or estimated savings, so it does not say how many riders use the noon and 2 p.m. trips or what financial effect the reduction might have.
