# Citation: Bluebook 22

Use this adapter for requested legal citation mechanics, not as authority for a legal conclusion. The forum's adopted edition and local rules control; an older adopted edition is not automatically an error. The official site did not expose the full rules during this review. Treat disputed edition-change claims as verification tasks, not certified requirements.

Keep the actual authority, source type, quoted meaning and locator. Never invent a reporter page, archive URL, saved artifact or claim of checking current law to complete a citation.

## Detect

- **Bluepages and Whitepages conventions mixed.** Briefs and memoranda use the Bluepages; law-review footnotes use the Whitepages. Do not leave citation signals or short forms in plain roman in a brief unless the signal is part of a prose sentence.
- **An assumed edition change overriding the forum's actual rule.** Verify new signal forms and other claimed migrations before imposing or rejecting them.
- **A signal where none belongs.** `See` implies an inferential step. An authority that states the proposition outright, or supplies a quotation, takes no signal at all.
- **Signals from different categories in one citation sentence.** Support, comparison, contradiction and background each start a new sentence.
- **`Id.` after a string cite**, or across an intervening authority. Either makes the referent ambiguous, and the Bluebook forbids both.
- **`Supra` for a case, statute, constitution, regulation, restatement, model code, uniform act or ordinary legislative material.** It is available for secondary sources and for congressional hearings, not for those primary authorities.
- **A short form with an unclear referent.** Do not apply a supposed five-footnote limit to every authority type without checking its scope.
- **An archive or `on file` assertion with no supporting record.** Whether preservation is required is a separate rule question; a plausible-looking link is not preservation.
- **Quotation cleanup based on an unverified parenthetical rule.** The treatment of `(cleaned up)` and `(citation modified)` requires the applicable manual and forum's instructions.
- **Education-law authority filtered through cases only.** Policy prose that relies on an enacted rule should usually cite the statute, regulation or agency guidance directly, not just a case describing it.
- **A missing pincite.** A citation to a case for a specific proposition points at the page, not just the first page.
- **An alteration inside a quotation left unmarked**, or a cleanup label used to conceal a substantive change.

## Write

Decide which format governs. The Bluepages govern practitioner documents: briefs, memoranda and papers filed with a court or served on another party. The Whitepages govern law-review and other scholarly legal writing. The clearest daily difference is typeface. Academic footnotes use three faces — ordinary roman, italics, and large and small capitals for institutional authors, book titles and periodical names. Practitioner documents use two, roman and italics or underlining, and never large and small caps. Both italicize signals, `id.` and `supra`, and in both a signal woven into an ordinary prose sentence stays roman. In academic footnotes a case name is roman in a full citation and italic in a short form or in a textual sentence; in a brief it is italicized or underlined throughout.

Signal first, and mean it. Distinguish direct support from inference, analogy, comparison, contradiction and background. Common forms include `see`, `cf.`, `compare ... with ...`, `but see` and `see generally`; choose the one that describes the actual relationship. Capitalize the first signal in a citation sentence and lowercase any that follow. Separate authorities under one signal with semicolons. Verify signal ordering, new forms and authority-order rules against the adopted edition rather than treating an unverified change summary as the rule.

Cite the case, the reporter, the pincite, and the court and year:

```
Case Name, Volume Reporter First Page, Pincite (Court Year).
```

A pincite locates the passage supporting the proposition. A first page alone does not do that, and a correctly formatted page number does not establish that the decision supports the argument. If the locator is missing, flag it for source checking rather than supplying a likely page.

For education law and policy, do not let cases crowd out the legal rule. Cite codified federal statutes as:

```
Title U.S.C. § Section (Code Year).
```

and regulations as:

```
Title C.F.R. § Section (Year).
```

Agency guidance that is not in the C.F.R. or Federal Register needs its own source-specific citation, often identifying the agency, title, date and URL. For an unofficial online code, retain the actual publisher, database and coverage date and check their required ordering. Do not invent a current session or update date from the calendar year.

Shorten with discipline. `Id.` refers only to the immediately preceding authority, with `Id. at [page]` for a different page and bare `Id.` for the same one. Where an authority intervenes, or where the preceding citation was a string, use a case short form instead — one distinctive party name, the volume, the reporter and `at`:

```
Party Name, Volume Reporter at Pincite.
```

`Supra` covers books, articles, hearings and other secondary material: `Author, supra note N, at page`. It does not cover cases, statutes, constitutions, regulations, restatements, model codes, uniform acts, or any legislative material other than hearings; those repeat in full or take their own short forms. Where a short form would be ambiguous, or the author is institutional, establish a label on first citation with `[hereinafter Short Label]`.

For internet sources, retain the identifiable author, title, host, publication or revision date and URL. Archive requirements and their scope were not verified from the full 22nd-edition rule. If the applicable rule requires preservation, use an actual permitted archive or retained copy and describe it accurately. Never fabricate a Perma link or write `on file` without a real retained artifact. An actual access date and an archival snapshot describe different things; neither may be invented to stand in for the other.

For the law of a Tribal Nation, identify the relevant jurisdiction and its own citation requirements instead of automatically replacing them with a commercial parallel. Archival and unpublished materials need their actual collection, item and repository details, not a generic website citation.

Check the actual AI-specific citation rule and forum policy; the full current rule was not independently reviewed here. Preserve the recorded prompt author, tool/model, date and retained output, but do not manufacture missing fields or assert that a screenshot exists. Do not publish or archive confidential prompts merely to satisfy an assumed citation formula. Citation, permission to use AI and required disclosure remain distinct.

Alter quotations visibly under the adopted rule. Brackets mark changes and ellipses mark omissions where required; preserve meaning. Before using a cleanup parenthetical, establish what changes it permits and whether the forum accepts it. Do not replace an accepted `(cleaned up)` with `(citation modified)` on the strength of an unverified edition-change claim.

## Examples

These model the academic form, so signals, `Id.` and `supra` appear in italics. In a brief, use the Bluepages typeface convention your forum expects — usually italics or underlining in citation sentences.

**A signal that overstates the distance**

**Supplied facts:** In this fictional academic citation exercise, Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118 (9th Cir. 2004), states the proposition directly at page 124. This is a supplied hypothetical holding, not advice about an actual statute or court decision.

> The statute does not reach conduct outside the state. *See* Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118, 124 (9th Cir. 2004).

> The statute does not reach conduct outside the state. Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118, 124 (9th Cir. 2004).

**No pincite**

**Supplied facts:** The synthetic source record gives the case metadata below and explicitly identifies page 124 as the passage supporting the proposition. Add that supplied locator, not a guessed one.

> Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118 (9th Cir. 2004).

> Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118, 124 (9th Cir. 2004).

**`Id.` after an intervening authority**

**Supplied facts:** The fictional notes 4 and 5 cite Winslow and Ortiz as shown. Note 6 refers to Winslow at page 126, not to Ortiz. Its reporter and locator are supplied.

> 4. Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118, 124 (9th Cir. 2004). 5. Ortiz v. Delmar, 511 F.3d 900, 906 (2d Cir. 2008). 6. *Id.* at 126. (meaning Winslow)

> 6. *Winslow*, 402 F.3d at 126.

**`Supra` reaching for a case**

**Supplied facts:** The intended fictional authority is Winslow v. Harker, 402 F.3d 118, and the supplied passage is at page 126. Note 4 identifies that same case.

> *Winslow*, *supra* note 4, at 126.

> *Winslow*, 402 F.3d at 126.

**An internet source with no archive**

**Supplied facts:** The synthetic record supplies Nadia Ali, the title, Winslow Institute, March 3, 2025, and the URL below. It supplies no archive URL, retained copy or applicable preservation rule.

> Nadia Ali, *Provenance and the Public Record*, Winslow Inst. (Mar. 3, 2025), https://example.org/provenance.

> Nadia Ali, *Provenance and the Public Record*, Winslow Inst. (Mar. 3, 2025), https://example.org/provenance.
