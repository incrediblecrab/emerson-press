# Education: Post-Graduate

Reader is a doctoral candidate, or a scholar reading doctoral work: past coursework, at work on a dissertation or on the articles that will come out of it. What changes here is the direction the evidence runs. Every tier below draws on the record. This one adds to it, and what gets added will be cited and built on by people the writer will never meet. The genre module in force sets the style manual and the `citation/` module carries it, because a tier never picks one.

Scholarly writing is an intervention in a conversation. The sentence is worn smooth from use, and most writers who repeat it never draw the consequence: if a piece is an intervention, you must be able to say what it changes — what a reader believed before and should believe after. Writing that cannot answer that is not a weak contribution; it is a summary wearing the apparatus of one.

This is where machine-written academic prose fails, and it fails structurally rather than superficially. The grammar is fine. The citations are formatted. What is missing is *stance*, in Hyland's sense: the writer's position toward the propositions, carried by hedges, boosters, attitude markers and self-mention. Its companion, *engagement*, is the separate business of positioning the reader, and a model fails at both for one reason. A model has no stake in a field. It has read the conversation and has no seat in it. So it produces the one genre that requires no position — the survey — and produces it fluently: *Smith argues X. Jones argues Y. Patel notes the complexity of the issue.* Every source draws the same equable, appreciative attention, because there is no ground from which to prefer one. The fix is never "add more analysis." You have to rebuild the paragraph from a position it does not currently hold. The sequence is the symptom. The missing seat is the disease.

The second structural failure is the missing warrant. Toulmin's terms are useful precisely here: a claim and its grounds are both easy to generate, and the warrant — the principle that licenses moving from this evidence to this claim — is the part that requires knowing the field. Student writing and machine writing fail in the same place for opposite reasons. The student has the warrant in mind and does not know it needs stating; the model does not have one.

## Detect

### Under-reach

- A thesis that announces instead of claims: *This paper will explore the various factors...* If nobody could disagree with it, it is a topic.
- Nothing in the piece says what changes if the argument is right, and the conclusion restates the introduction rather than saying what follows from it.
- Sources queued rather than synthesized — *Smith argues, then Jones argues* — with no argument running between them.
- Evidence and claim set side by side with no warrant. The quotation is there, the assertion is there, and the sentence explaining why the first licenses the second is missing.
- Counterargument absent, or built weak enough to knock over.
- Method, archive path or coding decisions described too thinly for another person to retrace them.
- A limitation a reviewer would raise on first reading, left for the reviewer to find.
- Contestable assertions left uncited, or a quotation dropped in unframed and expected to argue on its own.

### Over-reach

- A contribution claimed larger than the work supports: novelty the literature does not actually leave open, or a finding generalized past the case that produced it.
- Signposting inflation: *In this section I will now turn to a discussion of...*
- Boosters compensating for thin evidence: *clearly, obviously, it is undeniable that.*
- Settled points cited to demonstrate reading rather than to support anything.
- Terms of art the writer could not define if stopped and asked.
- Nominalization chains and abstraction standing where the field has a concrete noun, reached for to sound rigorous rather than to name something.
- First person avoided by reflex, leaving agentless passives that hide who chose the method.

### Either way

- Hedging uniform rather than calibrated, so a reader cannot tell which claims the writer would defend, and no hedge names what would settle it.
- Sources characterized in ways their authors would not recognize.
- A source cited through another source's account of it, unmarked.
- Volume of citation standing in for engagement, in either direction: too little to locate the claim in a conversation, or too much to see the argument through.

## Write

- Open with a claim someone in the field could argue with, in one sentence, where a reader can find it. In an essay that is the first page. In an article it is the end of the introduction.
- Answer the so-what in the introduction rather than saving it for a conclusion nobody reaches. Say what is different if you are right.
- State the warrant. When you move from evidence to claim, name the principle that licenses the move — the disciplinary assumption, the methodological commitment, the definition you are relying on. This is the highest-value sentence in most academic paragraphs and the one most often absent.
- Put sources in conversation with each other. Organize by question rather than by author, and say where they part and why. Two scholars who disagree because they measure different things is a paragraph. Two scholars summarized in sequence is a list.
- Frame every quotation and then interpret it. A quotation is evidence for your sentence, never a substitute for it. If the interpretation only restates the quotation in other words, cut one of them. Quote for the wording and paraphrase for the content.
- Write the method, the archive path or the coding decisions so another person could retrace the route and see how you arrived. Past tense, with the choices attributed to whoever made them.
- Report what you found before interpreting it, and keep the two separable, so a reader can accept your evidence and reject your reading of it.
- Hedge where the evidence is genuinely uncertain, and name what would settle it. A hedge with no resolution condition reports nothing. Hedging itself is a disciplinary obligation rather than a weakness — it states where knowledge currently stops.
- Name the limitation that would most worry a hostile reviewer, in the terms that reviewer would use, and state it before the reviewer gets there.
- Steelman the objection. Give the strongest version, in the terms its holders use, and then answer it. An argument that only defeats a weak opponent has demonstrated nothing.
- Write *I* when the sentence is about a choice you made. *I coded the transcripts* is more accurate and more accountable than *the transcripts were coded*. Reflexive avoidance of the first person is a school rule most fields abandoned. Check the field rather than the folk memory.
- Read the draft once as the reviewer who wants it to be wrong, and fix what that reading turns up before anybody else sees it.
- Order the argument so it cannot be reordered. If the paragraphs could be shuffled without loss, they are a list. A dissertation is the same demand at length: the territory section runs longer and the gap has to carry more weight, but nothing about the shape changes.
- A term the field genuinely needs is not over-reach, however forbidding it looks on the page. The over-reach at this tier is a claim of novelty or reach the work does not support.

## Mechanics

The writer is fluent throughout. What remains is the apparatus, and the manual governing it belongs to the genre module and the `citation/` module in force rather than to this tier:

- One citation system throughout a piece, never two, per the `citation/` module loaded beside this one.
- Terms defined at first use and then held. One concept, one name, for the length of the work.
- Quotations never silently repaired. Whether an oddity inside a quotation gets marked, and with what, is the manual's call: AP now directs a writer to paraphrase around it rather than tag it, and the scholarly manuals do not.
- Square brackets around anything you added inside a quotation, including a changed capital. Ellipsis for anything you removed, spaced as the manual directs.
- Block quotations set off at the threshold the manual sets, without quotation marks, with the citation placed where that manual puts it. A block quotation that carries the point the prose should have made is a defect no formatting fixes.
- Data, code, instruments and archival finding aids cited so a reader can reach the same material, not merely described.
- Epigraphs, acknowledgments and funding statements treated as part of the argument's honesty rather than as front matter, because in most fields they carry disclosure obligations.
- Generative-AI use disclosed where the venue asks, and the rule is converging: a tool is not an author, because authorship requires an accountability a tool cannot hold. Name the use and its location — drafting, code, analysis, language editing — as the venue directs, and read the venue's policy rather than assuming it. You remain answerable for every claim and every citation in the submitted text, including any a tool supplied.

## Evidence

Rung 6. The claim enters the record, which sets two tests it did not have to pass at any tier below: it has to survive adversarial review, and it has to be reproducible from what you wrote.

Adversarial review means the argument must hold under a reader who wants it to fail and has the standing to say so. So you put the limitation in before the reviewer finds it, give the counterargument its strongest form, and claim no more than the work supports.

Reproducible from what you wrote means a reader can get from the text back to what you did — rerun the analysis where there is one, or where there is not, walk the same archive and see how you reached the reading. A claim nobody can check has not entered the record, however confidently you state it.

Rung 5 does not lapse here. The writer still quantifies uncertainty wherever the work produced numbers, and the ban on fabricated figures still outranks both rungs: an interval or a robustness check invented to satisfy this one is worse than an absent one, because the record is what other people go on to rely on. Accountability does not divide either. Whatever produced any part of the text, the name on it answers for every claim and every citation in it.

Read this rung against the evidence floor of the `domain/` module beside it, and follow whichever is higher. A tier changes register. It never lowers the standard of proof a domain requires.

## Boundaries

Below, graduate work names a gap in a defined literature, warrants a contribution to closing it, and quantifies the uncertainty in its own results. That is the master's demand, and a committee in one room can satisfy itself that the demand has been met. Here the audience is the field, which is not assembled in one room and cannot be asked a follow-up question. So the niche has to be one the field would agree is open rather than one the writer can defend as open, the contribution has to be worth another scholar's citation, and the method or the archive path has to carry a stranger from your evidence to your conclusion with nobody standing there to explain it.

The ladder ends here, because there is no schooling level above this one. What changes past the doctorate is the venue and the genre rather than the level, so a postdoctoral or faculty writer loads this tier alongside whichever `domain/` module fits the piece. Load no education tier at all and the `domain/` module governs alone, which is the unmarked case and where most adult writing lands.

## Examples

Before: This paper will explore the various factors that contributed to the decline of the guild system in early modern Europe.

After: Guilds in the Low Countries collapsed a generation before those in the German states, and the difference was access to credit rather than competition from rural production.

Before: Smith (2019) argues that wages stagnated in this period. Jones (2021) argues that wages rose. Patel (2022) argues that measurement is difficult.

After: Smith and Jones disagree because they are measuring different things: Smith tracks hourly pay, Jones total compensation including employer-paid insurance. Patel shows that the gap between those two series accounts for most of the apparent dispute, which means the substantive question is not whether wages rose but whether insurance counts as wages.

Before: Forty percent of the parish's households were headed by day laborers by 1780. The enclosure acts therefore remade rural labor.

After: Forty percent of the parish's households were headed by day laborers by 1780, against twelve percent in 1750. Day labor is the category historians use to mark households with no claim on common land, so a shift of that size in thirty years is a change in tenure rather than in occupation — which is what the enclosure acts would predict, and what customary tenure would not.

Before: Ruiz notes that "the enclosure acts remade rural labor." This shows the importance of the enclosure acts.

After: Ruiz calls the enclosure acts a remaking of rural labor. The parish records support the claim narrowly: day laborers rose from 12% of households to 40% in thirty years, but only in parishes that actually enclosed. In the unenclosed parishes of the same county the figure barely moved, which makes the mechanism tenure rather than the general agricultural depression Ruiz also invokes.

Before: It could perhaps be suggested that the policy may have had some effect on enrollment, although further research would be needed.

After: The policy probably raised enrollment, but the two districts that kept usable records are also the two that advertised the change, so the estimate confounds policy with publicity. District-level data for 1994, which the state archive holds, would separate them.

Before: Participants were recruited and the interviews were coded thematically, revealing several key themes.

After: I recruited 22 participants through two community clinics and coded the transcripts against a codebook built from the first six interviews, revising it twice. The codebook and both revision logs are in Appendix B. Recruiting through clinics means everyone in the sample had already sought care, which is the limit on what these accounts can say about people who did not.

Before: This analysis interrogates the multivalent discursive formations through which institutional legitimacy is constituted and contested.

After: Accreditation reports are written to be approved, not to be read, and that shapes what they can say. I compare what three colleges told their accreditor with what they told their trustees in the same year.
