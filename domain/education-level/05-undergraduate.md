# Education: Undergraduate

An optional preset for writing or reviewing undergraduate assignments. It is not a default register for anyone with undergraduate credentials. The assignment, genre, actual familiarity and support needs control: a summary, lab report, explanation and argumentative essay have different jobs.

For an analytical assignment, connect sources and evidence to a defensible claim. Do not add a dissertation's apparatus merely to sound advanced.

WIDA's K–12 framework does not validate this preset. The academic-task guidance here is a house synthesis, not a claim that a particular AAC&U or CWPA version governs the user's program. Follow the institution's supplied rubric; do not infer which edition it adopted. The AP evidence link supports selected mechanics, not these pedagogical judgments.

## Detect

### Under-reach

- Opens by restating the prompt: `this essay will discuss`.
- A fixed essay formula that prevents the requested analysis, not five paragraphs by themselves.
- Paragraphs organized by source rather than by idea: Smith says X, then Jones says Y.
- Sources named but never compared — parallel summary with no synthesis.
- Evidence quoted and then abandoned; no analysis of the quotation itself.
- A relevant competing account or material limitation left unexamined when the argument needs it.
- No motive: the essay answers a question no one was asking. The thesis may be perfectly arguable and still carry no stakes — nothing establishes why it needed arguing.
- Conclusion restates the introduction instead of stating what was shown.

### Over-reach

- Dissertation drag: a literature-review posture over four sources.
- Territory moves compressed to a clause, terms of art left unglossed, as though writing for peers who already share them. Showing the reader you know it is part of the task here, so compression reads as evasion rather than authority.
- Theory names invoked as decoration, doing no work a plain term would not.
- Hedges stacked on ordinary classroom-scope claims: `it could perhaps be tentatively argued that`.
- Sentence length and subordination rising while idea density stays flat.
- Claims outrunning the evidence: `proves`, `demonstrates conclusively`, `the definitive reading`, for a single-text argument.
- Terminology worn as costume — a word the writer could not define under questioning.

### Either way

- Genre conventions ignored: a lab report written like an essay, a close reading written like a policy brief.
- Sources cited but not evaluated. No sense of which is stronger, or why.
- Uniform paragraph length and shape, each opening the same way.
- Numbers reported with no baseline. Compared with what?

## Write

- For an argumentative assignment, make a defensible claim and explain why it matters. A requested summary can remain a summary; do not invent a dispute to make it look analytical.
- Establish the task's motive or question early. A tension, gap or problem can help, but a compulsory opposition formula can misrepresent the sources.
- Put sources in conversation. Say how they relate — agree, extend, conflict — before saying what you think.
- Analyze the evidence you quote. The quotation begins the paragraph's work rather than ending it.
- Address a real counterargument or complicating case when relevant. Do not manufacture one to satisfy a quota.
- Follow the genre your discipline actually uses, and signal that you know which one you are in.
- Use a technical term when it does work plain wording cannot. Otherwise use plain wording.
- Reserve hedges for claims where evidence is genuinely limited. State well-supported points plainly.
- Keep sentence complexity proportional to idea complexity. A simple claim earns a simple sentence.
- Interrogate a source, do not just cite it: who funded it, how it was collected, what its sample leaves out, whether correlation is being sold as causation.
- Name the limits of your own argument before a reader has to.
- Close by stating what the essay established, not by re-summarizing.
- Keep the hard term when you can define it and it does work no plain phrase can. Drop it when it is only difficulty for its own sake.

## Mechanics

Do not infer fluency from enrollment or credentials. The selected genre and venue control mechanics; useful editing checks include:

- Essential and nonessential appositives, where the commas depend on how many candidates the phrase could refer to.
- Parentheses that add useful context without hiding the main clause; they are not defects by themselves.
- Fractions, composition titles, degree names and departments formatted under the selected manual rather than a schooling-based rule.
- Acronyms not imposed on a reader who would not recognize them.
- Quotation wording and meaning preserved, with any permitted alteration or editorial notation governed by the selected manual. Do not impose AP's handling on another citation style.
- `compose`, `comprise`, and `constitute`; descriptive versus possessive compounds.

The publication picks its style family, including exceptions. Bibliographic rendering belongs to `citation/`; reader support does not override it. Inconsistency warrants an edit, not an inference about who wrote the draft.

## Evidence

The legacy `evidence_rung: 4` marks a source-evaluation practice focus: relevant funding, collection, scope and uncertainty. Apply it to the claims actually being made. A comparative figure needs a meaningful comparison; an absolute count need not acquire an invented historical baseline.

The assignment determines the evidence form. This preset neither lowers domain safeguards nor requires new quantitative research for a qualitative argument.

## Boundaries

Use this preset only for the academic task it fits. Source synthesis and evaluation are not exclusive to undergraduate work, and an undergraduate reader seeking ordinary instructions does not need them imposed on the answer.

The graduate presets provide additional research-writing checks when that work is explicitly requested. They do not turn credentials into expertise, make all master's work original research or require numerical uncertainty where none is meaningful.

## Examples

These independent synthetic records supply the sources and fictional passages used below. They do not report a literature review performed by this repository.

**A literature introduction without an invented causal verdict**

**Supplied facts:** Two supplied fictional studies examine adolescent screen use and mood. One reports an association; the other explains why the observational design does not establish causation. Neither tests displacement of sleep against effects of platform content. The task asks for an introductory comparison.

Before: Social media is a very important topic in today's society. Many studies have looked at how it affects teenagers. This essay will discuss some of the effects of social media on mental health.

After: The supplied studies link screen use and adolescent mood but do not establish that one causes the other. This essay compares their designs and the limits of their causal claims.

**A reading without a caricatured school of thought**

**Supplied facts:** In the supplied fictional poem, a central image changes meaning each time it returns. The essay argues that this pattern makes a single stable reading difficult. No account of every structuralist critic is supplied.

Before: It could perhaps be tentatively suggested that, within the broader epistemological framework of post-structuralist discourse, the aforementioned phenomenon may potentially problematize existing paradigmatic assumptions regarding textual signification.

After: The poem's central image changes meaning each time it returns. This essay argues that those shifts make a single, stable reading hard to sustain.

**Synthesis that keeps a material limitation**

**Supplied facts:** In these fictional source notes, Smith attributes a decline to cost and Jones to habit. Lee's survey supports the habit account among its urban respondents. Patel notes that the sample excludes rural users. The author wants to examine Jones's account without generalizing beyond that evidence.

Before: Smith discusses the causes of the decline. Jones also discusses the decline. Additionally, Lee examines a related aspect. Patel further explores this topic.

After: Smith and Jones agree that the decline is real but differ over its explanation: cost or habit. Lee's survey supports the habit account among urban respondents; Patel notes that it excludes rural users. This essay examines the habit account within that limit.

**Theory as interpretation rather than causal proof**

**Supplied facts:** The assignment supplies a reading of Foucault that treats surveillance as productive rather than merely restrictive. In a fictional county record, electronic monitoring expands while the jail population falls. The record does not establish what caused the policy change.

Before: Foucault talks about power. This relates to the prison system in important ways that show how power works in society.

After: Electronic monitoring expanded while the county's jail population fell. Foucault's account of surveillance offers a way to read that shift as an extension of control, not simply a reduction in confinement. This interpretation does not establish what caused the policy change.

**Close reading tied to the supplied passage**

**Supplied facts:** The fictional passage says exactly, "The sea was gray and vast." Its viewpoint character, Mira, faces an uncertain journey. The task asks for a supported interpretation of the adjectives.

Before: The author uses imagery. "The sea was gray and vast." This shows the mood of the passage.

After: "Gray" gives the sea a muted color; "vast" leaves it without an obvious boundary. Together, the adjectives connect the landscape to Mira's uncertainty about the journey.
