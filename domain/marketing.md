# Domain: Marketing

Copy that sells. Landing pages, product descriptions, campaigns, press releases.

This is the only domain in the directory where the writer's interest is structurally opposed to the reader's. Everywhere else the two align: the technical writer wants the reader unstuck, the clinician wants the patient informed, the lawyer wants the client to understand their exposure. A copywriter is paid when the reader buys, whether or not buying was good for them. That conflict is not a moral accusation, it is a description of the incentive, and it is why this is the one kind of writing with a federal agency devoted to policing whether it is true.

Which produces the fact that organizes this module: **marketing is the only genre where the evidence must exist before publication as a matter of law, and where the reader never sees it.** The FTC's substantiation doctrine requires an advertiser to possess and rely on a reasonable basis for an objective claim at the time it is made — not to be able to find one later if challenged. Academia displays its citations. Law cites its authority in the sentence. Marketing holds its evidence in a file the reader will never open and asks to be believed anyway. Everything that makes this domain hazardous follows from that asymmetry.

Now the argument that matters for style, because it is where most copy actually dies. **Puffery is non-actionable and rhetorically dead.** Subjective, unfalsifiable boasts — *the world's best coffee*, *a magical experience* — are not actionable because they are not statements of fact a reasonable consumer would rely on. Read that as a writer rather than a lawyer: what buys the safety is that the claim cannot be checked, so nothing in it can be shown false. And the safety is decided after the fact and in context — a superlative that implies a measurable comparison is not puffery, whatever it sounded like in the draft. So when review cycles push copy away from the specific and toward the general, they are pushing it into a register that is safe because it is inert. The vague claim and the empty claim are the same claim. The specific, substantiated one — *38% fewer support tickets in the first quarter across 240 accounts* — is simultaneously the more persuasive and the more defensible, provided the evidence exists. Risk aversion practiced without evidence produces the worst available outcome: copy nobody believes and nobody remembers.

This is also where machine-written prose is not merely bad but actionable. The FTC's 2024 rule prohibits fabricated consumer reviews and testimonials, including AI-generated ones, with civil penalties per violation. A generated testimonial is not a style problem.

## Detect

### The claim

- An objective claim with no evidence behind it: performance, speed, savings, health, safety, efficacy, environmental benefit.
- A comparative with no comparison — *faster*, *more affordable*, *better results* — missing what it was measured against, how, and when.
- A statistic with no source, no sample, and no date. Round confident numbers (*10k+ users*, *99.9% uptime*) on a product with no history to produce them. One invented figure discredits every true number beside it.
- Testimonials that are composite, edited past recognition, incentivized without disclosure, or generated.
- Results presented as typical when they are the best case.
- *Eco-friendly*, *green*, *sustainable*, *non-toxic*, *natural* standing alone. General environmental benefit claims are effectively unsupportable unqualified.
- Health, structure-function, or efficacy language on a product not held to a medical standard.
- Disclosures in a footnote, a hover, a lighter gray, or after the button. A disclosure that a reader can miss has not been made.

### The register

- Named constructions: *It's not just X — it's Y. Say goodbye to X. Meet your new X. Supercharge your workflow. Unlock the power of X. In seconds, not hours.*
- Default intensifiers: *seamless, effortless, blazing fast, game-changing, revolutionary, next-level, robust, enterprise-grade.*
- Copy that survives a competitor's name being swapped in. If nothing breaks, the claim has no content.
- Dismissal as a personality: *no more X theater*, *X, reimagined*.
- Ordinals used as an ordering claim — 01 / 02 / 03 over items in no sequence.
- Punchy triads set as paragraphs. *Fast. Beautiful. Yours.*
- Benefits with no mechanism. *Save hours every week* with no account of what the product does.
- Manufactured urgency: countdowns that reset, scarcity that is not scarce.
- Pricing revealed a step at a time, or a subscription whose cancellation path is harder than its signup.

## Write

Make one claim, make it specific, and hold the evidence before publishing. *Cuts onboarding from three weeks to four days, measured across 240 accounts in Q1 2026* is a sentence you can defend and a sentence a reader can believe. Both properties come from the same source.

Name the comparison. Against what, measured how, on what population, when.

Attribute every number in the copy itself where it is load-bearing, and keep the substantiation file current for everything else. If the number cannot be sourced, cut it — never round it into plausibility.

Say what the product does before saying what it means for the reader. A benefit with no mechanism under it is the claim a reader discounts fastest, because they have read a thousand of them.

Use real customers, quoted accurately, with material connections disclosed clearly and in the same medium as the claim. Never write a testimonial. Never generate one.

Qualify environmental claims to the specific attribute and the specific extent: *packaging is 60% post-consumer recycled fiber*, not *sustainable packaging*.

Put disclosures where the claim is, in the same size and the same weight, before the decision rather than after it.

Say what the product does not do and who it is not for. It costs one sentence, it is the cheapest credibility available, and it is the line most likely to be quoted back approvingly.

Delete the intensifier and see whether the sentence still makes a claim. If it does not, the intensifier was doing all the work and the claim was empty.

## Mechanics

AP style for the prose, no citation apparatus in the copy itself. AP is the default because the press release is the format most likely to be read by a journalist, and because most house guides in the field are AP with local amendments.

- Attribution in the sentence for load-bearing numbers, with the date.
- Every claim mapped to a substantiation file: what supports it, who produced the support, when, and when it expires.
- Disclosures unavoidable — same medium, adjacent to the claim, no hover, no asterisk to a footer.
- Material connections stated in plain words, not a hashtag buried in a tag block.
- Prices complete on first appearance: total, term, renewal, and what is excluded.
- Trademarks and required legal lines carried where the jurisdiction demands, without letting them substitute for a disclosure that has to be conspicuous.
- Claims dated and re-verified on a schedule. A substantiated claim decays as the product changes.

## Formats

### Landing page

One page, one stranger, one question: is this for me?

This module governs the **claim**; `user-interface/website.md` governs the **page** — hero, kicker, badge, stat row, display type, and the surface conventions that go with them. Compose both. Where they overlap, the substantiation floor here is not negotiable by layout.

Lead with what the product does, in the reader's words, specifically enough that the wrong reader can leave. State the mechanism, then the evidence, then the ask. One primary action.

The characteristic failure is a page assembled out of interchangeable parts — generic headline, three-benefit grid, invented metrics, testimonial with no name. It reads as a template because it is one, and it converts accordingly.

### Product copy

Short, dense, decision-supporting. The reader is comparing.

Give specifications rather than adjectives: dimensions, materials, capacity, compatibility, what is in the box. *Fits a 16-inch laptop* beats *spacious* because it settles the question.

State limitations plainly. The return rate and the review score both improve, which is the commercial argument for honesty and it happens to be true.

Say who it is not for.

### Campaign email

Permission-based, competing with a full inbox, and the only marketing format the reader can end permanently with one click.

Subject line describes the contents truthfully. A subject that misrepresents to earn an open trades a long relationship for a single impression.

One offer, one action, the terms visible before the button, and an unsubscribe that works in one step.

Segment on behavior rather than writing to everyone and calling it a campaign.

### Press release

Marketing prose wearing journalism's clothes, which is what makes it hazardous and what makes it useful. A reporter will read it looking for a reason not to.

Lead with what is actually new, in a sentence that could run as written. Put the verifiable facts high: what, who, when, how much, available where.

Attribute every quote to a named person who said it, and cut the executive quote that says nothing. Mark the claims a reporter can check, and be reachable for the ones they will.

`domain/press.md` describes what the receiving newsroom does with it. Writing to that standard is the whole technique.

## Evidence

Rung 4 as the floor — the source is interrogated — and rung 5 for any claim touching health, safety, efficacy, or environmental benefit, where the standard is competent and reliable scientific evidence rather than an internal estimate.

Two properties make this the strangest rung in the directory. The evidence must exist **before** publication, not on demand afterward, so a claim written in anticipation of finding support has already failed. And it is **held rather than shown**: the reader is asked to extend trust without seeing the basis, which is why the domain earns tighter scrutiny than the visible-citation genres, not looser.

Fabrication here is not a rung failure. It is a legal one.

This is a floor, not a setting. Where the audience tier sits at a higher rung, the tier governs; where this floor is higher, it governs. Plain writing for a non-specialist is a change of register and never a lower standard of proof.

## Boundaries

Against `user-interface/`. The clearest split in the repository and a recent one. `user-interface/website.md` owns the marketing page's surface; this module owns the truth of what the page says, in every medium — email, print, script, packaging, release. A tell listed in both places is listed once as a page convention and once as a claim about the world.

Against `medical`. The dangerous overlap. Pharmaceutical, device, supplement, and wellness copy sit in both, and `medical`'s evidence floor and risk-communication rules govern the numbers. A mechanism, a testimonial, or a study in mice does not substantiate a health claim.

Against `press`. A press release is marketing; the story written from it is journalism. Copy that mimics reportorial form to borrow its credibility — the native ad, the sponsored post — must be labeled, and the label is a disclosure subject to the conspicuousness standard above.

Against `technical`. Product documentation drifts into this register whenever the organization that sells the thing also documents it. `technical` wins there absolutely: a document a stuck reader is using is not a sales surface.

Against `core/anti-slop.md`. Substantial overlap and a division of labor. `core` bans the constructions wherever they appear; this module explains why they cluster here, and adds the obligation `core` cannot know about — that the claim be substantiated before it ships.

With `audience/`. Register moves freely across tiers. The substantiation floor does not. Copy written for a reader with less capacity to interrogate a claim may not exploit that gap, and simplification that removes a material qualifier is a disclosure failure rather than an editing decision.

## Examples

**Puffery, which is safe because it persuades nobody**

> The world's most powerful project management platform. Seamlessly supercharge your team's workflow and unlock unprecedented productivity.

> Teams using shared boards closed sprints an average of four days earlier across 240 accounts in Q1 2026. It replaces the standup, not the spec.

**Numbers assembled to look like numbers**

> 10,000+ happy customers · 99.9% uptime · 5x faster · #1 rated

> 1,847 paying teams. 99.94% uptime over the last 12 months, measured by Pingdom, status page linked. Median import time 4.2 seconds versus 21 seconds on our previous engine, same 50,000-row file.

**A comparative with nothing to compare to**

> Our batteries last significantly longer.

> Rated 800 charge cycles to 80% capacity, against an industry norm of 500. Tested to IEC 61960 by an independent lab; report linked.

**Environmental claim standing alone**

> Eco-friendly, sustainable packaging. Good for the planet.

> The mailer is 100% post-consumer recycled paperboard and is curbside recyclable in most US municipalities. The inner sleeve is not — we have not found a replacement that survives shipping.

**A testimonial that should not exist**

> "This product completely transformed our business. I can't imagine working without it!" — Sarah M., Marketing Director

> "We cut our onboarding from three weeks to four days. Setup took a whole afternoon and the CSV importer choked twice." — Sarah Mendez, Director of Marketing Operations, Meridian Logistics. Meridian receives no compensation for this quote.

**Register borrowed from a template**

> It's not just a notebook — it's your second brain. Say goodbye to scattered thoughts. Meet your new creative companion. Fast. Beautiful. Yours.

> A5, 192 pages, 120gsm paper that takes fountain ink without ghosting. Lies flat. No perforation, no pocket, no elastic — we cut them to keep it at $18.
