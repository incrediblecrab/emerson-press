# Audience: Professional

Writing done after schooling ends, for a reader with something to do. Memos, briefs, reports, papers, release notes, board materials, patient handouts, public guidance. This is the default when the reader is unknown, because most adult writing lands here.

Nobody is grading you. For a reader who has to act, that fact reorganizes everything the schooling ladder taught. Conclusions come first rather than last. Hedging reads as evasion rather than rigor. Citation narrows to the evidence that changes the decision. The document succeeds if someone can act on it, not if it demonstrates command of a literature. For a peer reader it reorganizes far less — calibrated hedging and located citation survive intact — and the passage on writing to peers below says where the reversals stop.

What varies inside this tier is the reader, and the variation is sharp enough to reverse specific instructions. Three recur:

- the **decision-maker**, who will act on this and is short on time
- the **peer**, who knows the field, the methods, and the open questions
- the **non-specialist**, who is capable and does not share your vocabulary

The clearest place they diverge is a term of art. For a peer it is shorter and more exact than its gloss, and refusing it wastes their time. For a non-specialist the same word is a wall they stop at. One sentence can be correct for one reader and malpractice for another, so identify the reader before drafting rather than after.

Two errors are worth naming early. Treating *non-specialist* as a synonym for *simple* produces writing that manages to be condescending and uninformative at once: what cannot be assumed is the vocabulary, not the intelligence. Writing to peers, the error runs the other way — the apparatus that demonstrated competence to an evaluator now reads as padding. Genuinely expert prose is often plainer than the prose of someone reaching for authority, which is worth sitting with, because imitating expertise produces the opposite.

## Detect

### Under-reach

- Findings with no recommendation. The document ends where it should start.
- A recommendation with no cost, risk, timeline, owner, or alternative.
- No statement of what happens if nobody acts.
- Canonical results explained to peers who have taught them.
- Terms defined for an audience that defined them.
- Ideas flattened for a non-specialist on the theory that they cannot follow an argument.
- Analogy carrying the whole explanation, so the reader finishes with a feeling in place of a mechanism.
- Substance drained until only the reassuring shape of a document remains.

### Over-reach

- Academic register in a decision document: literature-review framing, methodology before the answer, extended hedging.
- Terms of art left unglossed for a reader who does not have them.
- Institutional shorthand and acronyms that mean nothing outside a building.
- Comprehensive citation of everything read, rather than the two facts that change the decision.
- Formalism past what the argument requires — notation or derivation as display.
- Claims outrunning the evidence, which among peers means outrunning what a reviewer will let pass.
- Novelty asserted rather than located. Saying a result is new in place of saying what it is new relative to.
- Reach beyond your own subfield stated with in-field confidence.

### Either way

- Bottom line buried. The reader reaches page two before learning what you want.
- Passive constructions hiding the actor: `it was determined that`, `mistakes were made`.
- Nominalizations replacing verbs: `conduct an evaluation of` for `evaluate`.
- Wall-of-text formatting for a document that will be scanned.
- A number with no period, baseline, or source, so its size means nothing.
- Hedging where the reader needs a position. In a memo, `it could be argued` reads as unwillingness to be accountable.
- The contribution or the ask never stated plainly in one sentence.
- Limitations acknowledged in a register that makes them sound harmless.
- Prose density mistaken for rigor: nominalization chains, agentless passives, abstraction where the concrete term is also the accurate one.

## Write

Whatever the reader:

- Lead with the answer. Recommendation or claim, then reasoning, then detail.
- Say who does what by when. Name the owner, the date, and the cost.
- Give the alternatives you rejected and why. That is what makes a recommendation trustworthy.
- Use active verbs and the shortest accurate word. Turn nominalizations back into verbs: `decide`, not `make a determination`.
- Quantify. Give the number, the period it covers, and the source.
- State uncertainty once, plainly, and say what would resolve it. Then commit.
- Apply the so-what test to every paragraph. If it does not change what the reader thinks or does, cut it.
- Write for scanning: informative headings, front-loaded paragraphs, lists only where the content is genuinely a list.
- Assume the document will be forwarded to someone with no context.
- Keep plain language, which is the shortest accurate version of something genuinely complicated.

Writing to a peer:

- Compress the territory. One or two sentences locate the problem for someone who already lives in it. CARS describes the move; compressing it for a peer is this module's application rather than a claim in the model.
- Use the exact term. Do not gloss it, and do not reach for a plainer word that is slightly wrong.
- Cite to locate a claim in the conversation, not to demonstrate reading.
- Say what would falsify the claim, or what result would have changed your conclusion.
- Address the strongest objection a reviewer in your subfield would raise, in their terms.
- Let plain syntax carry technical content. The vocabulary is already doing the specialist work.
- Mark the boundary of your own competence when the argument crosses it.

Writing to a non-specialist:

- Assume intelligence, not knowledge. That distinction does most of the work.
- Say why it matters in the first few sentences, in terms of something the reader already cares about.
- Gloss a term the first time and then use it. Do not route the reader around the real word when they are better served owning it.
- Prefer the concrete instance, then name the general statement it illustrates.
- Give every number a comparison: against what, over what period, compared to whom.
- Explain the mechanism rather than only the result. An analogy is scaffolding, and the building still has to go up.
- Vary sentence length deliberately. A short sentence after a long one is the cheapest emphasis available.
- Do not perform expertise. The reader cannot verify credentials and is not impressed by vocabulary.

## Mechanics

Grammar and style are assumed fluent. What is in play here is institutional:

- Corrections labeled plainly and pushed everywhere the error ran.
- Anonymous sourcing, where used at all, approved rather than assumed, with the reason stated in the document itself.
- A documented, shareable methodology behind any analysis you publish.
- Fabrication and staging prohibited without exception, in text and image.
- Field-standard reporting conventions followed exactly, per the `citation/` and `domain/` modules in force.
- Effect sizes given alongside significance; absolute alongside relative.
- Uncertainty reported in the form the field expects, and interpreted rather than merely stated.
- Prior work characterized as its authors would recognize.
- Notation defined once if nonstandard, and not at all if standard.
- Acronyms expanded on first use, or dropped.
- Accessibility treated as part of the writing: alt text, captions, headings that work in a screen reader, link text that means something out of context.
- Relevance test before naming anyone's race, age, disability, immigration status, or gender. Include the detail only when it changes the reader's understanding, and be able to say what work it is doing.
- Specific description preferred to a category label. Ask for a person's own usage rather than inferring it.
- One style family held consistently, per the `domain/` module in force.

## Evidence

Off the numeric ladder rather than on top of it. The five rungs measure a writer's schooling; this tier measures an institution's answerability, so the two are comparable but not sequential. Individual claims still need sourcing, and how much of that sourcing surfaces in the prose depends on the reader: peer-facing work shows rung-5 working, with the design and its limits stated; decision documents show only the evidence that changes the decision, at whatever rung that evidence sits; non-specialist prose may state a claim at rung 3, naming and characterizing a traceable origin, without walking the reader through the interval. What changes is how much of the work is visible, never how much was done. Where a `domain/` module sets a higher evidence floor, that floor governs regardless of reader.

What defines the tier is the accountability wrapped around all of it: who approved this, how it gets corrected, what was verified and what was not. Writing for peers adds a further obligation, since the claim will be cited, built on, and taught. Overclaiming propagates.

Where this tier and a `domain/` evidence floor disagree, the higher of the two governs. The tier sets register; it never lowers the standard of proof a domain requires.

## Boundaries

Below, graduate work contributes to what a field knows, for evaluators who reward comprehensive citation and calibrated hedging. Here the success criterion becomes enabling a decision or an understanding, as efficiently as the reader's attention allows. The document organizes around the reader's next action rather than the field's next open question.

There is no tier above. What varies from here is domain, not level — a policy brief, a journal article, a press release, and release notes are this tier in different `domain/` modules.

## Examples

Before: Following a comprehensive review of the various options that were considered, it was determined that a number of potential approaches may warrant further consideration by relevant stakeholders.

After: Recommend consolidating the three vendor contracts into one by Q1. Saves $180K annually. Requires 60 days of legal review and carries a service-gap risk in March, which the existing support retainer can cover.

Before: Mistakes were made in the handling of the customer data, and steps are being taken to ensure that such issues do not recur going forward.

After: We exposed 1,200 customer email addresses for six days in April. A misconfigured storage bucket caused it. We closed the bucket on April 22, notified affected customers on April 24, and added a deployment check that blocks public buckets. No other data was reachable.

Before: Machine learning is a subfield of artificial intelligence in which algorithms learn patterns from data. In this paper, we use a neural network, which is a model composed of layers of interconnected nodes.

After: We fine-tune a 7B decoder-only model on the paired corpus and compare against the standard retrieval baseline.

Before: It is possible that our results may perhaps suggest that there could be some relationship, though we acknowledge that further research would be needed before drawing conclusions.

After: The association holds in both cohorts and survives adjustment for the usual confounders. It is observational, so the causal reading depends on the instrument in Section 4 being valid, and we give the test for that.

Before: The intervention demonstrated statistically significant efficacy across the treatment cohort relative to controls (p < .01).

After: Patients who got the drug were about a third less likely to be hospitalized than those who did not. The gap is large enough that it is unlikely to be chance.

Before: The Fed's decision reflects ongoing tension between its dual mandate obligations and current PCE readings relative to the 2% target.

After: The Federal Reserve is asked to do two things that pull against each other: keep prices stable and keep people employed. Right now prices are rising faster than its 2% target, and cooling them usually means slowing hiring. That is the trade it is making.

Before: The team will endeavor to facilitate the implementation of the aforementioned initiatives in a timely manner.

After: Dana ships the migration by March 14. If the vendor slips, we fall back to the current system and revisit in Q3.
